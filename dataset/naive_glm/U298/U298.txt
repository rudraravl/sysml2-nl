This model describes a medical gas delivery system for a hospital or healthcare facility. It's designed to provide a continuous and reliable supply of medical-grade oxygen from a central source to various patient care areas, such as operating rooms, intensive care units (ICUs), and emergency rooms.

The system's core is built around redundancy to ensure an uninterrupted gas supply, which is critical for patient safety. It features two separate banks of high-pressure oxygen cylinders: a primary bank for normal use and a secondary (backup) bank.

A central manifold controller acts as the brain of the system. It continuously monitors the pressure of the primary cylinder bank. If this pressure drops below a predefined level, indicating the bank is nearly empty, the controller automatically and seamlessly switches the supply over to the secondary bank. This ensures that there is no interruption in the flow of oxygen to the patients.

After the gas leaves the active cylinder bank, it passes through a pressure regulator. This device steps down the very high pressure from the cylinders to a safe, standardized pressure suitable for the hospital's internal distribution pipelines. This network of pipes then carries the regulated oxygen throughout the facility to outlet terminals located on the walls in patient rooms and treatment areas.

For safety and maintenance, the entire system is monitored by an alarm panel. This panel alerts medical and maintenance staff when the system has switched over to the secondary supply, signaling that the primary cylinders need to be replaced. It also triggers a more critical alarm if the secondary bank's pressure also becomes dangerously low, indicating an impending emergency.
