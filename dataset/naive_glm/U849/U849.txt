This model describes an automated, electronic tipping-bucket rain gauge system, designed to accurately measure and record precipitation.

The system is composed of several key components that work together to capture and quantify rainfall. At the top is a collector funnel with a precisely defined surface area (100 cm²) that captures falling rain. This funnel channels the collected water into a tipping bucket mechanism below.

The core of the system is this tipping bucket mechanism. It consists of a small, two-sided bucket balanced on a pivot. As water flows from the funnel, it fills one side of the bucket. When a specific, calibrated volume of water is collected (5.0 mL), the weight of the water causes the bucket to tip over, emptying the water and bringing the other side of the bucket into position to collect more.

Each time the bucket tips, it triggers an electronic signal. This signal is sent to a measurement processor. The processor counts these tips over time. Knowing the exact volume of water per tip and the collection area of the funnel, it calculates important meteorological data: the total accumulated rainfall volume, the precipitation depth (in millimeters), and the current rainfall rate.

Finally, a data recorder receives these calculated measurements from the processor. It logs the rainfall depth, rate, and total volume at regular intervals (every 60 seconds) to create a time-stamped historical record of the precipitation event. The entire system is engineered to meet specific performance requirements, including an accuracy of ±2% and the ability to measure rainfall rates up to 300 mm/hr with a resolution of 0.5 mm.
