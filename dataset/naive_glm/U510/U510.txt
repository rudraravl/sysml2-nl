This model describes a "Handy Billy," a portable, gasoline-powered emergency pump system. It is designed for rapid deployment in situations like firefighting, dewatering flooded compartments on a ship, or other general water transfer emergencies.

The system is built around a core assembly mounted on a galvanized steel frame for durability and portability. A 4-kilowatt gasoline engine provides the power, fueled by a small, integrated fuel tank designed to allow for at least two hours of continuous operation. The engine is directly coupled to a centrifugal pump, which is the heart of the system.

In operation, the pump draws water from an external source, such as the sea or a flooded area, through a suction hose. A strainer attached to the end of the suction hose filters out debris to protect the pump from damage. The centrifugal pump then pressurizes the water and forces it out through a discharge hose. At the end of the discharge hose is a nozzle, which forms the pressurized water into a powerful, directed jet suitable for fighting fires or other tasks.

The system is designed to be lightweight enough (under 50 kg) for two people to carry and can be set up and running in under three minutes. Its operational lifecycle includes distinct states such as being idle, priming (where the pump and suction hose are filled with water to ensure proper function), running, and shutting down. It also includes monitoring for potential faults like engine overheating, low fuel, or pump cavitation.
