This is a model of an emergency medical response system, specifically centered around an advanced life support unit named "Squad 51." The system is designed to provide rapid, professional pre-hospital care, from the moment an emergency is reported until the patient is safely delivered to a hospital.

The core of the system is Squad 51, a unit composed of a highly-trained paramedic team and their specialized rescue vehicle. The team includes a Paramedic Captain, who has command authority, and at least two other paramedics. Their rescue vehicle is not just for transport; it's a mobile emergency room, fully stocked with a wide array of medical equipment (like defibrillators), disposable medical supplies, and specialized rescue tools.

The entire system operates in a coordinated ecosystem with two other key entities: a Dispatch Center and a Base Hospital.

1.  **Dispatch Center:** This is the central command that receives emergency calls, determines the nature and priority of the incident, and dispatches Squad 51 to the scene with critical information like the location and type of emergency.
2.  **Base Hospital ("Rampart General"):** This hospital acts as the medical authority for the paramedics in the field. The squad maintains constant communication with the hospital to transmit patient vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure, etc.) and receive real-time medical direction and authorization for advanced procedures.

The system's operation follows a clear, sequential process for handling an emergency call:

*   **Dispatch and Response:** The squad receives a call and immediately responds, traveling to the scene.
*   **On-Scene Assessment & Care:** Upon arrival, the paramedics assess the patient's condition. Based on the severity, they either perform standard treatments or contact the Base Hospital for orders on advanced life support.
*   **Stabilization and Transport:** After stabilizing the patient, they prepare them for transport.
*   **Hospital Transfer:** The squad transports the patient to the hospital, continuing care en route. Once at the hospital, they formally transfer the patient's care to the hospital staff.
*   **Return to Service:** The mission concludes with Squad 51 returning to their station, ready for the next emergency.

Throughout this entire process, the squad's status is tracked through different operational states, such as `in service`, `dispatched`, `on scene`, `transporting`, and `at hospital`. This ensures their availability and current activity are always known within the emergency response network.
