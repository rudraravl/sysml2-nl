This is a capacitive sensing system designed to detect the touch and proximity of objects, such as a human finger, without requiring physical pressure. It is the type of technology commonly used for touchscreens, touch-sensitive buttons on appliances, and proximity sensors in consumer electronics or automotive systems.

The system's core principle is to measure tiny changes in electrical capacitance. It operates by using an **Excitation Generator** to create a stable, high-frequency electric field around a **Sensing Electrode**, which is the physical surface a user would interact with. When a conductive object like a finger approaches or touches this electrode, it disrupts the electric field and changes the capacitance.

This change is detected and processed through a series of components. The weak electrical response from the electrode is first sent to a **Signal Conditioner**, which amplifies the signal and filters out environmental noise for a clear reading. A **Capacitance Converter** then takes this clean analog signal and converts it into a precise digital value representing the measured capacitance.

The "brain" of the system is the **Processing Unit**. It continuously compares the real-time capacitance measurement to a baseline value that was established during a calibration phase. If the change in capacitance exceeds a certain **proximity threshold**, the system reports that an object is nearby and can even estimate its distance. If the change is larger and crosses a higher **touch threshold**, it registers a definitive touch event. A dedicated **Power Management** unit ensures that all components receive a stable voltage to operate correctly.

The system operates in several states. It typically starts in an `idle` mode, then performs a `calibration` to learn the baseline capacitance of its environment. Once calibrated, it enters a continuous `monitoring` state. Upon detecting an object, it can transition to a `proximityActive` or `touchActive` state to track the interaction before returning to monitoring. This entire process allows the system to provide a reliable, non-mechanical way to create interactive surfaces and detect presence.
