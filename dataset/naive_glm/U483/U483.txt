This model describes a section of an industrial piping system designed to manage fluid flow. The core of the system is a specialized pipe fitting known as a **concentric reducer**.

The primary function of this system is to smoothly transition a moving fluid, such as a liquid or gas, from a larger diameter pipe to a smaller one. This is a common requirement in industrial processes like chemical plants, water treatment facilities, or HVAC systems where flow characteristics need to be altered. The term "concentric" means the reducer is symmetrical, shaped like a cone, ensuring the centerline of the pipe remains straight.

The system is composed of several key physical components:
*   An **upstream pipe segment** with a large diameter, which brings the fluid into the system.
*   The **concentric reducer** itself, which connects to the end of the upstream pipe. It gradually tapers down from the larger inlet diameter to a smaller outlet diameter. It is designed with a specific taper angle to prevent excessive turbulence and energy loss.
*   A **downstream pipe segment** with a smaller diameter, which receives the fluid from the reducer's outlet and carries it away.

As the fluid passes through the reducer, its properties change according to fundamental principles of fluid dynamics. Because the pipe narrows, the fluid must speed up to maintain a constant flow rate. This increase in velocity is accompanied by a predictable drop in pressure. The system is engineered to manage this transition efficiently, ensuring the mass of the fluid entering the reducer is the same as the mass exiting it.

To monitor performance and ensure proper operation, the system is instrumented with sensors at both the inlet and outlet of the reducer. These include **flow meters** to measure the volume of fluid passing through per unit of time and **pressure sensors** to measure the fluid's pressure. This instrumentation allows for the continuous verification that the reducer is functioning correctly—confirming that the velocity increases as expected and that the pressure loss remains within acceptable operational limits.
