This model describes a Video Disk Recorder (VDR) system, a device designed to receive live television broadcasts, record selected programs to an internal digital storage drive, and play them back on demand.

The system is composed of several key functional units working together. A **Tuner Unit** receives an incoming broadcast signal (e.g., from an antenna or cable), isolates a specific channel selected by the user, and outputs its raw video and audio streams.

For recording, these streams are passed to an **Encoder Unit**, which compresses the video and audio data into a more compact digital format. This process is crucial for efficiently using storage space. The compressed data, along with metadata like the program's title and channel, is then sent to the **Storage Unit**. This unit acts as the device's hard drive, managing a library of recorded programs with a total capacity of 500 GB. It handles writing new recordings, reading existing ones, and deleting them.

For playback, the process is reversed. A selected program is retrieved from the storage unit and sent to a **Decoder Unit**. The decoder decompresses the data back into standard video and audio streams, which are then sent to a display output, such as a television.

A central **Controller Unit** serves as the brain of the system. It receives commands from the user—such as record, play, pause, and stop—and orchestrates the actions of all other components. The controller operates based on a state machine, managing the system's current status, whether it is idle, actively recording, playing back a show, or paused. The system is designed to handle simultaneous operations, such as recording one program while playing back another.
