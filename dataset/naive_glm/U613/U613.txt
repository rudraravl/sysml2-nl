This is an integrated Bio-Field Effect Transistor (Bio-FET) detection system designed for the real-time, label-free detection and quantification of specific biomolecules in liquid samples. The system's primary function is to convert the biological event of a molecule binding to a sensor into a measurable electrical signal, allowing for highly sensitive analysis.

The system is composed of several key components that work in a sequence. At its heart is the **Bio-FET transducer**, which acts as the core sensor. This transducer features a specialized gate surface that is "functionalized," meaning it is coated with receptor molecules designed to selectively capture only a specific target biomolecule, like a lock and key. Underneath this gate lies a semiconductor channel.

The process begins with the **microfluidic delivery system**, which uses micropumps and fine channels to precisely control the flow of liquids. It first introduces a buffer solution to establish a stable baseline electrical reading, then delivers the sample containing the target biomolecule to the transducer's surface.

When the target biomolecules in the sample bind to the receptors on the functionalized gate, their inherent electrical charge alters the charge density at the gate's surface. This change in the electric field directly affects the conductivity of the semiconductor channel below, modulating the amount of electrical current that can flow through it between a source and a drain electrode.

This change in current, while directly proportional to the number of captured molecules, is extremely small. To measure it, a **transimpedance amplifier** converts this tiny current into a larger, more robust voltage signal. This analog voltage is then digitized by an **analog-to-digital converter (ADC)**.

Finally, a **signal processing unit** receives this digital data. It compares the signal from the sample against the initial baseline reading and uses a pre-loaded calibration curve to accurately calculate the final concentration of the target biomolecule. The system then outputs this concentration reading, providing a quantitative result for the analysis.
