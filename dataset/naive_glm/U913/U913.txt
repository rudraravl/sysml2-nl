This describes a Thermopile Laser Sensing Unit, a specialized device designed to accurately measure the optical power of a laser beam. It functions as a complete, self-contained measurement system, taking in laser radiation and outputting a precise power reading.

The core of the system is a thermopile sensor. When a laser beam enters the unit's optical input, it strikes this sensor. The sensor's surface absorbs the laser's energy, which heats it up. Through the thermoelectric effect, this temperature change generates a small electrical voltage that is directly proportional to the incident laser power.

Because this initial voltage signal is very weak, it is immediately routed to an internal signal amplifier that boosts its strength significantly. The amplified, but still analog, signal is then passed to an Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC). The ADC samples this voltage at a high rate and converts it into a digital value.

Finally, a dedicated signal processor takes this digital data. It performs calculations to correct for any known offsets and applies a specific calibration factor to convert the raw digital value into a final, highly accurate power measurement in Watts. This final measurement, complete with a timestamp, is then made available through the unit's data output port.

The entire unit operates through several states. It powers on into an idle mode, goes through a brief warmup to ensure thermal stability, and then enters a ready state. From there, it can perform measurements, returning to the ready state to await the next task. The system is designed to measure laser power from 1 milliwatt up to 10 watts with an accuracy of at least ±3% and provides a stable reading in under one second.
