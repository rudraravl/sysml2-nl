This model describes a Geiger-Muller radiation detector, commonly known as a Geiger counter. It is a complete system designed to detect the presence of ionizing radiation (such as alpha, beta, and gamma particles) and measure its intensity in real-time.

The core of the system is the **Geiger-Muller Tube**, a gas-filled sensor that serves as the primary radiation detector. For the tube to function, a **High-Voltage Supply** provides it with a stable, high-voltage electrical field, typically around 450 volts.

The detection process begins when a particle of ionizing radiation passes through the tube, stripping electrons from the gas atoms inside and creating a cascade effect known as an electron avalanche. This avalanche results in a small, detectable electrical pulse. Immediately after generating a pulse, the tube enters a very brief "dead time" (around 100 microseconds) where it is temporarily unable to detect new radiation events while it resets.

This raw electrical pulse from the tube is then sent to a **Pulse Processor**, which amplifies and shapes the signal to make it clean and distinct. This processed signal is then routed to two different components simultaneously for output:

1.  An **Audio Indicator** (a speaker) receives the pulse and produces an immediate, audible "click". This provides real-time, intuitive feedback to the user for each radiation event detected.
2.  A **Rate Counter** tallies these pulses over a set period, such as 60 seconds, to calculate the overall radiation intensity.

Finally, the calculated intensity, measured in "Counts Per Minute," is sent from the counter to a **Display Unit**, which shows the numerical value to the user. In summary, the system works by converting invisible radiation events into both instant audible clicks and a quantitative visual measurement, making it a crucial tool for safety monitoring and scientific applications.
