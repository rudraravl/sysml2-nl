This model represents an automated electromagnetic pumping subsystem designed to transfer electrically conductive fluids, such as liquid metals or highly saline solutions, between two tanks.

The core of the system is a specialized electromagnetic pump that operates without any moving mechanical parts. It functions by creating a powerful magnetic field and passing an electric current through the fluid as it flows through a dedicated channel. The interaction between the magnetic field and the current generates a force that propels the fluid forward, increasing its pressure and creating flow. This design makes it highly reliable and suitable for handling high-temperature or corrosive liquids where traditional pumps would fail.

The subsystem is composed of several key components working together. A `SourceTank` holds the initial supply of the conductive fluid, and a `DestinationTank` collects the fluid after it has been pumped. A dedicated high-capacity `PowerSupply` provides the significant electrical energy required to operate the pump.

To ensure precise and safe operation, the system is equipped with a comprehensive set of sensors and controls. An `inlet valve` controls the flow of fluid from the source tank to the pump. `Pressure sensors` are placed at both the inlet and outlet of the pump to monitor operating pressures, while a `flow meter` measures the actual rate at which the fluid is being transferred.

An integrated controller acts as the system's brain. It monitors the readings from the flow meter and pressure sensors and continuously adjusts the power delivered to the pump to maintain a desired flow rate. The controller also manages the system's operational states, which include being `off`, `priming` for operation, `running` normally, and handling `fault` conditions. This control logic ensures the system operates efficiently and can perform safety-critical actions, such as an emergency shutdown if pressure exceeds safe limits.
