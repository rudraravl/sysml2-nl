This is a ground-based weather radar system designed to detect, locate, and characterize atmospheric precipitation for weather forecasting and severe weather warnings.

The system is composed of several key interconnected components that work together to scan the atmosphere. A high-power **Transmitter** generates short, intense radio-frequency pulses. These pulses are sent to a large, steerable **Antenna System**, which is an 8.5-meter dish that focuses the pulses into a narrow beam and transmits them into the sky. This antenna can be precisely positioned and rotated to perform various scan patterns, such as a full 360-degree "volume scan" to build a comprehensive 3D picture of the surrounding weather.

After the pulse is transmitted, the antenna switches to listening mode. When the pulse hits precipitation like rain, snow, or hail, a small fraction of its energy is scattered back as an echo. The antenna collects this faint echo and passes it to a highly sensitive **Receiver**, which amplifies the signal.

The amplified echo is then sent to a powerful **Signal Processor**. This is the brain of the system, analyzing the echo's properties to determine key weather characteristics. It calculates the distance to the precipitation based on the echo's return time, the intensity of the precipitation from the echo's strength, and the wind velocity (speed towards or away from the radar) by measuring the Doppler shift in the signal's frequency. The processor uses this information to classify the type of precipitation (e.g., rain, snow, hail) and generate a final "weather product."

This processed weather data is then sent to a **Display Unit**, where it is rendered as familiar weather maps showing reflectivity and wind patterns for meteorologists to analyze. The entire system is powered by a dedicated **Power Subsystem** that converts high-voltage input into the stable power required by the electronics.

Operationally, the radar system cycles through different states, including an offline mode, a standby mode, and an active scanning loop where it continuously transmits pulses, receives echoes, and processes the data to provide updated weather reports approximately every five seconds.
