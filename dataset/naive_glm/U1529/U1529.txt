This model describes an automated water well system designed to extract groundwater from an underground aquifer and deliver it as a pressurized water supply to a distribution network, such as for a home or farm.

The system's main components work together in a clear sequence. A submersible pump is placed deep inside a well bore that extends down into a natural underground aquifer. When activated, the pump draws water from the aquifer and pushes it upwards through a rising pipe. A non-return valve is installed in the line to prevent water from flowing back down into the well when the pump is off.

At the surface, the water flows into a pressure vessel (or pressure tank). This tank stores a quantity of water under pressure, which acts as a buffer to provide a consistent flow to the end-users without the pump needing to run constantly. From the pressure vessel, the water is fed into a distribution network of pipes leading to various outlets.

The system's operation is managed automatically by a pressure controller. This controller monitors the water pressure inside the pressure vessel. When water is used and the pressure drops below a pre-set low point, the controller sends a signal to turn the pump on. The pump then runs to refill the tank, increasing the pressure. Once the pressure reaches a pre-set high point, the controller turns the pump off.

For safety and sustainability, the system also includes a water level sensor in the well. This sensor monitors the aquifer's water table. If the water level drops to a critical low, the control system will shut down the pump to prevent it from running dry, which would cause damage, and to protect the aquifer from being over-drawn. The pump can exist in several states, including on, off, and a maintenance mode.
