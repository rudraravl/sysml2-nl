This model describes a high-power Nd:YAG (Neodymium-doped Yttrium Aluminum Garnet) laser system, a type of solid-state laser used in industrial, scientific, or medical applications. Its primary function is to generate a powerful, controlled, and coherent beam of infrared light at a specific wavelength of 1064 nanometers.

The system is built from several key interconnected components that work together to produce the laser beam:

At its heart is the **Nd:YAG Crystal**, which is the active material where light amplification occurs. To energize this crystal, a **Xenon Flashlamp** produces intense bursts of light, a process known as optical pumping. This entire assembly is housed within an **Optical Resonator**, which is essentially a light-trapping chamber formed by two highly reflective mirrors. This resonator causes photons to bounce back and forth through the energized crystal, building up into a powerful, coherent beam.

For specialized high-energy applications, the system includes a **Q-Switch**. This component acts as a high-speed optical shutter inside the resonator. It allows the crystal to store a massive amount of energy before opening rapidly to release it all as a single, extremely short and high-power pulse.

Powering the system is a central **Power Supply** that converts standard 220V AC electrical power into the high-voltage DC needed to fire the flashlamp and run the control electronics. Due to the immense heat generated by the flashlamp and crystal, a closed-loop **Cooling System** continuously circulates water to maintain a stable operating temperature and prevent damage.

Once the laser beam exits the resonator, it passes through **Beam Delivery** optics that shape, focus, and direct the final output beam. A **Power Meter** samples the beam to provide real-time feedback on its output power.

The entire system's operation is orchestrated by a **Laser Controller**. This electronic "brain" manages the operating mode (such as continuous wave, pulsed, or Q-switched), monitors system status, regulates the output power based on feedback from the power meter, and ensures safe operation. The system follows a clear operational lifecycle, moving through states like 'off,' 'initializing,' 'standby,' 'active' (when lasing), and 'cooling,' and includes a 'fault' state for safety shutdowns.
