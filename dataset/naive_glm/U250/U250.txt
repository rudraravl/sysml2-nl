This model describes a medical device system called the Hyalobarrier Adhesion Prevention System. Its purpose is to prevent post-surgical adhesions, which are bands of scar tissue that can form between internal organs and tissues after surgery, often leading to complications.

The system consists of two main physical components: a specialized gel and an applicator. The core of the system is the Hyalobarrier Gel, a sterile, highly viscous gel made from hyaluronic acid. This gel is designed to be biocompatible, meaning it can be safely used inside the human body.

During a surgical procedure, a surgeon uses the applicator to apply a layer of the Hyalobarrier Gel directly onto the surfaces of the tissues or organs that are at risk of adhering to each other as they heal. The applicator ensures that a precise volume of gel is delivered to create a barrier of a specific, effective thickness.

Once applied, the gel acts as a temporary physical barrier, keeping the delicate, healing tissue surfaces separated. This barrier is designed to remain effective for approximately seven days, which is the critical period for adhesion formation. After this period, the gel naturally and gradually breaks down and is safely absorbed by the body, a process known as bioresorption. By the time the gel is fully absorbed, the underlying tissues have healed sufficiently on their own, preventing them from fusing together.

The entire lifecycle of the gel is managed, from its initial packaged state, through its application and active barrier phase, to its eventual complete absorption by the body. This ensures that the system provides protection when needed and then disappears without requiring a second medical procedure for removal.
