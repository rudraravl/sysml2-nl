This model describes a complete Wankel-type rotary engine system, an internal combustion engine designed to convert the chemical energy in an air-fuel mixture into mechanical power.

The core of the system is the engine itself, which is composed of several key components working in concert. It features a unique housing with a specially shaped (epitrochoidal) inner chamber. Inside this housing, three triangular rotors orbit around a central eccentric shaft. This orbital motion of the rotors is what drives the engine's cycle.

The engine operates through a continuous four-stroke process:
1.  **Intake:** An air-fuel mixture is drawn into the chambers formed between the rotors and the housing wall.
2.  **Compression:** As the rotor continues its path, the volume of the chamber decreases, compressing the mixture.
3.  **Combustion & Expansion:** An ignition system provides a spark, igniting the compressed mixture. The resulting explosion creates high pressure that pushes on the face of the rotor, generating the force that turns the eccentric shaft.
4.  **Exhaust:** The rotor continues to move, pushing the spent exhaust gases out through an exhaust port.

This entire cycle produces rotational power—torque and speed—which is delivered through the eccentric shaft.

To support this operation, the engine includes a dedicated cooling system that circulates coolant through passages in the housing to manage the intense heat generated during combustion and maintain a stable operating temperature. The system has distinct operational states, including `off`, `starting`, `running`, and `stopping`, and it responds to external start and stop commands.

This engine is designed to be a high-performance unit, with requirements to deliver at least 180 kW of power at 8000 rpm, maintain a thermal efficiency of 28%, and operate reliably for at least 5000 hours before needing a major overhaul.
