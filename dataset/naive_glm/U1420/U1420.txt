This model describes a complete gas turbine engine system, the kind used for power generation or in jet propulsion. Its primary function is to continuously convert the chemical energy from fuel into rotational mechanical power.

The system operates on a continuous thermodynamic cycle and is composed of several key integrated components:

*   **Compressor:** This component is at the front of the engine. It draws in ambient air and uses rotating blades to compress it to a very high pressure, which also significantly increases the air's temperature.
*   **Combustion Chamber:** The hot, compressed air flows from the compressor into this chamber. Here, a fuel system injects a steady stream of fuel, which mixes with the air and is ignited. This continuous burn produces an extremely hot, high-pressure gas.
*   **Turbine:** The high-energy gas from the combustor is directed into the turbine. As the gas rapidly expands through the turbine's blades, it causes the turbine to spin at a very high speed.
*   **Shaft:** A central mechanical shaft connects the turbine and the compressor. A large portion of the power generated by the spinning turbine is used to drive the compressor, which keeps the entire cycle running. The remaining, or net, power is delivered from the end of the shaft as the engine's useful mechanical output.
*   **Fuel and Control Systems:** A dedicated fuel system manages the pumping, pressure, and flow rate of fuel into the combustion chamber. The entire engine's operation is orchestrated by a control system, which monitors key parameters like rotational speed, temperature, and power output. It constantly adjusts the fuel flow to maintain stable operation, meet power demands, and handle startup or shutdown procedures.

In essence, the system works by sucking in air, squeezing it, burning fuel in it to make it expand, and then using that expansion to spin a turbine, which provides power to both sustain the process and drive an external load. The final output is rotational power from the main shaft and a stream of hot exhaust gas.
