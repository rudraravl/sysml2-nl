This is an industrial fluid transfer system designed to pump viscous fluids from a supply tank to a pressurized receiving tank. The core of the system is a specialized positive displacement pump known as a Wendelkolben, or helical piston, pump.

The system is comprised of several key components working in sequence. A large supply tank holds the initial fluid. From there, the fluid passes through an isolation valve and into the Wendelkolben pump. This pump is driven by a high-power electric motor. Inside the pump, a helical metal rotor turns eccentrically within a flexible elastomeric stator. This motion creates a series of sealed, progressing cavities that gently and continuously move the fluid from the low-pressure inlet to the high-pressure outlet without high shear forces, which is ideal for viscous or sensitive fluids.

To ensure proper and safe operation, the system is heavily instrumented. Pressure sensors are installed at both the pump's inlet and outlet to monitor its performance. A flow meter on the discharge line measures the actual rate of fluid transfer. For safety, a pressure relief valve is connected to the outlet piping; if the system pressure exceeds a predefined set point, this valve automatically opens to divert fluid and protect the pump and piping from damage. Finally, another isolation valve is placed on the discharge line before the fluid enters the receiving tank.

The system operates through various states, transitioning from stopped to starting, then to normal operation. It continuously monitors for abnormal conditions such as excessively high pressure or low flow, and can enter specific states to handle them. It also includes a fault state for immediate shutdown in case of a critical failure. The entire subsystem is designed for reliable, continuous operation with built-in safety and monitoring capabilities.
