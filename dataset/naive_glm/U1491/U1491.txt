This model describes a complete sail-powered vessel, such as a modern sailboat. The system is designed to harness wind energy to propel a hull through the water.

The vessel is composed of three main components: a hull, a rudder for steering, and a comprehensive sail system for propulsion. The hull is specified as being 12 meters long with a displacement of 4,000 kg.

The sail system itself is a detailed assembly. It features a 40 square meter sail made from Dacron fabric, which is supported by a 15-meter tall mast and a 5-meter long boom. The entire structure is held in place by rigging designed to withstand significant tensile forces.

Functionally, the system operates by capturing wind. It senses the wind's speed and direction and allows for a commanded sail angle to be set for trimming. The interaction between the wind and the sail's surface generates aerodynamic lift and drag. These forces are then resolved into two primary components: a forward propulsive force that pushes the vessel, and a lateral (sideways) force. This lateral force is what causes the vessel to heel, or tilt, in the water. The propulsive force is transferred from the sail to the hull, driving it forward, while the resulting speed and heel angle of the vessel are monitored.

The entire design is governed by clear operational requirements. It must generate a minimum amount of thrust under specific wind conditions, operate safely within a defined range of wind speeds, and meet structural integrity standards to prevent failure. Importantly, it includes a safety constraint to limit the vessel's heel angle to a maximum of 35 degrees during normal operation.
