This model describes a Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) sensing system, an advanced optical instrument used to precisely measure physical parameters like strain and temperature. It is commonly used for structural health monitoring of bridges, buildings, aircraft, and pipelines.

The system's operation is based on detecting minute shifts in the wavelength (color) of light. It consists of several key components working in sequence:

1.  **Broadband Light Source:** This component generates a wide spectrum of light, much like a white light but typically in the infrared range. It serves as the initial signal for the system.

2.  **Optical Circulator:** This is a crucial routing device that acts like a traffic director for the light signals. It takes the broadband light from the source and sends it towards the sensor. It then captures the light returning *from* the sensor and directs it to the measurement instrument, preventing the signals from interfering with each other.

3.  **Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG):** This is the core sensor element. It's a small, specially treated segment of an optical fiber that acts as a highly selective mirror, reflecting only one very specific wavelength of light while letting all other wavelengths pass through. The exact wavelength it reflects is determined by its physical structure.

4.  **Optical Spectrum Analyzer:** This is a high-precision instrument that measures the exact wavelength of the light reflected back from the FBG sensor.

5.  **Signal Processing Unit:** This is the "brain" of the system. It takes the wavelength measurement from the analyzer and performs calculations to convert it into a physical measurement.

The sensing process works as follows: When the Fiber Bragg Grating is subjected to physical changes—for example, when the structure it's attached to is stretched (strain) or its temperature changes—the physical structure of the FBG itself is altered slightly. This alteration causes the specific wavelength of light it reflects to shift in a precise and predictable way. The Optical Spectrum Analyzer detects this tiny shift, and the Signal Processing Unit translates that shift back into a precise measurement of the strain or temperature being applied to the sensor.
