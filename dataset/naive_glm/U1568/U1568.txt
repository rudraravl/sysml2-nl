This model describes a complete, automated Space Logistics System designed to deliver cargo and resupply orbiting space stations. The system's primary function is to act as a supply chain, transporting essential goods like food, water, oxygen, scientific equipment, and other supplies from Earth to a station in orbit, ensuring the continuous operation and well-being of its crew.

The system is composed of four main interacting components:

1.  **Mission Control:** This is the ground-based command center for the entire operation. It is responsible for planning each logistics mission, which includes defining the cargo manifest, calculating the flight plan, and determining the optimal launch window. During the mission, it continuously monitors the telemetry (position, velocity, system status) from the vehicles and can send corrective commands or authorize critical mission phases, such as the final approval for docking.

2.  **Launch Vehicle:** This is the rocket used to lift the cargo out of Earth's atmosphere and into an initial orbit. It consists of a powerful propulsion system, a guidance system to steer it during ascent, and a structural frame to hold the payload. Its role is to successfully carry the Cargo Spacecraft to its designated orbital insertion point before separating.

3.  **Cargo Spacecraft:** This is the autonomous "delivery truck" of the system. After separating from the launch vehicle, it uses its own propulsion and navigation systems to travel from its initial orbit to the space station. It is equipped with a specialized rendezvous system with sensors to safely approach the station, along with a physical docking mechanism featuring capture latches to securely attach. Its cargo module is environmentally controlled, maintaining specific temperature and pressure to protect perishable or sensitive items. The spacecraft operates through a sequence of phases: cruising, approaching the station, performing the final docking maneuver, transferring its cargo, and finally undocking to depart.

4.  **Space Station:** This is the destination and customer of the logistics service. It is an orbiting habitat with a crew that consumes resources like food, water, and oxygen. The station is equipped with docking ports to receive the cargo spacecraft. Its internal systems monitor inventory levels and can automatically generate a resupply request when consumables run low (e.g., less than 30 days' worth remaining). Once the cargo spacecraft is docked, the station's crew or automated systems manage the reception and storage of the newly delivered supplies.

Together, these components execute a full logistics mission, from the initial planning on the ground to the final delivery of goods in orbit, creating a reliable and automated supply line for sustained human presence in space.
