This is a mechanical lifting device, specifically a compound pulley system, designed to make it easier to lift heavy objects. Its main purpose is to reduce the effort needed to lift a load by providing a mechanical advantage, effectively amplifying the user's force.

The system is built from several core components working together:

*   A **Fixed Pulley**, which is anchored to a stationary point. Its role is to change the direction of the pulling force.
*   A **Movable Pulley**, which is attached directly to the load. This pulley moves up and down with the object and is the key to amplifying the lifting force.
*   A **Rope** that is threaded through both pulleys. A user applies effort by pulling on the free end of this rope.
*   The **Load** itself, which is the object being lifted.

Each pulley is made of a grooved **wheel** that the rope runs over, which spins on an **axle**. The system accounts for real-world factors like friction in the axle bearings and the overall efficiency of the force transfer.

The system operates through a clear sequence of states. It begins in an **idle** state, ready for use. Once a load is attached, it's in a **loading** state. When the user begins pulling the rope, the system transitions into its active **lifting** phase. After the object reaches its target height, it can be secured in a **holding** state. Finally, the load can be brought back down during a **lowering** state before being detached, returning the system to idle.

The design has specific performance and safety requirements. It must provide a mechanical advantage of at least two, effectively halving the force required to lift the load (before accounting for friction). It also has a target efficiency of over 85%, ensuring most of the user's effort is converted into useful lifting work. A critical safety constraint ensures that the tension experienced by the rope always remains below its maximum rated tensile strength to prevent failure.
