This model describes a cochlear implant system, a sophisticated medical device designed to restore a sense of hearing to individuals with severe to profound hearing loss. The system works by bypassing damaged parts of the inner ear and directly stimulating the auditory nerve with electrical signals that represent environmental sounds.

The system is composed of two main assemblies: a wearable external unit and a surgically implanted internal unit.

The external unit, typically worn behind the ear, is responsible for capturing and processing sound. It contains:
*   A **Microphone** that picks up ambient sounds, like speech and environmental noise.
*   A **Speech Processor** that acts as the "brain," analyzing the audio signal. It filters the sound and converts it into a special digital code, or an "encoded stimulus pattern," that represents the different frequencies and intensities of the original sound.
*   An **External Transmitter** that sends this encoded information wirelessly through the skin to the internal unit.

The internal unit is surgically implanted and contains:
*   An **Internal Receiver** that picks up the wirelessly transmitted code from the external unit.
*   A **Current Stimulator** that decodes the received information and generates precise, low-level electrical pulses based on the pattern.
*   An **Electrode Array**, a delicate, flexible wire with multiple contacts that is inserted into the cochlea (the inner ear). This array delivers the electrical pulses from the stimulator to specific locations along the auditory nerve.

In operation, the microphone captures a sound, which the speech processor converts into a detailed stimulation plan. This plan is transmitted to the internal implant, which then generates a pattern of electrical pulses. By stimulating different parts of the auditory nerve with the electrode array, the system mimics how a healthy ear would respond to sound, allowing the user's brain to interpret these signals as hearing. The system is designed to continuously cycle through capturing, processing, and stimulating to provide a real-time perception of sound. It also includes operational states like a power-saving standby mode when no sound is detected.
