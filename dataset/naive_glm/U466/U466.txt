This model describes a mechanical product dispensing system, the kind commonly found in spray bottles for household cleaners or pumps for cosmetic products like lotions and serums. Its purpose is to draw a liquid product from a container and dispense a precise, controlled dose, typically as an atomized spray or mist, when activated by a user.

The system is composed of several key mechanical components that work in a sequence:

1.  **Product Reservoir:** This is the main container or bottle that holds the liquid product to be dispensed. The system is designed to be aware of the product level within this reservoir, capable of identifying if it is full, at a normal level, or running low.

2.  **Actuator Mechanism:** This is the user interface, such as the trigger on a spray bottle or the push-down head on a lotion pump. When a user applies force, this mechanism translates that simple push or pull into the mechanical energy needed to drive the internal pump.

3.  **Pump Assembly:** This is the heart of the system. Driven by the actuator, it's a mechanical pump that performs a cycle to move the liquid. It first draws the product from the reservoir into an internal chamber (intake), then builds pressure within that chamber (compression), and finally forces the pressurized product out toward the nozzle (discharge). It's designed to operate for a certain number of strokes, for instance, during priming.

4.  **Valve System:** This component controls the direction of fluid flow. It operates based on pressure, remaining sealed until the pump generates sufficient force to open it. This allows the pressurized liquid to move forward to the nozzle. As pressure drops after a pump cycle, the valve closes again to prevent the product from flowing backward or leaking.

5.  **Nozzle Assembly:** This is the final stage where the product exits the system. It takes the high-pressure stream of liquid from the pump and forces it through a tiny, specially designed opening (orifice). This action atomizes the liquid, breaking it into fine droplets and forming a spray. The nozzle's design dictates the characteristics of the final output, such as the spray angle and droplet size.

In essence, this is a complete, self-contained mechanical system that translates a simple user action—like pressing a button—into a coordinated sequence of fluidic and mechanical events to deliver a product effectively.
