This model represents a traditional manual beer engine, also known as a hand-pump, used for dispensing cask-conditioned ale. Its purpose is to draw beer from a cellar cask and serve it into a glass without using pressurized gas, which preserves the beer's delicate carbonation and flavor profile.

The system consists of several key physical components working in sequence. The source of the beer is a cask, which holds the conditioned ale at a specific cellar temperature and near-atmospheric pressure. From the cask, a beer line connects to the main pump assembly.

The heart of the system is a pump cylinder operated by a long, vertical handle. The bartender's operation of this handle drives the entire dispensing process. When the handle is pulled, a piston inside the cylinder moves, creating a vacuum. This suction opens a one-way inlet valve, drawing a measured volume of beer from the cask, through the beer line, and into the pump's chamber.

When the handle is released or pushed back to its starting position, the piston reverses direction. This action pressurizes the beer inside the cylinder, closing the inlet valve and forcing open a separate one-way outlet valve. The beer is then pushed out of the cylinder, through another beer line, and out of the final dispensing tap into the glass. Because each full pull-and-push cycle dispenses a specific, consistent volume (typically half a pint), pouring a full pint requires two complete strokes of the handle.
