This model describes a high-precision particle detector system, the kind used in physics experiments to identify and measure the properties of subatomic or ionizing particles. Its main purpose is to detect incoming particles and precisely determine their energy and time of arrival.

The system works by converting the energy of a single particle into a measurable digital signal through a multi-stage process. The core components work in a chain:

1.  **Scintillator:** This is the primary sensor, a special crystal (in this case, Sodium Iodide) that produces a tiny flash of light when a particle passes through it. The intensity of the light is proportional to the energy deposited by the particle.
2.  **Photomultiplier Tube (PMT):** This highly sensitive device detects the faint flash of light from the scintillator and converts it into a weak electrical pulse. The PMT requires a very high voltage to operate, which is supplied by a dedicated power unit.
3.  **Signal Amplifier & Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC):** The weak electrical pulse from the PMT is first strengthened and shaped by an amplifier. It is then sent to an ADC, which converts the analog pulse into a digital value that a computer can read.
4.  **Data Acquisition System (DAQ):** This is the system's "brain." It receives the stream of digital signals from the ADC, processes them to reconstruct the details of each individual particle interaction (such as its energy, position, and an exact timestamp), and packages this information for output and analysis.

The overall system is supported by a **High-Voltage Power Supply** to run the PMT and includes a **Calibration Source** (a known radioactive material like Cesium-137) to regularly test and calibrate the detector for accurate and reliable measurements. The system is designed for high performance, requiring excellent detection efficiency, precise energy and time resolution, and the ability to handle up to a million particle events per second. It is also built for reliability, intended to run continuously for periods of 72 hours or more.
