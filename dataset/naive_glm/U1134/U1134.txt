This model describes a mobile manipulator robot, an autonomous system designed to pick up objects and transport them to different locations, such as in a warehouse, factory, or laboratory.

The robot is a single, integrated unit built on a wheeled **mobile base** that allows it to navigate its environment. This base is equipped with sensors that constantly determine the robot's position and orientation while also detecting nearby objects that can be manipulated.

Mounted on the base is a multi-jointed **manipulator arm**, which functions much like a human arm with a shoulder, elbow, and wrist. This arm has a specific reach and can lift payloads up to a certain weight (e.g., 5 kg). At the end of the arm is a **gripper** that acts as the robot's hand, capable of opening to a specific width and applying a precise amount of force to securely grasp or release objects.

The entire system is powered by a central, rechargeable **battery**. This battery supplies electricity to all the components, including the drive system in the base, the motors in the arm and gripper, and the electronics.

The "brain" of the robot is an onboard **control computer**. It processes data from a **perception module**—the robot's sensory system—to understand its surroundings. Based on this information, the computer makes decisions and sends commands to the mobile base for movement and to the manipulator arm for grasping and placing objects.

A typical mission for this robot involves a "pick and place" operation. The robot begins by determining its current location. It then navigates to a designated pickup area, uses its sensors to find the target object, and moves its arm to grab it. Once the object is secured, the robot travels to a delivery point, carefully positions the object, and releases it, completing the task without human intervention.
