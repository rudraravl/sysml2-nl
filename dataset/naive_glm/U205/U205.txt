This model describes a Doppler echocardiography system, a non-invasive medical ultrasound machine designed for comprehensive cardiac assessment. The system uses high-frequency sound waves and the Doppler effect to create real-time images of the heart's structure while simultaneously measuring the velocity and direction of blood flow through its chambers and valves.

The system is composed of several key components that work in concert. A handheld **Ultrasound Transducer** (the probe) is placed on the patient's chest, where it transmits ultrasound waves and receives the reflected echoes from moving blood cells. These echo signals are sent to a **Doppler Processor**, which analyzes the frequency shift in the returning waves to calculate critical blood flow data, including velocity, direction, and turbulence.

Simultaneously, an **Image Processor** uses the echo data to construct a two-dimensional, real-time image of the heart's anatomy. It combines this with the blood flow information to create color-coded overlays on the image, allowing clinicians to visualize blood flow patterns. A dedicated **Measurement Engine** then uses this data to perform advanced calculations, such as determining the pressure gradient across a heart valve, which is essential for diagnosing conditions like stenosis.

All of this visual and numerical information is presented on a high-resolution **Display System** for the clinician to interpret. A central **Control System** allows the operator to manage the examination by selecting different imaging modes (like Continuous Wave Doppler), adjusting the probe's settings, and controlling the overall procedure. The entire process enables clinicians to perform a detailed, real-time assessment of cardiac function and diagnose a wide range of heart conditions.
