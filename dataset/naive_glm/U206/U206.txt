This describes a Double-Lumen Endobronchial Tube (DLT) system, a specialized medical device used during thoracic (chest) surgery. Its primary purpose is to allow anesthesiologists to control a patient's breathing by ventilating each lung independently. This is critical for procedures that require one lung to be deflated and still, providing the surgeon with better access and visibility, while the other lung continues to function to oxygenate the patient's blood.

The system's main components are:

*   **The Double-Lumen Tube:** This is a specialized breathing tube with two separate channels (lumens) running its length. One channel is intended to deliver air to the right lung, and the other to the left. The tube features two inflatable cuffs: a tracheal cuff that seals the main windpipe and a bronchial cuff that is positioned to seal the airway leading to one of the lungs. When both cuffs are inflated to a safe pressure, they create a complete physical separation between the two lungs.

*   **The Ventilator:** This is a life-support machine that connects to the double-lumen tube and actively pushes a breathable gas mixture (with a specific oxygen concentration) into the lungs. The ventilator can be precisely controlled to deliver breaths based on different modes: ventilating both lungs, only the left lung, or only the right lung. It manages critical parameters like breathing rate, volume of air per breath, and peak airway pressure to ensure patient safety.

The system works by first carefully positioning the tube in the patient's airway. The tracheal and bronchial cuffs are then inflated to achieve "lung isolation." Once the lungs are separated, the ventilator is used to perform selective ventilation. For example, if a surgeon is operating on the right lung, the ventilator will be set to supply air only through the lumen leading to the left lung, while the right lung is allowed to collapse. This provides a still, deflated surgical field while ensuring the patient receives adequate oxygenation from the functioning left lung throughout the procedure.
