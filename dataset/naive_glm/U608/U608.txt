This model describes a mechanical barograph, a meteorological instrument designed to continuously measure and record atmospheric pressure over time. Its purpose is to create a graphical chart, called a barogram, which helps meteorologists analyze weather patterns.

The system is composed of several key mechanical parts that work together in sequence. At its heart is an **aneroid capsule**, a sealed, flexible metal container that physically expands and contracts in response to changes in the surrounding air pressure. This subtle movement is then magnified by a **mechanical linkage** system of levers.

The amplified motion is transferred to a long **recording arm** with an ink pen at its tip. This arm moves up and down, corresponding to the rise and fall of atmospheric pressure. To provide a time axis, the pen traces its line onto a sheet of calibrated **chart paper** which is wrapped around a **clockwork drum**. This drum is spring-driven and rotates at a constant, slow speed, typically completing one full rotation over a seven-day period.

The combined action of the pressure-sensitive arm moving vertically and the clock-driven drum rotating horizontally results in a continuous line graph of pressure versus time. The barograph has distinct operational states: it can be `idle`, actively `recording` pressure data, or in a `maintenance` state when the chart paper is full and needs to be replaced.
