This model represents an India Mark II hand pump, a robust, manually operated system designed to extract groundwater from deep boreholes, typically in rural or community settings. Its primary purpose is to provide a reliable source of drinking water without needing an external power source.

The system is composed of several key mechanical parts working in unison. Above ground, a long **Handle Assembly** provides the leverage for a person to operate the pump. This handle is connected to a long, rigid **Pump Rod** that extends deep into a drilled well, known as a **Borehole**.

At the bottom of the pump rod, submerged in the groundwater, is a **Piston Assembly** housed within a **Cylinder Barrel**. The core of the pump's function lies here. As the user moves the handle up and down, the pump rod forces the piston to reciprocate inside the cylinder.

The pumping action works in a two-stroke cycle. On the upstroke (pulling the handle up), the piston rises, creating a vacuum that pulls open a one-way **Foot Valve** at the base of the cylinder, drawing water from the surrounding aquifer into the cylinder. On the downstroke (pushing the handle down), the foot valve closes, trapping the water. The piston then moves down through this water, which is forced up through a valve in the piston itself.

With each subsequent upstroke, the volume of water above the piston is lifted higher through a vertical pipe called the **Rising Main**. This process is repeated, lifting the column of water progressively higher until it emerges from the **Discharge Spout** at the surface for collection. The pump is designed to operate continuously through these idle, downstroke, upstroke, and discharging states to provide a steady flow of water.
