This model represents a manual hand pump system designed to transfer a fluid from a source reservoir to a higher-pressure output. The system is entirely manually operated and does not require any external power.

The core of the system is the hand pump itself, which includes a handle assembly, a piston moving inside a pump cylinder, and two critical one-way check valves—one for the inlet and one for the outlet. The pump operates in a two-stroke cycle controlled by the user moving the handle.

On the "upstroke," as the handle is pulled up, the piston moves and creates a low-pressure area within the cylinder. This pressure difference causes the inlet check valve to open, drawing fluid from a source reservoir into the cylinder. During this phase, the outlet valve remains closed to prevent any backflow.

On the "downstroke," pushing the handle down causes the piston to pressurize the fluid trapped inside the cylinder. This pressure forces the inlet valve to shut while pushing the outlet check valve open, discharging the fluid to an output manifold. The check valves are designed to open only when a specific "cracking pressure" is overcome, ensuring the fluid flows in the intended direction.

The complete system shows the hand pump integrated with a fluid reservoir that supplies the fluid and a discharge manifold that receives the pressurized output. The design has specific performance requirements, such as delivering a minimum pressure and being operable with a limited amount of human force.
