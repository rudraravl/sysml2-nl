This model describes a digital Flat Panel Detector (FPD) system, a core component in modern X-ray imaging equipment used for medical diagnostics or industrial non-destructive testing. Its primary function is to capture X-ray radiation that passes through a subject and convert it into a high-quality digital image.

The system is composed of several key functional components that work in sequence. When an X-ray exposure begins, a **Scintillator Panel** absorbs the X-ray photons and converts their energy into visible light. Directly behind this panel is a **Photodiode Matrix**, a grid of light-sensitive sensors that captures this light and transforms it into a corresponding pattern of electrical charge.

Once the exposure is complete, the **Readout Circuitry** scans the entire photodiode matrix, amplifying and digitizing the charge from each pixel to form a raw digital image. This raw data is immediately sent to an onboard **Image Processing Unit**, which applies essential corrections (like dark-field and flat-field) to remove noise and artifacts, and then enhances the image for optimal quality.

The entire process is managed by a central **Controller Module**, which orchestrates the timing of the exposure, readout, and processing stages based on commands from an external system. A dedicated **Thermal Management System** actively cools the detector to maintain a stable operating temperature, which is critical for consistent image quality and long-term reliability.

The detector operates through a clear sequence of states for each image capture: it waits in a **standby** mode, prepares for exposure during **initialization**, actively captures radiation during the **exposure** phase, then moves through **readout** and **processing** before returning to standby, ready for the next acquisition. The final, processed image is then transmitted via a data output port to a console for review.
