This model describes a comprehensive hospital patient gown system, centered around a reusable patient gown designed to balance the needs of patient modesty with clinical accessibility. The system encompasses not just the physical gown, but also its interaction with patients, clinical staff, and the hospital's sanitation infrastructure.

The patient gown itself is a garment made from a breathable and durable cotton-polyester blend, designed to be comfortable for extended wear and to withstand high-temperature washing for sterilization. It features a simple and secure fastening system composed of tie strings at both the neck and waist. The design is deliberately open in the back, a key feature that provides medical staff with easy access for examinations and treatments while keeping the patient's front covered and maintaining their dignity.

This gown is part of a larger ecosystem. The system includes the patient, who may have varying levels of mobility and require assistance, and the clinical staff (nurses and physicians) who help patients with the gown and adjust it to perform medical procedures. It also incorporates a sanitation system, representing the hospital laundry, which is responsible for washing used gowns at a specific high temperature with medical-grade detergent to ensure infection control.

The gown follows a distinct operational lifecycle. It begins in a clean, sanitized state, ready for use. Once issued to a patient, it is worn and secured. During care, staff can temporarily adjust it to access specific body regions for examination before re-securing it. After use, the soiled gown is removed and sent to the laundry system for processing. Following a successful washing and inspection cycle, it returns to the clean state, ready for another patient. If a gown is damaged during use or laundering, it is discarded and removed from service.
