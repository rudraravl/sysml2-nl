This model describes the VITAband, a wearable health monitoring system designed to continuously track a user's vital signs in real-time. It functions as a personal health and safety device, similar to a sophisticated medical-grade smartwatch or wristband.

The core of the system is a suite of biometric sensors that constantly gathers health data. This includes a heart rate sensor, a pulse oximeter to measure blood oxygen saturation, a thermometer for body temperature, and an accelerometer to track physical activity by counting steps.

This raw data is fed to an onboard analytics processor, which acts as the device's brain. The processor analyzes the incoming vital signs and compares them against personalized safety thresholds that can be configured remotely. Based on this real-time analysis, the system determines the user's health status.

If the user's vitals are within the normal range, the device simply updates its local display and wirelessly transmits the data as routine telemetry. However, if any measurement crosses a warning or critical threshold—such as a dangerously low heart rate or blood oxygen level—the processor immediately generates an alert.

This alert is simultaneously shown on the device's display and transmitted wirelessly to an external system, such as a caregiver's smartphone or a hospital's monitoring station. The system is designed to be highly responsive, ensuring critical alerts are sent within seconds of detecting a problem.

The entire wearable is powered by a rechargeable battery, overseen by a power management unit designed to provide at least 72 hours of continuous operation on a single charge.
