This model represents a medical blood pumping system, likely for use in life support applications such as a cardiopulmonary bypass (heart-lung machine) or a ventricular assist device (VAD). Its primary function is to pump blood from a source, like a reservoir or a patient's vein, to a destination, such as the patient's arterial system, while vigilantly monitoring for and managing the formation of blood clots (thrombosis).

The system's core component is a sophisticated pump composed of a motor-driven impeller with multiple blades that spins within a chamber to propel the blood. The pump is heavily instrumented with sensors to ensure safe and effective operation. It includes pressure sensors at both its inlet and outlet ports, as well as a flow rate sensor to continuously measure its performance against a target flow rate of 5.0 liters per minute.

A critical safety feature is the integrated thrombus detector. This component actively monitors for the signs of blood clot formation, a major risk in such devices, by analyzing changes in blood viscosity and the pressure difference across the pump.

The system operates through a defined set of states. It begins in an `idle` state, moves through `priming` to prepare the pump, and then enters its normal `operating` and `monitoring` mode. If the thrombus detector suspects a clot, the system transitions to a `thrombusDetected` state and will automatically attempt a `clearing` procedure, likely by adjusting the pump speed. If this procedure fails, the system executes a safe `emergency` shutdown to prevent harm. The pump can also be stopped normally by command.
