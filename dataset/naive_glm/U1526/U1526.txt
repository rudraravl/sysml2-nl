This model describes an automated water storage and distribution system. It is designed to manage a large water reservoir, ensuring a consistent and safe supply for distribution while operating without manual intervention.

The core of the system is a large-capacity storage tank, capable of holding 100,000 liters of water. This tank is designed to operate within a safe fill range, specifically between 5% and 95% of its total capacity, to prevent overflows and structural stress from running completely empty.

Water enters the system through an inlet path that includes a pressure regulator and a flow valve. The regulator ensures that the pressure of the incoming water is safely managed (to 4 bar), while the valve controls whether the tank is actively filling. Similarly, the outlet path for distributing water also features a valve to control the outflow and a pressure regulator to ensure the water is delivered at a safe, consistent pressure (3 bar).

The entire operation is managed by a central system controller. This controller acts as the brain, receiving continuous data from two key sensors: a level sensor that monitors the tank's current fill percentage and a quality monitor that checks water characteristics like pH, turbidity, and temperature.

Based on the readings from the level sensor, the controller automatically decides when to fill or drain the tank. Its primary goal is to maintain the water level at an optimal target of 80%. If the level drops, it commands the inlet valve to open; if it gets too high, it can manage the valves to stop filling or allow draining. The system can report its status, such as 'filling', 'draining', 'normal', or 'full', based on these automated operations. This automated control ensures a reliable water supply, prevents dangerous conditions like overflows, and maintains the integrity of the system.
