This model describes an autonomous stream gauge system, a type of remote hydrological monitoring station designed for continuously collecting data from rivers and streams. Its primary purpose is to measure key water metrics for flood forecasting, water resource management, and environmental monitoring.

The system is a self-contained unit equipped with a suite of sensors. An ultrasonic sensor measures the water's surface level with high precision, while dedicated sensors measure the water's velocity and temperature. A central data processor acts as the brains of the station, taking the raw readings from these sensors and using them, along with pre-configured data about the river channel's geometry, to calculate the river's flow rate (discharge).

For reliability, the system features a data logger that can store tens of thousands of measurement records locally. This ensures no data is lost even if communication is temporarily interrupted. A telemetry unit is responsible for periodically transmitting the collected and processed data—including water level, flow rate, and temperature—to a remote monitoring center, typically every 15 minutes. This unit can also receive configuration commands from the center.

A critical function is the built-in alert manager, which constantly compares the measured water level against predefined flood thresholds. If the water rises to advisory, warning, or critical levels, the system automatically generates and transmits an alert.

The entire station is designed for long-term, autonomous operation in remote locations. It is powered by a solar panel that charges a backup battery, allowing it to function for extended periods without maintenance. The system can also manage its power consumption, switching to a low-power mode if necessary.
