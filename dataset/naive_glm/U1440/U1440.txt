This model represents a classic mechanical lever system, a fundamental tool designed to amplify force and make it easier to lift or move heavy objects.

The system is composed of a rigid Lever Arm that pivots on a stationary Fulcrum. An Effort Source, such as a person or a machine, applies an input force to one part of the arm. This force is then transferred through the arm to lift or move a Load Mass positioned at another point.

The core principle of the system is mechanical advantage, which is achieved by carefully positioning the fulcrum. The distance from the fulcrum to where the effort is applied is the "effort arm," and the distance to the load is the "load arm." By making the effort arm longer than the load arm, the system multiplies the input force, allowing a small effort to overcome a much larger load.

The lever operates through a clear sequence of states. It begins at rest. When force is applied, it enters an active state, attempting to lift the load. If the amplified force exceeds the load's weight, the system successfully lifts it. The load can then be held at a specific height or lowered back down by releasing the effort, after which the system returns to its resting state. The design requires that the lever arm remains structurally sound without bending or breaking under load, and that the fulcrum provides a stable, unmoving pivot point for the entire operation.
