This model describes a complete residential plumbing system for a home. Its primary purpose is to receive pressurized water from a municipal supply, distribute it as both hot and cold water to various fixtures like sinks and showers, and then safely carry away the wastewater to a sewer.

The system begins with an external municipal water supply, which feeds water into the house through a main shutoff valve. This valve allows the entire system to be isolated for maintenance or emergencies. Immediately after the valve, a pressure regulator reduces the high pressure from the city main to a safer, more stable level suitable for home appliances and pipes. A water meter is installed in-line to measure the total volume of water consumed, typically for billing purposes.

After the meter, the main cold water supply line splits. One branch directs cold water into a water heater. This is a tank-based unit that uses a heating element to raise the water temperature to a pre-set level. It operates in different states, actively heating the water when the temperature drops and then idling to maintain it, ensuring a ready supply of hot water.

From the heater, a dedicated hot water pipe runs parallel to the cold water line. Both lines feed into a distribution manifold, which acts as a central hub to branch the supply out to different areas of the house. At points of use, mixing valves blend the hot and cold water streams to deliver water at a comfortable, desired temperature to fixtures like a kitchen sink, bathroom sink, and shower. Some fixtures, like a toilet, are connected directly to the cold water line.

Once used, the wastewater from each fixture flows into a network of drain pipes. These pipes are designed with a specific slope to ensure gravity-fed drainage, and they combine into a larger main drain line. Finally, this main drain pipe connects to the municipal sewer system, safely removing all wastewater from the residence. The entire system is designed to operate within safe pressure and temperature limits to prevent scalding or pipe damage, and it is built to provide adequate flow to multiple fixtures at once.
