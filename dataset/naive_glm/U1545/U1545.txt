This model describes a complete Astronaut Extravehicular Activity (EVA) System, which is essentially a personal spacecraft that enables an astronaut to work safely outside a vehicle in the vacuum of space. The system's primary function is to provide a self-contained, mobile life support environment, communication capabilities, and health monitoring for missions like spacecraft maintenance, assembly, or scientific research for durations of up to eight hours.

The core of the system is the **Extravehicular Mobility Unit (EMU)**, which is the multi-component spacesuit itself. It consists of a helmet with a protective visor, a hard upper torso that serves as the main structural element, and flexible lower torso, arm, glove, and boot assemblies that allow for mobility. The EMU's primary job is to maintain a pressurized, breathable atmosphere and protect the astronaut from the harsh thermal environment and radiation of space.

Attached to the suit is the **Portable Life Support Subsystem (PLSS)**, the critical backpack that sustains the astronaut. The PLSS contains an oxygen tank to supply breathable air, a system to scrub the astronaut's exhaled carbon dioxide, and a water cooling system that circulates water through tubes in an undergarment to remove excess body heat. A primary battery within the PLSS provides all the electrical power needed for the suit's fans, electronics, and communication systems.

The astronaut's well-being is continuously tracked by a **Health Monitoring System**. This system uses biometric sensors to monitor the astronaut's heart rate, body temperature, and respiration. It automatically assesses this data and can generate caution, warning, or critical alerts if any vital signs go beyond safe limits.

A **Communication Subsystem** provides the essential two-way link between the astronaut, the parent spacecraft, and mission control on the ground. It transmits telemetry data—including suit status and the astronaut's vital signs—and receives commands. This system is also responsible for sending any emergency alerts generated by the health monitor.

The entire EVA process follows a strict operational sequence. It begins inside the **Spacecraft's** airlock, where the astronaut "prebreathes" pure oxygen to prevent decompression sickness. The airlock is then depressurized, and the astronaut exits to begin their work. Throughout the spacewalk, all subsystems work in a continuous loop: life support is regulated based on the astronaut's exertion, vitals are constantly monitored, and communications are maintained. If an emergency is detected, the system has procedures to abort the EVA and safely return the astronaut to the airlock, which is then repressurized to complete the mission.
