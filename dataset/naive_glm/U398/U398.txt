This model describes a high-integrity, smart electrical outlet system, designed for a fixed location or station. Its primary purpose is to deliver safe, stable, and regulated electrical power to any connected device while providing comprehensive protection against common electrical hazards.

The system is built from several key components that work in series to treat the incoming power before it reaches the end device. Power first enters a **Circuit Breaker**, which acts as the first line of defense against overcurrent situations, tripping and cutting power if the connected device draws too much amperage.

Next, the power flows through a **Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI)**. This is a critical safety component that constantly monitors for tiny current leakages, such as those that might occur if the electricity finds an unintended path to the ground. If a fault is detected, it cuts the power almost instantly to prevent electric shock.

After the safety checks, the electricity passes through a **Voltage Regulator**. This component ensures the power delivered is stable and consistent, protecting sensitive electronics from sags or surges by maintaining the voltage within a specific tolerance.

Finally, the regulated and protected power is delivered to the **Outlet Receptacle** itself, which is a standard North American socket (NEMA 5-15R). This is the physical interface where a user can plug in their device.

The entire outlet system operates through different states: it can be `Offline`, in `Standby` (ready but not delivering power), `Active` (supplying power to a device), or `Tripped` (if the circuit breaker or GFCI has been activated due to a fault). In the tripped state, the outlet isolates the power until it can be safely reset.
