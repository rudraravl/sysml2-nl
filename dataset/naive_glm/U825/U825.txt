This is a Pitot tube airspeed measurement system, a critical instrument found in aircraft for determining the speed of the aircraft relative to the surrounding air. The system's primary function is to provide pilots with a continuous and accurate airspeed reading, which is essential for safe flight.

The system operates by measuring the difference between two types of air pressure. It uses a specially designed Pitot probe, which extends into the airflow, to capture the total pressure (also known as stagnation pressure) at its forward-facing opening and the static pressure from ports on its side. This pressure difference, known as dynamic pressure, is directly related to the aircraft's speed.

The system is composed of four main components that work in sequence. First, the **Pitot Probe** senses the raw air pressures. These pressures are then fed to a **Pressure Sensor**, which calculates the dynamic pressure. The resulting value is sent to an **Air Data Computer**, which is the core processing unit that applies an aerodynamic formula (Bernoulli's principle), using the dynamic pressure and a standard value for air density to calculate the aircraft's indicated airspeed. Finally, this calculated speed is transmitted to the **Airspeed Indicator**, a display in the cockpit that presents the information to the pilot.

This entire process runs as a continuous cycle to provide the flight crew with real-time, constantly updated airspeed information. The system is designed to be highly accurate and responsive, operating reliably across a wide range of flight altitudes and temperatures.
