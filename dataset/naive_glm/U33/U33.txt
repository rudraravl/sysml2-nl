This model describes an Adaptive Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) system, an intelligent, implantable medical device designed for treating neurological and neuropsychiatric conditions like Parkinson's disease.

Unlike traditional systems that deliver constant stimulation, this is a "closed-loop" or "smart" device. It continuously monitors a patient's brain activity in real-time, detects specific neural signals (biomarkers) that are indicators of symptoms, and automatically adjusts its therapeutic electrical stimulation to counteract them. This adaptive approach is designed to provide more personalized and efficient therapy, reduce side effects, and extend the device's battery life.

The system is composed of several key implantable components that work in concert:

*   **Sensing and Analysis:** A **Sensing Electrode** array is implanted in the target brain region to listen to neural activity. The raw signals from this electrode are processed by a **Biomarker Extractor**, a specialized digital signal processor that filters the data to isolate and measure the specific pathological brain patterns.

*   **Control and Decision-Making:** The quantified biomarker data is sent to the **Closed-Loop Controller**, which acts as the system's brain. This controller compares the current brain activity against a therapeutic threshold. If pathological activity is detected, it calculates the optimal stimulation parameters—such as the electrical current, pulse frequency, and pulse duration—needed to suppress it.

*   **Therapy Delivery:** The controller's commands are executed by a **Pulse Generator**, which produces the precise therapeutic electrical pulses. These pulses are then delivered to the brain tissue through a separate **Stimulation Electrode**, effectively calming the abnormal neural activity.

*   **Support and Communication:** The entire implanted device is powered by a long-lasting, rechargeable **Implantable Battery**. A **Wireless Telemetry** unit enables two-way communication with an external programmer, allowing clinicians to monitor device performance, retrieve diagnostic data, and fine-tune the therapy parameters non-invasively.

In essence, the system operates in a continuous sense-analyze-treat cycle. It senses the brain state, determines if therapy is needed, delivers a tailored dose of stimulation, and then repeats the process, ensuring that therapy is applied only when and how much is necessary.
