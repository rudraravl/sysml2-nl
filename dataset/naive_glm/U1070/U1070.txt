This model describes an industrial robot cell from HD Hyundai, designed for high-precision, automated material handling and assembly tasks in a manufacturing environment. The system's primary function is to autonomously identify, pick up, and move workpieces from one location to another.

The core of the cell is a six-axis manipulator arm with a reach of 1.7 meters, capable of handling payloads up to 25 kg. It is engineered for high precision, achieving a positioning repeatability of ±0.03 mm, which is critical for assembly operations. Attached to the arm is a gripper, or end-effector, that can open up to 180 mm and apply a finely controlled gripping force of up to 600 Newtons to securely handle a variety of components.

To perceive its environment, the system is equipped with a high-resolution vision sensor. This "eye" of the robot scans the workspace to detect workpieces, identifying their exact position and orientation before the arm attempts to pick them up.

The entire operation is orchestrated by a central Robot Controller, which serves as the brain of the system. It processes data from the vision sensor, plans the arm's optimal path to a target, and sends precise commands for movement and gripping. A separate, dedicated Safety Controller constantly monitors the robot's speed and position within a 1.5-meter safety zone. It can detect potential collisions or respond to an emergency stop signal, halting all motion to ensure a safe working environment. The entire cell is powered by a Power Distribution unit that takes a 400V industrial supply and provides the necessary power to the arm and its sensors.

A typical operational cycle involves the robot first performing a safety check. It then uses its vision system to locate a workpiece. The controller plans a trajectory to the object, and the arm moves to the pick-up position. The gripper grasps the object, and the arm then follows a second planned path to move the workpiece to its destination, releases it, and returns to a home position.

The system operates through various states, including `idle` when inactive, `initializing` during startup, `ready` when awaiting a command, and `operating` while performing a task. It can also be `paused`, or enter an `error` or `emergencyStop` state if a fault or safety event occurs.
