This model represents a classic, operator-assisted telephone switchboard system. It's designed to manually connect voice calls between subscribers on a local telephone network and potentially route calls to external networks.

The system's core is a central **Switching Matrix**, which acts as a physical hub that can create temporary electrical connections between any two telephone lines. This switchboard supports up to 100 individual **Subscriber Lines**, each with a unique phone number and a status that indicates if it's idle, ringing, or busy in a call.

To connect a call, a human operator uses an **Operator Console**. The system includes five such consoles, allowing multiple operators to handle calls simultaneously. When a subscriber picks up their phone, the system detects this and notifies an operator. The operator then communicates with the caller to determine the destination number.

Based on the operator's instructions, the Switching Matrix attempts to establish a connection. A dedicated **Signal Generator** provides the necessary audio cues for the users, such as a dial tone when they first pick up the phone, a ringing signal for the person being called, or a busy signal if the destination line is already in use.

Once a call is connected, the Switching Matrix routes the audio signals between the two parties, allowing them to speak. The system continuously monitors the lines, and when the call is finished (i.e., when a user hangs up), it terminates the connection, freeing up the lines and the matrix pathway.

Finally, a **Call Recording System** automatically logs the details of every completed call, including the caller, the receiver, the start time, and the duration. This information is used for billing and operational analysis. The switchboard also features ten external "trunk" lines for connecting to other, larger telephone networks.
