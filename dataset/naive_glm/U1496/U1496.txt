This model represents a comprehensive municipal sewage treatment plant designed to process raw wastewater. Its purpose is to remove contaminants through a multi-stage process, ultimately producing treated water that is safe to discharge into the environment and stabilized sludge suitable for disposal.

The plant's operation is broken down into a series of interconnected physical, biological, and chemical treatment units. The process begins at a **Screening Unit** and a **Grit Chamber**, which perform preliminary treatment by physically removing large objects, debris, and heavy inorganic materials like sand from the incoming raw sewage.

From there, the wastewater flows into a **Primary Clarifier**, a large settling tank where gravity separates suspended solids from the liquid. The settled solids form "primary sludge," which is piped away for separate processing, while the partially clarified water, now called "primary effluent," moves on to the next stage.

The core of the treatment occurs in an **Aeration Basin**, where a biological process takes place. Air is pumped into the basin to encourage the growth of microorganisms, which consume the organic pollutants in the water. This process relies on "activated sludge," a microbe-rich substance that is continuously mixed with the wastewater.

Following biological treatment, the water enters a **Secondary Clarifier**. Here, the activated sludge settles out from the treated water. A portion of this sludge is recycled back to the Aeration Basin to maintain the microorganism population, while the excess "waste sludge" is removed.

The now-clarified water undergoes a final purification step in a **Disinfection Chamber**, where chemicals like chlorine are added to eliminate any remaining harmful pathogens before the clean, treated effluent is discharged from the plant.

Simultaneously, the primary sludge and waste activated sludge collected from the clarifiers are processed in an **Anaerobic Digester**. In this oxygen-free environment, microorganisms break down the organic matter in the sludge, stabilizing it, reducing its volume, and minimizing pathogens and odors. The resulting "digested sludge" is then removed for final disposal.

The entire plant operates through a defined lifecycle, moving between states such as `offline`, `startup`, `operational`, and `shutdown`. During normal operation, it can be temporarily shifted into a `maintenance` state to allow for service before resuming its continuous treatment process. The system is designed to meet strict regulatory requirements for the quality of its discharged water and the stability of its processed sludge.
