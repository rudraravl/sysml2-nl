This model represents a large-scale, autonomous wind generator system, commonly known as a wind turbine, designed to convert the kinetic energy of wind into electrical power for a grid.

The physical structure consists of a tall tower which supports a nacelle, the main housing that contains the core machinery. Attached to the front of the nacelle is a rotor assembly, which includes a central hub and three large rotor blades. These blades are designed to capture wind, creating a mechanical torque that spins the rotor.

Inside the nacelle, a drivetrain converts this mechanical energy into electricity. The slow, high-torque rotation of the rotor is first fed into a gearbox, which increases the rotational speed. This high-speed rotation then drives a generator, which produces electrical power. The system also includes a yaw system that can rotate the entire nacelle to ensure the rotor is always facing directly into the wind for maximum efficiency, and a powerful brake system for both controlled shutdowns and emergency stops.

A central control system manages all operations. It monitors wind speed and direction, starting the turbine only when the wind is strong enough (cut-in speed) and shutting it down in dangerously high winds (cut-out speed) to prevent damage. During operation, the control system continuously adjusts the pitch angle of the rotor blades to regulate speed and optimize power output. The turbine operates in several distinct modes: an idle state when parked, a controlled start-up sequence, the active power-generating state, a normal stopping procedure, and an emergency shutdown state for safety.
