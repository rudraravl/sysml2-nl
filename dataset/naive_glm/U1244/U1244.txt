This model describes an autonomous industrial robotic system, specifically a 6-axis collaborative robot designed for automated pick-and-place tasks.

The system is composed of several key physical components working together. The central element is a **robotic arm** with six revolute joints (a base, shoulder, elbow, and a three-axis wrist), which gives it the flexibility to position and orient objects anywhere within its reach. Attached to the end of the arm is a **gripper** that can be commanded to grasp objects with a specific, controlled force.

The "brain" of the system is the **robot controller**, which plans and executes motion sequences. It can receive a queue of commands, each specifying a target position, orientation, speed, and acceleration, allowing for smooth and precise movements. Guiding these movements is a **vision sensor**, which acts as the system's eyes. It can capture and process images to detect and identify workpieces, determining their exact location and orientation to guide the arm for an accurate pickup.

A crucial aspect of the system is its integrated **safety controller**. This component continuously monitors the robot's operation to ensure it runs safely, especially around humans. It can enforce speed and force limits and is responsible for handling stop signals, triggering different safety states like a full emergency stop, a temporary protective stop, or a reduced-speed operating mode.

In operation, the system can function in several modes, including manual, automatic, and a teaching mode. A typical automated sequence involves the vision system identifying a workpiece, the controller planning a path to it, the arm moving into position, the gripper grasping the object, the arm then moving to a drop-off point, and the gripper releasing the object. The entire process is governed by a state machine that manages transitions between initializing, idle, active operation, and an emergency stop state, which immediately halts all motion and locks the joints.
