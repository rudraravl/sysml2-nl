This is a medical device for Heated Humidified High-Flow Therapy (HHHFT), also known as High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) therapy. It is designed to provide respiratory support to patients by delivering a precisely controlled mixture of air and oxygen that is warmed to body temperature and saturated with water vapor.

The system is composed of several key components that work in a sequence. It begins with separate sources for medical-grade air and pure oxygen, which are fed into a **Gas Blender**. This blender precisely mixes the two gases to achieve a specific oxygen concentration set by a clinician. The blended gas is then propelled by a **Flow Generator** to create the high-velocity stream of gas required for the therapy.

From there, the gas passes through a **Humidifier**, which adds moisture until it is nearly fully saturated, and then a **Heater**, which warms the gas to body temperature (approximately 37°C). This conditioning process is critical for patient comfort and to prevent drying out the airways. The warm, moist gas travels through a **Heated Tubing** to the patient, which maintains the temperature and prevents water from condensing in the tube. Finally, the gas is delivered to the patient through a **Nasal Cannula**.

The entire process is managed by a central **Control System**. This controller uses a set of sensors—for flow rate, temperature, and humidity—located near the end of the delivery tube to continuously monitor the gas being delivered. Based on these readings, the controller constantly sends commands to the gas blender, flow generator, and heater to make fine adjustments, ensuring the therapy delivered to the patient exactly matches the prescribed settings.

The device operates through various states, including an idle state, a priming phase to warm up, an active state for delivering therapy, and the ability to be paused or shut down. The system is designed with strict safety and performance requirements, such as maintaining a stable temperature, delivering a wide range of flow rates, providing high levels of humidity, and ensuring accurate oxygen concentration.
