This model represents a medical system for mechanical ventilation, centered around a tracheal tube that connects a patient to a ventilator. The system is designed to establish and maintain a secure artificial airway, allowing a machine to breathe for a patient who is unable to do so on their own.

The core component is the **Tracheal Tube**, a flexible tube inserted through the mouth into the patient's windpipe (trachea). This tube features a main channel, or **lumen**, for air to pass through, and a soft, **inflatable cuff** near its tip. When inflated, this cuff creates a seal against the tracheal wall, preventing air leakage and protecting the lungs from foreign substances. The tube also has a specially designed **beveled tip** for easier, less traumatic insertion and a safety opening called the **Murphy eye** to ensure airflow even if the main tip becomes blocked.

The tube acts as a bridge between the **Patient's airway** and a **Ventilator**. The ventilator is a life-support machine that controls the breathing process by setting the volume of each breath (tidal volume), the breathing rate, and the concentration of oxygen in the air delivered.

The system's operation follows a precise clinical procedure. The tube is first prepared and then carefully inserted into the patient's trachea. The cuff is then inflated to a specific, safe pressure—enough to create a seal but not so much as to cause injury. Once connected, the ventilator delivers controlled breaths through the tube to the patient's lungs. The entire process is carefully monitored for airway pressure and airflow to ensure the tube remains clear and the patient is ventilated safely and effectively. When mechanical ventilation is no longer needed, the cuff is deflated, and the tube is removed.
