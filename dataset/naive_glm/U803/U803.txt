This is a Passive Infrared (PIR) sensing unit, a common type of motion detector used in security systems, automatic lighting, and occupancy sensors. Its purpose is to detect the movement of warm bodies, such as people or animals, by sensing changes in the infrared (heat) radiation in its environment.

The unit is composed of several key components that work in sequence to detect motion. The process begins with a Fresnel lens array, a specialized, segmented lens that collects ambient infrared radiation from a wide field of view and focuses it onto the core sensor.

The focused heat energy then strikes a pair of pyroelectric sensor elements. These sensors are highly sensitive to changes in temperature and convert the incoming infrared radiation into a very small electrical voltage. When a warm body moves across the sensor's view, the focused heat moves from one sensor element to the other, creating a distinct change in the voltage output.

Because this initial signal is extremely weak, it is passed to a signal amplification stage, which significantly boosts the voltage to a level that can be analyzed. The amplified signal is then sent to a signal processing unit. This unit filters the signal to remove noise and compares it against a predefined threshold. If the signal's characteristics indicate that a warm body has moved through the detection zones, the processor generates a final digital output signal.

The entire system is powered by an external source, which is managed by an internal voltage regulator to ensure all components receive a stable and correct voltage for reliable operation. The sensor operates through a cycle of states, including a warm-up period upon power-up, a continuous monitoring state, and a detection-and-processing sequence when motion is registered.
