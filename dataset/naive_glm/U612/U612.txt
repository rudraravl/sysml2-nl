This is a temperature-controlled electrical switch, commonly known as a bimetallic thermostat. It's a self-contained device designed to automatically open or close an electrical circuit when the ambient temperature crosses a specific setpoint.

The core of the system is a bimetallic strip, which acts as both the temperature sensor and the actuator. This strip is a composite component made by bonding together two layers of different metals—one with a high rate of thermal expansion and one with a low rate. When the temperature changes, the two metals expand by different amounts, forcing the strip to bend.

This physical bending motion is harnessed to operate the switch. As the bimetallic strip curves or straightens with temperature fluctuations, it moves an electrical contact. When the strip bends enough, it pushes the movable contact against a fixed contact, closing a gap and completing an electrical circuit. When the temperature returns to its original state, the strip straightens, pulling the contacts apart and breaking the circuit.

The entire assembly is designed to operate at a specific "setpoint" temperature and includes a "hysteresis" value, which creates a small temperature range to prevent the switch from rapidly cycling on and off. This device requires no external power or electronics for its sensing and actuation, making it a simple, reliable component for applications like home thermostats, thermal safety cut-offs in appliances, and circuit breakers.
