This model describes a Tristimulus Colorimeter, a scientific instrument designed for the objective and precise measurement of color. It analyzes an incoming light source, or the light reflected from an object's surface, to provide standardized, quantitative color data. The final output includes CIE tristimulus values (XYZ), chromaticity coordinates (xy), and correlated color temperature (CCT).

The system's operation begins when light enters through an optical port into an integrating sphere. The sphere's purpose is to ensure the light is uniformly diffuse, eliminating inconsistencies from the angle of the light source and providing a more accurate reading.

From the sphere, the light is directed to a motorized filter wheel. This critical component contains three specific optical filters (X, Y, and Z) which are designed to mimic the color response of the human eye according to the CIE standard observer. The system operates by sequentially rotating each of these filters into the light path.

For each filter position, the resulting colored light strikes a dedicated high-sensitivity silicon photodiode sensor. This sensor converts the intensity of the filtered light into a very weak electrical current. This small current is then boosted and converted into a measurable voltage by a transimpedance amplifier. An analog-to-digital converter then digitizes this voltage, turning the analog measurement into a numerical value. The system uses three separate but identical channels for this process—one for each of the X, Y, and Z filters.

A central microprocessor unit serves as the brain of the instrument. It controls the rotation of the filter wheel and collects the three distinct digital readings from the A/D converters. Using a pre-loaded calibration matrix, the processor computes the final XYZ tristimulus values. It then performs further calculations to derive the more intuitive chromaticity coordinates and the correlated color temperature.

Finally, the complete color measurement is sent to an integrated display module for the user to view and can also be output through a data port for external logging or analysis. The entire measurement process is an automated sequence: when a user initiates a measurement, the wheel rotates through its X, Y, and Z positions, taking a reading at each stop before presenting the final calculated color data.
