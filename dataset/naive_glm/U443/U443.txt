This model describes a Veress Needle Insufflation System, a medical device used in laparoscopic (or minimally invasive) surgery. Its primary purpose is to safely create a "pneumoperitoneum" – an inflated abdominal cavity – by delivering pressurized carbon dioxide (CO2) gas. This inflation creates a clear, expanded space, allowing surgeons to see and work effectively.

The system consists of two main components: the Veress Needle itself and a CO2 Insufflator.

**The Veress Needle** is a specialized, disposable needle designed for safe entry into the abdominal cavity. Its key feature is a dual-component design for safety:
*   A **Sharp Cannula**, which is the sharp, hollow outer tube designed to penetrate the layers of the abdominal wall. It has a standard diameter of 2.1mm and is typically 120mm long.
*   A **Spring-Loaded Obturator**, which is a blunt-tipped inner stylet. While the needle is pushed through the resistant tissue of the abdominal wall, the spring keeps the blunt obturator retracted, exposing the sharp cannula tip. Upon entering the low-resistance space of the peritoneal cavity, the spring instantly pushes the blunt obturator forward, shielding the sharp tip. This is a critical safety mechanism to prevent accidental puncture of internal organs.

The needle assembly also includes a **stopcock** (a small valve) to manually open or close the gas flow path and an integrated **pressure sensor** at its base to monitor the pressure at the needle's tip.

**The CO2 Insufflator** is the gas management unit. It connects to a high-pressure CO2 cylinder and precisely regulates the gas delivered to the needle. It controls both the flow rate and the target pressure to ensure the abdomen is inflated safely and maintained at a stable pressure (typically between 1.6-2.0 kPa) throughout the surgical procedure.

The overall operational procedure involves several distinct phases:
1.  **Insertion:** The surgeon carefully pushes the needle through the abdominal wall. The system experiences increasing resistance during this phase.
2.  **Cavity Entry:** A sudden drop in resistance and pressure indicates the needle tip has successfully entered the peritoneal cavity, and the safety obturator extends.
3.  **Placement Verification:** Before inflating, the surgeon performs checks (like aspiration or saline drop tests) and monitors the initial low-pressure reading to confirm correct placement.
4.  **Insufflation:** Once placement is verified, the insufflator delivers CO2 through the needle at a controlled, low flow rate. The abdominal cavity gradually inflates until the target pressure for surgery is reached.

The system operates through various states, from being idle before insertion, advancing through tissue, verifying placement, actively insufflating, and finally reaching the target pressure, signaling readiness for the main surgical procedure.
