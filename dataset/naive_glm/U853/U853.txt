This model describes a simple proximity sensing system that uses a magnetically operated reed switch to control an electrical circuit. The system is designed to detect the presence of a magnet and, in response, activate or deactivate an electrical load, such as a light or an alarm.

The core of the system is the reed switch, a small electromechanical component. It consists of two flexible, ferromagnetic metal contacts, or "reeds," made from a nickel-iron alloy. These reeds are sealed inside a hermetically sealed glass tube filled with an inert gas like nitrogen to protect them from dust and corrosion.

The system's operation is straightforward. An external magnet acts as the trigger. When this magnet is brought close to the reed switch, its magnetic field causes the two internal reeds to attract each other. Once the magnetic field is strong enough to overcome the reeds' stiffness, they snap together, making physical contact. This closes the electrical circuit, allowing current to flow from a power source to an electrical load, turning it on.

When the magnet is moved away, the magnetic field weakens. At a certain point, the magnetic attraction is no longer strong enough to hold the reeds together, and they spring apart, breaking the electrical contact. This opens the circuit and turns the load off.

The system exhibits hysteresis, meaning the magnetic field strength required to close the switch is higher than the strength at which it opens. This prevents the switch from rapidly fluttering or "chattering" if the magnet is held at the edge of its activation range. The complete system comprises the magnet, the reed switch, a power source to drive the circuit, and the electrical load that is being controlled.
