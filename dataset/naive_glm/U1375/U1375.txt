This model represents Al-Jazari's Elephant Clock, an elaborate and ornate water-powered automaton from the 13th century. It is a complex timekeeping device designed not only to mark time but also to serve as an impressive display of mechanical artistry.

The clock's operation is centered around a precise water-driven mechanism hidden within the elephant's body. A large upper reservoir holds the water, which acts as the system's power source. This water flows out through a special regulator designed to maintain a perfectly constant drip rate, ensuring accurate timekeeping regardless of the water level in the main reservoir.

The regulated water slowly fills a floating bowl. Over a period of exactly thirty minutes, the bowl becomes heavy enough to sink. This sinking action is the key trigger for the entire system. As the bowl submerges, it pulls on a series of ropes and pulleys connected to a trigger lever mechanism located in the tower, or "howdah," on the elephant's back.

The tripped lever initiates a rapid and coordinated sequence of events. It causes a ball reservoir to release a single metal ball. This ball drops into the mouth of a mechanical dragon automaton, which then swivels downward to release the ball. The ball falls from the dragon, striking a cymbal held by a seated figure, creating a sound to mark the half-hour. Simultaneously, the lever mechanism advances a main time dial and moves the pointer of a "scribe" figure to indicate the current time.

Finally, the same trigger action that starts this sequence also causes the sunken bowl to tip over, emptying its water. Now light again, the bowl floats back to the surface, and the entire 30-minute cycle begins anew. This continuous, self-resetting process allows the clock to run autonomously, marking every half-hour with a spectacular display of motion and sound.
