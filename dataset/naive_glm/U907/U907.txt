This is a digital temperature gauge system designed for continuous monitoring and alerting based on environmental or process temperatures. The system is composed of three primary, interconnected components: a temperature sensor, a central processor, and a digital display.

The sensor is the physical component responsible for taking temperature measurements. It operates within a specific range, from -40°C to 150°C, and is designed to be accurate to within 0.5°C. It continuously captures temperature data and sends it out as a structured reading that includes the value, a timestamp, and a quality metric.

The processor is the "brain" of the gauge. It receives the raw data from the sensor at a regular interval of one second. It then applies calibration adjustments and processes the reading. A key function of the processor is to evaluate the temperature against two configurable thresholds: a 'warning' limit (set to 80°C) and a 'critical' limit (set to 100°C). Based on this comparison, it determines the system's current alert status—normal, warning, or critical.

The display unit provides the user interface. It receives the final, processed temperature and the current alert status from the processor. It then presents this information to the user, showing the numerical temperature and a clear visual indicator of the alert level.

The entire gauge operates in distinct modes. It can be in a low-power `standby` mode, an `active` mode for normal monitoring, or a `calibration` mode for maintenance. During active monitoring, the system's internal state reflects the temperature conditions, transitioning between normal, warning, and critical states as the temperature fluctuates past the set thresholds. This ensures an operator is always aware of the system's thermal status.
