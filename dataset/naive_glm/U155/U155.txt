This model represents a single-use, spring-driven medical autoinjector, similar to an EpiPen, designed for the rapid and safe self-administration of a fixed dose of medication. Its primary function is to allow a user to perform a subcutaneous injection quickly and reliably, especially in emergency situations, without needing medical training.

The device is composed of several key mechanical components that work together in a pre-defined sequence. It contains a pre-filled **Medication Cartridge** holding a specific drug, such as epinephrine. For safety, the autoinjector is initially inert, protected by a **Safety Cap** that must be manually removed to arm the device.

To use it, a person presses the device against the injection site (e.g., their outer thigh). This applies force to an **Activation Mechanism**, which requires a specific pressure to trigger, preventing accidental discharge. Once activated, a powerful, pre-loaded **Spring-Driven Plunger** is released. This action instantly drives the **Needle Assembly**, deploying a concealed needle to a precise, predetermined depth into the tissue.

Simultaneously, the force from the plunger acts on the medication cartridge, pushing the medicine through the needle in a controlled flow and at a specific pressure. The entire injection process is designed to be completed within about 10 seconds. The device includes a **Status Indicator** that provides visual or audible feedback (like a "click") to inform the user when the injection has started and when it is complete.

After the full dose is delivered, and the user pulls the device away from their skin, a **Needle Shield** automatically slides over the exposed needle and locks permanently in place. This crucial post-injection safety feature prevents accidental needlestick injuries and ensures the device can be disposed of safely.
