This model represents an automated in-line check weigher system, a type of industrial machine commonly found on production and packaging lines for quality control. Its purpose is to weigh products continuously as they move along a conveyor belt and automatically sort them based on whether their weight falls within a specified range.

The system is composed of several key mechanical and electronic components working in sequence. Products first enter on an **Infeed Conveyor** which transports them at a controlled speed. From there, they move onto a **Weighing Station**, a specialized section of the conveyor with a high-precision scale that measures the mass of each product while it is in motion.

The measured weight is instantly sent to a central **Control Unit**. This controller compares the product's actual weight against a pre-set target weight and its acceptable upper and lower tolerances (e.g., a target of 500g with a ±5g tolerance).

Based on this evaluation, the controller sends a signal to a **Reject Mechanism** located downstream from the scale. If a product is determined to be underweight or overweight, the mechanism is activated—this could be a pneumatic pusher, an air jet, or a drop-flap—which physically diverts the non-compliant product off the main line.

Products that are within the acceptable weight range pass the reject station untouched and continue onto an **Accept Conveyor** for the next stage of processing. The rejected items are channeled onto a separate **Reject Conveyor**, collecting them for inspection or disposal. The entire system operates in a continuous cycle, moving from an idle state to an active state where it performs its weighing, evaluating, and sorting functions for each product that passes through.
