This model describes a portable, battery-powered medical device designed to administer therapeutic treatments to a patient. Its primary function is to deliver a controlled dose of therapy according to prescribed parameters, while simultaneously and continuously monitoring both the patient's vital signs and its own operational status to ensure safety.

The system is composed of several key integrated subsystems. A **power subsystem**, consisting of a rechargeable battery and a power management unit, provides stable and regulated electrical power to the entire device. This ensures it can operate reliably for extended periods, such as a minimum of eight hours, without being plugged in.

The core of the device is the **therapy subsystem**, which contains the unit responsible for actually delivering the treatment. This unit receives its power from the power management system and administers the therapy (e.g., a specific dose rate over a set duration) directly to the patient based on parameters configured by a medical professional.

A critical component is the dedicated **safety monitor**. This system constantly checks the patient's vitals—such as heart rate, blood pressure, and temperature—and the device's internal status. If it detects an unsafe condition, such as an abnormal vital sign or a device malfunction, it has the authority to automatically stop the therapy, trigger an alarm, and put the device into a safe state.

All these components are coordinated by a central **control processor**. This processor acts as the brain of the device, managing the overall operating mode and ensuring that therapy is only delivered when all safety conditions are met. A **user interface** with a display allows a clinician to power on the device, configure treatment parameters, start and pause the therapy, and view the system status and any alarms.

The device operates through a well-defined lifecycle. After being powered on, it performs a self-test. If successful, it enters a ready state, waiting for a user to configure the treatment plan. Once configured and started, it enters an active state of delivering therapy while continuously monitoring. The treatment can be paused and resumed as needed. If a safety issue arises, it enters an alarm state to alert the operator. Upon successful completion of the treatment, the device finalizes the session, can generate a report, and returns to a standby mode, ready for the next patient. An emergency stop function is also included to immediately and safely shut down the device.
