This model describes a handheld electronic stud finder, a tool designed to locate wooden or metal studs hidden behind wall surfaces like drywall. It is powered by a 9V battery pack, which has its own internal states to track its charge level from full to depleted.

The core of the device is a capacitive sensor that constantly measures the density of the material it is placed against. This sensor data is interpreted by an internal signal processor. The processor filters the raw readings and compares them to a baseline measurement to determine if a denser object, like a stud, is present.

When a stud is detected, the stud finder alerts the user through two methods: a visual indicator with multiple LEDs that likely light up to show proximity, and an audio indicator that emits a beeping tone.

The device operates in a clear sequence. When first turned on, it enters a calibration mode, where the user holds it against an empty section of the wall to establish a baseline reading. After calibration, it switches to scanning mode. As the user slides the device across the wall, it continuously takes measurements. When it detects the edge or center of a stud, it activates the lights and sounds to pinpoint the location for the user. According to its specifications, the stud finder is required to detect studs through up to 38mm of wall material and locate them with an accuracy of within 13mm.
