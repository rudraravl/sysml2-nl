This model describes a gravity-fed intravenous (IV) infusion system, the kind commonly used in hospitals to deliver fluids to a patient at a controlled rate.

The complete system consists of a sterile fluid bag (IV Bag) suspended above the patient, a transparent drip chamber, flexible delivery tubing, and a connection for the patient's catheter. The entire system is designed to work using gravity; the height of the IV bag provides the pressure to move the fluid.

The core of the system is the drip chamber. As fluid flows from the IV bag, it passes through a small orifice in the chamber, forming distinct, visible drops. This transparent chamber allows a nurse or clinician to visually count the number of drops per minute, which is the primary method for setting and monitoring the infusion rate. The chamber also includes an air vent to prevent a vacuum from forming in the bag, ensuring a steady flow.

The flow rate is manually controlled by a roller clamp on the delivery tubing. By sliding the roller, a caregiver can constrict or open the flexible tube, precisely increasing or decreasing the speed at which the fluid is delivered. The fluid then travels through the tubing to the patient connection point, such as a luer lock, which securely attaches to the patient's IV line.

The system operates through various states, including an initial priming phase to fill the drip chamber and purge air from the tubing. Once primed and ready, the infusion can be started, paused, or stopped. The system is designed to monitor for unsafe conditions, such as the drip chamber running empty or becoming overfilled, and can generate alarms to alert the caregiver.
