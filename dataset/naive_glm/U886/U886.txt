This system is a soil moisture sensing unit, an electronic device designed to be placed in the ground to measure the water content and temperature of the soil.

The core of the unit is a capacitive sensor probe that directly interacts with the soil. It works by measuring the soil's capacitance, a property that changes depending on how much water is present. Alongside this, an integrated temperature sensor takes a simultaneous temperature reading.

These two raw measurements—capacitance and temperature—are then handled by a signal processor. This component's job is to convert the raw capacitance value into a more meaningful soil moisture percentage. It uses the temperature reading to correct and calibrate the measurement, ensuring accuracy across different environmental conditions.

A central microcontroller acts as the brain of the unit. It orchestrates the measurement process, collects the finalized moisture percentage and temperature data from the processor, packages it into a single data reading complete with a timestamp, and sends this information out through a data port. The microcontroller also manages the unit's power consumption.

The device operates through a clear sequence of states: upon activation, it calibrates itself, then enters a continuous cycle of measuring, processing the data, and reporting the final reading. The entire unit is designed to be highly responsive, providing a complete reading within two seconds of being activated, and is specified to be accurate within +/- 3 percent. It is built to function across the full spectrum of soil conditions, from completely dry to fully saturated.
