This model describes an automated Drum Pumping System designed to transfer fluids from a source drum to a destination tank. The system is built for reliable, safe, and efficient operation in an industrial setting.

The complete system consists of a source drum that holds the initial fluid, a destination tank to receive it, and the central drum pump that performs the transfer. The pump itself is a sophisticated assembly powered by an electric drive motor. This motor spins a bladed drum rotor, which is the component that physically moves the fluid through the system. Suction and discharge pipes connect the pump to the drum and tank, and a high-integrity mechanical seal prevents leaks during operation.

The system's operation is managed by an intelligent control sequence. Before starting, it performs safety checks, ensuring there is enough fluid in the source drum and sufficient empty space in the destination tank. The pump then enters a "priming" state to prepare for fluid transfer, after which it begins its main "running" state.

Throughout the process, the system actively monitors its own performance, including flow rate and pressure. It has critical safety features built in. For instance, it constantly checks for signs of cavitation—a harmful condition where vapor bubbles form and collapse—and will automatically shut down to prevent damage if it's detected. It also stops automatically once the destination tank is nearly full to prevent spillage. The pump can be in various states reflecting its status, such as idle, priming, running, stopping, or faulted.
