This model describes an industrial pumping subsystem designed specifically to transport a difficult fluid known as froth. The system's primary purpose is to move this froth, which is a mixture containing significant amounts of gas and solid particles, from a large supply tank to a receiving tank through a network of pipes.

The core of the system is a heavy-duty froth pump, specifically engineered to handle abrasive and aerated fluids. It features a robust design with a wear-resistant, high-chrome white iron casing and a specialized three-blade impeller to prevent clogging and efficiently move the froth. A double mechanical seal system is used to prevent leaks. This pump is powered by a high-power electric motor, which is directly connected to the pump's drive shaft.

The fluid's journey begins in a supply tank. From there, it flows through a section of wide-diameter, rubber-lined steel suction piping to the pump's inlet. After being pressurized by the pump, the froth is pushed through a narrower discharge pipe, also rubber-lined to resist wear. A control valve is located in the discharge line to precisely regulate the flow rate before the froth is finally delivered into the receiving tank.

The entire subsystem is equipped with instruments for monitoring and control. Pressure gauges are installed on both the suction and discharge sides of the pump, and a flow meter on the discharge line measures the final output. This sensor data is critical for the pump's operational control system.

The pump's operation is managed through a sequence of states to ensure safety and efficiency. It starts in a 'stopped' state, moves to a 'priming' phase to ensure the pump is full of fluid, then 'accelerates' to its target speed. During normal 'running', the system continuously monitors for adverse conditions like low flow, excessive discharge pressure, or cavitation (the formation of damaging vapor bubbles). If an issue is detected, the system can react accordingly. It also includes defined procedures for normal and emergency shutdowns.
