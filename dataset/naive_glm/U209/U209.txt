This is a high-precision electronic system designed to generate two perfectly synchronized electrical signals. Its main purpose is to produce a pair of oscillating signals, both at 1000 Hz, and to actively maintain a constant, stable 90-degree phase difference between them. This type of dual-phase signal is crucial for advanced applications like coherent signal processing, which can be found in sophisticated radar, modern telecommunications equipment, or scientific instrumentation.

The system is built around several key components that work together in a continuous feedback loop:
*   A **Primary Oscillator** and a **Secondary Oscillator** act as the sources, each generating a fundamental 1000 Hz electrical signal.
*   A **Phase Synchronizer** is the brain of the system. It constantly monitors the signals from both oscillators, measures the precise phase difference between them, and compares it to the target of 90 degrees.
*   If any drift or error is detected, the synchronizer calculates the necessary adjustment and sends a control signal back to the oscillators to correct their phase, ensuring they remain perfectly locked in the desired relationship.
*   A dedicated **Power Supply** provides stable, regulated power to the electronics, converting a 12V input to a 5V operational voltage.
*   A **Monitoring Unit** oversees the entire operation, tracking the quality of the synchronization, counting any errors, and determining the overall status of the system.

The system operates through several distinct modes. It starts in **standby**, moves to **initializing** upon startup, and then enters an **active** mode where it works to achieve the phase lock. Once the signals are stable and the 90-degree phase relationship is met with high quality, it transitions to a **synchronized** state. If the signal quality drops significantly, it enters a **degraded** mode and attempts to recover the lock, ensuring robust and reliable performance.
