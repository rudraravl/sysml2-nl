This is an electromagnetic inductive pumping system designed to transfer electrically conductive fluids, such as liquid metals or saline solutions, from a source reservoir to a destination tank without using any moving mechanical parts.

The core of the system is the inductive pump itself. It consists of a set of three-phase electromagnetic coils wrapped around a laminated steel core, creating a channel or duct made of stainless steel through which the fluid flows. By energizing the coils with a variable-frequency power supply, the pump generates a traveling magnetic field along the length of the duct. This magnetic field induces electrical eddy currents within the conductive fluid. The interaction between these currents and the magnetic field creates a Lorentz force, which smoothly and continuously pushes the fluid forward.

The complete system includes the source and destination tanks, along with a sophisticated control architecture. A dedicated pump controller manages the entire operation, using a PID feedback loop for precise flow regulation. Flow sensors at both the inlet and outlet of the pump provide real-time data on flow rate and pressure back to the controller. Based on a commanded flow rate, the controller constantly adjusts the frequency of the high-power, three-phase power supply to speed up or slow down the fluid movement.

The pump operates through several distinct states: idle, starting, running, stopping, and a protective faulted state. For safety and longevity, the pump includes an integrated cooling system and a temperature sensor to monitor the electromagnetic coils. If the temperature exceeds safe limits during operation, the controller will automatically shut down the system and enter the faulted state, preventing damage. The pump can only be restarted once it has cooled to a safe level. This design is ideal for applications requiring high reliability, low maintenance, and precise handling of fluids where mechanical components like impellers and seals would be problematic.
