This model represents a digital aspirated psychrometer, a scientific instrument designed to accurately measure atmospheric humidity. The system determines humidity by comparing the temperature difference between a dry environment and a wet one, a principle fundamental to psychrometry.

The core of the system consists of two specialized thermometers. A **Dry-Bulb Thermometer** measures the standard ambient air temperature. A second, **Wet-Bulb Thermometer**, has its sensing element covered by a fabric wick. This wick is kept constantly moist by drawing water from a small, integrated **Water Reservoir**.

To ensure consistent and accurate readings, a **Ventilation Fan** is used to draw a continuous and controlled stream of air across both thermometers. As air flows over the wet wick, the water evaporates, causing a cooling effect. The temperature of the wet-bulb thermometer drops to a level dependent on the air's humidity—the drier the air, the faster the evaporation and the lower the wet-bulb temperature.

The temperature readings from both thermometers are sent to a central **Humidity Processor**. This processing unit acts as the brains of the device. It first validates the incoming temperature data and then uses the difference between the dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures, along with the atmospheric pressure, to calculate the precise relative humidity.

Finally, the calculated humidity, as well as the raw dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures, are sent to a **Display Unit**. This screen presents the environmental data to the user, providing a clear and constantly updated reading of atmospheric conditions. The entire system is engineered to operate within a specific temperature range and to provide stable, accurate humidity measurements within a defined response time.
