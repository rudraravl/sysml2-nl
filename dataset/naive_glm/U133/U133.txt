This is a minimally invasive, catheter-based surgical device called a Valvulotome, designed to treat chronic venous insufficiency. The system's purpose is to perform a procedure known as a valvulotomy, which involves precisely cutting the leaflets of faulty or "incompetent" valves within a patient's veins to restore proper blood flow.

The device consists of four main physical components that work together:

1.  **Handle Assembly:** This is the part the surgeon holds and manipulates outside the patient's body. It features an ergonomic grip and controls, such as a lever, that allow the surgeon to precisely deploy and operate the cutting tip.
2.  **Catheter Shaft:** A long, thin, and flexible tube that connects the handle to the cutting mechanism. Its flexibility allows the surgeon to navigate it through the body's complex network of veins to reach the target valve.
3.  **Rotational Mechanism:** Housed at the tip of the catheter, this component spins the cutting blade at a controlled speed to perform the incision.
4.  **Cutting Blade:** The active tip of the device. It is a sharp, small blade that remains safely retracted within the catheter during navigation. Once the device is in position at the target valve, the surgeon can deploy the blade to perform the cut.

In a typical procedure, the surgeon inserts the catheter into a blood vessel and carefully guides it to the location of the incompetent venous valve. Once correctly positioned, the surgeon uses the handle controls to deploy the cutting blade. The rotational mechanism is then activated to spin the blade, which incises the valve leaflets. The system is designed to provide feedback on the forces being applied to prevent accidental damage or perforation of the blood vessel wall. After the incision is successfully made and blood flow is verified to have improved, the blade is safely retracted back into the catheter, and the entire device is withdrawn from the patient's body.
