This is a real-time sound locator system designed to detect and pinpoint the three-dimensional position of an acoustic source. It operates by using a technique called Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA), which measures the slight time differences in a sound wave reaching multiple sensors.

The system's core component is a microphone array, which consists of four individual microphones arranged in a specific spatial configuration. When a sound is made, it reaches each of these microphones at a slightly different moment. The raw electrical signals from the microphone array are sent to a signal conditioner, which amplifies them and filters out unwanted background noise, preparing them for analysis.

These clean, digitized signals are then fed into a central Digital Signal Processor (DSP). The DSP is the brain of the system; it precisely calculates the time delays between the signals arriving at each microphone. Using these time delays and the known physical layout of the microphone array, the processor triangulates the sound's origin in space. This calculation determines the sound source's direction (both horizontally and vertically), its distance, and its intensity.

Once the location is calculated and validated for confidence, the information is passed to a display unit. This unit, which could be an LED matrix or a screen, provides a visual representation to the user, showing where the sound is coming from. A dedicated power supply provides regulated electrical power to all components in the system. In essence, the system continuously listens to its environment and updates the display in real-time to track acoustic events.
