This model describes a wheel speed sensing unit, a critical component in a vehicle's Anti-lock Braking System (ABS). Its purpose is to accurately measure the rotational speed and acceleration of a car's wheel and report this data to the main vehicle control system.

The system is composed of two primary physical parts: a tone ring and an ABS sensor. The tone ring is a circular, toothed metal ring that is mounted on the wheel hub and rotates along with the wheel.

The ABS sensor is a stationary electronic device mounted close to the tone ring. It consists of two main functional parts:
1.  **A Magnetic Sensing Element:** This is the core transducer that detects the passing teeth of the rotating tone ring. As the teeth move past the sensor, it generates a raw, unprocessed electrical signal whose frequency is directly proportional to the wheel's speed. This element is designed to operate in a wide range of temperatures, from -40°C to 125°C.
2.  **A Signal Conditioner:** This unit takes the raw, noisy signal from the magnetic element and processes it. It amplifies the signal, filters out electrical noise, and then calculates the final, precise measurements. These calculations determine the wheel's angular velocity and acceleration. The conditioner also continuously validates the signal to assess its own health, assigning a status (like operational, degraded, or failed) and a confidence score to the final data.

In operation, the sensing unit functions through several states. When the vehicle is turned on, the sensor powers up and runs a self-test. If it passes, it enters its normal operational state, continuously measuring wheel speed. If the signal quality drops, it can switch to a degraded mode with reduced performance to maintain basic functionality. If a critical fault is detected, it enters a fault mode and safely shuts down to avoid sending erroneous data to the vehicle's braking system.
