This model describes a Schiøtz tonometer, a mechanical medical instrument designed to measure the pressure inside a patient's eye, known as intraocular pressure (IOP). It operates on the principle of indentation, determining the eye's internal pressure by measuring how deeply a weighted plunger can press into the cornea.

The system is composed of several key mechanical parts working in concert. A curved **footplate** provides a stable base that rests gently on the surface of the eye. Through the center of this footplate, a precisely weighted **plunger** is free to move. This plunger's weight, which can be modified by adding one of several **interchangeable weights** from a set (e.g., 5.5g, 7.5g, 10.0g), provides the known force needed for the measurement.

When placed on the eye, the force from the weighted plunger causes it to indent the cornea. The higher the pressure inside the eye, the more resistance it offers, resulting in a shallower indentation. This very small physical displacement of the plunger is then mechanically amplified by a **lever mechanism**. This amplified movement drives a pointer on a **calibrated scale**, providing a raw reading to the operator.

The measurement process involves distinct operational states: the tonometer is first at rest (**idle**), then carefully placed on the eye (**positioning**), at which point it performs the indentation (**measuring**), and finally displays the result (**reading**). The operator then uses the scale reading and the specific weight that was applied to look up the final intraocular pressure value (in mmHg) from a standardized conversion table. The entire assembly is held together by a structural **housing frame**.
