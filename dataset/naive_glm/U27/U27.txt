This model describes the Thymio, a small, two-wheeled educational robot designed to teach programming and robotics concepts in an interactive way.

The robot features a compact body and is propelled by a differential drive system, using two independently controlled DC motors for its wheels. This setup allows it to move forward, backward, and turn with precision.

For navigation and environmental awareness, the Thymio is equipped with an extensive array of proximity sensors. It has five sensors facing forward, two facing the rear, and two pointing downwards. These sensors allow it to detect obstacles in its path, objects behind it, and even the edge of a surface like a table, preventing it from falling.

Users can interact with the robot through a set of capacitive touch buttons on its top shell for starting programs or giving commands. The robot communicates its status and actions back to the user through both visual and audible feedback. It features a ring of multi-color RGB LEDs on top and two underneath, which can change color to indicate different modes or events. A small piezo speaker allows it to produce sounds and simple melodies.

At its core, a central microcontroller acts as the robot's brain, processing sensor data, making decisions, and controlling the motors and feedback systems. The entire unit is powered by an internal rechargeable battery.

In a typical autonomous operation, the robot continuously scans its surroundings for obstacles. If its path is clear, it will cruise forward. Upon detecting an object, it automatically calculates and executes an avoidance maneuver, changing its direction to navigate around the obstacle. During this process, it uses its lights and sounds to signal its state, such as turning red and beeping when it's actively avoiding a collision, making its behavior clear and intuitive for learners.
