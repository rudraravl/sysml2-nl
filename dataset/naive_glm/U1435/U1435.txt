This model describes an automated irrigation system designed to maintain optimal soil moisture for agricultural fields or large landscaped areas while conserving water. The system is a complete, self-regulating solution that handles everything from water storage to intelligent delivery.

The core of the system is a central water reservoir that feeds a high-pressure water pump. When activated, this pump draws water from the reservoir and sends it through a primary control valve. This valve, which can be fully opened, partially opened, or closed, regulates the flow and pressure of water delivered to a network of sprinkler heads. The system uses four sprinkler heads, configured to cover different zones with specific spray patterns, such as a 180-degree sector or a full 360-degree circle, ensuring comprehensive coverage of the target area.

The "brain" of the system is an irrigation controller that makes decisions based on real-time field data. Multiple moisture sensors are placed throughout the irrigated area, constantly measuring the percentage of moisture in the soil. These sensors feed their readings to the controller.

The system operates in a continuous, automated cycle. The controller constantly monitors the sensor data. If the soil moisture drops below a pre-set minimum threshold, the controller automatically initiates a watering cycle. It sends a command to the primary valve to open and simultaneously activates the water pump. As the sprinklers distribute water, the sensors continue to provide feedback. Once the soil moisture reaches the desired target level, the controller sends commands to close the valve and shut down the pump, completing the cycle. This ensures that water is used only when and where it's needed, preventing overwatering and conserving resources.
