This model describes a Net Radiometer System, a scientific instrument designed to measure the balance between incoming and outgoing longwave radiation at the Earth's surface. This is a crucial measurement in fields like meteorology, climatology, and agriculture.

The system's core consists of two precision sensors called pyrgeometers, mounted back-to-back. One sensor faces upward to measure the downward radiation coming from the sky and atmosphere. The other sensor faces downward to measure the upward radiation being emitted and reflected from the ground or surface below.

Each of these pyrgeometers generates a raw electrical signal, specifically a voltage, which is proportional to the radiation it detects. These signals, along with the sensors' own body temperature readings, are fed into a central Signal Processor. This processor is the brain of the system; it uses specific calibration factors to convert the raw voltages into standardized, scientifically meaningful irradiance values (measured in Watts per square meter). After converting both the upward and downward signals, it calculates the "net radiation" by subtracting the outgoing value from the incoming one.

The complete, processed data set—which includes the downward, upward, and net irradiance values, along with a sensor temperature reading and a timestamp—is then sent to a Data Logger. This component stores a history of these measurements at a defined sampling interval, allowing for long-term data collection and analysis. A dedicated Power Supply provides regulated power to all the electronic components, ensuring stable and reliable operation.

Operationally, the system transitions between an idle state and an active measuring state. When measuring, it follows a continuous cycle: acquiring raw signals, converting them to irradiance, computing the net value, validating the measurement to ensure it's within expected bounds, and finally, storing the valid data before beginning the next acquisition. The system is engineered to be highly accurate (within ±10 W/m²), responsive (achieving 95% of its reading in under 10 seconds), and capable of measuring a wide range of radiation conditions.
