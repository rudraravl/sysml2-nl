This model describes a complete intravenous (IV) therapy system, designed to provide safe and controlled access to a patient's vein. The system is used for medical procedures like administering fluids and medication or drawing blood samples for testing. It includes both the disposable physical device and the electronic equipment used to control and monitor the process.

The core of the system is a venous access device that consists of several interconnected components:

*   **Venous Catheter:** A thin, flexible polyurethane tube that is inserted into a patient's vein (such as the antecubital or cephalic vein in the arm).
*   **Safety Needle:** A needle used to guide the catheter into the vein during insertion. It features a safety mechanism that retracts the needle after use to prevent accidental needlesticks.
*   **Hub:** A connection point on the outside of the catheter with multiple needleless ports, allowing for the connection of infusion lines or syringes without requiring additional needles.
*   **Infusion Pump:** An electronic, battery-powered device that pushes fluids through the line at a precise, controlled rate. It keeps track of the total volume delivered.
*   **Flow Regulator and Pressure Sensor:** These are critical safety and control components. The regulator ensures the fluid flows at the correct rate, while the sensor continuously monitors the pressure within the line.

The system's operation follows a clear lifecycle. It begins as a sterile, packaged device. A clinician first locates a suitable vein on the patient. The catheter is then inserted using the safety needle. Once a blood return confirms correct placement, the catheter is advanced, the needle is withdrawn, and its safety feature is engaged.

Once deployed, the system can be used for several functions:

*   **Administering Infusions:** The infusion pump is connected and programmed to deliver a specific volume of fluid or medication at a set rate. During this process, the system actively monitors the line pressure. If the pressure becomes too high, which could indicate a blockage (occlusion), the pump automatically stops and triggers an alarm to alert medical staff.
*   **Drawing Blood Samples:** A blood sample can be drawn through the hub. The procedure involves first discarding a small initial volume to clear the line, then collecting the required sample, and finally flushing the catheter with saline to keep it clear.
*   **Maintaining Patency:** To prevent the catheter from clotting when not in active use, the system is designed to be flushed with saline at regular intervals, typically every eight hours.

The entire system is designed with safety in mind, ensuring biocompatible materials are used, flow rates are accurately controlled, and pressure is constantly monitored to prevent injury to the patient's vein. After use, the catheter is removed from the patient and the entire disposable assembly is safely discarded.
