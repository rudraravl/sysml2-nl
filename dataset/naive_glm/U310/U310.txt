This is a portable medical device known as a Microcurrent Electrical Neuromuscular Stimulator (MENS). It is designed to deliver very low-level, precisely controlled electrical currents to a patient's body through skin-surface electrodes. The primary purpose of this therapy is to reduce pain, promote tissue healing, and enhance muscle function by applying electrical pulses that mimic the body's natural bioelectrical signals.

The system is a self-contained, battery-powered unit with several key components working together:

*   **Power System:** A rechargeable **Battery Module** provides the necessary electrical power for the entire device, designed for several hours of continuous operation.
*   **User Interface:** This is how a clinician or user interacts with the device. It features a display and controls to configure a therapy session by setting parameters like the intensity of the current, the frequency and width of the electrical pulses, and the total duration of the treatment. It also provides real-time feedback on the device's status, battery level, and therapy progress.
*   **Signal Generation and Control:** The core of the device is a **Pulse Generator** that creates the specific therapeutic electrical pulses. It can operate in various modes, such as delivering a continuous stream of pulses, short bursts, or a modulated pattern. The generator receives its instructions from the user interface.
*   **Delivery and Safety:** The electrical pulses are delivered to the patient via an **Electrode Assembly**, which consists of two or more pads placed on the skin. Before reaching the electrodes, the signal passes through a **Current Regulator**. This crucial component fine-tunes the output to ensure the delivered current is accurate and stable. A dedicated **Safety Monitor** works in parallel, constantly checking the output to prevent excessive current or voltage.
*   **Feedback Loop:** A key feature is the integrated **Impedance Sensor**, which continuously measures the electrical resistance of the patient's tissue through the electrodes. This measurement is vital for both safety and effectiveness. If the sensor detects that an electrode has poor contact or has fallen off (indicated by a sudden, large change in impedance), the system will automatically enter a fault state and stop the therapy to ensure patient safety. This feedback also allows the current regulator to adjust its output to maintain a consistent therapeutic effect despite natural variations in skin resistance.

In a typical therapy session, a user sets the desired parameters, applies the electrodes, and starts the treatment. The device then performs a self-test, checks for good electrode contact, and begins delivering the regulated microcurrent pulses for the specified duration, all while continuously monitoring for any unsafe conditions.
