This model describes an electroosmotic pumping subsystem, a type of microfluidic device designed for the precise, non-mechanical transport of fluids. It is commonly used in lab-on-a-chip systems, medical diagnostic devices, or scientific instruments where small volumes of specific solutions must be handled with high accuracy.

The system's core component is the electroosmotic pump. Unlike a traditional mechanical pump, it has no moving parts. Instead, it moves a specific fluid by applying an electric field. The pump contains a very narrow microchannel through which the fluid travels. At the inlet and outlet of this channel are two platinum electrodes (an anode and a cathode).

The overall system is set up for a complete fluidic circuit. A source reservoir holds the fluid, which has defined chemical properties like pH and ionic strength. This fluid is fed into the electroosmotic pump. A dedicated power supply provides a controlled voltage to the pump's electrodes. This voltage creates an electric field that propels the fluid through the microchannel. After passing through the pump, the fluid is delivered to a collection chamber. The system's performance is monitored by dedicated flow and pressure sensors.

Operationally, the pump goes through several states. It begins in an idle state, proceeds to a priming phase to ensure the microchannel is full, and then enters its main operating state. While operating, the flow rate can be precisely controlled by adjusting the voltage from the power supply, enabling normal, high-flow, or low-flow modes. The system is designed to be reliable, capable of operating continuously for long periods and handling a certain amount of back pressure, and includes a fault state to shut down safely if an error occurs.
