This model describes an automated medical infusion pump system designed to deliver therapeutic substances, such as liquid medications like insulin, to a patient at a precisely controlled rate. The system is built to ensure the safe, accurate, and monitored delivery of a prescribed therapy.

The core of the system consists of several interconnected components. A `SubstanceReservoir`, like a medication cartridge or bag, holds the therapeutic substance. An `InfusionPump` provides the mechanical action to draw the substance from this reservoir and deliver it to the patient. The pump's operation is managed by a `FlowController`, which acts as the system's brain, constantly adjusting the pump's speed to maintain the exact flow rate required for the therapy.

To ensure patient safety, a dedicated `SafetyMonitor` continuously observes the system's performance. It checks if the flow rate is within pre-defined safe limits and monitors the volume of medication remaining in the reservoir. If the flow rate becomes too high or low, or if the reservoir is nearly empty, the monitor will trigger an alarm and halt the delivery. The entire system connects to the patient via a `PatientInterface`, which would typically be an infusion set with tubing and a cannula.

The device operates through a clear sequence of states. It starts in `standby`, then moves to `loading` when a new reservoir is installed, followed by `priming` to clear air from the delivery path. Once ready, it enters the `infusing` state to actively deliver the medication. During infusion, the therapy can be paused and resumed. Upon successful delivery, it enters a `completed` state. If a safety issue is detected, the system transitions to an `alarm` state, stopping the infusion and alerting the user.
