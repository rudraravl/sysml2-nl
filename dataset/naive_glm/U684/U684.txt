This model describes a digital force gauge system, a device designed to accurately measure and display applied mechanical forces, such as pushing or pulling.

The system is composed of several integrated electronic components. At its core is a strain gauge sensor, which is the primary element that detects the physical force and converts it into a very small electrical voltage. Because this initial signal is weak, it is immediately sent to a signal amplifier to boost its strength.

Once amplified, the analog voltage signal is passed to an analog-to-digital converter (ADC). This component samples the signal at a high rate and converts it into a digital format that the system's "brain," a microprocessor unit, can understand.

The microprocessor is responsible for all the data processing and system management. It takes the raw digital value from the ADC, applies a unique calibration factor and a zero-offset adjustment, and calculates the final force reading in Newtons. This processor also manages the device's different operational states, such as being idle, actively measuring, or undergoing calibration.

The final, calculated force measurement is sent to a display unit, which presents the reading clearly to the user with a refresh rate fast enough for real-time monitoring. The entire device is powered by a dedicated power supply. A key feature of this system is its ability to be calibrated; a user can initiate a calibration sequence, apply a known reference force, and the microprocessor will automatically adjust its internal factors to maintain high accuracy. The gauge is specified to measure forces up to 5000 Newtons with an accuracy of at least 1%.
