This model describes a complete steam-powered pulsometer pumping system. It is designed to move water from a source to a discharge tank using the direct pressure of steam, notable for its lack of complex moving parts like pistons or impellers.

The core of the system is the pulsometer pump, which features a distinctive dual-chamber design that allows for a nearly continuous, albeit pulsating, flow of water. Each chamber works through a cyclical process. First, steam is admitted into an empty chamber, where it condenses on the cool chamber walls. This condensation creates a vacuum, which sucks water up from the source through a one-way suction valve, filling the chamber.

Once the chamber is full of water, a steam control valve opens again, admitting high-pressure steam that pushes directly on the surface of the water. This pressure forces the water out of the chamber through a one-way discharge valve and into the discharge pipe. As the water level drops, the cycle repeats. The two chambers operate in a coordinated, alternating sequence: while one chamber is using its vacuum to fill with water, the other is using steam pressure to discharge its contents. This alternating action is crucial for maintaining a steady pumping operation.

The entire system is comprised of several key components working together. A steam boiler generates the necessary high-pressure steam to power the pump. The pump itself draws water from a source, such as a well or sump, and delivers it to a higher elevation or a storage tank. One-way check valves at the suction and discharge ports of each chamber are critical for controlling the direction of water flow. Finally, a condensate receiver collects the condensed steam after it has been used in the pumping cycle. The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, including maintaining a minimum flow rate and operating within a defined range of steam pressures.
