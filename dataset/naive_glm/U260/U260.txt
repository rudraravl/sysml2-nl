This model represents a modern, electronic injector pen system designed for the self-administration of liquid medication, such as insulin. It's a reusable device that uses replaceable medication cartridges and disposable needles.

The system is comprised of several key physical components working together. A **medication cartridge** serves as the reservoir for the drug and is installed within the pen's main body. A disposable **needle assembly** is attached to the tip of the pen before each use. This assembly includes the needle itself and a protective cap.

To operate the pen, a user interacts with a **dose selection mechanism**, typically a dial, to set the precise amount of medication to be injected. An electronic **display** provides clear feedback to the user, showing the selected dose, the remaining battery level, and any system errors via status indicators.

Internally, an electromechanical **plunger drive mechanism** is the core of the injection system. Once the user activates the injection, this drive applies a controlled force to a plunger, pushing the medication out of the cartridge, through the needle, and into the user. The system monitors the pressure and force applied to ensure a consistent and complete dose is delivered.

A critical aspect of the design is the integrated **safety lock mechanism**. This system prevents accidental injections by blocking the activation button when the pen is not ready for use, such as when the cap is still on. After the injection is complete, it also manages the deployment of a needle shield to prevent accidental needle-stick injuries before the needle is disposed of.

The entire process follows a specific operational sequence: attaching a new needle, selecting the dose, priming the pen to clear air bubbles, performing the injection, and finally withdrawing the pen, which activates the safety shield for safe needle disposal.
