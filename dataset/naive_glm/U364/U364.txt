This model represents a Puritan Bennett mechanical ventilator system, a life-support device designed to provide respiratory support to patients who are unable to breathe sufficiently on their own. The system delivers precisely controlled mechanical breaths by managing the flow, pressure, and oxygen concentration of medical gases.

The core of the system is a sophisticated breath delivery unit. It begins with an internal **Air Compressor** and a connection for an external **Oxygen Source**. These two gases are fed into a **Gas Mixer**, which blends them to achieve the exact oxygen concentration prescribed by a clinician. The delivery of this blended gas is managed by a pair of critical valves: an **Inspiratory Valve** opens to deliver a breath to the patient, and an **Expiratory Valve** opens to allow the patient to exhale, while also maintaining a constant positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) to help keep the lungs open.

A central **Breath Controller** acts as the brain of the ventilator. It orchestrates the entire respiratory cycle based on settings configured through a **User Interface**. This controller determines the timing of inspiration and expiration, the volume of air delivered, and the target pressure. It can also detect a patient's own attempt to breathe and synchronize the machine's support with their effort.

To ensure safe and accurate operation, the system is equipped with an extensive sensor suite. **Pressure and Flow Transducers** continuously measure the gas being delivered to and returned from the patient. An **Oxygen Sensor** verifies the concentration of the mixed gas, and a **Volume Monitor** calculates the total volume of air delivered in each breath by integrating the flow data over time.

This real-time data is used for two primary purposes. First, it provides closed-loop feedback to the Breath Controller, allowing it to make micro-adjustments during each breath to meet the set targets. Second, the data is constantly checked by an **Alarm Module**. This module monitors for dangerous conditions such as excessively high pressure, low delivered volume, or a lack of breathing (apnea), triggering immediate audiovisual alarms to alert medical staff. The entire system is connected to the patient via a **Patient Circuit**, which is the set of tubes that carries the gas from the ventilator to the patient and back.
