This model represents a Doosan collaborative robot system, an advanced automation solution designed for manufacturing environments where humans and robots work in close proximity. The system is engineered to perform precise and repetitive tasks like picking up components, assembling them, and conducting quality inspections.

The core of the system is a highly articulated **six-axis robotic arm**. This arm has six rotating joints—from the base and shoulder to a three-part wrist—giving it exceptional flexibility to reach and orient itself in complex ways. It is both strong, capable of handling payloads up to 15 kg, and remarkably precise, with a positioning repeatability of just 0.05 millimeters. Each joint is powered by a servo motor and equipped with sophisticated sensors that measure its exact angle and the torque being applied.

Attached to the end of the arm is an **adaptive gripper**, which acts as the robot's hand. It uses linear actuators to open and close its fingers, allowing it to securely grasp a variety of objects. Integrated force sensors enable it to apply a specific, controlled amount of pressure, ensuring it can handle delicate components without crushing them.

The robot's operation is guided by a **vision system** that serves as its eyes. Using a high-resolution depth camera, this system can scan a workspace, identify different workpieces, and determine their precise 3D location and orientation. This allows the robot to pick up randomly placed objects and verify that parts have been placed correctly.

A central **main controller** acts as the brain of the system. It processes data from the vision system to plan the most efficient path for the arm to follow. It sends commands to the arm and gripper and constantly monitors the robot's state, including the position and velocity of all its joints.

Safety is a critical aspect of this collaborative system. A dedicated **safety controller** works alongside the main controller to ensure safe operation around people. It continuously monitors the torque sensors in the arm's joints; if an unexpected force is detected—such as a collision with a person or object—it can trigger an immediate emergency stop. The safety system also includes a laser scanner to detect obstacles in the robot's vicinity and a physical emergency stop button. The entire system is powered by a central power supply unit that provides the necessary electrical energy to all components.

In a typical operation, the vision system first identifies a workpiece. The main controller then computes a safe trajectory for the arm to approach and grasp the object with the gripper. While moving, the safety system monitors for any potential collisions. After picking up the part, the robot transports it to a designated location, places it with high precision, and the vision system verifies the final placement before the arm returns to its home position to begin the next cycle.
