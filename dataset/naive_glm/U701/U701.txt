This model describes a Doppler Ground Speed Radar System, a sophisticated sensor package designed to measure the true speed of a vehicle (like an aircraft or a ground vehicle) relative to the surface below it. Its primary function is to provide highly accurate velocity data, which is independent of factors like wheel slip, wind speed, or GPS signal availability.

The system is composed of several key electronic components that work in concert. A central **Power Supply Unit** takes the main vehicle power and converts it to the specific, stable voltages needed by the radar's sensitive electronics.

The measurement process begins with the **RF Transmitter**, which generates a very specific, high-frequency microwave signal. This signal is then sent to the **Antenna System**, which focuses it into a narrow beam and directs it towards the ground at a precise, fixed angle.

As the vehicle moves, this radar beam reflects off the ground. The reflected signal, which is now very weak, is captured by the same antenna and passed to the **RF Receiver**. The receiver's job is to amplify and filter this faint echo to isolate the relevant signal information.

The heart of the system is the **Doppler Processor**. It analyzes the received signal and compares its frequency to the frequency of the original signal sent by the transmitter. Because of the vehicle's motion, the frequency of the reflected signal will be slightly different—this is the Doppler effect. The processor precisely measures this frequency shift and, by combining it with the known angle of the antenna beam, calculates the vehicle's true speed over the ground. This final velocity measurement is then made available as a data output for other vehicle systems, such as navigation or flight control.

The radar operates through a well-defined set of states. When powered on, it first enters an **initialization** state to run self-tests and calibrations. Once complete, it moves to its main **operational** state, where it continuously repeats the measurement cycle of transmitting, receiving, and computing. The system can be placed in a low-power **standby** mode and can quickly resume operation when needed. For safety and reliability, it also features a **fault** state; if any internal problems are detected, it will automatically shut down its transmitter and report the error, awaiting a reset command.
