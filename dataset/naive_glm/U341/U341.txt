This model represents a medical ostomy appliance system, a device designed to hygienically collect bodily waste from a surgically created opening in the abdomen, known as a stoma. The system's primary goal is to safely contain waste, protect the surrounding skin, control odor, and provide the user with comfort and discretion.

The system consists of two main components that work together: a skin barrier and a collection pouch.

The **Skin Barrier** is an adhesive patch, typically made of a skin-friendly hydrocolloid material, that sticks securely to the body around the stoma. Its crucial functions are to create a leak-proof seal and to protect the sensitive skin from contact with waste. It is designed to be water-resistant and to maintain its adhesion for a specific duration, typically a few days.

The **Collection Pouch** is the bag that attaches to the skin barrier to contain the bodily waste (effluent) and gas (flatus) that passes through the stoma. It is made from a special odor-barrier film and is often opaque for privacy. Many pouches are drainable, allowing them to be emptied without removing the entire system. A key feature integrated into the pouch is an **Odor Filter**, commonly made of activated carbon, which allows gas to vent from the pouch while neutralizing any associated smells.

In operation, the system functions continuously. As the stoma expels waste and gas, the skin barrier directs it into the collection pouch. The pouch fills over time, and the user must monitor its level. The system has a clear operational lifecycle: after being applied, it actively collects waste. When the pouch becomes full, it is emptied. When the skin barrier's adhesive begins to degrade after its intended wear time, the entire appliance is removed and replaced with a new one.
