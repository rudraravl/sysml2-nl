This model represents the Berlin urban transit system, encompassing the city's U-Bahn and S-Bahn rail network. It's designed to provide efficient, reliable public transportation for a large volume of passengers traveling between numerous stations across the city.

The system is composed of several key, interconnected components:

*   **Transit Vehicles:** These are the trains that transport passengers. Each train has a specific passenger capacity, a unique identifier, and is constantly tracked for its position and speed within the network.
*   **Stations:** These are the physical locations where passengers enter and exit the system. Each station has a unique ID, a specific location, and a number of platforms to service the trains.
*   **Control Center:** This is the central operational hub that manages and coordinates the entire network. It assigns routes to specific trains and communicates with them via a signaling system to send commands and receive position reports.
*   **Power Grid:** This provides the necessary electrical power to the transit vehicles, enabling their movement along the tracks.
*   **Ticketing System:** This manages all aspects of fare collection. It interfaces with passengers at stations, issues tickets that are valid for certain times and zones, and tracks the system's daily revenue.

In operation, the Control Center orchestrates the system by dispatching vehicles, assigning them to predefined routes, and coordinating schedules. A train's typical operational cycle involves moving between stations according to its route plan. Upon arriving at a station, it allows passengers to get off (alight) and new passengers to board before departing for the next stop. This entire process is managed to ensure safety and on-time performance across a network that includes over 170 stations and serves approximately 1.5 million passengers daily.
