This model describes a high-precision electromechanical actuator system built around a piezoelectric film. Its primary purpose is to convert electrical voltage into very small and precisely controlled mechanical movements, or displacements. This type of system is used in advanced applications like adaptive optics, micro-positioning systems, haptic feedback devices, and active vibration control.

The core of the system is a layered assembly consisting of an active piezoelectric film sandwiched between two conductive electrode layers, all mounted on a rigid substrate for structural support. When a voltage is applied to the electrodes, it creates an electric field across the film, causing the piezoelectric material to deform and produce a mechanical movement.

The entire system operates as a closed-loop control system to ensure high accuracy. A control processor acts as the brain, determining the target position. It commands a specialized voltage driver to apply a specific voltage signal to the film's electrodes, initiating the movement. A high-resolution displacement sensor, likely optical or capacitive, constantly measures the film's actual movement and sends this data back to the control processor. The processor then compares the measured position to the target, continuously adjusting the voltage signal until the desired displacement is achieved and maintained with high precision.

The system's operation follows a clear lifecycle: when a command is given, the film transitions from an inactive state to a charging state as the electric field builds. It then enters an actuating state where it actively deforms until it reaches its target, at which point it becomes stabilized, holding the position. When the voltage is removed, the film relaxes and returns to its neutral, inactive state. The system is also designed with safety features for handling high voltages, such as an emergency shutdown capability.
