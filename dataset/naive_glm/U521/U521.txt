This is a model of a precision fluid pumping subsystem designed to transfer a liquid from a source to a destination at a controlled rate.

The core of the system is an impedance pump, which functions without a traditional rotating motor. Instead, it uses a piezoelectric actuator that rapidly deforms or vibrates when a specific voltage and frequency are applied. This actuator is connected to a flexible membrane, causing it to oscillate. This oscillation expands and contracts a small pump chamber. In coordination with one-way inlet and outlet check valves, this action draws fluid into the chamber from a source reservoir and then pushes it out toward a destination receiver.

The complete subsystem operates as a closed-loop control system. It includes the source reservoir holding the fluid, the impedance pump, and a destination receiver. To ensure precise operation, a pressure sensor and a flow sensor are installed at the pump's outlet, continuously monitoring its performance.

A central controller manages the entire process. It receives a target flow rate as its goal and compares this to the real-time data from the sensors. If the actual flow rate deviates from the target, the controller automatically adjusts the voltage and frequency supplied to the pump's piezoelectric actuator, thereby changing the pumping speed and force to correct the flow.

The system has several operational states. It starts in an idle state and moves to a priming state to fill the pump before entering its main running mode. During operation, it can shift between a normal state and an adjusting state if it needs to correct the flow. The system also has defined procedures for a normal shutdown, an emergency stop, and a reset to return to an idle state.
