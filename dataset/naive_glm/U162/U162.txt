This is an advanced electronic surveillance system named BELTRI, which stands for Bistatic Electronic Intelligence Tracking and Reconnaissance Instrument. Its purpose is to passively detect, track, and identify targets by analyzing the electromagnetic signals they emit or reflect.

The system is composed of several key hardware components that work in concert. It uses an array of four antennas to capture radio frequency signals from the environment. These signals are then fed into a highly sensitive receiver module, which prepares them for analysis. A powerful signal processing unit then examines these signals to extract detailed information about a potential target, including its position, velocity, signal strength, and a classification of its type.

Once the signal processor has identified and characterized a target, the information is sent to a transmitter module. This module then communicates the intelligence data at high speed to remote operators or a command center for further action. The entire system is powered by an internal power supply unit, and a central control processor manages the overall operation and workflow.

The BELTRI system operates in several distinct modes. It can be in an 'idle' standby state, an active 'scanning' mode where it sweeps the environment for signals of interest, or a 'tracking' mode where it focuses on a specific target that has been detected. The system transitions between these states based on commands it receives or its own detection events, allowing for autonomous operation in the field.
