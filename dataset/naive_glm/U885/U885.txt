This system is a "soft sensor" designed to estimate the concentration of a product within an industrial process, such as a chemical reactor. Instead of measuring the concentration directly, which can be difficult or expensive, it calculates an estimate based on other, more easily measured physical properties: temperature, pressure, and flow rate.

The system is composed of several key modules that work in sequence. First, a Data Acquisition Module uses dedicated physical sensors to collect raw, real-time data on temperature, pressure, and flow. This raw data is then passed to a Signal Processor, which cleans and filters it to remove noise and prepare it for analysis.

The core of the system is a Mathematical Model, which takes the processed physical data and applies a pre-calibrated formula (in this case, a multiple linear regression model) to compute the product's estimated concentration. This model relies on a set of specific calibration coefficients that must be periodically updated to maintain accuracy.

Finally, a Validation Module reviews the calculated estimate. It checks the result against certain thresholds and can detect outliers, ensuring the final output is reliable. The system then provides its final output, which includes the estimated concentration value, a confidence score, and a quality metric.

The entire process operates in a continuous cycle, moving through distinct states such as acquiring data, processing, estimating, and validating. It is designed to be highly accurate (within 3% of the actual value), fast (producing an estimate within 200 milliseconds of receiving new data), and extremely reliable for continuous operation.
