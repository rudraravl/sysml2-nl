This is a comprehensive, self-contained Air Pollution Sensing Unit designed for outdoor environmental monitoring. The entire system is housed in a durable, weatherproof, UV-resistant plastic enclosure, intended to be mounted on a pole with a stainless steel bracket.

The primary function of this unit is to measure key air quality indicators. It works by drawing ambient air through a replaceable HEPA filter, which ensures that a clean sample reaches the internal sensors. Inside, a sophisticated sensor array measures a wide range of pollutants. This includes laser-based sensors for fine and coarse particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), as well as specialized electrochemical sensors for harmful gases like Carbon Monoxide (CO), Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2), and Ozone (O3). A metal-oxide sensor is also used to detect Sulfur Dioxide (SO2). In addition to pollutants, the unit records essential environmental conditions like temperature, humidity, and atmospheric pressure.

The device operates through a managed, cyclical process. When triggered, its sensors first warm up. Then, it begins a measurement cycle by acquiring raw data from the air sample. This data is immediately processed by an internal signal processor that filters out electronic noise. Next, the cleaned data is adjusted using stored calibration factors to ensure its accuracy. Using these corrected pollutant levels, the system calculates an overall Air Quality Index (AQI). Before sending the data out, the entire measurement is validated to ensure its integrity.

The final, validated measurement package, which includes the AQI, detailed pollutant concentrations, and a timestamp, is then transmitted wirelessly. It uses the LoRaWAN protocol, which is a low-power, long-range communication method ideal for deploying networks of these sensors across a city or region. The entire system is powered by a dedicated power management unit that regulates the incoming voltage for all internal components. The unit's operational states—such as idle, warming up, measuring, and handling errors—are carefully managed to ensure reliable and consistent operation.
