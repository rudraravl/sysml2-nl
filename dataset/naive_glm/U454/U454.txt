This model describes a winged infusion set system, commonly known as a "butterfly needle," used in medical settings for administering intravenous (IV) fluids or medications and for drawing blood samples from a patient.

The core of the system is the single-use, sterile winged infusion set. It is a composite device featuring a thin, beveled needle designed for smooth entry into a patient's peripheral vein. Attached to the base of this needle are two flexible plastic "wings." These wings serve a dual purpose: they provide an ergonomic grip for the clinician to precisely control the needle during insertion, and they lie flat against the skin afterward to be taped down, stabilizing the needle for the duration of the infusion.

A short, flexible tube made of medical-grade plastic is connected to the needle. This tubing provides the pathway for fluid and includes several key components. A safety clamp is present to quickly and completely stop the flow, while a separate flow regulator, such as a roller clamp, allows for the fine-tuned adjustment of the fluid's delivery rate. The tubing ends with a standardized luer lock connector, which ensures a secure, leak-proof, and sterile connection to a main IV line from a fluid reservoir (like an IV bag of saline).

The entire system operates as an intermediary between a fluid source and a patient. The typical procedure involves preparing the patient's skin, inserting the needle into a vein, securing the device with its wings, connecting it to the fluid reservoir, and then setting the desired flow rate. The system can be used to deliver a specific volume of fluid over a period, can be paused, and is designed to be safely removed once the treatment is complete or if a blood sample has been collected. The lifecycle of the device is carefully managed, from its initial sterile packaging through insertion, active infusion, and eventual removal and disposal.
