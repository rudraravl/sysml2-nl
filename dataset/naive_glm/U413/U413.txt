This model describes a Thorpe tube flowmeter, a common medical device used to measure and control the flow rate of gases, such as oxygen, delivered to a patient.

The core of the system is a vertically-mounted, transparent tapered glass tube that is wider at the top than at the bottom. Inside this tube is a small float, typically a ball, that is free to move up and down. The entire tube assembly is encased in a protective housing to ensure its structural integrity and safety under pressure from the gas supply.

The flowmeter's operation is based on a simple physical principle. Medical gas enters through an inlet port at the bottom of the tube, flows upward around the float, and exits through an outlet port at the top. The moving gas exerts an upward drag force on the float, while gravity pulls the float downward. The float rises until it reaches a point of equilibrium where these two forces are perfectly balanced. Because the tube is tapered, a higher flow rate is required to create enough pressure to lift the float to a wider section of the tube. Consequently, the height at which the float stabilizes is directly proportional to the gas flow rate.

To make this measurement useful, a calibrated scale with graduated markings is placed alongside the glass tube. A healthcare provider can directly read the volumetric flow rate (e.g., in liters per minute) by observing the position of the float against this scale. The system also includes an integrated flow control valve, which allows the operator to precisely adjust the gas flow to the desired rate.

The device operates through distinct states. It begins in a "no flow" state where the float rests at the bottom. When gas flow is started, the float rises and may oscillate slightly before settling into a "steady state" at its equilibrium height. If the flow is adjusted, the float moves to a new stable position. If the gas flow exceeds the instrument's maximum measurement capability, the float will be pushed to the very top of the tube, indicating an "overrange" condition. For accurate operation, the entire device must be mounted and used in an upright, vertical orientation.
