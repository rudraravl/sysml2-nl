This model describes a medical treatment system for managing dry eye disease. The central component is a punctal plug, a tiny, biocompatible device designed to be inserted into the tear duct to conserve tears on the surface of the eye.

The system is comprised of the physical plug itself and its interaction with the eye's anatomy. The plug has specific physical dimensions (diameter and length) and is made from materials like silicone, collagen, or hydrogel. Its purpose is to physically block the punctum, which is the small opening in the corner of the eyelid that drains tears into the lacrimal drainage system. By doing this, it reduces the rate at which tears leave the eye, a function measured by its "occlusion efficiency."

The treatment process involves several stages. The plug starts in an "uninserted" state. A clinician then performs an insertion procedure, placing the plug into the patient's punctum (typically the lower one). A critical part of this process is ensuring the plug is correctly sized for the patient's anatomy to achieve a stable, "seated" position. If the plug is not properly fitted, it may become "displaced."

Once seated, the plug actively occludes the tear duct, increasing the volume of the tear film on the ocular surface. The goal is to raise the tear volume to a target therapeutic level, thereby improving lubrication, protecting the eye's surface, and alleviating the symptoms of dryness. The system's effectiveness is continuously monitored by assessing whether adequate tear retention is achieved. If the plug becomes displaced or is no longer effective, it may need to be removed or replaced.
