This model describes a complete hydraulic rudder system, likely for a large marine vessel like a ship or submarine, designed for precise directional control and steering.

The core of the system is the **rudder blade**, a large control surface that is submerged in the water. By changing its angle relative to the flow of water, it generates a powerful lateral force, which creates a turning moment (or yaw) to steer the vehicle.

The rudder blade is moved by a powerful **hydraulic actuator**. This component acts as the muscle of the system, converting high-pressure hydraulic fluid into the mechanical force needed to position and hold the massive rudder against the immense pressure of the water, especially at speed. The actuator receives its power from a dedicated **hydraulic power unit**, which includes a fluid reservoir and pumps to maintain the required operating pressure.

Control of the system originates from a **steering mechanism**, which could be a ship's helm or an input from an autopilot system. This sends a command for a desired rudder angle to the hydraulic actuator. To ensure accuracy, an **angle sensor** is mounted on the rudder assembly. This sensor constantly measures the rudder's actual angle and provides this information as feedback to the control system, allowing the actuator to make precise adjustments until the commanded angle is achieved and maintained.

The system's operation is managed through several states. It can be in a **neutral** state with the rudder centered, actively **deflecting** to a new commanded position, or **holding** a steady angle to maintain a turn. For safety, it also includes an **emergency** mode that is activated in the event of a system failure. The system is designed to meet strict performance requirements for its steering force, positioning accuracy (within half a degree of the command), and response time.
