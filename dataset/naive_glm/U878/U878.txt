This is a comprehensive shock detection system designed to continuously monitor physical impacts and sudden accelerations. Its primary purpose is to sense these events, classify their severity, log them for future analysis, and provide immediate, multi-level alerts to protect equipment or ensure safety.

The system is composed of several key components that work together in a sequence. It starts with an **Acceleration Sensor** that constantly measures physical forces. This raw sensor data is then passed to a **Signal Conditioner**, which filters out electrical noise and interference to produce a clean, reliable acceleration reading.

This conditioned signal is then fed into the core of the system, the **Shock Detection Processor**. This processor analyzes the signal in real-time, comparing the force of any impacts against a set of pre-configured thresholds. When an impact exceeds a threshold, the processor identifies it as a shock event, determines its duration, and classifies its severity into one of five levels: none, minor, moderate, severe, or critical.

Once a shock event is confirmed and classified, the processor sends out an alert. This alert is routed to two independent components simultaneously: an **Event Logger** and an **Alert Notification System**. The Event Logger records the full details of every event—including the time, severity, and peak acceleration—into a historical log capable of storing a large number of records for later review and analysis.

At the same time, the Alert Notification System provides immediate feedback. This system has a tiered response based on the event's severity. A minor shock might only activate a visual light indicator. A moderate shock would also trigger an audible alarm. For severe and critical events, the system activates both visual and audible alarms while also sending a remote notification to a control center or operator's device, ensuring that the most serious impacts receive immediate attention.
