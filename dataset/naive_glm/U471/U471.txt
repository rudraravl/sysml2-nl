This model represents a complete hydraulic power system, designed to generate and control high-pressure fluid to power an external actuator.

The core of the system is a variable-displacement axial piston pump. This pump is mechanically driven by a motor via a drive shaft. Internally, the pump contains a rotating cylinder block with nine pistons. As the block spins, the pistons ride on an angled swash plate, causing them to move in and out of their cylinders. This reciprocating motion draws in low-pressure hydraulic fluid and expels it at high pressure. The angle of the swash plate can be adjusted, which changes the piston stroke length and therefore controls the volume of fluid pumped per revolution, allowing for a variable flow rate.

The entire system operates as a circuit. It begins with a reservoir that stores the hydraulic fluid. From the reservoir, fluid is drawn through a suction filter to remove contaminants before entering the pump. The motor spins the pump, which pressurizes the fluid.

This high-pressure fluid then flows out of the pump and through a pressure filter for further cleaning. From there, the fluid is directed to a hydraulic actuator, which uses the fluid power to perform mechanical work. For safety, a pressure relief valve is installed in the high-pressure line; if the system pressure exceeds a safe limit, this valve opens to divert excess flow and prevent damage. The hydraulic fluid eventually circulates through a cooler to manage its temperature before returning to the reservoir to complete the loop.

The system also includes a sophisticated control and monitoring function. It manages the pump's operation, including controlled start-up and shutdown sequences, and features an emergency stop capability. It actively monitors system pressure and fluid temperature to ensure they remain within safe operating limits, and can assess the pump's overall performance, flagging its status as normal, degraded, or critical.
