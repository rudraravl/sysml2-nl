This model describes a sophisticated, multi-sensor surveillance and situational awareness system called the Omniview System. Its primary purpose is to provide a complete, real-time, 360-degree view of an environment, automatically detect and track objects within that view, and archive the data for later analysis.

The physical system is built on a platform equipped with eight high-resolution optical sensors. These sensors are strategically placed to cover all angles—front, back, left, right, top, bottom, and the diagonal corners—ensuring there are no blind spots. Each sensor captures high-quality video frames (at 4K resolution and 30 frames per second) and sends this raw image data, along with a timestamp and its unique ID, to a central processor.

The core of the system's intelligence lies in a sequence of processing units. First, an **Image Stitching Processor** receives the feeds from all eight sensors simultaneously. It uses advanced algorithms to seamlessly combine these individual video streams into a single, high-resolution panoramic image. This process is designed to be very fast, with a total delay of less than half a second, ensuring the panoramic view is presented in near real-time.

Once the complete panoramic image is created, it is immediately passed to an **Object Detection Unit**. This unit, powered by a deep neural network, intelligently scans the scene to identify, classify, and track objects of interest. For each object it finds, it records its precise 3D position, velocity, and object type (e.g., "vehicle," "person"), along with a confidence level for the detection and a unique tracking ID. The system is capable of tracking up to 100 objects simultaneously with high accuracy.

The entire process is managed by a central **System Controller**, which handles operator commands, manages the system's operating modes, and reports on its status and health. The system operates through a series of states, including Standby, Initializing, Calibrating, and Active Surveillance. All the generated data—both the panoramic imagery and the lists of detected objects—is stored in a high-capacity **Data Archive**, which retains the information for at least 30 days. The entire system is designed to run continuously, powered by an external source.
