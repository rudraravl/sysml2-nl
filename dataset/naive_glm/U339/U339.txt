This describes a clinical system used to measure testicular volume and assess a patient's stage of pubertal development using a medical instrument called an orchidometer. The system is not just the physical tool but encompasses the entire procedure, including the clinician, the patient, and the method for recording the results.

The core of the system is the physical orchidometer instrument itself, which is a chain of twelve standardized, ellipsoidal beads. Each bead corresponds to a specific volume, ranging from 1 to 25 milliliters, and serves as a physical reference for comparison during a clinical examination.

The assessment process is performed by a clinician on a patient. It begins with the clinician physically examining (palpating) one of the patient's testicles to get an initial estimate of its size. The clinician then selects the bead from the orchidometer chain that most closely matches the estimated size. Through a process of tactile and visual comparison between the testicle and the reference bead, a final volume is determined. This procedure is then repeated for the other testicle.

Once both volumes are measured, the larger of the two measurements is used to classify the patient's development into one of five pubertal stages, known as Prader stages.

Finally, a complete measurement record is created, capturing the patient's identification, the measured volumes for both the left and right testicles, and the determined Prader stage. This record is then stored in a recording system, such as an electronic health record, to track the patient's developmental progress over time. The entire system follows a defined workflow, from preparing for the examination to measuring, classifying the stage, and documenting the results.
