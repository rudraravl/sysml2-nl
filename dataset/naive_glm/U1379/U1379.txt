This model describes a total artificial heart system, a life-sustaining medical device designed to completely replace the function of a patient's failing biological heart. Its purpose is to maintain continuous blood circulation, pumping deoxygenated blood to the lungs and then distributing newly oxygenated blood throughout the body.

The system is built around a central unit that mimics the four-chambered structure of a natural heart. It includes two primary pump chambers: a "right ventricle" that handles deoxygenated blood from the body and sends it to the lungs, and a "left ventricle" that pumps oxygenated blood from the lungs out to the rest of the body. The flow of blood is precisely managed by four mechanical valves (mitral, aortic, tricuspid, and pulmonary) that open and close in sequence to ensure blood moves in the correct direction and to prevent backflow.

A powerful electric motor provides the mechanical force needed to drive the pump chambers, contracting and relaxing them to simulate a heartbeat. The entire system is powered by an internal, rechargeable battery designed to provide several hours of continuous operation.

The artificial heart's operation is governed by a sophisticated electronic controller, which acts as the system's brain. This controller receives constant feedback from a suite of integrated sensors that monitor critical vitals like blood pressure and flow rate. Based on this data and pre-set targets, the controller adjusts the motor's speed and force to regulate the heart rate and blood pressure, allowing the device to respond to the body's changing physiological needs.

The system is designed to operate through a continuous cardiac cycle, alternating between a filling phase (diastole) and an ejection phase (systole). It also monitors its own health, tracking battery levels and performance metrics, and can enter different operational states, such as `normal`, `warning`, or a `critical` emergency mode to alert the patient and medical personnel of any issues.
