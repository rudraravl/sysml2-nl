This model represents an industrial automation system designed to autonomously manage a chemical process reactor. The system's primary function is to ensure safe and efficient production by continuously monitoring and controlling the physical conditions within the reactor.

The core of the system is a **Process Reactor**, a vessel where the chemical reaction takes place. This reactor is designed to operate within specific temperature and pressure limits.

To monitor the process, the system uses a **Pressure Sensor** and a **Temperature Sensor**, which constantly measure the conditions inside the reactor. These real-time measurements are sent to a **Programmable Logic Controller (PLC)**, which acts as the central brain of the operation.

The PLC processes the sensor data, compares it against desired setpoints, and executes control logic to manage the reaction. Based on its calculations, the PLC sends commands to two key actuators: a **Control Valve**, which adjusts the flow of materials into or out of the reactor, and a **Conveyor Motor**, which transports materials for the process.

An operator oversees and interacts with the entire system through a touch-enabled **Human-Machine Interface (HMI)**. This control panel displays real-time process data, status updates, and critical alarms. If conditions exceed safe operating limits, the system automatically generates an alarm on the HMI to alert the operator.

The entire process operates through a series of distinct states. The reactor follows a production cycle that includes filling, heating, reacting, cooling, and draining. Similarly, the PLC has its own operational states, such as startup, running, fault, and maintenance, ensuring the control system itself is managed and monitored throughout its lifecycle.
