This model represents a mechanical piston pumping system designed to transfer a fluid from a low-pressure reservoir to a higher-pressure receiver. The system is a classic example of a positive displacement pump.

The core of the system is the Piston Pump, which is composed of several key mechanical parts. A Crankshaft Drive provides rotational power, which is converted into the back-and-forth (reciprocating) motion of a Piston inside a Cylinder. This motion drives the entire pumping action.

The system's operation is based on a two-stroke cycle:
1.  **Intake Stroke:** The piston pulls back, creating a low-pressure area within the cylinder. This pressure difference draws fluid from a source Reservoir through a one-way Inlet Check Valve and into the cylinder chamber.
2.  **Discharge Stroke:** The crankshaft then pushes the piston forward, compressing the fluid inside the cylinder. This increased pressure closes the inlet valve and forces open a separate one-way Outlet Check Valve, pushing the fluid out of the pump and towards a destination Receiver.

This cycle of intake and discharge repeats continuously to create a steady, albeit pulsating, flow of fluid. The entire assembly is connected, with fluid flowing from the source reservoir, through the pump's inlet valve, into the cylinder, out the outlet valve, and finally to the receiving container.

The pump's operation is managed through a series of states. It starts in an 'idle' state and, upon receiving a start command, moves into a 'priming' phase to ensure the pump is filled with fluid. Once ready, it enters the main 'operating' state, continuously performing the intake and discharge cycles. The transition between these strokes is triggered by the piston reaching the extreme ends of its travel (top and bottom dead center). A stop command initiates a controlled 'shutdown' before the system returns to its idle state.
