This is a waterladder pumping subsystem designed to lift water from a low-level supply reservoir to a higher discharge reservoir. The system is intended for continuous, efficient, and reliable operation.

The core of the system is the **Waterladder Pump**, a mechanical device that uses a series of 12 buckets attached to a continuous lifting chain. This chain is looped around a large rotating drum. As the drum turns, it pulls the chain, causing the buckets to dip into the water at the inlet, fill up, and then be lifted vertically. At the top of their travel, the buckets tilt and discharge the water into an outlet pipe.

The pump is powered by a dedicated **Drive Motor**, which converts electrical power into the mechanical torque and rotational speed needed to turn the pump's drum.

The complete subsystem includes a **Supply Reservoir** where the water is drawn from and a **Discharge Reservoir** located at a higher elevation where the water is deposited. An **inlet control valve** is situated between the supply reservoir and the pump to regulate the incoming water flow. To monitor performance, the system is equipped with instruments including **pressure gauges** at the pump's inlet and outlet, and a **flow meter** to measure the volume of water being moved to the discharge reservoir.

The system's operation is managed through a series of states. It remains in a **stopped** state until it receives a start command. Upon receiving the command, it enters a **starting** sequence to gradually accelerate to its operational speed. During the **running** state, it continuously lifts and discharges water. A stop command initiates a **stopping** sequence to safely decelerate. For safety, the system will enter a **fault** state and shut down if it detects excessive motor torque, which could indicate a blockage or mechanical failure. A manual reset is required to clear the fault and return the system to a stopped state.
