This is a real-time, automated sensor system designed to continuously measure the concentration of chloride ions in a liquid sample. It operates on the principle of fluorescence quenching, where the presence of chloride ions reduces the intensity of light emitted by a special fluorescent dye. The system is built for applications requiring precise and ongoing monitoring, such as water quality analysis, industrial process control, or biomedical research.

The measurement process is a carefully orchestrated sequence of optical and electronic events:

First, a sample fluid is pumped through a small quartz flow cell. Inside the system, a specialized LED generates ultraviolet light at a specific wavelength. This light is passed through a high-precision optical filter to isolate the exact wavelength needed to excite a fluorescent dye contained within a sensing chamber, which is integrated with the flow cell.

When this purified light hits the dye in the presence of the sample, the dye fluoresces, emitting light at a different, longer wavelength (a visible glow). However, any chloride ions present in the sample interfere with this process, "quenching" the fluorescence and making the glow dimmer. The higher the chloride concentration, the less intense the fluorescent light becomes.

This emitted light is then captured and passed through a second optical filter, which blocks the original UV light and allows only the fluorescent glow to reach a sensitive photodetector. The photodetector converts the intensity of this light into a weak electrical signal. This signal is then amplified and digitized by a signal conditioner.

Finally, a central microprocessor receives this digital data. Using a stored calibration table, it precisely calculates the original chloride concentration in the sample based on how much the fluorescence was quenched. The final concentration value is then output for monitoring or logging.

The entire system operates through a defined lifecycle, including a warm-up phase for the electronics to stabilize, a calibration mode for accuracy, the primary measuring state, a data processing step, and a flushing cycle to clean the flow cell between measurements. It is also designed to detect faults and enter an error state if a problem occurs, ensuring reliable operation.
