This model describes an incentive spirometer, a medical device used for pulmonary therapy to help patients improve their lung function. It's designed to encourage users, often recovering from surgery, to take slow, sustained, and deep breaths to prevent respiratory complications.

The physical device consists of a stable base unit that holds a large, transparent measurement chamber. A patient breathes in through a mouthpiece, which is connected to the chamber by a flexible tube. As the patient inhales, the suction draws air through the system, causing a piston or indicator ball inside the main chamber to rise.

The measurement chamber features a volume scale with markings (e.g., in milliliters) that allows the patient to see a real-time measurement of the volume of air they have inhaled. This provides instant visual feedback, encouraging them to try and reach a prescribed target volume, which can be adjusted by a healthcare provider.

In addition to measuring volume, the system includes a flow rate coach. This is a separate indicator, often a small ball in its own chamber, that provides feedback on the speed of the patient's inhalation. The goal is a slow, steady breath, and the coach indicates whether the flow is too low, optimal, or too high.

The typical use of the device involves a patient attempting to raise the main volume piston to their target level while keeping the flow rate indicator within the optimal range. Once the target volume is reached, the patient is instructed to hold their breath for a recommended duration before exhaling. The system then resets, with the piston returning to the bottom, ready for the next breathing exercise.
