This is a model of the Dodrill-GMR mechanical heart-lung machine, a pioneering medical device developed in the 1950s to provide life support during open-heart surgery. Its purpose is to temporarily take over the complete function of a patient's heart and lungs, a process known as cardiopulmonary bypass. This allows surgeons to operate on a still, bloodless heart while ensuring the patient's body continues to receive oxygenated blood.

The system works by creating an external circuit for the patient's blood. The process begins when deoxygenated blood is drained from the patient's major veins through a tube called a Venous Cannula and collected in a Venous Reservoir, which acts as a buffer. From this reservoir, a mechanical Roller Pump, which is the "heart" of the system, actively propels the blood through the rest of the circuit.

The blood is first pushed into a Bubble Oxygenator, the machine's artificial lung. Here, oxygen is bubbled directly through the blood, allowing it to absorb oxygen and release carbon dioxide. Following oxygenation, the blood passes through a Heat Exchanger Unit that precisely controls its temperature, warming or cooling it as required for the surgery. To ensure safety, the blood then flows through a Blood Filter to remove any small particles, debris, or air bubbles.

Finally, the newly oxygenated, temperature-controlled, and filtered blood is returned to the patient's arterial system through an Arterial Cannula to circulate throughout the body. The entire process is continuously supervised using a Monitoring Console, which displays critical parameters like blood flow rate, pressure, temperature, and oxygen saturation, allowing the medical team to manage the patient's condition throughout the procedure.
