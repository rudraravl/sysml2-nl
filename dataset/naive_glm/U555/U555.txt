This is a rotodynamic pumping subsystem designed to transfer a fluid from a supply reservoir to a discharge reservoir. The system is managed by an automated controller that monitors performance and regulates fluid flow.

The core of the system is a rotodynamic pump, which uses a multi-bladed impeller, spinning within a volute casing, to increase the pressure and flow of the fluid. This impeller is driven by a high-speed electric motor, connected via a stainless steel shaft supported by ball bearings. A mechanical seal is in place to prevent fluid from leaking along the rotating shaft.

The entire operation is managed by a dedicated pump controller. This controller monitors the system using pressure sensors at both the pump's inlet and outlet, as well as a flow meter to measure the fluid transfer rate. Based on these readings and a target flow rate, the controller can adjust a control valve to regulate the system's output.

The system's operational lifecycle includes a startup sequence to bring the pump to speed, a continuous operating phase where it actively monitors pressure and regulates flow, and a shutdown procedure. An important safety feature is its ability to automatically shut down if a critical pressure condition is detected.

The system is engineered to meet specific performance criteria, including maintaining a minimum flow rate, generating a significant head pressure, and operating with high overall efficiency. Furthermore, it is designed for high reliability, with a requirement to operate continuously for thousands of hours between maintenance cycles.
