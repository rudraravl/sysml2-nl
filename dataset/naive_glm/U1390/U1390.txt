This is a passive, self-watering capillary irrigation system designed to automatically provide a consistent amount of moisture to a planting bed without any pumps, electronics, or external power.

The system consists of three primary components: a water reservoir, a porous capillary wick, and a planting bed. The water reservoir is a container that holds the main water supply for the plants. The capillary wick, likely a rope or strip of felt-like material, has one end submerged in the reservoir and the other end placed into the soil of the planting bed. The planting bed contains the soil and the plants that require watering.

The system works on the physical principle of capillary action. The porous nature of the wick causes water to be drawn up from the reservoir, against gravity, and transported along its length. When the wick makes contact with the soil in the planting bed, the water is released, moistening the growing medium.

This process is self-regulating. As plants absorb water and moisture evaporates from the soil, the soil becomes drier, which in turn draws more water from the wick. Conversely, when the soil is sufficiently moist, the rate of water transfer from the wick slows down or stops. This creates an equilibrium, maintaining the soil moisture at a stable level ideal for plant growth.

The system operates through several states: it's first initialized by priming the wick, then it actively wicks and delivers water to the soil. It aims to achieve an equilibrium state where the water supply perfectly matches the demand from the plants and evaporation. If the reservoir runs out of water, the system enters a depleted state and ceases to function until it is manually refilled.
