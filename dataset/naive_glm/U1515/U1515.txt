This model represents a Thunderbolt siren, an electromechanical outdoor warning system designed for large-scale emergency alerts and civil defense applications. Its primary function is to generate an extremely loud, omnidirectional warning sound to cover a wide area.

The system is built around a powerful electric motor, which serves as the core of its operation. This motor is fed by a dedicated power supply that converts standard utility power into the high-current electricity needed to run the siren. The entire operation is managed by a central control unit, which processes external commands to activate, test, or deactivate the siren.

When activated, the control unit energizes the motor, which drives two key mechanical components. First, it spins a large rotor assembly, a rotating structure featuring five projecting horns. Second, it simultaneously drives a mechanical chopper. This chopper modulates the airflow to create the siren's characteristic and distinctive warning tone at a specific frequency. This raw, modulated sound is then channeled through and projected by the spinning horns of the rotor assembly, which distributes the sound powerfully in all directions.

The siren's operation follows a clear sequence. From a standby state, it receives an activation command. It then enters a startup phase where the motor spins up the heavy rotor assembly to full operating speed, a process designed to take less than ten seconds. Once at speed, it produces its full-intensity warning sound continuously until a deactivation command is received. At that point, power to the motor is cut, and the assembly coasts down to a stop, ready for the next activation.
