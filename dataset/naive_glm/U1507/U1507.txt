This model describes a complete, stationary steam engine system designed to convert thermal energy into mechanical rotational power. It operates on a closed-loop cycle where water is heated into high-pressure steam, used to drive a piston, and then condensed back into water to be reused.

The core of the system is a **Boiler**, which heats water to generate high-pressure steam. This steam flows through a **Throttle Valve**, which regulates the amount of steam entering the engine's power unit. The engine's speed is managed by a **Governor**, a mechanical device that measures the rotational speed of the output shaft and automatically adjusts the throttle valve to maintain a consistent speed under varying loads.

The regulated, high-pressure steam enters a **Cylinder**, where it expands and pushes a **Piston**. This action converts the steam's thermal energy into a powerful linear force. This linear motion is translated into rotational motion by a **Connecting Rod** linked to a **Crankshaft**. To ensure smooth and continuous rotation, a heavy **Flywheel** is attached to the crankshaft, using its inertia to even out the power pulses from the piston strokes.

After the steam has expanded and pushed the piston, the resulting low-pressure exhaust steam is piped to a **Condenser**. Here, it is cooled and turned back into liquid water. This condensed water is then sent to a **Feed Pump**, which pressurizes it and returns it to the boiler to begin the cycle anew.

The engine's operation is managed through several states: it starts in a **stopped** state, transitions to **starting** and **warming** as the boiler builds pressure, and then enters a stable **running** state. A shutdown command moves it to a **stopping** state before it finally returns to stopped. The entire system is designed to meet specific requirements for power output, thermal efficiency, operational safety (by limiting boiler pressure), and precise speed regulation.
