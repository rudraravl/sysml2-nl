This model describes a dental sinus augmentation and implant system, which is a surgical procedure and the associated medical components used to restore teeth in the upper jaw when there is not enough natural bone to support a traditional dental implant.

The system involves several key components. The patient's anatomy includes the **Maxillary Sinus**, an air-filled cavity above the upper teeth, and the **Residual Bone**, which is the remaining jawbone beneath the sinus floor. The medical devices used are a **Sinus Implant Fixture**, which is a screw-like titanium post that acts as an artificial tooth root, and **Bone Graft** material, which is a substance that promotes new bone growth.

The overall process, known as Sinus Augmentation, aims to increase the amount of bone in the upper jaw. The procedure begins by carefully elevating the delicate sinus membrane to create a space. This new space is then filled with the bone graft material. Following this, the implant fixture is inserted, anchoring into both the patient's residual bone and the newly placed graft.

After the surgical procedure, a critical biological process called osseointegration occurs, where the patient's bone naturally grows into and fuses with the implant's surface over several months. The success of the system depends on the implant being biocompatible, achieving initial stability upon placement, and fully integrating with the bone to provide a strong, long-lasting foundation. The ultimate goal is to restore the patient's masticatory (chewing) function by attaching a dental crown to the stable, integrated implant.
