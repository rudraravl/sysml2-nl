This model describes a Y-Set Fluid Distribution System, an automated piece of industrial equipment designed to precisely divide a single incoming fluid stream into two separate, controlled outlet streams. Its primary purpose is to manage and balance the flow of a fluid (like water, oil, or a chemical) to two different downstream processes, ensuring each receives a specific, proportional amount.

The system is built around a central physical component, a **Y-Set Junction**, which is a simple pipe fitting that splits the incoming flow into two paths. However, the intelligence of the system lies in the components surrounding this junction.

Here's how it works: a fluid enters the system and its initial flow rate, pressure, and temperature are measured by an **inlet flow sensor**. After passing through the Y-junction, each of the two resulting streams flows through its own independent **flow control valve**. These valves are the system's active components; they can be adjusted to restrict or increase the flow through their respective pipes. After each valve, another **flow sensor** measures the conditions of the individual outlet stream.

The entire operation is managed by a central **Flow Distribution Controller**. This controller is given a desired split ratio (for example, 60% to outlet 1 and 40% to outlet 2). It continuously reads the data from the two outlet flow sensors and compares the actual, measured split ratio to the desired target. If it detects a deviation outside of an acceptable tolerance, the controller calculates the necessary adjustments and commands the two flow control valves to open or close slightly. This creates a constant feedback loop, allowing the system to automatically correct for pressure fluctuations and maintain the precise flow distribution required.

The system operates in several states. It can be **idle** (ready but not operating), go through a **startup** sequence, and enter its primary **distributing** state. While distributing, it is either in a **balanced** state, where the flow ratio is correct, or an **adjusting** state, where it is actively correcting an imbalance. Finally, it can execute a safe **shutdown** sequence to stop the flow.
