This model describes a Borescopy Inspection System, a high-tech tool used for visually inspecting internal and hard-to-reach areas, such as the inside of jet engines, industrial pipelines, or complex machinery without taking them apart.

The core of the system is a flexible **Insertion Probe** that contains a miniature camera (imaging sensor) and an LED light source at its tip. This probe is designed to be maneuvered deep inside a structure. The tip is steerable, allowing an operator to bend and rotate it to get a clear view of specific areas of interest.

An operator manages the inspection using an **Operator Interface** and a **Display Console**. Through the controls, the operator can precisely guide the probe's position—controlling its insertion depth, rotation, and the bend angle of its tip—and adjust the intensity of the illumination for the best possible image.

As the probe navigates the inspection area, its camera sends a live video feed back to the main system. This raw video is first sent to a **Processing Unit** that enhances the image quality, for example by adjusting brightness and contrast. The enhanced video is then simultaneously displayed to the operator and fed into an **Analysis Module**. This module is the "smart" part of the system; it uses software to automatically analyze the images in real-time to detect, classify, and measure defects such as cracks, corrosion, or foreign debris.

On the **Display Console**, the operator sees the live video feed with markers and data overlaid on the screen, highlighting the location, type, and severity of any defects the system identifies. Finally, a **Recording System** logs the entire inspection session, saving the video, still images, and all the structured defect data for compliance, documentation, and later review.

The system operates through distinct phases: it powers on and prepares, then the probe is inserted. During the inspection, it enters a cycle of scanning, analyzing images, and recording data. The operator can continuously reposition the probe to inspect different areas until the job is complete, at which point the probe is retracted and the system returns to an idle state.
