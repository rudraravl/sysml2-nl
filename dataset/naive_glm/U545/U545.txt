This model describes a steam-powered pulsometer pumping system. It is designed to move water from a source reservoir to a higher-pressure delivery tank using the direct force of steam, notable for its lack of complex moving parts like pistons or turbines.

The core of the system is the pulsometer pump, which features two identical operating chambers. These chambers work in an alternating cycle to create a continuous, albeit pulsating, flow of water. The entire system is supported by a steam boiler that provides the power, a source reservoir for the water supply, a delivery tank to receive the pumped water, and a condensate tank to collect used steam.

The operation for a single chamber follows a four-stage cycle:
1.  **Steam Admission:** High-pressure steam from the boiler is let into a chamber that is full of water.
2.  **Discharging:** The steam pressure pushes directly on the surface of the water, forcing it out of the chamber, through a one-way discharge check valve, and into the delivery pipe.
3.  **Steam Condensation:** Once the chamber is empty of water, the steam inside condenses (cools back into a small amount of water), creating a strong vacuum.
4.  **Filling:** This vacuum pulls water from the source reservoir through a one-way suction check valve, refilling the chamber and preparing it for the next cycle.

The pump's two chambers operate in opposite phases. While one chamber is using steam pressure to discharge water, the other is in its vacuum phase, drawing in new water. This synchronized, alternating action ensures that the pump is almost always actively moving water, providing a more consistent output. A set of check valves for each chamber ensures that water can only flow in the intended direction—from the source, into the chambers, and out to the delivery tank.
