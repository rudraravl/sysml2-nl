This is a Fluorescence Image-Guided Surgery System, a real-time surgical navigation tool designed to help surgeons more accurately remove tumors. Its primary function is to make cancerous tissue visibly distinct from healthy tissue during an operation, enabling more precise and complete tumor resection while preserving surrounding critical structures.

The system works by using a special fluorescent dye, such as Indocyanine Green (ICG), which is administered to the patient via a Dye Injection System before the procedure. This dye is designed to accumulate in higher concentrations within tumor tissue.

During the surgery, a Near-InfraRed (NIR) Light Source illuminates the surgical area. This light causes the dye to glow, or fluoresce. A specialized Optical Filter Assembly ensures that a high-sensitivity CCD Camera captures only the light emitted by the glowing dye, filtering out the initial excitation light and other ambient light.

The raw image data from the camera is sent to an Image Processing Unit. This computer enhances the image, boosts the contrast, and generates a clear, real-time visual overlay that highlights the tumor's boundaries. This processed image and overlay are then displayed on a Surgical Display in the operating room. This provides the surgeon with a live, color-coded map, allowing them to see exactly where the tumor ends and healthy tissue begins.

The system operates through several states, starting in standby mode, moving through a dye injection phase, and then entering a continuous active imaging loop during the surgical procedure. This loop of illuminating, capturing, processing, and displaying provides constant visual feedback to the surgeon until the resection is complete.
