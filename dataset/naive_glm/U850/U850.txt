This model describes a rain sensing unit designed to automatically measure precipitation. Its core function is to detect rainfall, calculate the rate at which it's falling, and keep a running total of the accumulated rainfall depth.

The system is built around a capacitive rain sensor. This component detects the presence of raindrops on its sensing surface by measuring a change in its electrical capacitance. This raw capacitance signal is then passed to a signal conditioner, which filters out noise and amplifies the signal to make it more reliable.

A central calibration processor takes this refined signal and converts it into a meaningful precipitation rate (e.g., in millimeters per hour). To ensure accuracy across varying weather conditions, this processor uses an internal temperature sensor to compensate for temperature effects on the readings.

The calculated instantaneous rain rate is then sent to a data accumulator. This component integrates the rate over time to determine the total accumulated rainfall depth. Finally, the unit packages the current rate, total depth, a timestamp, and its current operational status into a data report that is sent to an external system.

The entire unit operates through a series of states. It can be powered off, in a standby mode awaiting commands, actively measuring, or running a calibration routine. An external system can send commands to start or stop measurements, reset the accumulated total, or trigger a new calibration. The unit is designed to be robust, operating over a wide temperature range (-40°C to +85°C) and providing accurate precipitation measurements within 10 seconds of rainfall starting.
