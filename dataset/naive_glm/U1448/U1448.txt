This is a reliability management system designed to monitor critical equipment and proactively schedule maintenance to prevent failures. Its primary purpose is to ensure high system availability and minimize unplanned downtime by continuously tracking the "Mean Time Between Failures" (MTBF) for its components.

The system is composed of several key parts that work together. At its core are the **Critical Components**—such as a primary, secondary, and backup unit—which are the actual pieces of equipment being managed. These components track their own operational hours and report any failures that occur.

A central **Reliability Monitor** acts as the system's brain. It gathers operational data and failure events from all the critical components. Using this information, it continuously calculates the current reliability and the overall MTBF for the entire system.

Based on the data from the monitor, a **Maintenance Controller** decides when service is needed. If the system's calculated reliability drops below a predefined threshold, or if a component nears its expected failure point, the controller automatically schedules and dispatches commands for preventive maintenance.

Finally, a **Data Recorder** logs every significant event, including all component failures and maintenance activities. This creates a complete historical record, which can be used for long-term performance analysis and improving maintenance strategies.

The system operates through several distinct states. During **Normal Operation**, it is continuously monitoring its health. It can transition to a **Scheduled Maintenance** state to perform preventive service and then return to normal. If reliability degrades unexpectedly, it can enter a **Degraded Operation** mode, possibly by activating a backup component to maintain functionality. In the event of a catastrophic problem, the system is designed to enter a **Critical Failure** state, initiating an emergency shutdown to prevent further damage.
