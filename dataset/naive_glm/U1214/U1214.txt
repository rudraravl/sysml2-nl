This model describes a smart electrical metering system, designed to replace traditional analog meters in homes or businesses. The core of the system is the Smart Meter device itself, which is installed between the main utility grid and the customer's premises to measure and manage electricity consumption.

The Smart Meter is composed of several key components that work together. A **Metering Module** acts as the primary sensor, continuously measuring the voltage and current being drawn from the grid to calculate both the instantaneous power being used and the cumulative energy consumed over time.

This raw data is then passed to a **Data Processor**, which analyzes it to identify usage patterns and detect potential anomalies. The processed consumption data is then sent to a **Communication Module**. This module serves as a two-way radio, transmitting the usage information back to the utility company's central system and also receiving data, such as updated electricity pricing (tariff rates).

The meter includes an **Alert Manager** that constantly monitors the power consumption. If the power draw suddenly exceeds a pre-set safety threshold, it generates an alert that is immediately transmitted by the communication module. Internally, a **Billing Calculator** uses the measured energy consumption and the latest tariff rates received from the utility to calculate the running cost of the electricity used.

A critical feature of the system is a built-in **Relay**, which acts as a remote-controlled switch. This allows the utility company to remotely disconnect or reconnect the power supply to the premises, a key function for managing service.

Overall, the system operates in a continuous cycle: it measures energy flow, processes the usage data, calculates billing information, communicates with the utility's infrastructure, and actively monitors for abnormal consumption patterns or power overloads to ensure safe and efficient energy management.
