This model describes a modern household refrigerator system designed to preserve perishable food by maintaining a stable, low-temperature internal environment.

The system's core is a vapor-compression refrigeration cycle that actively moves heat from the inside of the refrigerator to the outside air. This cycle consists of four main components working in a closed loop: a **compressor**, a **condenser**, an **expansion valve**, and an **evaporator**. The compressor pressurizes a refrigerant gas, which then flows to the condenser coils (typically on the back or bottom of the unit). Here, a fan helps dissipate heat into the room, causing the refrigerant to cool and turn into a high-pressure liquid. This liquid then passes through the expansion valve, where its pressure drops suddenly, making it very cold. Finally, the cold refrigerant flows through the evaporator coils inside the refrigerated compartment, absorbing heat from the interior and cooling the air. A fan inside the compartment circulates this cooled air to maintain a consistent temperature.

The entire system is housed within an insulated structure to minimize heat leakage from the outside. The insulated storage area is the **interior compartment** where food is kept.

A central **controller** acts as the brain of the refrigerator. It uses a **temperature sensor** to monitor the internal temperature and a **door sensor** to detect if the door is open or closed. Based on a target temperature setpoint, the controller decides when to turn the compressor and fans on or off. To save energy and prevent overworking the system, the cooling cycle will not run if the door is detected to be open. The system is powered by an internal **power supply** that distributes electricity to the controller, compressor, and fans.
