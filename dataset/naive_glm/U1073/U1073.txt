This model represents an autonomous six-legged walking robot, or hexapod, designed for exploration and navigation across varied and complex terrain.

The robot is built around a central body with six identical, multi-jointed legs. Each leg has three motorized joints (coxa, femur, and tibia), providing a high degree of flexibility and movement. Attached to each leg is a ground sensor that can detect the distance to the surface and confirm when the foot is in contact with the ground. This sensory input is crucial for navigating uneven surfaces.

At the heart of the system is a central control processor that acts as the robot's brain. It is powered by a rechargeable battery pack designed to allow for at least an hour of continuous operation. The controller receives data from all six ground sensors, processes this information to understand the surrounding terrain, and then calculates the necessary movements for locomotion.

The robot operates in several modes, including a standby mode while waiting for commands and an active locomotion mode for movement. When instructed to move, the controller initiates a continuous cycle: it scans the environment using its sensors, selects an appropriate walking pattern (gait) based on the terrain conditions and any detected obstacles, and then generates and sends precise angle and torque commands to each of the 18 leg joints. This process ensures all six legs move in a coordinated and stable manner, with a key design principle of always keeping at least three legs on the ground at any time to maintain balance. If an obstacle is detected, the robot can enter a special obstacle avoidance mode to navigate around it before resuming its path.
