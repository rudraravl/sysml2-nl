This is a sensory testing system designed to quantitatively measure tactile sensitivity and pain thresholds in biological subjects, such as in clinical or laboratory research.

The core of the system is an instrument called a Von Frey hair. This is a simple tool consisting of a handle attached to a single, fine filament, which can be made from materials like nylon. The critical feature of each filament is that it is precisely calibrated to buckle or bend at a specific, known amount of force. The system utilizes a set of these instruments, each with a different filament designed to buckle at a different force, covering a wide range of sensitivity levels.

To perform a test, an operator selects a Von Frey hair and gently presses the tip of its filament against the subject's skin. The operator applies force gradually until the filament buckles. This buckling action is key, as it ensures that the exact calibrated force of that filament—and no more—is delivered to the subject, providing a consistent and repeatable stimulus.

The overall testing process involves a sequence of actions to determine the subject's sensitivity threshold. The operator positions the instrument, applies force until it buckles, and then carefully observes the subject for a response, such as a withdrawal, flinch, or vocalization. The outcome is recorded, and if no response is observed, the operator will typically select the next filament in the series (one with a higher buckling force) and repeat the test until a response is detected. The lowest force that consistently elicits a response is identified as the subject's sensitivity threshold.
