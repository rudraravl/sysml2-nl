This model represents a Pennington clamp, a surgical instrument designed to atraumatically grasp, hold, and manipulate soft tissue during medical procedures.

The clamp is a mechanical, hand-operated tool built with several key components working in concert. It consists of two ergonomic handles that a surgeon squeezes, a central pivot joint that connects the handles to the jaws, and two opposing jaws that make contact with the tissue. The pivot provides a mechanical advantage, multiplying the force applied by the surgeon's hand into a stronger clamping force at the jaw tips.

The jaws themselves have serrated gripping surfaces to ensure a secure hold on tissue without slippage. A critical feature of the Pennington clamp is its integrated ratchet lock. As the handles are squeezed and the jaws close onto the tissue, this multi-toothed locking mechanism automatically engages. This allows the clamp to maintain a constant, secure grip at a specific pressure without the surgeon needing to apply continuous force.

The clamp's operation follows a clear sequence: it starts in an open state for positioning, moves to a closing state as the operator applies pressure, and then enters a locked state once the ratchet engages to securely hold the tissue. To release the tissue, the operator simply disengages the lock, allowing the jaws to open. The entire system is designed with a primary focus on providing a reliable, non-damaging grip required for delicate surgical work.
