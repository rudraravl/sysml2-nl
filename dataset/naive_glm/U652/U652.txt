This model describes a high-precision displacement receiver system, an electronic device designed to accurately measure physical movement or changes in position and convert this information into a digital output. This type of system is commonly used in industrial automation, robotics, quality control inspection, and scientific instrumentation where precise position measurement is critical.

The system works through a sequential, multi-stage process to ensure high accuracy:

1.  **Sensing:** It starts with a `Sensor Core`, the primary component that physically detects the mechanical displacement. This sensor converts the movement into a raw, low-level analog electrical signal. The quality of this initial measurement depends on the sensor's sensitivity and its effective linear range.

2.  **Signal Conditioning:** The raw signal from the sensor is immediately sent to a `Signal Conditioner`. This stage is crucial for cleaning up the signal; it filters out unwanted electrical noise and amplifies the signal to a more robust level, making it easier for the subsequent components to process accurately.

3.  **Digitization:** The conditioned analog signal is then converted into a digital format by an `Analog-to-Digital Converter` (ADC). The ADC samples the signal at a specific rate and with a certain resolution (e.g., 16-bit), transforming the continuous voltage into a stream of discrete numerical values.

4.  **Processing:** Finally, a `Data Processor` takes these digital values. Using pre-defined calibration coefficients, it performs calculations to correct for any known imperfections in the sensor or electronics. This final step computes the precise position and outputs it as clean, usable digital data.

The entire system has distinct operational states, including initialization upon startup, a ready state while waiting for commands, an active measuring state, a special mode for calibration, and a fault state if an error is detected. It is engineered to meet specific performance requirements for its measurement range, accuracy, repeatability, and sampling rate, ensuring it is reliable for its intended application.
