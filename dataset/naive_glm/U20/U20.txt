This model describes an autonomous robotic system featuring two "tortoise" robots, named Elmer and Elsie, designed to demonstrate emergent, life-like behaviors through simple light-seeking rules. The system is a modern interpretation of the classic cybernetics experiments by William Grey Walter.

Each tortoise robot is a self-contained, mobile unit equipped with a photocell sensor to detect light, a drive motor and steering mechanism for mobility, a headlamp to act as a light source, and an internal rechargeable battery for power. Elmer and Elsie are fundamentally the same type of robot but have slightly different physical characteristics, such as mass, maximum speed, and battery capacity, giving them unique behavioral "personalities."

The robots' core function is to seek out light. They continuously sense the ambient light in their environment. In dim conditions, a robot will automatically switch on its headlamp. When it detects a strong light source—either from its environment or, more importantly, from the other robot's headlamp—it will move towards it. This simple, reflexive behavior leads to complex, unscripted interactions. The robots become mutually attracted to each other's headlamps, causing them to approach, circle, and seemingly "dance" with one another in a dynamic way.

The system is designed for continuous, long-term operation. Each tortoise independently monitors its own battery level. When its charge runs low, it automatically disengages from its light-seeking behavior. It then uses a dedicated light beacon emitted by a central charging station to navigate back, dock itself, and recharge. Once its battery is sufficiently full, it undocks and resumes its exploration and interaction. The complete system includes the two robots and their shared two-dock charging station, all operating within a defined physical space.
