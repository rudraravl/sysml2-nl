This model describes a high-fidelity telemanipulator system, which allows a human operator to control a remote robotic arm with a sense of touch and sight. The system is designed for precise manipulation tasks in environments that are either hazardous or inaccessible to humans.

The system consists of two main physical components: a **Master Station** for the operator and a **Slave Manipulator** which is the remote robotic arm. These two are linked by a dedicated **Communication Channel**.

The **Master Station** serves as the operator's control console. It includes a specialized input device that captures the operator's hand and wrist movements in three dimensions (position and orientation) as well as commands to open or close the gripper. The station also features a visual display that shows a live video feed from the robot's perspective and a haptic display that pushes back on the operator's hand, allowing them to "feel" the forces and torques the remote robot encounters when it touches an object.

The **Slave Manipulator** is the robotic arm that performs the physical work. It is equipped with a gripper (or end-effector) for grasping objects, a camera for visual feedback, and a sophisticated force-torque sensor at its wrist. This sensor measures the precise interaction forces when the gripper makes contact with its environment.

The core operation is a continuous, rapid cycle. The operator's movements at the Master Station are translated into motion commands, which are sent to the Slave Manipulator. The robotic arm then mirrors these movements. Simultaneously, the camera and force sensor on the slave capture visual and force data, which is transmitted back to the Master Station. The operator sees the remote scene on their display and feels a realistic reflection of the contact forces on their hand controller. This closed loop of motion commands and sensory feedback creates an immersive sense of telepresence and enables delicate, force-sensitive tasks.

The system's behavior is managed through several operational states, including `Offline`, `Standby` for preparation, and `Active` for task execution. For safety, it includes an `Emergency` state that can be triggered to halt all motion immediately, along with inherent limits to prevent the robot from exerting excessive force on its surroundings. This particular system is designed for a 1.5-meter reach, can handle payloads up to 10 kg, and operates with a very low communication delay to ensure control feels stable and responsive.
