This model represents an automated temperature control system designed to monitor and maintain a stable environmental temperature. It's a common setup for devices like thermostats, incubators, or industrial process controllers.

The core of the system is a central microcontroller that acts as its brain. This microcontroller is powered by a dedicated power supply, which also provides the necessary voltage and current to all the other components.

The system works by continuously gathering data from two separate temperature sensors. These sensors measure the ambient temperature and send this information as analog voltage signals to the microcontroller. The microcontroller's internal hardware, specifically its Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC), converts these signals into digital temperature readings.

The microcontroller's software then processes this data, comparing the measured temperature to a predefined target temperature (e.g., 25 degrees). If the measured temperature is too low, the microcontroller sends a digital "on" signal to a heating element. This activates the heater, which raises the ambient temperature. Once the temperature reaches the target, the microcontroller sends an "off" signal to deactivate the heater. This entire process of sensing, comparing, and controlling repeats in a continuous loop to keep the temperature stable.

The microcontroller itself is designed to be robust, operating through various states. Under normal conditions, it is active and running its control loop. However, it can enter a low-power sleep mode in response to a power loss event and has a dedicated shutdown and recovery process for handling critical errors, ensuring system reliability.
