This model represents an Acoustic Radiometer, a specialized instrument designed to measure the total power of a sound wave by detecting the subtle physical force it exerts on a target.

The system operates by exposing a specialized, highly absorbent target to an incoming sound field. When the sound wave impacts this target, it imparts a tiny, steady force known as radiation pressure. This minuscule force is detected by a highly sensitive force sensor, which converts the physical push into a raw electrical signal.

This raw signal is then passed to a signal conditioner, which amplifies and filters it to remove noise and ensure a clean, reliable reading. The conditioned signal is then fed into a computation unit. Using known physical properties of the medium (like its density and the speed of sound) and a calibration factor, this processor calculates the original acoustic power of the wave based on the force it measured.

Finally, the calculated power measurement is sent to a display unit, providing a clear reading to the operator. The entire system is a cohesive unit composed of these interconnected components: the target, sensor, conditioner, processor, and display. It is designed for high-precision applications, requiring an accuracy of at least ±3%, and is capable of continuous operation by cycling through its measuring, processing, and displaying states to provide rapid updates.
