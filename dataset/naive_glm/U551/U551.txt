This model describes a fluid transfer system designed to move a liquid from a supply tank to a receiving tank using a mechanical reciprocating pump. The system is equipped with instrumentation to monitor its performance, including pressure gauges and a flow meter.

The core of the system is the reciprocating pump, which uses a positive displacement mechanism to move fluid. It is composed of several key mechanical parts working in concert:

*   A **crankshaft** provides the initial rotational power.
*   A **connecting rod** links the crankshaft to a piston, converting the crankshaft's rotation into the back-and-forth linear motion of the piston.
*   The **piston** moves within a sealed **cylinder**, creating changes in volume and pressure.
*   Two **check valves**, one at the inlet and one at the outlet of the cylinder, ensure that the fluid flows in only one direction.

The pump operates in a cyclical two-stroke process. During the **suction stroke**, the piston pulls back, creating a low-pressure area in the cylinder. This opens the inlet valve, drawing fluid in from the supply tank. During the **discharge stroke**, the piston pushes forward, compressing the fluid. This pressure closes the inlet valve and forces the outlet valve open, expelling the fluid into the discharge line towards the receiving tank.

The complete subsystem includes a large **supply tank** that holds the source fluid and a **receiving tank** to collect the pumped fluid. For operational awareness, a pressure gauge is fitted to both the suction and discharge lines of the pump, and a flow meter measures the output rate of the fluid being transferred.

The pump's operation is managed through a series of states. It begins in an **idle** state. Upon receiving a start command, it transitions to a **starting** state to ramp up its operational speed. Once running, it continuously alternates between its **suction** and **discharge** phases. A stop command initiates a **stopping** sequence, allowing it to safely spin down and return to idle. The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, including minimum flow rate, maximum pressure, and operational efficiency, and is intended for use with fluids within a specified range of viscosity and temperature.
