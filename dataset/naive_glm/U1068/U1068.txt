This is a high-performance, graphene-based electronic system designed for advanced, high-bandwidth communication and signal processing applications. Its primary function is to receive a high-speed electrical signal, amplify it, and then use that amplified signal to modulate an optical (light) signal, enabling the conversion of electrical data into an optical format suitable for technologies like fiber-optic communication.

The system is built around two key components that leverage the unique properties of graphene: a graphene transistor and a graphene optical modulator.

The transistor serves as the electrical amplification stage. It uses a single-atom-thick sheet of graphene as its conducting channel, which allows for exceptionally fast electron movement (high carrier mobility) and excellent conductivity. This enables the amplification of electrical signals at very high frequencies, beyond the capabilities of many conventional semiconductor materials.

The amplified electrical signal from the transistor is then used to control the optical modulator. This component, which also integrates a thin graphene layer, takes an incoming beam of light and modifies its intensity based on the voltage of the control signal. In this way, the information from the electrical signal is encoded onto the light beam.

To ensure stable and reliable operation, the system includes an integrated thermal management unit in the form of a heat sink. This component is thermally connected to the active graphene elements to efficiently dissipate the heat generated during high-speed processing. The system operates through several distinct states, including initialization, standby, calibration, and a fully active mode. During active operation, it continuously amplifies and modulates signals while actively monitoring its temperature to prevent overheating, automatically entering a fault state if critical temperature or current limits are exceeded.
