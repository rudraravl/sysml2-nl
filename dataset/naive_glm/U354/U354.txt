This model describes a Pinard Horn Fetal Auscultation System, a purely mechanical, non-electronic medical device used for listening to a fetus's heartbeat during pregnancy. It is essentially a specialized acoustic stethoscope shaped like a small trumpet, designed for midwives and clinicians to perform non-invasive fetal monitoring.

The system is composed of three primary components that work together to capture and amplify sound. First, a wide, open bell is placed firmly against the mother's abdomen. This bell acts as a collector, gathering the faint sound waves of the fetal heart that travel through the abdominal wall. These captured sounds are then funneled into a hollow, central resonating tube. The specific length and diameter of this tube cause the sound waves to resonate, naturally amplifying them without any power source. Finally, the amplified sound is channeled to a flat earpiece, which the clinician presses against their ear to listen clearly.

The overall use of the system is a clinical procedure involving a patient, the Pinard horn, and a trained practitioner. The process begins with the clinician carefully positioning the horn on the abdomen to locate the fetal heartbeat. If the sound is too weak, they must adjust the position. Once a clear heartbeat is detected, the clinician listens and counts the beats over a period of time to determine the fetal heart rate. The entire system relies on physical acoustics, providing a simple, safe, and reliable method for routine fetal assessment.
