This model represents the complete system for the STS-133 mission, the final flight of the Space Shuttle Discovery. The primary purpose of this mission was to deliver critical hardware and supplies to the International Space Station (ISS).

The system consists of two main spacecraft: the Space Shuttle Orbiter Discovery and the International Space Station. The Discovery orbiter is a crewed vehicle, configured for this mission with a crew of six. It features a large payload bay designed to transport heavy cargo into orbit. For maneuvering in space, it is equipped with a powerful Orbital Maneuvering System (OMS) for major orbital changes and a fine-tuned Reaction Control System (RCS) with numerous small thrusters for precise attitude control and docking.

The cargo for this specific mission includes three key components:
1.  **The Permanent Multipurpose Module (PMM):** A large, pressurized module designed to be permanently attached to the ISS, providing significant additional volume for storage and scientific experiments.
2.  **The Express Logistics Carrier 4 (ELC-4):** An external platform carrying large spare components for the space station.
3.  **Robonaut 2:** An advanced humanoid robot designed to assist astronauts with tasks both inside and outside the station.

The mission unfolds through a series of distinct phases. It begins with the launch and ascent of Discovery into a precise low-Earth orbit. The orbiter then performs a carefully choreographed rendezvous sequence to catch up with and approach the ISS. This culminates in a delicate docking procedure where the two spacecraft physically connect via their docking ports.

Once docked, the main operational phase begins. This involves the transfer of the cargo from Discovery's payload bay to the ISS. The crew also performs two planned spacewalks (Extravehicular Activities or EVAs) to install the PMM and perform other maintenance tasks on the station's exterior. After all objectives are completed, Discovery undocks from the ISS, performs a final inspection fly-around, and prepares for its return to Earth. The mission concludes with a deorbit burn to slow the orbiter, atmospheric entry, and a runway landing.
