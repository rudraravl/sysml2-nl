This model represents the OneTouch Ultra, a portable electronic medical device designed for personal blood glucose monitoring, typically used by individuals with diabetes. The system's main function is to accurately measure the concentration of glucose in a small capillary blood sample obtained from a user.

The system consists of a handheld meter and disposable, single-use test strips. The meter itself is a complex electronic device containing several key components that work together. At its core is an **Electrochemical Sensor**, which interfaces with the test strip. When a user applies a blood sample to the enzyme-coated strip, a chemical reaction occurs that generates a tiny electrical current directly proportional to the amount of glucose in the blood.

This analog electrical signal is then converted into a digital value by an **Analog-to-Digital Converter**. The "brain" of the device, a **Microcontroller**, processes this digital signal. Using a pre-programmed calibration curve, it calculates the final blood glucose concentration, typically displayed in mg/dL. This result is then shown to the user on a **Liquid Crystal Display (LCD)**.

The device also features **Nonvolatile Memory** capable of storing up to 450 historical readings with timestamps, allowing users to track their glucose levels over time. The entire system is powered by a small internal **Battery**.

The operational workflow is straightforward for the user. The meter powers on from an `Off` state to `Standby` when a test strip is inserted. Once the meter detects that a blood sample has been applied to the strip, it enters a `Measuring` state, which lasts approximately five seconds. Upon completion, it transitions to `Displaying Result`, where the reading is shown on the screen and simultaneously saved to memory. If the reading is outside the measurable range (20-600 mg/dL), the meter displays an error message instead.
