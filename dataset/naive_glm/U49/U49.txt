This model describes a drug-eluting coronary stent system, a medical device used in interventional cardiology to treat blocked or narrowed coronary arteries. The system is designed to restore and maintain blood flow to the heart muscle.

The complete system consists of two main components: a metallic coronary stent and a balloon catheter that serves as its delivery vehicle.

The stent itself is a small, expandable mesh tube, constructed from biocompatible materials like Cobalt-Chromium or Platinum-Chromium. It features a special drug coating designed to release medication over a prolonged period, typically for at least 180 days. This medication helps prevent restenosis, which is the re-narrowing of the artery after the procedure. The stent is engineered to have a specific radial strength, allowing it to exert enough outward force to hold the artery open after being expanded.

The balloon catheter is a long, flexible tube that carries the collapsed stent to the site of the blockage in the artery. An inflatable balloon is located at its tip, underneath the stent.

The system is used in a procedure where the catheter is guided through the patient's blood vessels to the narrowed artery. Once positioned correctly, the balloon is inflated to a precise pressure. This inflation expands the stent, pressing it firmly against the artery wall and compressing the plaque that caused the blockage. This action widens the vessel and restores blood flow. After the stent is fully deployed and locked in place, the balloon is deflated, and the catheter is removed, leaving the stent behind as a permanent scaffold to keep the artery open.
