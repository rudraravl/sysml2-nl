This model describes a Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation (tDCS) system, a medical device designed to modulate brain activity. Its purpose is to deliver a controlled, low-intensity electrical current to a patient's scalp through a pair of electrodes (an anode and a cathode) to influence neuronal function in targeted brain regions.

The system is composed of several key components working together. A central **Current Generator** produces the precise electrical stimulus, which follows a specific therapeutic profile: it gradually ramps the current up to a target level, maintains it for a set duration, and then safely ramps it back down. This generator is powered by a dedicated **Power Supply**.

A clinician or operator interacts with the system through a **User Interface** to configure the stimulation session. They can set critical parameters such as the current intensity, total duration of the therapy, and the ramp-up/ramp-down times.

Patient safety is paramount and is managed by two dedicated components. An **Impedance Monitor** continuously checks the quality of the connection between the electrodes and the patient's scalp by measuring electrical resistance. A poor connection could compromise the therapy or cause skin irritation. A comprehensive **Safety Controller** acts as an overall guardian, constantly monitoring the current levels, session duration, and impedance status. If any of these parameters exceed predefined safety limits, the controller can automatically trigger an alert and initiate an emergency shutdown of the system to ensure the patient's well-being.
