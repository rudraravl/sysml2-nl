This is an industrial fluid pumping system designed to transfer a liquid, such as water, from a source reservoir to a pressurized discharge vessel. The entire subsystem is instrumented for performance monitoring and flow control.

At the heart of the system is a powerful centrifugal pump, driven by a 22 kW electric motor. This pump is composed of an impeller that spins to move the fluid, a volute casing that directs the flow, and a stainless steel shaft connecting them to the motor. The system's main function is to draw fluid from a 50 cubic meter source reservoir through a 15-meter suction pipe. The pump then increases the fluid's pressure and pushes it through a longer 100-meter discharge pipe into a destination vessel.

To ensure proper operation, the system is equipped with a suite of sensors and controls. Pressure sensors are installed at both the inlet and outlet of the pump, and a flow meter is placed in the discharge line to continuously measure the system's performance. A control valve on the discharge side allows for precise adjustment of the fluid flow rate.

The pump operates through a defined lifecycle. It begins in an `idle` state, ready for operation. Upon receiving a start command, it enters a `starting` phase where the motor spins up the impeller. Once at operating speed, it transitions to the `running` state. During normal operation, the system is monitored for potential problems like `cavitation` (if the suction pressure drops too low) or `overload` (if the motor draws too much power). The system is designed to return to normal operation if these conditions are corrected. Finally, a stop command initiates a `stopping` sequence, bringing the pump safely back to an idle state.
