This model represents the complete operational system for an academic journal, specifically the "Journal of Electrical and Computer Engineering." Its primary function is to manage the entire lifecycle of a scholarly article, from an author's initial submission to its final publication and archival.

The system is composed of several interconnected subsystems that work together to facilitate a rigorous peer-review process. It begins with a **Submission Management System**, which is the entry point for authors to submit their manuscripts. Once a manuscript is received, it is passed to the **Review Management System**. This subsystem is responsible for coordinating the critical peer-review process by selecting three to five qualified experts from a large pool of reviewers and collecting their detailed feedback. These reviews assess the manuscript's technical quality, novelty, and clarity.

The completed reviews are then forwarded to the **Editorial Decision System**. Here, an Associate Editor evaluates the feedback and makes a recommendation to accept, reject, or request revisions to the manuscript. For major decisions, an Editor-in-Chief provides final approval.

If a manuscript is accepted, it moves to the **Production System**. This component handles all post-acceptance activities, including professional typesetting, proofreading, assigning a permanent Digital Object Identifier (DOI), and preparing the article for final publication with details like volume, issue, and page numbers. Finally, the published article is permanently stored and made accessible through a digital **Archive System**.

The entire process follows a well-defined workflow where a manuscript progresses through states such as 'submitted', 'under review', 'accepted', 'in production', and finally 'published'. The system is designed to uphold key scholarly principles, including rigorous peer-review quality, timely publication, editorial independence, and the permanent, secure archival of all published research.
