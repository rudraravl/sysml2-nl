This model describes a complete solar power system for a spacecraft, designed to be its primary source of electrical energy. Its function is to autonomously capture sunlight, convert it into regulated electricity, and distribute it to the spacecraft's various subsystems, while also storing energy for periods when the sun is not visible.

The core of the energy generation system is a large, deployable **Solar Array**. This array is composed of four individual **Solar Panels**, each of which is populated with 32 high-efficiency **Solar Cells** that perform the actual conversion of light into electrical current.

To maximize power generation, the system must constantly keep the solar array pointed directly at the sun. This is achieved through an automated sun-tracking subsystem. A **Sun Sensor** continually detects the sun's precise location and intensity. This information is fed to an **Attitude Controller**, which acts as the brain of the pointing system. The controller calculates the necessary orientation and sends commands to a **Gimbal Actuator**, a motorized joint that physically rotates and tilts the entire solar array to track the sun's movement.

The raw power generated by the array is conditioned by a **Power Regulator**. This component stabilizes the electricity, ensuring a consistent and safe voltage (e.g., 28V) is delivered to the rest of the spacecraft.

From the regulator, power flows to the **Power Distribution Unit (PDU)**, which serves as the central energy manager. The PDU intelligently routes power based on need. It supplies electricity to the various spacecraft systems, referred to as **loads**, which include components like the communications system, science payloads, and thermal control. The PDU also manages the system's rechargeable **Battery**. When the solar array generates more power than the spacecraft needs, the PDU uses the surplus to charge the battery.

The battery is crucial for mission survival. When the spacecraft enters an **eclipse** (passing through a planet's shadow), the solar array can no longer generate power. During these periods, the PDU automatically switches over, drawing stored energy from the battery to keep all essential systems running.

The entire system operates through a series of distinct states. It begins in a **Stowed** configuration for launch. Once in orbit, it enters a **Deploying** state to unfold the solar arrays. It then proceeds to **Sun Acquisition** to find and lock onto the sun. Its normal state is **Nominal Operation**, where it actively tracks the sun and generates power. It transitions to the **Eclipse** state when in shadow and reverts to **Safe Mode**—a minimal power consumption state—if a system fault is detected.
