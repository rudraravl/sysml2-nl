This model represents a pantograph power collection system for an electric rail vehicle, such as a train or tram. Its primary purpose is to establish and maintain a continuous electrical connection with an overhead power wire, drawing high-voltage power (typically 25,000 volts) to run the vehicle's traction motors and other onboard systems.

The system is a complex mechanical and electrical assembly mounted on the vehicle's roof. Its core is an articulated arm structure, composed of a lower and upper arm, which can be raised and lowered. At the very top of this structure is a collector head, which holds a replaceable carbon contact strip. This strip is the component that physically slides along the overhead wire to collect electricity.

A pneumatic actuator provides the main force to raise the arms into position and lower them when not in use. Once raised, a spring assembly works to apply a constant, controlled upward pressure against the wire. This ensures a stable electrical contact, even as the vehicle moves and the height of the overhead wire varies slightly. The system is designed to actively adjust its height to maintain a nominal contact force between 70 and 120 Newtons, preventing both poor connection and excessive wear.

For safety, the entire assembly is mounted on a large insulator. This critical component prevents the high-voltage electricity from passing into the body of the vehicle, protecting the equipment and passengers. The collected power is then routed from the pantograph into the vehicle's internal power bus.

The pantograph operates in several states: it can be safely stowed, in the process of raising or lowering, fully operational and collecting power, or in a fault state if contact is lost. It also includes an emergency lowering function for rapid, safe retraction.
