This model describes a manual, hand-operated bicycle pump system. It is designed to draw in ambient air from the atmosphere, compress it, and then transfer it into an inflatable object, such as a bicycle tire. The complete system includes the pump itself, the surrounding atmosphere which acts as the air source, and the inflatable target that receives the compressed air.

The pump is constructed around a central pump body, or cylinder, which houses a movable piston connected to a handle. The user operates the pump by pulling and pushing this handle. This action moves the piston up and down within the pump's main chamber, changing its internal volume to compress the air.

The flow of air is controlled by two one-way check valves. An intake valve allows air to enter the chamber from the atmosphere when the handle is pulled up, but closes to prevent it from escaping on the downstroke. An outlet valve allows the compressed air to exit the chamber and flow towards the tire when the handle is pushed down, but prevents high-pressure air from the tire from flowing back into the pump. A flexible hose connects the pump's outlet to the inflatable target, delivering the compressed air.

The pumping operation is a two-stroke cycle:
1.  **Upstroke:** When the user pulls the handle up, the piston creates a low-pressure area in the chamber. This causes the intake valve to open, drawing atmospheric air into the pump body.
2.  **Downstroke:** When the user pushes the handle down, the intake valve closes, and the piston compresses the trapped air. When the pressure inside the chamber exceeds the pressure in the hose and tire, the outlet valve opens, forcing the compressed air through the hose and into the target.

The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, such as being able to deliver air at a pressure of at least 400 kPa. A critical design constraint is that the intake and outlet valves must never be open at the same time to ensure proper compression and prevent air from escaping back to the atmosphere.
