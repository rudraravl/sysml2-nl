This model describes a comprehensive system for managing a formal critique and approval workflow, designed to handle various types of professional work like documents, designs, code, and proposals. Its primary function is to automate and standardize the process of submitting work, receiving structured feedback, making revisions, and obtaining a final decision.

The system is built from several specialized components that work together to manage the entire process. When a piece of work is first submitted, it is placed in a **Work Queue** to await processing. This ensures that all submissions are handled in an orderly fashion.

From the queue, the work is passed to a **Review Engine**. This is the core analytical component that conducts a thorough evaluation. It identifies issues, assigns a severity level (from minor to critical), provides specific recommendations for improvement, and calculates an overall quality score for the work. The output is a formal critique document.

After the initial review, the work and its critique are evaluated. If revisions are needed, the item is sent to a **Revision Tracker**. This component logs all changes made by the author and ensures that the revisions properly address the feedback from the critique. The system allows for a set number of revision cycles, creating an iterative loop for improvement. Once revised, the work is sent back for another review.

If the work meets the required standards, it proceeds to the **Approval Authority**. This component makes the final decision, comparing the work's quality score against predefined approval criteria. It has the power to formally approve the work or, if it still fails to meet standards after revision, to reject it.

Throughout this entire workflow, a **NotificationService** operates in the background, keeping all stakeholders informed. It sends alerts to the submitter about the status of their work, notifies reviewers of new assignments, and communicates the final approval or rejection decision. The entire process is governed by a defined lifecycle, where each work item moves through distinct states such as 'submitted,' 'under review,' 'critiqued,' 'revised,' and finally 'approved' or 'rejected'. The system also enforces rules, such as maximum review times and minimum quality scores, to ensure timely and high-quality outcomes.
