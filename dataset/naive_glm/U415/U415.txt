This model represents a system for conducting a medical oral examination, centered around a single-use wooden tongue depressor. The primary purpose of this system is to allow a clinician to safely and effectively hold down a patient's tongue to get a clear view of their oral cavity and throat for diagnostic purposes.

The central object is the tongue depressor itself, a simple medical instrument with specific physical characteristics. It is a 150mm long, 18mm wide, and 2mm thick flat stick made of a specific density wood with a longitudinal grain. It consists of two main parts: a long, smooth blade designed to make contact with the patient's tongue, and a shorter handle for the clinician to grip.

The system also includes the two human actors: the clinician who wields the tool and the patient being examined. The interaction is straightforward: the clinician holds the depressor's handle and uses the blade to gently press down on the patient's tongue.

The process of the examination is clearly defined. It begins by ensuring the tongue depressor is sterile and the patient is ready. The clinician then positions the depressor, applies a light, sustained force (around 2.5 Newtons) to hold the tongue down, and performs a visual inspection of the throat. This hold is maintained until the examination is complete, after which the depressor is withdrawn.

The tongue depressor follows a distinct lifecycle. It starts in a sterile, packaged state. Once opened, it is ready for use. During the examination, it is in an active state. After a single use, it is discarded and is no longer considered sterile, highlighting its design as a disposable medical tool to prevent cross-contamination. The system has key safety and functional requirements, ensuring the material is non-toxic, strong enough to not splinter under pressure, and shaped correctly to be effective.
