This model describes a smart garment or e-textile system designed for continuous, wearable health monitoring. It's essentially intelligent clothing that tracks a person's vital signs in real-time.

The system is built into a flexible, stretchable, and washable fabric substrate. Woven into this fabric are conductive fibers that act as the system's wiring, connecting various electronic components.

Embedded directly within the garment are a suite of miniature sensors:
*   An **ECG sensor** monitors the heart's electrical activity to calculate an accurate heart rate.
*   A **thermistor** measures the wearer's skin temperature.
*   An **Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)** tracks body movement, including acceleration in three dimensions.
*   An **impedance sensor** measures the body's electrical resistance to estimate hydration levels.

All these components are powered by an integrated, flexible battery module designed for comfort and long-term wear. A central signal processing unit acts as the brain of the system. It collects the raw electrical signals from all the sensors, analyzes them to derive meaningful health data (like beats per minute or degrees Celsius), and then assembles this information, along with the current battery level, into a single health data packet.

This complete packet is then transmitted wirelessly via a built-in Bluetooth transceiver to an external device, such as a smartphone app or a medical monitoring station, for analysis and tracking.

The system operates through several states. When turned on, it runs a self-test and calibrates its sensors. In its normal monitoring state, it actively collects and transmits data. To conserve energy, it can enter a reduced monitoring mode when the battery is low. If the battery becomes critically low, it will attempt to transmit a final emergency data packet and save its current readings before shutting down safely.
