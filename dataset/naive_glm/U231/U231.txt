This model describes the Fotofinder, an advanced medical imaging system used in dermatology for total body photography and the early detection of skin cancer, such as melanoma. Its primary function is to create a complete, high-resolution photographic map of a patient's skin, which can be automatically analyzed and stored for future comparison.

The system is composed of several integrated hardware components. A central high-resolution camera, capable of capturing detailed images (6000x4000 pixels), works in tandem with a specialized illumination unit. This unit provides consistent, color-calibrated lighting to ensure that images taken at different times are comparable and free from shadows or color distortions.

The entire process is managed by a central control computer running a dedicated medical operating system, which an operator interacts with through a large touchscreen user interface. When an operator begins a "total body mapping" session for a patient, the system executes an automated sequence. The camera captures a series of images covering all major body regions, including the front, back, and both sides of the patient.

Once captured, these raw images are sent to an internal image processing unit for enhancement. Following this, the images are fed into a powerful AI analysis engine. This AI engine, with a high degree of accuracy, scans every image to automatically detect, identify, and provide a risk assessment for suspicious skin lesions.

All the data from the session—including the patient's ID, the set of captured images, and the AI-generated analysis results—is compiled and saved in a large-capacity data storage system. This allows clinicians to create a historical record for each patient, making it possible to track changes in moles and other skin lesions over time. Finally, the system can generate a comprehensive report summarizing the findings of the session.

The system operates through a clear set of states, moving from idle to ready, then through the sequential stages of an imaging session: capturing, processing, analyzing, storing, and report generation, before returning to a ready state for the next patient.
