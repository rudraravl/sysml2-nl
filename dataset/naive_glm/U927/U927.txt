This model describes a high-precision, non-contact distance measurement sensor that operates on the principle of laser triangulation.

The device works by projecting a laser beam from a central source onto a target object. The reflection of this laser light is then observed by two separate photodetectors, which are mounted a fixed and known distance apart from each other (a 12 cm baseline).

The core of the system's function relies on a processing sequence. First, an angle calculator determines the precise angle of the reflected light as it arrives at each of the two detectors. A central processing unit then uses these two angles, along with the fixed baseline distance between the detectors, to calculate the exact distance to the target object using trigonometric principles. After calculation, the unit performs a validation check on the reading to ensure its reliability.

This entire sensing unit is designed as a self-contained module. It requires a 5-volt power supply for operation and interfaces with an external data acquisition or control system. It can be triggered to take a measurement and sends back the calculated distance value, along with a flag indicating whether the measurement is valid. The system is specified to measure distances ranging from 10 cm to 10 meters with an accuracy of 1 millimeter, and it can provide new measurements at a rate of 50 times per second.
