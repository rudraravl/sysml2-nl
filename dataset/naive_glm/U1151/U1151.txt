This is an open-source environmental monitoring station designed to continuously measure and display real-time temperature, humidity, and atmospheric pressure. A key characteristic of this system is its commitment to open-hardware principles, meaning all design schematics, component lists, and firmware are publicly available, allowing anyone to build, modify, or repair it.

The system is composed of several distinct hardware components working together. At its core is an open-source microcontroller, the "brain" of the device, which runs custom firmware to coordinate all operations. This microcontroller gathers data from a suite of three dedicated environmental sensors: one for temperature, one for humidity, and one for atmospheric pressure.

A dedicated power supply unit takes a standard input voltage, likely from a USB source, and efficiently regulates and distributes the correct power to the microcontroller, the sensors, and the display screen.

Once the microcontroller receives the raw data from the sensors, it processes these measurements to validate and format them. The resulting information, including the current temperature, humidity, pressure, and system status, is then sent to a small LCD display where it is shown to the user.

The monitor operates through a clear sequence of states. When first powered on, it initializes the sensors and then enters a continuous monitoring loop. In this loop, it repeatedly acquires data, processes it, and updates the display. The system is also designed to recognize faults, enter an error state if a problem occurs, and can be reset to resume normal operation.
