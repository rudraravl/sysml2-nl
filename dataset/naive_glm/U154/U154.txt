This model describes an autoclave sterilization system, centered around the function of a chemical indicator tape. The system is designed to sterilize equipment using high-pressure, high-temperature steam, and the tape provides a simple visual confirmation that the process was successful.

The core component is the autoclave tape itself. This tape is made of two main parts: a special adhesive backing and a chemical indicator. The adhesive is designed to be robust, maintaining its bond strength and sticking to packages even when subjected to the extreme heat (up to 150°C) and pressure of the sterilization cycle. The chemical indicator is the functional part of the tape that visually communicates the sterilization status. Initially, this indicator is a beige color.

This tape is used within a complete autoclave machine, which consists of a sealed chamber, a steam generator, and a control system. The process begins when the control system receives a command to start a sterilization cycle. The steam generator then heats water to produce saturated steam at a precise temperature (121°C or 394.15 K) and pressure (15 psi or 205 kPa). This steam is fed into the chamber, which holds the items to be sterilized, including packages sealed with the autoclave tape.

As the tape is exposed to these specific steam conditions, the chemical indicator reacts. After a minimum exposure of 15 minutes to the target temperature and pressure, the indicator's chemistry changes, causing its color to shift permanently from beige to green. This color change signifies that the conditions necessary for sterilization have been successfully met. The control system monitors this process and, upon completion, sends a signal that the cycle is finished. The visible green stripes on the tape then serve as a clear, immediate confirmation for an operator that the package inside has been through a proper sterilization cycle.
