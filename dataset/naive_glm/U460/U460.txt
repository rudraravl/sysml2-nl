This is a high-precision fluid pumping system designed to transfer a liquid from a supply tank to a receiving vessel at an extremely accurate and controlled flow rate. It's the kind of system used in industrial processes like chemical dosing, manufacturing, or pharmaceuticals where precise volumes of fluid are critical.

The system consists of four main components working in sequence. A **Supply Tank** holds the initial fluid, which is then drawn into the core of the system: a **Metering Pump**. This pump is a sophisticated device with its own motor, a mechanical pump head (containing a piston, cylinder, and valves), and an electronic controller. The pump works by repeatedly drawing in and pushing out a very specific amount of fluid with each stroke of its piston.

The pump's controller manages its operation to achieve a target flow rate. It does this by adjusting the motor's speed, which in turn changes the stroke rate (how many strokes per minute). As the fluid leaves the pump, it passes through a **Flow Sensor** that continuously measures the actual flow rate. This measurement is sent back to the pump's controller as feedback. The controller compares the actual flow to the target and makes fine adjustments to the motor speed, ensuring the output remains stable and accurate. Finally, the precisely metered fluid is delivered to a **Receiving Vessel**.

The system operates through several states, including an idle state, a priming sequence to prepare the pump, a normal running state for active pumping, and a shutdown state. It also includes critical safety features. The pump constantly monitors for potentially damaging conditions such as excessive discharge pressure or cavitation (the formation of vapor bubbles due to low suction pressure). If a fault is detected, the system will trigger an alarm and automatically shut down to prevent damage. The entire system is engineered to meet strict requirements for accuracy, repeatability, and responsiveness to changes in the desired flow rate.
