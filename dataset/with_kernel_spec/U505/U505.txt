This model describes a Gas Lift System, a type of artificial lift technology used in the oil and gas industry to enhance production from wells with insufficient natural pressure. The primary function of this system is to inject compressed gas deep into the well to help lift oil to the surface.

The system consists of several key components working in concert. At the surface, a **Gas Compressor** takes low-pressure gas and pressurizes it for injection. This high-pressure gas is then sent down the well through the **Annulus**, which is the space between the outer well casing and the inner production tubing.

Distributed at various depths along the production tubing are several **Gas Lift Valves**. These are pressure-sensitive valves that automatically open when the pressure in the annulus reaches a specific setpoint. When open, they allow the high-pressure gas from the annulus to be injected directly into the **Production Tubing**.

Simultaneously, fluid (oil and gas) from the underground **Reservoir** flows into the bottom of this same production tubing. As the injected gas mixes with the reservoir fluid, it reduces the overall density and weight of the fluid column. This "aerated" or lightened fluid requires much less pressure to be lifted. The remaining pressure from the reservoir is then sufficient to push the lighter fluid column up the production tubing to the surface facilities.

The entire operation is managed by a **Flow Controller**. This control unit continuously monitors the rate of oil production at the surface and adjusts the amount of gas being injected by the compressor. This ensures that the well operates efficiently to meet a target production rate without wasting energy or injection gas.
