This model describes a water pumping system designed to move fluid from a low-level reservoir to a higher-level tank using an inclined wood screw pump.

The system is composed of five main physical components. It starts with a **Sump Reservoir**, which acts as the source basin holding the fluid. This reservoir has defined minimum and maximum levels to ensure proper operation.

From the sump, the fluid is drawn into a **Wood Screw Pump**, the core of the system. This is a large, inclined mechanical screw (Archimedes' screw) that lifts the fluid as it rotates. The pump is characterized by its specific dimensions, such as its diameter, length, and the pitch of its helical blades, and is set at a fixed inclination angle. Its operation is managed through distinct states including idle, starting, running, and stopping.

The rotational power for the pump is provided by a connected **Electric Motor**. The motor's output shaft is mechanically coupled to the pump's drive shaft, transferring torque to turn the screw. The motor has its own control system, allowing it to start, accelerate to a nominal speed, run continuously, and then decelerate to a stop.

After being lifted by the pump, the fluid passes through a **Flow Meter**. This sensor continuously measures the volume of fluid moving through the pipe, providing real-time data on the system's performance.

Finally, the fluid is deposited into a **Discharge Tank**, which is positioned at a higher elevation. This tank serves as the final destination for the pumped fluid and includes a maximum level constraint to prevent overflowing.

The entire system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, including maintaining a minimum flow rate, achieving a certain level of efficiency, and being reliable enough for at least eight hours of continuous operation.
