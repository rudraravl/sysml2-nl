This is a Wartenberg wheel, a handheld medical instrument designed for neurological assessment. Its primary purpose is to test a patient's sensory nerve function by providing controlled tactile stimulation to the skin.

The device consists of an ergonomically designed handle connected by a low-friction axle to a single rotating wheel. This wheel is studded with a series of evenly-spaced pins—typically around 20. A key safety feature is that these pins are designed with blunt tips to provide a distinct tactile sensation without piercing or damaging the patient's skin.

In a clinical setting, a practitioner holds the handle to apply a gentle, controlled force and rolls the wheel across a specific area of the patient's skin. As the wheel turns, each pin makes sequential contact, delivering a series of controlled, pinpoint pressure stimuli. The patient's ability to perceive these sensations is then evaluated. This process helps clinicians assess nerve integrity, map out dermatomes (areas of skin supplied by a single spinal nerve), and identify potential neurological deficits.

The entire system is designed to be lightweight and portable for easy clinical use. The dimensions of the handle, the diameter of the wheel, and the number of pins are all specified to ensure the stimulus delivered is consistent, repeatable, and effective for diagnosis, while adhering to strict safety limits on the maximum force that can be applied. During an examination, the device moves between states such as being ready, positioned on the skin, and actively stimulating as it rolls.
