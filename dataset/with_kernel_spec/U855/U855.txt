This model represents a Ring Laser Gyroscope (RLG), a highly sensitive and accurate sensor designed to measure angular velocity, or the rate of rotation. It's a type of inertial sensor commonly used in high-performance navigation systems for aircraft, spacecraft, and ships.

The system's operation is based on the Sagnac effect, which detects changes in the properties of light when the system is rotating. At its core, the RLG consists of a closed-loop optical path, called a ring cavity, which is formed by a series of highly reflective mirrors.

The process begins with a laser gain medium, typically a Helium-Neon gas-filled tube, which generates a stable, coherent laser beam. This beam is directed into the ring cavity, where a beam splitter divides it into two separate beams that travel around the closed loop in opposite directions—one clockwise and one counter-clockwise.

When the gyroscope is stationary, both beams travel the exact same distance and arrive back at their starting point in perfect phase with each other. However, if the gyroscope starts to rotate, the path for the beam traveling in the direction of rotation becomes slightly longer, while the path for the beam traveling against the rotation becomes slightly shorter. This creates a tiny difference in the frequency of the two beams.

A photodetector is positioned to combine the two beams after they have completed their circuit. Due to the frequency difference caused by rotation, the beams interfere with each other, creating a "beat frequency" in the form of an electrical signal. This beat frequency is directly proportional to the rate of rotation.

A dedicated signal processor receives this electrical signal from the photodetector. Using the known physical dimensions of the ring cavity (its perimeter and area) and the wavelength of the laser, the processor calculates the precise angular velocity. This final measurement is then output as time-stamped rotation data.

The system has several operational states, including Standby, a Warmup period for thermal stability, a Locking state where the laser frequency is stabilized within the cavity, and a final Operating state where it continuously measures and reports rotation.
