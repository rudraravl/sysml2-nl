This model describes a portable medical device for Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation (TENS), designed to provide non-invasive pain relief. The system works by delivering controlled, low-voltage electrical pulses through self-adhesive electrode pads placed on the user's skin. These pulses stimulate the underlying nerves to help block or reduce pain signals sent to the brain.

The device is composed of several integrated components. At its core is a **Microcontroller Unit** that acts as the system's brain, managing the therapy settings and ensuring safe operation. Users interact with the device through a **User Control Panel**, which allows them to turn the unit on, select different therapy modes (such as continuous, burst, or modulated stimulation), and adjust the intensity of the electrical pulses to a comfortable level.

Power is provided by an internal, rechargeable **Battery Pack**. The microcontroller's instructions are sent to a **Pulse Generator Circuit**, which creates the precise electrical pulses with specific frequencies, widths, and amplitudes. These pulses are then delivered to the user via two **Electrode Pads** (an anode and a cathode) that are placed on the skin over the area of pain.

A critical feature of the system is its integrated **Safety Monitoring System**. This system continuously measures the electrical impedance of the skin through the electrode pads. If an electrode loses proper contact or falls off, the impedance will rise significantly, and the safety system will automatically shut down the therapy to prevent any potential harm or discomfort. It also enforces a strict limit on the maximum electrical current that can be delivered.

The device operates in several states, including `off`, `standby` for setup, and `active` during a therapy session. If a problem like poor electrode contact is detected, it enters a `fault` state.
