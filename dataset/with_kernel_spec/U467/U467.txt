This model describes an Archimedes screw pump system designed to continuously lift water from a lower reservoir to a higher one.

The core of the system is the screw assembly itself, which consists of a long, steel helical blade wrapped around a central cylindrical shaft. This entire rotating mechanism is enclosed within a stationary, inclined tubular housing. The assembly is set at a fixed inclination of 30 degrees to optimize the lifting process.

A powerful electric drive motor provides the rotational force needed to turn the screw. The system is situated between a lower basin, which acts as the water source, and an upper basin where the lifted water is discharged.

When activated, the motor spins the screw assembly. The lower end of the screw, submerged in the lower basin, scoops up volumes of water with each rotation. As the screw turns, these trapped pockets of water are carried progressively upward along the length of the housing until they are released into the upper basin.

A controller manages the system's operation through several distinct states. It begins in an 'idle' state, moves through 'starting' and 'accelerating' phases upon receiving a start command, and then enters a steady 'operating' state for continuous water pumping. A stop command initiates a 'stopping' sequence to safely bring the screw to a halt. The system is designed to operate continuously as long as it senses that sufficient water is available in the lower basin. The primary requirement is to lift water at a minimum flow rate of 0.1 cubic meters per second.
