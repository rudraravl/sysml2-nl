This model describes a classic syphon system, designed to passively transfer fluid from a higher reservoir to a lower one without any external pumps or power. The entire process is driven by gravitational potential energy and atmospheric pressure.

The system is composed of three main physical parts:

1.  **Source Reservoir:** An elevated container that holds the fluid to be transferred. It has a specific capacity and its fluid level (surface height) is a critical factor for the system's operation.

2.  **Receiving Reservoir:** A container positioned at a lower elevation to collect the transferred fluid. Like the source, it has a maximum volume to prevent overflow.

3.  **Syphon Tube:** A continuous tube that connects the two reservoirs. A key feature of its design is that it arches upwards, with its highest point (the apex) rising above the fluid level in the source reservoir before descending into the receiving reservoir. The tube's length and diameter are important factors that influence the rate of fluid flow.

The system operates through distinct phases. Initially, it is **idle**. To begin the transfer, the syphon must be **primed**, which involves completely filling the tube with fluid to eliminate any air pockets. This creates a continuous column of liquid.

Once primed, the system becomes **active**. The weight of the fluid in the longer, descending portion of the tube creates lower pressure at the apex than the atmospheric pressure pushing down on the source reservoir's surface. This pressure difference pushes the fluid from the source up into the tube, over the apex, and down into the receiving reservoir.

The flow will continue automatically as long as the fluid level in the source reservoir is higher than the level in the receiving reservoir and the source outlet is submerged. The process stops when the source reservoir is depleted, the receiving reservoir is full, or the fluid levels in both containers equalize.
