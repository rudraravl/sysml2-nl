This is a Nichols radiometer, a highly sensitive scientific instrument designed to measure the extremely faint pressure exerted by electromagnetic radiation (light).

The core of the system consists of a small, reflective mirror suspended by a very thin and delicate torsion fiber. This entire assembly is enclosed within a vacuum chamber to eliminate interference from air currents, which would otherwise overwhelm the tiny forces being measured.

When a beam of light enters the chamber and strikes the mirror, the momentum of the photons exerts a minute force on the mirror's surface. This force creates a torque, causing the mirror and the suspension fiber it is attached to to twist by a small angle.

Because the resulting deflection is incredibly small, an optical lever system is used to amplify and accurately measure it. This system effectively magnifies the tiny rotation of the mirror into a readable measurement. By knowing the degree of deflection and the physical stiffness of the torsion fiber, the instrument can precisely calculate the force applied by the light, and therefore the radiation pressure.

The radiometer operates in a simple cycle: it sits in an idle state until a light beam is turned on, at which point it enters a measuring state to determine the radiation pressure. When the beam is turned off, it returns to idle.
