This model describes a Trigeminal Nerve Stimulation System, a medical device designed for therapeutic pain management. It's intended to treat conditions like chronic facial pain, migraines, and cluster headaches by delivering controlled electrical pulses to the trigeminal nerve, one of the main nerves in the head.

The system is a closed-loop neurostimulator, meaning it not only delivers therapy but also senses the body's response and can adjust the treatment in real-time. It is composed of several key parts that work together:

*   A **Control Processor** acts as the central brain, managing the therapy sessions based on settings programmed by a clinician, such as the duration and intensity of the treatment. It continuously checks for safety and ensures the stimulation stays within acceptable limits.
*   A **Power Module**, containing a battery, provides the necessary electrical power for the entire device.
*   A **Signal Generator** creates the precise electrical pulses used for the therapy. It can produce different types of signals, such as continuous or burst-mode stimulation, based on instructions from the control processor.
*   An **Electrode Array** is the component that makes direct contact with the patient's tissue near the target nerve branch. This array, which has multiple contact points, delivers the electrical pulses from the signal generator to the nerve.
*   A **Feedback Sensor** measures the nerve tissue's response to the stimulation. It monitors key biological indicators like tissue impedance (a measure of electrical resistance) and the nerve's own electrical potential, providing a real-time picture of how the therapy is affecting the patient.

The device operates through a well-defined sequence. After being powered on, it enters a standby mode. Before starting a treatment, it performs a calibration sequence, measuring the baseline properties of the patient's nerve tissue to ensure a proper connection and tailor the therapy. During a session, it enters an active treatment and monitoring loop where it simultaneously delivers stimulation and senses the neural feedback. If the feedback indicates an unsafe condition, such as the impedance being too high or too low, the system is designed to automatically enter a fault state and shut down to ensure patient safety. This constant monitoring and feedback loop allows the system to potentially adjust stimulation parameters during treatment to maintain optimal therapeutic effect safely.
