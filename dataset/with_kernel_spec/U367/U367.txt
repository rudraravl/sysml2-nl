This is a medical monitoring system designed to continuously assess a patient's heart rhythm and detect potential arrhythmias. Its main purpose is to analyze the electrical activity of the heart's upper (atrial) and lower (ventricular) chambers and calculate a "Relative Atrial Index" (RAI) to identify abnormalities.

The system is built from several specialized components that work together in a continuous cycle:

1.  **ECG Sensor:** This component is responsible for acquiring the raw electrical signal from the patient's heart (an ECG).
2.  **Analyzers:** The raw ECG signal is sent to two separate analyzers. An **Atrial Analyzer** processes the signal to detect and measure the rate of the atria, while a **Ventricular Analyzer** does the same for the ventricles.
3.  **RAI Processor:** This is the core analytical unit. It receives the atrial and ventricular rates from the analyzers and calculates the RAI by dividing the atrial rate by the ventricular rate. It then compares this ratio to pre-set clinical thresholds to classify the patient's current rhythm status as "normal," "elevated," or "critical."
4.  **Alert Controller:** This component monitors the final report from the processor. If the rhythm status is classified as "critical," it automatically generates an alert signal, designed to notify clinicians of a potentially serious cardiac event requiring intervention.

In operation, the system provides real-time, uninterrupted cardiac monitoring. It constantly acquires data, analyzes the distinct chamber rates, computes the risk index, and generates reports. This allows for the early detection of conditions where the atria are beating faster than the ventricles, a common sign of certain arrhythmias.
