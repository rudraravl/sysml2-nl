This model represents a complete **Hybrid Operating Room System**, an advanced medical facility that integrates a sterile surgical suite with high-end medical imaging equipment. This allows for complex, minimally invasive procedures to be performed with the aid of real-time, high-resolution imaging, combining diagnosis and treatment into a single session.

The system is composed of several highly integrated subsystems that work together to support a surgical procedure. The core components include:

*   An **Imaging System**, likely a large, mobile C-arm X-ray machine, which can be positioned around the patient to capture live images during surgery. It consists of an X-ray source and a detector, and its operation follows a sequence of positioning, acquiring images, processing them for clarity, and transmitting them to displays. The system carefully tracks and manages the radiation dose delivered to the patient.

*   A versatile **Surgical Table** that can be electronically adjusted in height, tilt, and position both laterally and longitudinally to give surgeons optimal access and to align the patient with the imaging system.

*   A comprehensive **Patient Monitoring System** that continuously tracks the patient's vital signs. It includes specialized sensors like an ECG monitor for heart rate, a pulse oximeter for oxygen saturation, a blood pressure monitor, and a temperature sensor. This system is critical for patient safety and can automatically generate alarms if vitals fall outside safe limits.

*   An **Anesthesia Delivery System** which manages the patient's sedation and life support. It controls the flow rate of anesthetic gases and mechanically assists with ventilation, managing the rate and volume of breaths.

*   A sophisticated **Surgical Lighting System** that provides high-intensity, shadow-reduced illumination over the surgical site. The lighting can be focused, and its intensity and color temperature can be adjusted to ensure clear visibility for the surgical team.

*   An **Environmental Control System** that maintains the sterile conditions of the operating room. It manages air quality through high-efficiency filtration, controls room temperature and humidity, and maintains a positive air pressure to prevent contaminants from entering.

*   A central **Display System**, featuring multiple large, high-resolution screens. These displays are crucial for the surgical team, as they show the live medical images from the imaging system, real-time patient vital signs, and other relevant data, all in one place.

*   A **Surgical Navigation System** that provides real-time tracking of surgical instruments. This system can pinpoint the exact location of an instrument within the patient's body, often displaying its position as an overlay on the medical images, enabling extremely precise, image-guided interventions.

During a typical image-guided procedure, these systems operate in concert. The patient is positioned on the table, anesthesia is administered, and the lights are adjusted. As the surgery begins, the patient's vitals are constantly monitored. The imaging system is used to take pictures, which are immediately sent to the display screens. Surgeons use these live images, along with the navigation system's instrument tracking, to guide their work. The entire process is managed to ensure patient safety, for instance by monitoring the total radiation exposure and responding to any critical changes in the patient's vital signs.
