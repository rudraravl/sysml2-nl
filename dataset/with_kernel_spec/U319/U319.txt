This model describes a mechanical nail clipper, a handheld tool designed to trim fingernails and toenails. It operates using a compound lever system to amplify the user's grip force, which is then applied to a pair of cutting blades.

The clipper is composed of several key physical parts: an upper and lower lever that form the handle, a moving blade, a fixed blade, and a central pivot pin that connects the levers. The lower lever is integrated with the moving blade, while the fixed blade is mounted to the clipper's main body.

The operational process begins when the clipper is in its default 'open' state, allowing a nail to be positioned between the blades. As the user applies a squeezing force to the levers, they pivot, causing the moving blade to converge with the fixed blade. Once the blades make contact, they enter a 'cutting' state, applying sufficient pressure to shear through the nail material. This action successfully severs the nail and produces a small nail fragment. When the user releases their grip, the clipper returns to the open position, ready for the next use.

The design is optimized for performance and safety, with requirements specifying it must be able to cut nails up to a certain thickness with a reasonable amount of user force, and that key components like the pivot pin are robust enough to prevent failure during operation.
