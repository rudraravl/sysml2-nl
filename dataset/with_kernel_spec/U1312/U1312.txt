This model describes the "Rotimatic," a fully automated countertop appliance designed to make fresh flatbreads like rotis, chapatis, or tortillas. The system handles the entire process from raw ingredients to cooked flatbreads without any manual intervention during its operation.

The machine is composed of several integrated components that work in a precise sequence. It includes separate dispensers for flour, water, and oil, which hold the bulk ingredients. When a user selects a recipe and quantity, the system's central controller orchestrates the process.

First, the dispensers accurately measure and release the specified amounts of flour, water, and oil into a mixing unit. This unit then combines the ingredients to form a rough dough. The dough is transferred to a kneading unit, which works it to achieve the correct consistency and elasticity.

Once the bulk dough is prepared, a divider mechanism portions it into a single, consistently sized dough ball. This ball is then sent to a rolling unit, which presses and flattens it into a thin, circular shape of a pre-set thickness. Finally, the flattened dough is moved onto an internal heated cooking plate, where it is cooked at a specific temperature for a set duration. The finished, hot flatbread is then ejected into an output tray for collection.

The entire process is cyclic; after one flatbread is made, the system automatically portions the next dough ball from the bulk dough and repeats the rolling and cooking steps until the entire batch is complete. The system controller manages the timing and coordination of all these mechanical parts, ensuring each flatbread is made with consistent quality in terms of size, thickness, and cooking level.
