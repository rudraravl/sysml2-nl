This model describes a complete Extra-Vehicular Activity (EVA) System, which is essentially a personal spacecraft for an astronaut, commonly known as a spacesuit or Extravehicular Mobility Unit (EMU). The system is designed to enable an astronaut to safely exit a spacecraft and perform work in the harsh environment of space.

The core of the system is the **Extravehicular Mobility Unit (EMU)**, which is a complex assembly of interconnected components. The astronaut's life is sustained by the **Primary Life Support Subsystem (PLSS)**, a backpack unit that provides breathable oxygen from a tank, removes exhaled carbon dioxide with a filter system, and supplies electrical power from a battery. It also includes a water-cooling system that circulates coolant to regulate the astronaut's body temperature.

The main body of the suit is the **Pressure Garment Assembly**, a multi-layered outfit designed to maintain a pressurized, oxygen-rich environment around the astronaut. It consists of an inner pressure layer to hold the atmosphere, a restraint layer to prevent the suit from ballooning, and an outer Thermal Micrometeoroid Garment to protect against temperature extremes and small space debris.

The astronaut's head is protected by the **Helmet and Visor Assembly**, which features a strong polycarbonate shell and a gold-coated visor to reflect intense solar radiation. A ventilation system circulates fresh oxygen into the helmet. Specialized **Extravehicular Gloves** provide manual dexterity while protecting the hands and include heaters for warmth.

For communication, the astronaut wears a **Communication Carrier Assembly** (like a "Snoopy cap") with a microphone and speaker connected to a radio transceiver. This allows for constant contact with the spacecraft and ground control. The astronaut monitors the suit's health and controls its functions, such as temperature, using a chest-mounted **Display and Control Module**, which shows vital information like oxygen and battery levels.

For safety, the astronaut is physically connected to the spacecraft via a **Safety Tether**. In the event of a tether failure, the suit is equipped with a **Simplified Aid For EVA Rescue (SAFER)**, a small nitrogen-propelled jetpack that allows the astronaut to fly back to safety.

The entire EVA process follows a series of distinct phases, starting with **prebreathing** pure oxygen to prevent decompression sickness, followed by **depressurization** of the airlock, exiting the spacecraft (**egress**), performing work (**operations**), and then returning (**ingress**) and **repressurizing** the airlock. The system is designed to operate in various states from standby preparation to active mission execution, with its overall status constantly monitored for nominal, degraded, or critical conditions.
