This model describes a BEAM Photovore Robot, a small, autonomous robot designed to seek out and move toward light sources. Its behavior is inspired by simple biological organisms, and it operates entirely on analog electronics without any digital processor or programming. The name "BEAM" stands for Biology, Electronics, Aesthetics, and Mechanics, reflecting its design philosophy.

The robot's core function is to convert light into motion. It is equipped with photoresistors that act as light sensors, and solar cells that harvest energy from that light. This harvested energy is not used immediately but is stored in a capacitor, which functions like a temporary battery.

An analog trigger circuit serves as the robot's simple "brain." It continuously monitors the amount of electrical charge building up in the capacitor. When the charge reaches a specific, predefined threshold, the trigger circuit activates. This causes the capacitor to suddenly discharge all its stored energy in a single, powerful pulse.

This burst of energy is sent to two DC motors, each driving a wheel. This results in the robot lurching forward for a brief moment. After the energy is spent, the capacitor is empty, the motors stop, and the robot becomes still again, beginning the cycle of passively collecting light energy once more. This charge-and-discharge cycle creates a characteristic jerky, step-by-step movement as the robot moves, pauses to recharge, and moves again.

By having separate light sensors and power generation for its left and right sides, the robot exhibits "photovore" (light-eating) behavior, naturally turning and moving toward the brightest light in its environment to maximize its energy collection.
