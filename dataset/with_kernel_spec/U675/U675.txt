This is an automated fish counting system designed for continuously monitoring fish migration in waterways like rivers or fish ladders. Its purpose is for ecological research and fishery management, providing accurate, real-time data on fish populations.

The system is composed of several interconnected electronic modules. An underwater **Optical Sensor** serves as the system's eye, detecting fish as they pass. It is sensitive enough to identify objects as small as five centimeters and can determine the fish's estimated size and direction of travel (upstream or downstream).

Data from the sensor is sent to a **Signal Processor**. This unit acts as the system's brain, analyzing the raw detection signal to distinguish actual fish from other objects like debris or water turbulence. This filtering ensures that the count is accurate and reliable.

Once a detection is validated as a fish, the information is passed to a **Counting Module**. This module maintains separate running totals for fish moving upstream and downstream, as well as a net count. It is designed to handle a high volume of traffic, processing up to 300 fish per minute.

The current counts are sent to a **Display Controller**, which provides a real-time, on-site readout of the upstream, downstream, and net totals. Simultaneously, a **Data Logger** records each count along with a timestamp. This logger has a large storage capacity, designed to hold over a year's worth of data, which can be exported for detailed analysis.

The entire system is powered by a single **Power Supply** that converts a standard electrical input into the low-voltage DC required by all the electronic components. The system is built to be environmentally robust, with waterproof sensor components, and can operate reliably in a wide range of outdoor temperatures.
