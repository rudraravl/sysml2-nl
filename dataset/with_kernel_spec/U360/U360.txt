This model describes a high-precision positioning system designed to move a physical contact probe to exact coordinates in three-dimensional space. It is the kind of system you would find in an automated quality control machine, a coordinate measuring machine (CMM), or a scientific instrument used for detailed surface inspection.

The system's core is an assembly of three linear actuators, responsible for moving the probe along the X, Y, and Z axes within a defined workspace of 500mm x 500mm x 300mm. At the end of this mechanical assembly is the probe itself, a small, physical device with a 1mm tip used to make contact with the object being measured.

The entire operation is managed by a central Motion Controller. This controller is the brains of the system, receiving external commands for a target position. To achieve this position with extreme accuracy, it operates in a closed-loop fashion. A high-resolution Position Sensor constantly tracks the probe's actual location and sends this feedback to the controller.

The process works as follows: the controller compares the target position with the actual position reported by the sensor. If there is a difference, it calculates the necessary correction and sends precise drive signals to the X, Y, and Z actuators. The actuators move the probe, the sensor reports the new position, and the controller repeats the calculation. This loop continues very rapidly until the sensor confirms the probe has reached the target coordinate within a specified tolerance of one-thousandth of a millimeter (1 micrometer).

A dedicated power supply provides the necessary electrical energy to the motion controller, the actuator assembly, and the position sensor, completing the integrated system.
