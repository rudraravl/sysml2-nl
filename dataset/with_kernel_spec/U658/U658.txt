This is an Electrochemical Fatigue Crack Sensing System, a specialized piece of equipment designed for the non-destructive testing and health monitoring of structural components, such as metal parts in aircraft or industrial machinery. Its primary function is to detect, measure, and track the growth of fatigue cracks on the surface of these components.

The core of the system is a sensing unit that operates by placing an array of small platinum electrodes onto the surface of the material being inspected. An electrolyte fluid is used to create a conductive path between these electrodes and the component's surface. A measurement circuit then applies a small electrical voltage and measures the resulting electrical resistance across this path.

The presence of a fatigue crack disrupts this electrical path, causing a measurable change in resistance. The system continuously compares the current resistance reading to a known baseline value to detect these changes. The sensor's array of multiple, precisely spaced electrodes allows the system not just to detect a crack, but also to determine its physical characteristics.

The complete system consists of several interconnected modules working together. The raw, low-level electrical signal from the sensor is first sent to a signal conditioner, which amplifies it and filters out any electrical noise. This cleaned-up analog signal is then converted into digital data by a high-speed data acquisition module. Finally, a dedicated crack analysis processor uses this digital data, along with calibration factors, to calculate the precise depth, length, and growth rate of the crack. The entire unit is powered by a regulated power supply.

The system follows a defined operational cycle. It starts in an idle state, moves to an initializing state for calibration and self-testing upon activation, and then enters a measuring state to perform the actual crack analysis. After a measurement is complete, it reports the data before returning to an idle state, ready for the next command. The system also includes fault detection and shutdown procedures for safe and reliable operation.
