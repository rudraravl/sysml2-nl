This model describes an automated fluid transfer subsystem designed to move a liquid from a source tank to a destination tank.

The heart of the system is a pistonless pump, which uses a motor-driven, high-speed rotating impeller housed within a casing (volute) to pressurize and move the fluid. This pump is a complete mechanical assembly, including the motor, the shaft connecting it to the impeller, and bearings to ensure smooth rotation.

The overall subsystem is a closed-loop, monitored, and controlled process. Fluid is drawn from a source storage tank and enters the pump. After being pressurized by the pump, the fluid flows out through a series of components: first past a pressure sensor (transducer), then a flow rate sensor (transducer), and finally through a control valve before being deposited into the destination tank.

The system's operation is governed by an automated control sequence. It continuously monitors the discharge pressure using the sensor. If the pressure deviates from a specified operating range, the system automatically adjusts the opening of the control valve to bring the pressure back to the target level.

Furthermore, the pump itself has a sophisticated lifecycle with several distinct operating states. It progresses from being `off` through a `startup` phase (which includes priming and acceleration), into its main `operational` state, and finally through a controlled `shutdown`. Even during normal operation, it can intelligently switch between `normalFlow` and `reducedFlow` states in response to pressure changes, ensuring safe and efficient performance. The design also allows for different pump configurations, such as a high-capacity variant with a more powerful motor and larger components, to be used within the same subsystem architecture for different performance requirements.
