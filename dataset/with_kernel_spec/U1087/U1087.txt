This model describes a comprehensive Horizon Scanning System, an automated surveillance and early-warning platform designed for continuous environmental monitoring. Its primary purpose is to detect, track, and classify objects across a wide area—such as on the sea or in the air—to provide situational awareness and alert operators to potential threats.

The system is built around a suite of three complementary sensors: a long-range Radar capable of detecting objects up to 75 kilometers away, a high-resolution Electro-Optical (visual) sensor, and a sensitive Infrared (thermal) sensor. These sensors are not independent; they are orchestrated by a Scan Coordinator, which directs them to systematically sweep across designated sectors of the horizon in a continuous cycle.

Data from all three sensors is fed into a central Fusion Processor. This component acts as the brain of the system, intelligently combining the inputs to create a single, unified operational picture. By fusing data, the system can confirm detections with higher confidence, reduce false alarms, and gain a more complete understanding of a target than any single sensor could provide.

Once an object is confidently detected, the system attempts to classify it by comparing its characteristics against a regularly updated Threat Database of known signatures. It then evaluates the potential threat level of the classified object.

All of this information is presented to a human user at an Operator Console. This console displays the location and status of all tracked targets. If the system identifies a high-priority threat, it automatically issues an alert to the console, drawing the operator's immediate attention for a tactical response. The entire process, from initial sensor detection to threat evaluation, is designed to be extremely fast, completing in under a second to ensure timely warnings. The system operates in various states, including standby, an initialization and calibration phase, and the primary active scanning mode.
