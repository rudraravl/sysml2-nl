This model describes a complete clinical system and procedure for gastric intubation, which involves placing a flexible tube through a patient's nose, down the esophagus, and into the stomach. The system is designed to provide temporary access to the stomach for therapeutic purposes such as removing gastric contents (decompression), administering liquid nutrition (enteral feeding), or delivering medication.

The primary components of this system include:

*   A **Nasogastric Tube**: This is a flexible, medical-grade tube of a specific size and length, designed to be passed through the nasal passage. It has a port at its external end for connecting to other devices and openings at the internal end for delivering or removing substances.
*   A **Suction Apparatus**: This is a medical device that connects to the nasogastric tube to apply controlled, often intermittent, negative pressure. This is used to drain fluid and gas from the stomach, relieving pressure and distention.
*   A **Feeding Pump**: This is an automated pump that delivers liquid nutritional formulas or water to the patient at a precisely controlled rate through the tube.
*   **Verification Equipment**: These are tools, such as those for testing the pH of aspirated stomach fluid, used by the clinician to confirm that the tube has been placed correctly in the stomach and not in the airway.

The system is operated by a qualified **Clinician** on a **Patient**. The entire process follows a strict clinical workflow. It begins with the clinician assessing the patient's condition to ensure they are a suitable candidate. The clinician then selects the correct tube size, measures the required insertion depth, and prepares the tube for insertion.

During the procedure, the tube is carefully advanced through the patient's nose to the pre-measured depth. A critical step follows where the placement is rigorously verified. If the tube is not in the correct location, it must be repositioned. Once correct placement is confirmed, the tube is secured externally to the patient's nose or cheek to prevent it from moving.

After successful placement, the tube's status transitions to "in use." Depending on the medical need, it can be connected to the suction apparatus for decompression or the feeding pump for nutrition. The system requires ongoing monitoring; the tube must be periodically flushed to ensure it remains clear and unblocked (patent). If a blockage occurs, the clinician must attempt to clear it. The procedure concludes when the tube is no longer needed and is carefully removed.
