This is a model of an industrial safety system designed to detect and monitor combustible gases. The system is based on pellistor sensor technology, which is used to prevent explosions in environments where flammable gases might be present.

The core of the system is a Pellistor sensor. This sensor operates by using a pair of tiny, matched ceramic beads. One bead, the "catalytic bead," is coated with a platinum catalyst. The other, the "compensation bead," is inactive. Both beads are heated to a high operating temperature by passing an electric current through them.

In the presence of a combustible gas, the gas will burn (oxidize) on the hot surface of the catalytic bead. This reaction releases heat, causing the bead's temperature and, consequently, its electrical resistance to increase. The compensation bead, being inactive, is unaffected by the gas but will change its resistance in response to ambient temperature or humidity. This clever design allows the system to compensate for environmental changes and isolate the effect of the gas itself.

These two beads form two legs of an electrical circuit known as a Wheatstone bridge. When no combustible gas is present, the bridge is balanced. However, when the catalytic bead's resistance increases due to gas oxidation, the bridge becomes unbalanced, producing a small voltage signal. The magnitude of this voltage is directly proportional to the concentration of the combustible gas.

This raw voltage signal from the sensor is then passed to a signal conditioner, which amplifies and filters it to create a more stable and usable signal. A concentration calculator then processes this conditioned signal, using pre-set calibration values to convert the voltage into a precise gas concentration reading, typically expressed as a percentage of the Lower Explosive Limit (%LEL).

Finally, an alarm controller continuously monitors this concentration reading. The controller is programmed with multiple safety thresholds, such as a "warning" level and a more critical "alarm" level. If the gas concentration rises above these thresholds, the controller activates an external alarm, alerting personnel to the potentially hazardous conditions. The entire system is powered by a dedicated power module.
