This is a system for rock climbing, encompassing the climber, the climbing environment, and the essential safety equipment needed for a vertical ascent. It models the physical interactions, energy management, and safety protocols involved in climbing a set route.

The system's core components include:

*   **The Climber:** The human athlete, defined by physical characteristics such as body mass, maximum grip strength, reach, and current stamina level. The climber physically interacts with the wall by applying force to holds and is connected to a safety system that manages their weight and provides fall protection.
*   **The Climbing Wall:** The physical environment for the ascent. It is a vertical or overhanging surface with a specific height, angle, and surface friction. The wall is set with predefined routes, which are made up of a series of individual holds, each with a specific location, quality, and maximum load capacity.
*   **The Safety System:** This is a set of interconnected equipment designed to protect the climber from falls. It consists of:
    *   **Harness:** A wearable device that distributes the climber's weight and provides a secure attachment point for the rope.
    *   **Safety Rope:** A dynamic rope that connects the climber's harness to a belay system. It has specific properties like length, diameter, and maximum tensile strength, and is designed to stretch to absorb the energy of a fall.
    *   **Belay System:** A friction device managed by a belayer on the ground. It controls the rope, allowing it to be paid out as the climber ascends and locked off to arrest a fall.

The system operates through a sequence of actions that simulate a real climb. The climber repeatedly evaluates the next available hold based on its position, their reach, and their remaining stamina. After selecting a target hold, they attempt to secure a stable grip and then execute the physical move, which consumes energy and gains height.

Throughout the ascent, a safety monitoring process is active, continuously checking rope tension, the climber's stamina, and grip stability. If conditions become unsafe (e.g., stamina is critically low or rope tension is too high), the climb is aborted. The climber's overall progress is tracked through different states, such as preparation, active climbing, resting on the wall, and descending, with an emergency state for unexpected events.
