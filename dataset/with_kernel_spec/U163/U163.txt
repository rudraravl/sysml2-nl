This model describes a Biliblanket Phototherapy System, a medical device designed for treating jaundice in newborn infants. The system's primary purpose is to safely and effectively reduce high bilirubin levels in a baby's blood by exposing their skin to a specific type of blue-green light.

The system is composed of several key components that work together. A central **Control Module** acts as the brain, allowing a user to set treatment parameters like intensity and duration. This module is powered by a dedicated **Power Supply** that converts standard wall outlet voltage into low-voltage DC power for the system's electronics.

The therapeutic light is generated by a high-intensity **LED Light Engine**. This engine is specifically designed to produce light at a precise blue-green wavelength (peaking around 475 nm), which is most effective for breaking down bilirubin. The light from this engine is channeled into a flexible **Fiber Optic Pad**—the "biliblanket" itself. This soft, woven pad distributes the light evenly over its surface, allowing it to be wrapped comfortably around the infant to maximize skin exposure.

To ensure patient safety, a **Temperature Sensor** is integrated with the fiber optic pad. It continuously monitors the pad's surface temperature and sends this data back to the Control Module. If the temperature exceeds a safe limit (38°C), the system will automatically shut down the light and trigger an alarm.

In operation, the system has several states. It remains in a **Standby** mode until a treatment is initiated. Once activated, it enters an **Active** state, delivering a continuous, controlled dose of light. During treatment, it constantly monitors for safety issues like overheating. The therapy concludes and the system returns to standby either when the pre-set timer expires or if it is manually stopped.
