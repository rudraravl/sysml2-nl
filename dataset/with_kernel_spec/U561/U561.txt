This model describes an industrial fluid transfer system designed to move a liquid from a source reservoir to a process vessel using a specialized pump. The system's key feature is its ability to generate a precisely controlled, pulsating or wave-like (sinusoidal) flow, rather than a constant, steady stream.

The core of the system is the sinusoidal pump, a type of positive displacement pump. It consists of a uniquely shaped, two-lobed sinusoidal rotor that rotates within a stationary housing called a stator. As the electric motor drives the rotor, the interaction between the rotor's wave-like geometry and the stator creates sealed cavities. These cavities progress from the pump's inlet to its outlet, gently carrying the fluid with them and producing the characteristic pulsating flow. The pump also includes an internal sealing system to maintain its integrity and efficiency.

The pump is powered by a dedicated electric motor, which provides the necessary torque and rotational speed. The entire assembly draws fluid from a source reservoir, which is a storage tank that holds a supply of the liquid. After passing through the pump, the fluid is delivered to a process vessel, which is a container designed to hold the fluid under a specific operating pressure for further industrial processing.

The system's operation is managed through a series of states, including stopped, starting, running, and stopping. During its running state, the pump can operate in different modes, such as a normal operation mode and a high-pressure mode, allowing it to adapt to changing system demands. The system is engineered to meet specific performance requirements, ensuring it maintains the desired sinusoidal flow pattern, stays within pressure limits, and operates with high efficiency.
