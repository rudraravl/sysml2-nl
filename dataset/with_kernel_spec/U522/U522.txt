This model describes an industrial impeller pump system designed to increase the pressure and flow rate of a fluid. It is a complete electromechanical assembly built for continuous, reliable operation.

The system is composed of four primary physical components working in sequence: a motor, a shaft, an impeller, and a casing.

1.  **Motor:** An electric motor serves as the power source. It receives electrical power and a control command, converting this energy into rotational motion. This particular motor is rated for 5 kilowatts and operates at 92% efficiency.

2.  **Shaft:** A solid, 30 cm long stainless steel shaft connects the motor to the impeller. Its function is to transmit the torque and rotational speed from the motor to the impeller, effectively driving the core pumping mechanism.

3.  **Impeller:** This is the heart of the pump. It is a rotating component with six precisely angled blades. As the shaft spins the impeller, the blades impart kinetic energy to the fluid, accelerating it outwards through centrifugal force.

4.  **Casing:** The casing is the stationary housing that encloses the impeller and directs the liquid. It channels the incoming low-pressure fluid into the center of the spinning impeller. As the fluid is thrown outward by the impeller, the casing's shape is designed to slow the fluid down, converting its high velocity into high pressure before directing it out of the system's discharge port.

The overall operation is a continuous process. Fluid enters the system through an inlet port, flows through the casing to the impeller where it is accelerated, and is then discharged from the casing's outlet port at a higher pressure and flow rate.

The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, ensuring it can deliver a target flow rate and pressure rise without exceeding a maximum power consumption limit. It is also engineered for high reliability, with a requirement to operate continuously for a minimum of 10,000 hours without failure.
