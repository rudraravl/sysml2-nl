This model represents an Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO) system, a sophisticated medical device that acts as an artificial lung for patients suffering from severe respiratory failure. The system's primary function is to circulate a patient's blood outside the body, remove carbon dioxide, enrich it with oxygen, and then return the treated blood to the patient.

The core of the system is a **Hollow Fiber Membrane**, which is where the crucial gas exchange takes place. Deoxygenated (venous) blood flows on one side of the membrane, while a controlled supply of fresh oxygen flows on the other. The membrane's properties allow oxygen to diffuse into the blood and carbon dioxide to diffuse out, which is then vented as waste gas.

Blood is moved through this external circuit by a **Blood Pump**, which draws blood from the patient and propels it through the system at a precisely controlled flow rate. Before reaching the membrane, the blood passes through a **Heat Exchanger** to regulate its temperature, ensuring it is returned to the patient at a safe, normal body temperature.

The entire operation is managed by a central **Control Module**. This module is the brain of the system, constantly receiving data from a suite of sensors that monitor the blood's oxygen saturation, pressure, temperature, and flow rate. Based on these readings, the controller makes real-time adjustments, such as changing the pump speed or modifying the oxygen flow via a **Gas Flow Regulator**, to meet specific therapeutic targets.

For patient safety, the system is equipped with several protective features, including a **Safety Valve** designed to release pressure if it exceeds a critical threshold. The control module also triggers alarms for conditions like low oxygen saturation, high pressure, or temperature anomalies.

The system operates through several distinct states. It starts in **Standby** and must go through a **Priming** phase, where the circuit is filled with a saline solution to remove air. Once primed and ready, it enters the **Operating** state to actively process blood. The system can be stopped normally or shut down immediately in an emergency.
