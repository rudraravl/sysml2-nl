This is a laboratory or medical incubator system designed to create and maintain a precisely controlled environment for biological specimens, cell cultures, or neonatal care. Its primary function is to regulate temperature, humidity, and carbon dioxide (CO2) levels within a sealed, enclosed chamber.

The system is built around a central **Chamber** which houses the sensitive materials. To manage the internal environment, the incubator is equipped with several key components: a **Heating Element** to raise the temperature, a **Humidification System** to add moisture to the air, and a **CO2 Injection System** to maintain the required gas concentration.

A sophisticated **Control Unit** acts as the brain of the incubator. It constantly receives information from a suite of sensors inside the chamber, including a **Temperature Sensor**, a **Humidity Sensor**, a **CO2 Sensor**, and a **Door Sensor**. A user sets the desired environmental targets (e.g., 37°C, 95% humidity, 5% CO2) through a **User Interface**. The Control Unit then compares the real-time sensor readings to these targets and automatically sends control signals to activate the heater, humidifier, or CO2 injector as needed to maintain a stable environment.

The system includes an integrated **Alarm System** that activates if any of the environmental parameters drift too far from the set points, alerting an operator to a potential problem. For safety, the Door Sensor ensures that the heating, humidification, and CO2 systems are disabled whenever the chamber door is opened.

The incubator operates through several states. After being powered on, it enters a **"Startup"** mode to reach the target conditions. Once stable, it transitions to a **"Maintaining"** state. If the door is opened, it switches to a safe **"Door Open"** mode. If conditions deviate significantly from their targets, it enters an **"Alarm"** state.
