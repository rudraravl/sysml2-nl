This model describes an automated Defect Detector System, a type of industrial quality control system used in manufacturing. Its primary purpose is to automatically inspect products as they move along a production line, identify any physical flaws or defects, and then report on its findings to ensure consistent product quality.

The system is composed of four main subsystems that work together in a sequence:

1.  **Imaging Subsystem:** This is the "eye" of the system. It is responsible for capturing high-resolution digital images of each product under inspection. It can actively adjust its lighting to ensure the best possible image quality for analysis and operates at a specific frame rate to keep up with the production line's speed.

2.  **Processing Subsystem:** This is the "brain." It receives the images from the imaging subsystem and uses sophisticated algorithms to analyze them. Its job is to first preprocess the image (e.g., improve contrast, reduce noise) and then run a detection process to locate any defects. For each defect it finds, it determines its type, physical position, and severity, and assigns a confidence score to its finding.

3.  **Reporting Subsystem:** This subsystem acts as the record-keeper. It takes the list of defects identified by the processing unit and compiles them into a comprehensive inspection report. This report includes a unique ID, a full list of all detected defects, a total defect count, and a final pass/fail status for the product. The subsystem determines this status by comparing the number of defects to a predefined acceptable limit.

4.  **Alert Subsystem:** This is the system's alarm mechanism. It monitors the final inspection reports. If a report indicates a product has failed inspection or contains a defect with a severity level above a critical threshold, this subsystem issues an alert. This would likely notify a human operator to intervene, remove the faulty product, or investigate a potential production issue.

In operation, a product is imaged, the image is processed for defects, a report is generated, and an alert is triggered if necessary. The entire system is engineered for high performance, with requirements for very high detection accuracy (98%), speed (completing an inspection in under 1.5 seconds), a low rate of false alarms, and high operational reliability (99.5% uptime) to ensure it can function effectively in a demanding manufacturing environment.
