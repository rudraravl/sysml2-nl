This model describes the MERCI Retriever System, a medical device setup used for mechanical thrombectomy. It is designed to physically remove blood clots from the arteries in the brain of a patient experiencing an acute ischemic stroke. The primary goal of the system is to restore blood flow to the affected brain tissue by extracting the blockage.

The system is composed of several coordinated components. A large **Guide Catheter** is first inserted into the patient's vascular system to create a stable pathway from the access point (typically the groin) up to the vessels in the neck. A much smaller and more flexible **Microcatheter** is then threaded through the guide catheter and carefully navigated through the brain's winding arteries to the precise location of the clot. The entire procedure is monitored using a **Fluoroscopy System**, which provides real-time X-ray imaging, allowing the surgeon to see the instruments inside the patient's body.

The key component is the **MERCI Retriever Device**, which features a set of corkscrew-like helical loops made of a thin filament. This device is advanced through the microcatheter to the clot. Once in position, it is deployed, causing it to expand to the diameter of the blood vessel and entangle the thrombus.

The clot removal process follows a specific sequence. After navigating to the blockage, the retriever is deployed and allowed to sit for a set period (a dwell time) to fully engage with the clot. Then, the retriever and the captured clot are slowly withdrawn. During this withdrawal, an **Aspiration Pump** is activated, creating suction at the catheter tip to capture any fragments of the clot that might break off, preventing them from causing further blockages downstream. After an attempt, the surgeon assesses the success by checking if blood flow has been restored. If the blockage isn't cleared, the process can be repeated a limited number of times until successful reperfusion is achieved.
