This is a Nanoneedle Delivery System, a highly precise robotic instrument designed to inject therapeutic materials directly into individual biological cells. The system's primary goal is to perform this delicate task with nanometer-scale accuracy while being minimally invasive, ensuring that the target cells remain alive and viable after the procedure.

The system is composed of several key components that work in concert. At its core is an ultra-fine **Nanoneedle Tip**, which is sharper and smaller than 100 nanometers in diameter. This needle is mounted on a **Piezoelectric Positioner**, a high-resolution actuator that can move the needle in three dimensions with incredible precision, achieving accuracies better than 100 nanometers.

To deliver the substance, a **Microfluidic Injector** is connected to the nanoneedle. This injector can pump extremely small, controlled volumes of a therapeutic payload, typically in the range of picoliters (trillionths of a liter). An integrated **Imaging System**, such as a high-magnification microscope, provides the "eyes" for the system, allowing it to locate and target specific cells.

The entire process is automated and follows a precise sequence. First, the imaging system identifies a target cell. The piezoelectric positioner then carefully approaches the cell. The nanoneedle tip is equipped with sensors that can detect the subtle changes in force and electrical impedance that occur upon touching the cell's membrane. A **Feedback Controller** uses this real-time sensor data to manage the operation.

Once contact is confirmed, the positioner gently pushes the needle through the cell membrane to a specified depth. The microfluidic injector then delivers the payload, after which the positioner carefully withdraws the needle. This closed-loop control is critical for preventing damage to the cell and ensuring the injection is successful.
