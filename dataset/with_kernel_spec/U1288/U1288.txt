This model describes a GreenPeak wireless mesh networking system, designed for ultra-low-power communication in smart home, building automation, and other Internet of Things (IoT) applications. The system enables distributed environmental monitoring over a large area using battery-powered devices.

The network is composed of multiple autonomous wireless sensor nodes. Each node is a self-contained unit built from four main components: a long-life **Battery Pack** that powers the device, an **Environmental Sensor** that measures ambient temperature and humidity while also monitoring its own battery level, a **Microcontroller** that serves as the brain managing all operations, and an **RF Transceiver** that sends and receives data wirelessly using the IEEE 802.15.4 communication standard.

To achieve multi-year battery life, each node operates in a power-saving cycle. It remains in a deep "sleep" mode for most of the time. Periodically, it wakes up to perform its tasks: it takes a reading from its sensor, packages the data into a wireless frame with its address, and transmits it. After sending its data, it may listen briefly for incoming messages before returning to its sleep state to conserve energy.

These individual nodes work together to form a robust and self-healing mesh network. Within the network, nodes can have different roles: a central **coordinator** node establishes and manages the network, **router** nodes relay messages from other devices to extend the network's range, and **end devices** primarily focus on sensing and sending their own data. This multi-hop structure allows a message from a distant sensor to hop through one or more routers to reach its final destination, ensuring reliable communication across a large residential or commercial space.
