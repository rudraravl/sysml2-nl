This model represents the complete business and operational system of a flat-pack furniture retailer, like IKEA. It covers the entire lifecycle of a furniture product, from initial design to its final assembly in a customer's home.

The system's core product is furniture that begins as a collection of individual parts. These parts are designed and manufactured, then packaged into a compact, easy-to-transport flat-pack box. Each box includes all necessary components and a set of assembly instructions.

The overall operation is broken down into several distinct functional areas that work together:

*   A **Design Studio** is responsible for creating new furniture concepts based on customer feedback and cost targets.
*   A **Production Facility** then manufactures the individual furniture parts and packages them into the final flat-packs.
*   These packages are shipped to a **Distribution Warehouse**, which manages the inventory, tracks stock levels, and handles replenishment requests.
*   In the **Retail Showroom**, assembled versions of the furniture are displayed to allow customers to see and interact with the final products.
*   When a customer makes a purchase at the **Checkout Counter**, the order is processed, and the system retrieves the corresponding flat-pack(s) from the warehouse for the customer to take home.
*   A **Customer Service Desk** is available to provide support, such as helping with assembly instructions.

Finally, the **Customer** plays a crucial role in the system by taking the flat-pack home and performing the final assembly themselves.

The entire system is guided by several key principles: affordability for mass-market appeal, efficiency through the flat-pack model to reduce shipping and storage costs, and a reliance on customer self-assembly using clear, simple instructions. It also emphasizes good design quality and environmental sustainability through the use of renewable and recyclable materials.
