This model describes a historical water-raising machine, known as a saqiya, based on the 9th-century Islamic engineering innovation of the crank-and-connecting-rod mechanism. Its primary purpose is to lift water efficiently from a low-level source, like a river, to an elevated aqueduct for irrigation.

The system is powered by an animal, such as an ox or donkey, which walks in a continuous circle to turn a large, horizontal wooden wheel. This wheel's steady rotation drives a crankshaft. The core of the machine is the mechanism that links this rotating crankshaft to a piston assembly via a connecting rod. This innovative linkage converts the continuous rotary motion of the wheel into a reciprocating, up-and-down linear motion for the piston.

As the piston moves up and down within its cylinder, it actively pumps water. On one stroke, it draws water in from the source river, and on the next, it pushes the water out into the elevated aqueduct. The system operates through several states: it can be idle, start up as the animal begins walking, run in a continuous pumping state, and be stopped or taken offline for maintenance. This design represents a significant early example of sophisticated mechanical engineering used for practical agricultural purposes.
