This model describes an automated water well pumping system, designed to extract water from an underground well and deliver it on-demand to a user, such as a home or irrigation system. It's a complete, self-regulating subsystem that manages water flow and pressure.

The core of the system is a **Well Pump**, which is submerged in a **Well Casing** deep underground. When activated, the pump draws water from the well and pushes it to the surface. To prevent water from flowing back down into the well when the pump is off, a one-way **Check Valve** is installed on the pump's suction line.

Once at the surface, the water is pushed through another check valve and into a **Pressure Tank**. This tank is a critical component that stores a reserve of water under pressure, using a bladder of compressed air. This storage system prevents the pump from needing to turn on and off every single time a tap is opened, which greatly extends the pump's lifespan and provides more consistent water pressure.

The entire operation is automated by a **Control Panel** that works in conjunction with a **Pressure Switch**. The pressure switch constantly monitors the water pressure inside the pressure tank. When water is used and the pressure drops to a pre-set low point (the "cut-in" pressure), the switch signals the control panel to turn the pump on. The pump then runs, refilling the tank until the pressure reaches a pre-set high point (the "cut-out" pressure), at which point the switch signals the control panel to shut the pump off. The control panel also provides a manual override for direct control.

For monitoring and safety, the system includes a **Flow Meter** to measure the rate and total volume of water being pumped. The pump itself has built-in protection; it operates through different states including idle, priming, and running. If it detects an issue, such as drawing too much electrical current, it will automatically enter a fault state to prevent damage and await a manual reset.
