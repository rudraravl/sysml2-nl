This describes a Negative-Pressure Wound Therapy (NPWT) system, a medical device designed to accelerate the healing of serious wounds. The system works by applying a controlled, continuous vacuum to the wound site, which helps remove excess fluid (exudate), reduce swelling, and promote the growth of new tissue.

The core of the system consists of several interconnected components. A specialized wound dressing is applied over the wound to create an airtight seal. This dressing is connected via tubing to a disposable exudate canister, which safely collects and stores the fluid drawn from the wound. A central vacuum pump is the engine of the system, generating the negative pressure that pulls air and fluid from the dressing and through the canister.

A sophisticated control module acts as the system's brain. It uses a pressure sensor to constantly monitor the vacuum level at the wound site. Based on this feedback, it automatically activates or deactivates the pump to maintain the precise therapeutic pressure prescribed by a clinician.

The system is designed with safety and reliability in mind. It continuously checks the level of fluid in the collection canister and will trigger an audible alarm unit to alert a caregiver when the canister is nearly full and requires replacement. The control module can also detect air leaks, for instance, if the dressing's seal is compromised. If it detects a leak that prevents the target pressure from being maintained, it will activate an alarm.

The device operates through several distinct states, including an initialization phase upon startup, an active therapy state where it runs continuously, a paused state for addressing alarms, and a final shutdown state at the end of treatment. Overall, this is a self-regulating medical system built for continuous operation over several days, providing a safe and effective healing environment with intelligent monitoring and alerts.
