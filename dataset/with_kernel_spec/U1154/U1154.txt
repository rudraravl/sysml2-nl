This model describes a sophisticated supervisory control system designed to manage a highly automated process while ensuring a human operator can effectively intervene when needed. It is specifically built to address the "out-of-the-loop" problem, where an operator's situational awareness and performance degrade after long periods of passively monitoring a reliable automated system.

The system consists of four main parts working together: a Human Operator, an Automation Controller, the actual Controlled Process, and a unique Situational Awareness Monitor.

In normal operation, the **Automation Controller** autonomously manages the **Controlled Process**—which could be anything from a vehicle's navigation to an industrial plant's operations. The **Human Operator**'s role is to supervise. However, the system recognizes that during long, uneventful periods of automation, the operator's attention can drift. They can transition from being actively engaged to passively monitoring, and eventually become completely disengaged and "out of the loop."

This is where the **Situational Awareness Monitor** comes in. It actively assesses the operator's level of engagement. If it detects that the operator's awareness has dropped below a safe threshold, it will proactively generate an alert or prompt, not because of a system failure, but to intentionally re-engage the operator and bring them back into the loop.

If the Automation Controller detects a genuine anomaly or failure in the Controlled Process, it will issue a critical alert and attempt to hand control over to the Human Operator. The system is designed with the understanding that an operator who has become disengaged will have a significantly slower reaction time. The overall goal is to use the Situational Awareness Monitor to prevent deep disengagement, ensuring that when a real crisis occurs, the operator can take manual control more quickly and effectively to ensure the system's safety and stability.
