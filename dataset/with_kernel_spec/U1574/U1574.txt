This model describes a complete Touch Sensing Unit, a sophisticated electronic module designed to detect and precisely measure physical contact and force. It is a self-contained system that processes touch inputs and provides clean, classified data.

The unit is built around a central Touch Sensor which uses a dual-method approach. It contains a capacitive element to detect the initial presence of a touch and a force transducer to measure the exact amount of pressure being applied. This sensor is capable of operating in different modes, switching between an idle state and an active state where it continuously takes measurements.

Raw data from the sensor is immediately processed by a Signal Conditioner. This component is crucial for ensuring data quality; it filters out electrical noise, amplifies the weak sensor signal to a more usable level, and applies calibration adjustments to maintain accuracy.

The refined signal is then passed to a Data Acquisition Unit. This unit's job is to digitize the measurement, add a precise timestamp, and store the result in a buffer. The overall system operates by having the sensor, conditioner, and data acquisition unit work in parallel to produce a high-speed, continuous stream of information.

The final output is a complete data packet containing the measured force, the capacitance reading, a timestamp, and a useful classification of the touch intensity, categorized as 'no contact', 'light touch', 'firm touch', or 'heavy touch' based on predefined force thresholds. The system is designed to measure forces up to 10 Newtons with high accuracy (±2%) and deliver measurements at a rate of at least 50 times per second.
