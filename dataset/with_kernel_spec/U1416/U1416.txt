This model describes a complete hydraulic fracturing system, commonly known as a "fracking" system, designed to extract natural gas from deep underground shale formations. Its primary function is to create and prop open fractures in rock layers to release trapped gas, which is then brought to the surface for collection.

The system is composed of several key surface and subsurface components that work in a coordinated sequence. On the surface, there are storage facilities for the primary ingredients: a **Water Source**, a **Proppant Storage** unit (for holding materials like sand), and a **Chemical Storage** unit for various additives. These raw materials are fed into a **Blending Unit**, which mixes them to create a specialized fracturing fluid.

This fluid is then sent to a series of powerful **High-Pressure Pumps**, which are the core of the operation. These pumps increase the fluid's pressure to extremely high levels, sufficient to fracture rock thousands of feet below ground. The pressurized fluid is directed into the **Wellhead Assembly**, which is the control and safety interface at the top of the well.

From the wellhead, the fluid travels down the **Wellbore Assembly**, a long, steel-cased shaft drilled deep into the earth. At the target depth, the fluid exits through perforations in the casing and fractures the surrounding shale formation. The proppant in the fluid then lodges in these new cracks to hold them open.

After the injection phase, pressure is released, and a mixture of natural gas and "produced water" flows back up the well. This mixture is processed by a **Separation Facility** which separates the valuable natural gas from the water and other contaminants.

Throughout the entire operation, a comprehensive **Monitoring System** uses an array of pressure sensors, flow meters, and seismic monitors to track subsurface conditions and equipment performance in real-time. This ensures the operation is conducted safely, efficiently, and within operational limits. The system follows distinct operational phases, moving from an idle state through mixing, pressurizing, injecting, soaking, flowback, and finally to long-term gas production.
