This model describes an automated household dishwasher designed to wash, rinse, and dry dirty dishes. The system is comprised of several key components that work together under the direction of a central control module to execute a complete cleaning cycle.

The main body of the dishwasher is the **Wash Chamber**, which houses the dishes and contains upper and lower **Spray Arms**. These arms rotate and spray pressurized, heated water to provide the mechanical cleaning action. A **Filter Assembly** within the chamber traps food particles to prevent them from being redeposited on the dishes.

The dishwasher's operation relies on a sophisticated water management system. A **Water Inlet Valve** controls the flow of fresh water into the chamber. A **Heating Element** then raises the water to specific temperatures required for washing and sanitizing. A powerful **Circulation Pump** drives the hot water through the spray arms, while a separate **Drain Pump** expels the used, dirty water at the end of each phase. A **Detergent Dispenser** is also included, designed to release a detergent pod at the correct time during the main wash phase.

Sensors provide crucial feedback to the control system, including a **Temperature Sensor** to monitor water heat and a **Water Level Sensor** to ensure the chamber fills to the correct volume. For safety, a **Door Latch** system confirms the door is closed and locks it during the cycle to prevent leaks and interruption.

The entire process is managed by a **Control Module**, which interprets user selections from a control panel (like choosing a wash cycle and starting the machine) and orchestrates the sequence of operations. A typical dishwashing cycle proceeds through distinct states:
1.  **Pre-wash:** A preliminary spray with unheated water to rinse off loose debris, which is then drained.
2.  **Main Wash:** The chamber fills, the water is heated, detergent is released, and the dishes are washed with hot, soapy water for an extended period before being drained.
3.  **Rinse:** The system fills with fresh water, heats it to a high temperature for sanitization, and rinses off all remaining detergent and soil.
4.  **Drying:** The final phase uses residual heat or a dedicated heating function to dry the dishes.

The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, including high cleaning efficiency (removing at least 98% of soil), water conservation, achieving a minimum sanitizing rinse temperature, and ensuring the door remains safely locked throughout all wet phases of the cycle.
