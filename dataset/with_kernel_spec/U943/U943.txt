This model describes a self-contained, remote Water Sensing Unit designed for environmental monitoring of bodies of water like rivers or reservoirs. The unit's primary function is to measure, process, and wirelessly transmit data on several key water parameters, including water level, flow rate, temperature, and a comprehensive quality index covering turbidity, pH, and conductivity.

The system is composed of four main internal components that work in sequence. A multi-parameter Water Sensor takes the initial raw readings from the water. This data is then passed to a Signal Processor which filters and refines the information—for example, by using a Kalman filter to improve accuracy. Once processed, a long-range Data Transmitter sends the final data package using a low-power protocol like LoRaWAN, suitable for remote locations. The entire unit is powered by a dedicated Power Supply with a battery designed to last for at least a year, minimizing the need for maintenance.

Operationally, the unit cycles through different states to manage its tasks and conserve energy. It typically rests in an `idle` state. When triggered or at a scheduled interval, it performs a measurement cycle that involves taking readings, processing the data, and transmitting the results before returning to its idle state. The system can also be remotely commanded to enter a calibration mode for maintenance and has a built-in error state for fault conditions. Overall, this is a robust, autonomous field device intended for long-term deployment to provide continuous, reliable data for water management and environmental science.
