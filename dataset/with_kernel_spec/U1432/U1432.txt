This model represents an electronic circuit designed to manage and store energy using an inductor. The system is built around a specific inductor, which is composed of a ferrite core wrapped with a 100-turn wire winding. The primary function of this system is to store electrical energy within a magnetic field and to smooth out the flow of electrical current.

The circuit operates with a 12-volt power supply that provides energy to the inductor. This current then flows to a load resistor, which represents a device consuming the power. A dedicated sensor is integrated into the circuit to actively monitor the current.

The system's key behavior is its opposition to changes in current. When power is first applied, the inductor enters a "charging" state, building its magnetic field and resisting the initial current surge. Once the current stabilizes, the system reaches a "steady state." If the power is removed or fluctuates, the inductor enters a "discharging" state, releasing its stored energy to try and keep the current flowing, thereby smoothing out the electrical supply. The system also models a "saturated" state, an overload condition where the inductor's core can no longer store more magnetic energy.
