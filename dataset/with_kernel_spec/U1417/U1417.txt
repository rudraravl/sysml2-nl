This model represents a complete electronic fuel injection system for a four-cylinder internal combustion engine. Its primary purpose is to precisely manage the delivery of fuel and its mixture with air to ensure optimal engine performance, efficiency, and low emissions.

The system is composed of several interconnected physical components. Fuel is stored in a **fuel tank** and drawn out by a high-pressure **fuel pump**. It then passes through a **fuel filter** to remove impurities before reaching a **pressure regulator**, which maintains a stable pressure throughout the system. This pressurized fuel is distributed via a **fuel rail** to four individual **fuel injectors**.

Simultaneously, air enters the engine through a **throttle body**, which contains a valve that opens and closes based on the driver's input to control the volume of airflow. The air then passes into an **intake manifold**.

The heart of the system is the **Engine Control Unit (ECU)**, a central computer that orchestrates the entire process. The ECU receives continuous data from a suite of sensors: a **Mass Air Flow sensor** measures the amount of air entering the engine, a **Throttle Position Sensor** reports how far the throttle is open, a **Crankshaft Position Sensor** measures the engine's speed (RPM), and an **Oxygen Sensor** monitors the exhaust gases to determine if the fuel mixture is rich or lean.

Using this input, the ECU calculates the precise amount of fuel needed at any given moment. It then sends precisely timed electrical pulses to each of the four fuel injectors, controlling how long they open. The injectors spray a fine, atomized mist of fuel directly into the intake manifold, where it mixes with the incoming air to form a combustible mixture ready for the engine's cylinders. This closed-loop system allows for constant, real-time adjustments to maintain an ideal air-fuel ratio under all driving conditions.
