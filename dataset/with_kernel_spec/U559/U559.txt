This model describes an industrial scoop wheel system designed for continuously lifting and transferring bulk materials, such as grain or sand, from a low point to a higher one.

The core of the system is a large, 4-meter diameter rotating wheel equipped with twelve scoop buckets. This wheel is driven by a 22-kilowatt electric motor, which provides the necessary power and torque for rotation. The entire operation is managed by a central control unit that processes commands from an operator.

The process begins when bulk material is fed into an input hopper at the base of the system. As the motor turns the main wheel, the attached scoop buckets dip into the hopper, filling up with material. The rotation of the wheel then lifts the filled buckets upwards. Once a bucket reaches the top of the arc, it naturally tips over due to its position and gravity, emptying its contents into a discharge chute. This chute then guides the material to its next destination, such as a storage silo or another conveyor. The empty bucket continues its rotation back down to the bottom to repeat the cycle.

The system operates in several distinct modes. It starts in a "stopped" state and, upon receiving a start command, moves through "starting" and "accelerating" phases until it reaches its steady "running" speed. During operation, it can be brought to a controlled halt via a "stopping" state or shut down immediately in an emergency. This ensures both efficient material handling and operational safety.
