This model represents a Bourdon gauge system, a mechanical instrument designed for measuring and visually indicating fluid pressure in industrial applications. It operates without any external power, relying on a clever mechanical principle to provide a direct reading.

The core of the system is the Bourdon tube, a curved, elastic metal tube with an elliptical cross-section, made from a material like beryllium copper. This tube is housed within a protective stainless steel enclosure that features a clear polycarbonate window for viewing and a standard threaded connection for installation into a pipe or vessel.

Its operation is straightforward: when pressurized fluid from a process enters the Bourdon tube, the pressure difference between the inside and outside of the tube causes it to slightly uncurl or straighten. This results in a small, precise linear movement at the free end of the tube. This small movement is then captured by a linkage mechanism, a system of levers and gears that mechanically amplifies the motion and converts it into a much larger rotation.

This rotation is directly coupled to a pointer assembly. As the linkage rotates, it sweeps the pointer across the face of a calibrated dial. The dial is marked with divisions and units (like MPa or psi) corresponding to the pressure range, allowing an operator to read the system pressure at a glance.

The gauge operates through different states: it is in standby when at ambient pressure, becomes active when pressure is applied and the pointer is moving, and is considered stable when the pressure has settled and it's holding a steady reading. The entire assembly is designed to be robust, accurate to within 1.5% of its full scale, and fast-responding, making it a reliable and common instrument for monitoring pressure in various industrial settings.
