This model describes a high-precision laboratory system designed to measure the electrical resistivity and conductivity of various material samples. Its primary purpose is to characterize the fundamental electrical properties of materials, which is essential for applications in material science, semiconductor research, and quality control.

The system is built around a four-point probe measurement technique, which ensures high accuracy by eliminating errors from probe contact resistance. The core components work together to perform this measurement. A stable **Voltage Source** supplies a precise current, which is measured by a sensitive **Current Meter (Ammeter)** as it passes through the outer two points of the **Four-Point Probe** assembly touching the material sample. A high-impedance **Voltmeter** simultaneously measures the voltage drop across the inner two probe points.

To analyze how a material's properties change with temperature, the system includes a **Temperature Controller** that can precisely heat or cool the sample to a target temperature and maintain it with high stability.

A central **Data Acquisition Unit** collects the raw electrical and temperature readings from all the sensors. This data is then processed by a **Control Unit** which calculates the material's resistance, resistivity, and conductivity. The calculations also compensate for the effect of temperature on the material's properties, providing a more accurate characterization.

The entire measurement process is automated and follows a clear operational sequence. The system first calibrates itself, then stabilizes the sample's temperature. It then performs the measurement, processes the raw data, and finally reports the calculated material properties before returning to an idle state, ready for the next test.
