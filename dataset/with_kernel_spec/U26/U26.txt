This model describes the Terabot-S, an autonomous mobile robot designed for tasks like industrial inspection, material handling, and remote operations. Its primary purpose is to navigate complex environments on its own, use its sensors to see and avoid obstacles, and perform precise tasks with its robotic arm, all while reporting its status back to an operator.

The robot is built on a wheeled **Mobile Platform** that allows it to travel at speeds up to 3.5 meters per second. Mounted on this platform is a **Manipulator Arm** with six joints, which gives it the dexterity to pick up, move, and interact with objects. The arm is capable of lifting payloads up to 8 kg and has a reach of 1 meter.

For perception, the Terabot-S relies on a combination of sensors. A **Lidar Sensor** scans the environment with lasers to detect obstacles up to 50 meters away, creating a 3D map for navigation. This is complemented by a high-definition **Vision System** (a camera) that provides visual information for identifying objects and understanding the terrain.

The "brain" of the robot is a central **Processing Unit** that makes decisions and coordinates all actions. It processes commands and sends them to a dedicated **Navigation Unit**, which takes the fused data from the Lidar and camera to plan safe and efficient paths. The Processing Unit also sends precise control commands to the Manipulator Arm. The entire system is powered by a rechargeable 48-volt **Battery Pack**.

Functionally, the robot can operate in several modes. It can run in a fully **autonomous mode**, where it follows a mission plan by continuously sensing its environment, planning its next move, and then executing the motion and manipulation tasks. It can also be controlled directly by a human in a **teleoperation mode**. The system is designed to be self-sufficient, automatically returning to a charging station when its battery level is low before resuming its tasks.
