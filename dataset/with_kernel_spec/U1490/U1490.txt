This is a mechanical safety relief system designed to automatically protect a pressurized vessel, such as a boiler or storage tank, from catastrophic failure due to over-pressurization. The system's primary function is to vent excess pressure to a safe discharge location when operational limits are exceeded.

The system consists of three main components: the protected vessel, the safety valve itself, and a discharge system. The safety valve is installed directly on the vessel to continuously monitor its internal pressure.

The valve itself is a self-contained mechanical device composed of a body, a pressure sensor, a spring-loaded valve disc, and the spring mechanism. Under normal operating conditions, the force of the pre-loaded spring is greater than the force exerted by the fluid pressure, holding the valve disc firmly shut and creating a tight seal.

The system operates through a distinct cycle of states. When the pressure inside the vessel rises and reaches a pre-determined "set pressure," the force from the fluid overcomes the spring's force. This causes the valve disc to lift off its seat, transitioning the valve from a `fullyClosed` to an `opening` state. As it opens fully, it enters a `fullyOpen` state to relieve pressure, venting the fluid from the vessel through the valve's outlet and into the discharge system at its maximum rated capacity.

As fluid is discharged, the pressure inside the vessel begins to drop. Once the pressure falls to a lower, safe threshold known as the "blowdown pressure," the spring force once again overcomes the fluid pressure. This pushes the valve disc back down, transitioning the valve through a `closing` state until it is `fullyClosed` again, re-sealing the vessel. This automatic reseating allows the protected system to return to normal operation without manual intervention.
