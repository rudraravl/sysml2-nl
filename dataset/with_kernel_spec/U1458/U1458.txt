This model represents a comprehensive offshore oil production platform, designed to extract, process, store, and export hydrocarbons from subsea reservoirs.

The system's operation begins on the seabed with a set of six subsea wells that extract a raw mixture of oil, gas, and water from an underground reservoir. This raw fluid from all wells is collected and combined by a subsea manifold system before being transported to the platform on the surface through large vertical pipes called risers.

Once on the platform, the fluid enters a large separation system, which is the core of the processing facility. This system separates the mixture into its three main components: crude oil, natural gas, and produced water. Each of these streams is then directed to its own specialized treatment system. The crude oil is sent to an oil treatment system to be stabilized and prepared for export. The natural gas is routed to a compression system, which increases its pressure. The produced water, which is an environmental byproduct, is sent to a water treatment facility to be cleaned before disposal.

The platform is a self-sufficient facility. It uses some of the compressed natural gas to fuel its own power generation system, which produces all the electricity needed to run the platform's machinery and control systems.

After treatment, the stabilized crude oil is held in large onboard storage tanks. From there, an export system pumps the oil off the platform, likely to a pipeline or a waiting tanker ship. The entire operation is managed by a central control system that automates the production process. A separate and critical safety system continuously monitors the platform for hazards like gas leaks or fires. This system operates in different states, such as 'normal,' 'warning,' and 'emergency,' and can initiate an emergency shutdown of all operations if a critical hazard is detected.
