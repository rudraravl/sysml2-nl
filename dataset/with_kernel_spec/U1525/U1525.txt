This is a comprehensive water hammer mitigation and control system designed to protect large-scale fluid pipelines from dangerous pressure surges. The system is designed to manage the potentially destructive 'water hammer' phenomenon, which occurs when the flow of water in a pipe is stopped or changed abruptly, creating a high-pressure shockwave.

The physical system consists of a primary water source, such as a reservoir, which feeds a long steel pipeline. At the end of the pipeline is a main control valve that regulates the flow of water. The system's primary function is to detect, analyze, and actively counteract pressure surges that are typically caused by the rapid closure of this valve.

To achieve this, the pipeline is equipped with an array of sensors, including pressure sensors placed both upstream and downstream of the valve, and a flow meter to measure the water's velocity. These sensors feed real-time data to a central surge controller, which acts as the brain of the system.

When the controller detects a potential surge event, or is commanded to close the valve, it initiates a series of protective actions. A combination of specialized mitigation devices are connected to the pipeline to absorb the energy of the pressure wave:
*   A **Surge Tank** provides a large, open volume for excess water to safely flow into, absorbing the initial pressure wave.
*   An **Air Vessel** acts as a more active shock absorber, using a cushion of pre-charged, compressed air to dampen the pressure spike.
*   As a final failsafe, a **Relief Valve** is set to automatically open and discharge water if the internal pressure exceeds a critical safety threshold, preventing catastrophic pipe failure.

The system operates through distinct phases: it monitors the pipeline during normal flow, carefully manages the valve's closing speed to prevent surges, actively mitigates any surges that do occur using its protective equipment, and then safely returns the pipeline to a stable condition.
