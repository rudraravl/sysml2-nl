This is a Tide Gauge System designed for continuously monitoring and recording water level changes in coastal, estuarine, and harbor environments. Its primary purpose is to provide accurate, real-time sea level data for applications such as maritime navigation, coastal engineering, climate research, and flood warning.

The system is composed of several key components that work together. At its core is a submerged sensor package containing a high-precision **Pressure Transducer** to measure hydrostatic pressure and a **Temperature Sensor** to correct for water density variations. This raw sensor data is collected by a **Data Acquisition Unit**, which serves as the system's local processor. This unit samples the data at a high frequency, converts the pressure readings into a precise water level, and buffers the measurements for analysis.

The system doesn't just measure the current water level; it also analyzes trends over time. It calculates the rate of tide changes and predicts the times and levels of the next high and low tides. This analysis is crucial for detecting dangerous conditions. The unit constantly compares the current water level and its rate of change against configurable alarm thresholds for events like potential flooding or unusually low water levels.

All the processed data and any alarm statuses are sent to a **Telemetry System**. This is the communications module, which transmits the tide analysis and alerts to a remote monitoring station at regular intervals, typically every five minutes.

The entire system is engineered for autonomous operation in remote, harsh marine environments. It is powered by a **Power Management Unit**, which includes a high-capacity battery and receives power from a solar panel, allowing it to function for long periods without maintenance. All of these electronic components are protected and held in place by a durable **Mounting Structure**, built from corrosion-resistant materials like marine-grade stainless steel, designed to be installed at significant depths and withstand the forces of the ocean.
