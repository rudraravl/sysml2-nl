This describes a Karman Cannula System, a medical device designed for performing manual vacuum aspiration, a procedure used to evacuate the contents of the uterus.

The system is composed of three main components that work together: a flexible cannula, a manual vacuum aspirator, and a collection chamber.

The **flexible cannula** is a thin, flexible tube made of medical-grade plastic. One end, the tip, is designed for insertion into the uterine cavity. The other end, the base, connects to the rest of the system.

The **manual vacuum aspirator** is a hand-operated pump or syringe used by a clinician to generate negative pressure (a vacuum). This device is the power source for the aspiration process.

The **collection chamber** is a sealed container that sits between the aspirator and the cannula. It serves to collect and measure the uterine contents that are removed during the procedure.

In operation, the aspirator is connected to one port of the collection chamber, and the cannula is connected to another. A clinician first uses the hand-operated aspirator to create a vacuum within the sealed chamber and cannula. The cannula is then carefully positioned within the uterus. When the vacuum is applied, it gently suctions the uterine contents through the cannula and into the collection chamber. The procedure continues until the evacuation is complete or the collection chamber reaches its capacity. The system is designed with specific safety limits on vacuum pressure to prevent injury and ensure the procedure is performed effectively.
