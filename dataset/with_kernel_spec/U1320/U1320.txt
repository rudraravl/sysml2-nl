This is an automated window blind system designed to regulate the amount of natural light entering a room. Its core function is to automatically adjust its position to maintain a desired level of ambient light, but it also allows for manual user control.

The system is composed of several key interconnected components. A central controller unit acts as the brain, processing information and making decisions. It receives continuous input from an ambient light sensor, which measures the brightness of the room. Based on this sensor data and a user-defined light threshold, the controller determines the ideal position for the blinds.

To physically move the blinds, the controller sends commands—such as raise, lower, or stop—to an electric motor. This motor provides rotational force, which is then translated into a lifting force by a mechanical lift mechanism that likely consists of cords, drums, and gears. This mechanism is directly connected to the slat assembly—the actual set of horizontal blinds that block or permit light. The entire system is powered by a dedicated power supply that converts standard household AC voltage into the low-voltage DC required by the controller, motor, and sensor.

Operationally, the system can be in an idle state, actively raising, or actively lowering the blinds. In its automatic mode, it continuously monitors the room's light level and adjusts the blinds to keep the light near a target setting. Users can override this automation at any time with manual commands. The system is designed with safety features, including the ability to detect obstructions and limit motor force to prevent damage, as well as performance goals for speed, positioning accuracy, and long-term durability. It is also capable of integrating with larger smart home or building automation systems.
