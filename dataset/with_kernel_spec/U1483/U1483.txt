This is a simple electrical circuit designed to demonstrate the fundamental behavior of a resistor. Its primary function is to control and limit the flow of electrical current from a power source. The system is composed of a voltage source, which acts like a battery or power supply, a resistor, and a ground which serves as a common electrical reference point.

In operation, the voltage source applies an electrical potential across the resistor's two terminals. The resistor, based on its specific resistance value, opposes the flow of electricity. This opposition limits the amount of current that can pass through the circuit, a relationship defined by Ohm's Law. A critical side effect of this process is that the resistor converts electrical energy into heat, causing its temperature to rise.

The system continuously monitors its own operating conditions to ensure it remains within safe limits. The resistor has defined ratings for the maximum power it can dissipate and the highest temperature it can withstand. Its operational lifecycle is described through several states: it is 'idle' when no power is applied. When energized, it becomes 'conducting'. If the power dissipation causes its temperature to approach its limit, it transitions to a 'heating' state, and if the limit is exceeded, it enters an 'overheated' state, indicating a potential for damage or failure. This behavior models how a real-world resistor operates and the thermal constraints that govern its use.
