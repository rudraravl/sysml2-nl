This is a Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD) system, a highly sensitive optical detector designed to detect individual photons with exceptional timing accuracy. Its primary function is to convert the arrival of a single particle of light into a measurable, macroscopic electrical signal.

The core of the system is the SPAD diode itself, a specialized semiconductor device. To achieve single-photon sensitivity, a dedicated Bias Supply maintains the diode at a precise voltage just above its natural breakdown point, putting it in a highly sensitive, "ready" state. A Cooling System is also included to keep the diode at a low, stable temperature, which significantly reduces false detections caused by random thermal energy (known as dark counts).

The detection process begins when a single photon strikes the diode and is absorbed, generating an electron-hole pair. Due to the high bias voltage, this single event triggers a rapid, self-sustaining cascade of electrons—an electrical "avalanche." This avalanche produces a significant, sharp current pulse.

This current is immediately handled by a Passive Quenching Circuit. This circuit's first job is to "quench," or stop, the avalanche by quickly lowering the voltage across the diode. Once the avalanche is stopped, the circuit then resets the diode by restoring the high bias voltage, preparing it to detect the next photon. The time it takes to quench and reset is known as the "dead time," during which the detector is temporarily blind.

Simultaneously, the electrical pulse from the avalanche is sent to a Discriminator Circuit. This circuit cleans up the signal and converts the analog avalanche event into a standardized, clean digital output pulse. Most importantly, it assigns a highly precise timestamp to this pulse, marking the exact moment the photon was detected. The final digital pulse is the system's primary output, used for applications like photon counting or high-precision timing measurements.
