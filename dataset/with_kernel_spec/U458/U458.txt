This model describes a Yankauer suction system, a medical device essential for surgical and other clinical procedures. Its primary purpose is to remove fluids, such as blood and irrigation solutions, along with debris from a patient's airway or a surgical site. By providing continuous vacuum-assisted aspiration, the system helps maintain a clear and visible operating field for medical staff.

The system is a complete assembly of several key components working together. At the heart of the system is an adjustable **Vacuum Regulator**, which generates the controlled negative pressure needed for suction. To prevent contamination of this vacuum source, a **Bacterial Filter** is installed in the line, ensuring that no aspirated materials or airborne pathogens can reach the regulator.

Aspirated fluids and materials are drawn into a disposable **Collection Canister**, which is designed with a specific capacity and features an overflow shutoff mechanism to prevent spills when it becomes full. This canister is connected via long, flexible **Suction Tubing** to the handheld instrument used by the clinician.

The instrument itself is a rigid **Yankauer Tip**. This component is carefully designed with a bulbous, rounded end and side holes to allow for effective fluid removal without causing trauma to delicate patient tissues. The tip often includes a finger-controlled vent, which allows the operator to quickly modulate the suction pressure at the point of use.

The entire system operates through different states. It is typically in an `idle` state when turned on but not in use. During a procedure, it enters an `active` state to provide suction. The system is also designed to handle common issues, transitioning to an `obstructed` state if a blockage is detected in the line, or a `canisterFull` state to alert the user that the collection container needs to be changed before suction can resume.
