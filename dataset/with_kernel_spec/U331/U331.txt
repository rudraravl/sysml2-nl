This model describes a comprehensive nurse call button system for a healthcare facility, designed to allow patients to request assistance from nursing staff.

The system is composed of several key physical components. In each patient room, there is a **Call Button** unit, identifiable by a specific room and bed number. When a patient presses this button, it initiates a call for help.

This action triggers two simultaneous notifications. First, a **Room Alert Panel**, typically located in the hallway outside the patient's room, activates with both audible alarms and visual flashing lights to alert any nearby staff. Second, the call signal is transmitted over a network to a central **Nurse Station Console**.

The Nurse Station Console is the central hub where staff monitor all incoming requests. It maintains an active list of calls, showing which patient is calling from which room. From this console, a staff member can digitally **acknowledge** a call, which sends a signal back to update the system status and may dim or change the alert at the room panel to show that a response is underway. Once the patient has been attended to, the staff can **cancel** the call, which deactivates the room's alert panel and resets the patient's call button.

A central **Communication Controller** manages the network traffic, routing call signals from the buttons to the station and alert panels, and routing acknowledgement and cancellation signals back from the station. The entire system is built for high reliability and safety, with requirements for rapid alert activation (within two seconds), continuous network availability, and operation at a safe, low voltage. It is also designed to log all call events for quality assurance and record-keeping purposes.
