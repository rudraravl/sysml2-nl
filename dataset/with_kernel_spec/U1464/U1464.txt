This model describes a Peaucellier-Lipkin linkage, a sophisticated mechanical system designed to convert continuous rotary motion into a perfectly straight line. It is a planar mechanism built entirely from rigid bars connected by pivoting joints.

The system is composed of several key parts. It is anchored to a base at two distinct fixed pivot points. A rotating input crank is attached to one of these fixed pivots. The other end of this crank is connected to a vertex of a rhombus-shaped assembly, which is itself constructed from four rigid bars of equal length. The vertex of the rhombus opposite to the crank's connection point is attached to the second fixed pivot.

Two additional coupler bars, which are of equal length to each other, connect the remaining two vertices of the rhombus to a single output point. This output point, often called the tracer point, is where the straight-line motion is produced.

In operation, as the input crank is rotated, it pushes and pulls on the rhombus assembly. The geometric constraints imposed by the fixed pivots and the interconnected bars force the rhombus to deform in a very specific way. This controlled deformation guides the coupler bars, causing their shared tracer point to move along a mathematically perfect straight line. The system's primary function is to provide this precise linear guidance for mechanical applications where sliding contacts, which can introduce friction and wear, are undesirable.
