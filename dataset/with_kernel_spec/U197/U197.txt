This model describes a Cutting Balloon Catheter System, a specialized medical device used in angioplasty procedures to treat narrowed or hardened arteries (stenotic lesions). Its primary function is to both widen the artery and make controlled incisions into the plaque buildup, which helps prevent the vessel from re-narrowing.

The system consists of several key components working together. The main part is the `Cutting Balloon Catheter` itself, which is a long, flexible tube called the `Catheter Shaft`. This shaft is designed to be navigated by a physician through a patient's vascular system to reach the site of a blockage. It contains two internal channels (lumens): one for a guidewire to steer the device, and another to transport fluid for inflation.

At the tip of the catheter is a specialized `Balloon Membrane`. Mounted longitudinally on the surface of this balloon are three or four tiny, microsurgical blades known as `Atherotomes`. These blades are the defining feature of the system.

The procedure is controlled by an external `Inflation Device`, which includes a syringe assembly and a pressure gauge. A physician connects this device to the catheter's hub outside the patient's body. To perform the angioplasty, the physician first navigates the catheter to the blockage. They then use the inflation device to slowly inject a `Contrast Medium` (a fluid visible under X-ray) into the catheter. This fluid flows through the inflation lumen to the balloon, causing it to inflate.

As the balloon expands, it performs two actions simultaneously: it pushes outward to dilate and widen the narrowed vessel, and it presses the sharp atherotomes into the arterial plaque. This creates a series of small, precise, longitudinal incisions. This "scoring" of the plaque allows the vessel to expand with less force and reduces the elastic recoil (the tendency of the vessel to snap back to its narrowed state) after the procedure.

The entire operation follows a distinct lifecycle, moving from an idle state to navigating through the vessel, being positioned at the lesion, inflating to engage the blades, deflating, and finally being withdrawn from the patient. The system is designed to offer a more effective and less traumatic solution for treating hard, calcified plaques compared to conventional balloon angioplasty.
