This model describes an automated vacuum pumping subsystem designed to evacuate a fluid, such as a gas, from a source vessel and transfer it to a receiving vessel, thereby creating a controlled vacuum environment.

The system's core function is performed by a central **Vacuum Pump**, which is the active component that moves the fluid. This pump is connected to two containers: a **Source Vessel**, from which the fluid is removed, and a **Receiving Vessel**, where the evacuated fluid is stored.

The fluid's path is managed by two **Isolation Valves**, one on the suction side between the source vessel and the pump, and another on the discharge side between the pump and the receiving vessel. These valves can be opened or closed to control the flow and isolate different parts of the system.

The entire process is automated and managed by a **Vacuum Controller**. This controller acts as the brain of the system. It receives real-time pressure data from a **Pressure Transducer** located on the suction line of the pump. The controller compares this measured pressure to a pre-set target vacuum level. Based on this comparison, it sends commands to the vacuum pump, telling it which operating mode to use—for example, a high-power "evacuating" mode to quickly lower the pressure, or a "maintaining" mode to hold the vacuum steady once the target is reached.

The system's operational lifecycle includes several states: it can be idle, in a startup or shutdown sequence, actively evacuating, maintaining a set pressure, or in a fault state if a problem is detected. Additional sensors, including a **Flow Transducer** and another pressure sensor on the discharge line, monitor the pump's performance and the overall system health.

For safety, a mechanical **Safety Relief Valve** is installed on the receiving vessel. If the pressure in this vessel exceeds a safe limit, this valve will automatically open to vent the excess pressure and prevent system damage or failure.
