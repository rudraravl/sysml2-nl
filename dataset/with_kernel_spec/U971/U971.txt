This is a system for an autonomous, interactive animatronic character, the kind you might find in a theme park, museum, or public exhibition. Its main purpose is to provide entertainment by detecting nearby visitors and responding with pre-programmed, lifelike performances.

The physical character is composed of a body with several articulated joints, each powered by a dedicated servo actuator. These servos control the movement of the head, neck, shoulders, elbows, jaw, and eyes, allowing for a wide range of expressive motions. The system also includes two proximity sensors that act as its "eyes," enabling it to detect when people are nearby and how far away they are. An integrated speaker system provides the character's "voice" or sound effects, which are synchronized with its physical movements.

At the heart of the system is a central animation controller that serves as the brain. This controller coordinates all the components. It receives signals from the proximity sensors, and based on this input, it selects an appropriate animation sequence from its library. For instance, it might perform a simple "greeting" animation for a visitor far away and a more complex, engaging animation for someone who comes closer.

The controller then sends specific commands to the servo actuators to create fluid motion and simultaneously sends commands to the speaker system to play synchronized audio. During the performance, the servos send feedback to the controller, reporting their current position, torque, and temperature to ensure safe and accurate operation.

The character operates through a clear cycle: it waits in a low-power standby mode until a visitor is detected. It then transitions into a performance state, executing its animated routine. After the performance is finished, it returns to a neutral starting position and goes back into standby, ready for the next interaction. The entire system is designed with safety and reliability in mind, with defined limits on motor force and requirements for long-term operation.
