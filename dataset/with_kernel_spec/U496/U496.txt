This model describes a hydraulic energy storage and generation system, similar to a pumped-storage hydroelectricity plant. The system is designed to perform two primary functions: storing energy by pumping a fluid to a higher elevation, and recovering that energy by letting the fluid flow back down through a turbine to generate electricity.

The system is composed of two main operational loops: a pumping loop and a turbine loop.

In the **pumping loop**, an electric motor drives a centrifugal pump. This pump draws fluid from a low-level source reservoir. Inside the pump, a rapidly spinning component called an impeller uses blades to transfer energy to the fluid, increasing its pressure and velocity. The fluid is then channeled through a snail-shaped casing called a volute, which efficiently converts this high velocity into high pressure. This pressurized fluid is then sent uphill to an intermediate reservoir, effectively storing the electrical energy used by the motor as gravitational potential energy in the fluid.

In the **generation loop**, fluid is released from the intermediate reservoir. It flows downwards due to gravity into a hydraulic turbine. The moving fluid strikes the blades of the turbine's rotating part, known as a runner, causing it to spin. This rotational mechanical energy is then used to turn a generator, which converts it into electrical power. After passing through the turbine, the fluid flows through a specially designed pipe called a draft tube to recover remaining energy before being discharged into a final, low-level reservoir.

To ensure proper operation and safety, the system is equipped with pressure sensors and flow meters at key points, such as after the pump and before the turbine. The entire system operates through distinct states, including idle, starting, running, and stopping for both the pump and the turbine. During operation, it continuously monitors its performance, with the ability to detect and react to conditions like high pressure in the pump or power overload in the turbine. The system's design and performance are based on fundamental fluid dynamics principles known as Euler's equations for turbomachinery.
