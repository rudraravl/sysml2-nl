This model describes a complete peristaltic pumping system, designed to accurately transfer fluids from a source reservoir to a receiving tank. It's a self-contained unit often used in applications where precise, gentle, and non-contaminating fluid handling is required.

The core of the system is the peristaltic pump itself. It works by using a drive motor to turn a rotor assembly equipped with three rollers. These rollers rotate inside a stationary pump housing and sequentially squeeze a section of flexible tubing against an internal track, known as the occlusion bed. As the rotor turns, this point of compression moves along the tube, pushing a pocket of fluid ahead of it. This mechanism ensures the fluid only ever touches the inside of the tubing, preventing contamination.

The pump is supported by several key components: a fluid reservoir to hold the source liquid, a receiving tank to collect the transferred fluid, a dedicated power supply for the motor, and an electronic pump controller. This controller is responsible for setting the pump's speed to achieve a desired flow rate and monitoring its operational status.

The system operates through several distinct states. It starts in an `idle` state, moves to a `priming` state to initially fill the pump tubing with fluid, and then enters the `running` state for continuous operation. It can be commanded to stop, and it will automatically enter a `faulted` state if it detects an issue, such as the outlet pressure exceeding its maximum safe limit. The system also has specific performance and maintenance requirements, including maintaining a highly accurate flow rate, staying within pressure limits, and tracking the usage of the flexible tubing, which has a finite lifespan based on the number of times it has been compressed.
