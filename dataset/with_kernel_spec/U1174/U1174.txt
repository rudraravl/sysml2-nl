This model describes the Rainbow RB-5, a six-axis collaborative robot arm designed for automated pick-and-place and assembly tasks, particularly within a manufacturing or workshop environment.

The physical robot consists of a series of six rigid links connected by six motorized joint actuators. This six-joint structure gives the arm the flexibility to position and orient its end-effector in a wide range of locations within its workspace. At the end of the arm is a gripper, which acts as the robot's hand to grasp and release objects with a controlled force.

The system's "brain" is a central controller module that receives high-level commands, such as "move to this position," and translates them into precise control signals for each of the six joint actuators. The robot is also equipped with a sophisticated sensor suite. A vision sensor (a camera) provides sight, allowing it to identify parts and perform quality inspections. A force-torque sensor, likely located at the wrist, gives the robot a sense of touch, enabling it to detect contact and control forces during delicate operations.

Operationally, the robot follows a clear workflow. After being powered on, it enters an initialization and calibration phase to precisely determine the position of all its joints. Once ready, it can execute complex trajectories, moving its gripper smoothly between multiple waypoints. A critical feature is its continuous safety monitoring; if the robot collides with an object or person, the force sensor detects the impact, and the system immediately enters an emergency stop state to ensure safety. The robot's primary function is to perform tasks like picking up a part from a feeder, moving it to an assembly point, and then releasing it, all with high accuracy and repeatability.
