This model represents the Szczecin Water Pump Station, a critical piece of municipal infrastructure designed to supply water to a city's distribution network. Its primary function is to draw water from a large intake reservoir and pump it into the city's pipes, ensuring adequate pressure and flow to meet demand.

The system is composed of several key physical components working in concert. It starts with a large **Intake Reservoir**, which holds a significant volume of water and acts as the source. The heart of the station is a powerful **Centrifugal Pump**, which is responsible for moving the water. This pump is driven by a high-efficiency **Electric Motor**.

The station operates as a fully automated, closed-loop system. A network of sensors provides real-time data for control. A **Level Sensor** monitors the water level in the intake reservoir to ensure it doesn't fall too low, which could damage the pump. At the pump's output, a **Pressure Sensor** and a **Flow Meter** continuously measure the water pressure and flow rate being sent to the **Distribution Network** (the city's pipes).

A central **Pump Controller** serves as the brain of the operation. It receives data from all the sensors and compares it against the target pressure and flow rates required by the city. Based on this information, the controller continuously adjusts the speed of the electric motor. This regulation of the motor's speed directly controls the pump's output, allowing the station to dynamically match the city's water demand while maintaining operational safety and energy efficiency.
