This model represents an industrial fluid transfer system built around a progressing cavity pump. Its primary function is to precisely move fluid from a supply reservoir to a discharge vessel while carefully controlling flow rate and pressure. This type of system is often used for fluids that are viscous (thick) or contain solids.

The core of the system is the progressing cavity pump, which consists of a helical metal rotor spinning within a flexible, double-helix stator made of an elastomer like rubber. As the rotor turns, it forms sealed cavities that progress along the pump, gently and continuously pushing the fluid from the suction inlet to the discharge outlet. The pump assembly is sealed at both ends and connected to a robust stainless steel driveshaft.

Propulsion is provided by a three-phase induction electric motor. The motor's speed, and therefore the pump's flow rate, is precisely regulated by a Variable Frequency Drive (VFD). The VFD adjusts the electrical frequency supplied to the motor, allowing for smooth adjustments to the pumping speed to meet specific process requirements.

The entire system is heavily instrumented for monitoring and control. The fluid's path begins at a supply reservoir and flows through a series of sensors before entering the pump. These sensors measure the suction pressure and temperature. After being pressurized by the pump, the fluid passes through another set of instruments that measure the discharge pressure, temperature, and actual flow rate, before it finally enters the discharge vessel.

Operationally, the system follows a controlled lifecycle with multiple states, including idle, priming, starting, running, and stopping. During its running state, it can perform normal operations, adjust its flow rate based on commands, or enter a protective state if it detects a dangerous condition like cavitation (the formation of vapor bubbles due to low suction pressure). For safety, the system includes an emergency stop function that can halt operations from any state. This comprehensive monitoring and control scheme ensures the system operates efficiently, safely, and prevents damage to the pump.
