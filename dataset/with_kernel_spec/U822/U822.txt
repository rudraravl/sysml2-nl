This model describes an electronic piezometer system designed for long-term, autonomous monitoring of pore water pressure within soil or rock. Its primary purpose is to provide critical data for assessing the stability of structures like dams and embankments, monitoring groundwater conditions, and understanding the hydraulic behavior of the ground in civil engineering and geological projects.

The system is a self-contained unit powered by an internal battery designed for extended deployment. At its core is a pressure transducer that is in direct contact with the groundwater. This transducer senses the ambient fluid pressure and temperature and converts these physical properties into a raw electrical signal.

This weak signal is then processed by a signal conditioner, which amplifies it and filters out electronic noise to ensure a clean and reliable measurement. Following this, an analog-to-digital converter transforms the cleaned analog signal into a digital value.

A central microprocessor serves as the brain of the piezometer. It takes this raw digital value and applies stored calibration coefficients to correct for sensor-specific characteristics and temperature variations, resulting in a highly accurate pressure reading. The processor then packages this calibrated pressure measurement with the current temperature, the device's known depth, and a precise timestamp into a single data record.

These data records can be stored in an onboard memory module, allowing the device to log data over long periods. For remote access, a communication module periodically transmits the collected data to an external data logger or a central server.

The piezometer operates through several distinct states to manage its functions and conserve energy. It typically rests in a low-power standby mode. At scheduled intervals, it wakes up to enter an active measurement state to take a reading, followed by a data transmission state to send the information. The system also supports a calibration mode, allowing its accuracy to be fine-tuned in the field, and a low-power state to preserve battery life when energy is critical.
