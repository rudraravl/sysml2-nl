This is a photoresistor-based light sensing system designed to measure ambient light intensity and convert it into useful electrical signals. Its primary purpose is to enable automated responses to changing light conditions, such as in automatic streetlights, camera exposure controls, or day/night detection systems.

The system is composed of three main electronic components that work in sequence:

1.  **Photoresistor:** This is the core light-sensing element. It's a special type of resistor whose electrical resistance changes based on the amount of light it's exposed to. In the dark, its resistance is very high, and as the light gets brighter, its resistance drops significantly.

2.  **Voltage Divider Circuit:** This circuit takes the changing resistance from the photoresistor and, using a fixed-value resistor and a constant power supply (e.g., 5 volts), converts it into a variable voltage signal. As the light intensity changes the photoresistor's resistance, this circuit outputs a corresponding analog voltage that is proportional to the light level.

3.  **Signal Conditioner:** This component receives the analog voltage from the voltage divider and processes it. It acts as an interpreter, using predefined low and high voltage thresholds to classify the light level into one of three distinct, simplified conditions: `dark`, `dim`, or `bright`.

In operation, ambient light hits the photoresistor, causing its resistance to change. The voltage divider translates this resistance change into an analog voltage signal. This signal is then analyzed by the signal conditioner, which determines the overall light condition. The complete module provides both the raw analog voltage for precise measurements and the simplified digital state for simple control logic.
