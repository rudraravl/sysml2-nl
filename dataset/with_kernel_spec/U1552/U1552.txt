This is a six degree of freedom (6-DOF) robotic arm manipulator, designed for precise automated positioning, orientation, and manipulation of objects in a three-dimensional workspace.

The arm is constructed from a series of rigid structural links (an upper arm, forearm, and wrist) connected by six distinct rotating joints: a base, a shoulder, an elbow, and a three-axis wrist that provides roll, pitch, and yaw movements. This six-joint configuration allows the arm to control its end-effector's position (x, y, z) and orientation (roll, pitch, yaw) completely and independently. At the end of the arm is an end-effector, or tool, which can grasp objects with a specified force. The system is designed to handle a maximum payload of 15 kilograms within a workspace radius of 1.5 meters.

The manipulator's operation is managed by a sophisticated control system. A Motion Planner calculates the optimal trajectory to move the end-effector from its current position to a desired target pose. It determines the sequence of intermediate poses (waypoints) and solves the complex inverse kinematics problem to find the exact angle required for each of the six joints. A Motion Controller then translates these planned movements into specific torque commands for each joint's motor, ensuring smooth and accurate motion while continuously monitoring for safety violations, such as exceeding joint angle or torque limits.

Each joint is powered by an actuator that applies a precise amount of torque to create movement. To understand its own position, the arm is equipped with high-resolution position sensors on each joint, which constantly report their current angle, velocity, and acceleration back to the controller. The entire system is powered by a dedicated 48-volt power supply.

In operation, the system transitions through several states. It starts in an 'idle' state, ready for commands. When a new motion command is received, it validates that the target is reachable, plans the path, and then enters an 'executing' state to perform the movement. Upon successful completion, it returns to idle, but if an issue arises, such as a command to move outside its physical limits, it will enter an 'error' state for safety.
