This model describes a complete, automated pharmaceutical manufacturing plant designed to produce finished tablets from raw chemical ingredients. The system functions as a sequential production line, where materials are processed through a series of specialized pieces of equipment to ensure a consistent, high-quality final product.

The process begins in a **Mixing Vessel**, where the raw materials—an active pharmaceutical ingredient, inactive excipients, and a solvent—are combined to create a uniform solution. This solution is then transferred to a **Crystallizer**, which carefully controls temperature and cooling rates to form pure, solid crystals of the active ingredient.

From the crystallizer, the mixture of crystals and solvent moves to a **Filter Press**. This component separates the solid crystals from the liquid, producing a wet, filtered product and a stream of waste solvent that can be treated or recovered. The wet product is then conveyed to a **Fluid Bed Dryer**, which uses controlled temperature and airflow to remove any remaining moisture, resulting in a dry, solid material.

To ensure uniformity for the final tablet, this dried product is processed through a **Mill**, which grinds it into a fine powder with a consistent particle size. This milled powder is the final intermediate material before becoming a tablet. It is fed into a **Tablet Press**, a machine that uses high compression force to form the powder into individual tablets of a precise weight, hardness, and shape.

Throughout this entire manufacturing sequence, a central **Process Automation System** acts as the brain of the operation. It continuously monitors critical parameters like temperature, pressure, and flow rates from each piece of equipment and sends control commands to maintain optimal conditions. A dedicated **Quality Control Lab** periodically tests samples of the finished tablets. If any deviations from quality standards are found, the automation system can adjust the process parameters in earlier stages—such as mixing or crystallization—to correct the issue, ensuring the entire batch meets strict regulatory requirements for potency, weight, and dissolution.
