This model describes a clinical acoustic stethoscope, a medical instrument designed for auscultation—the process of listening to the internal sounds of a patient's body. Its primary function is to capture, amplify, and transmit sounds from the cardiovascular, respiratory, and gastrointestinal systems to a clinician's ears for diagnostic purposes.

The system is composed of several key physical parts that work in sequence. It starts with a dual-head chest-piece, which is placed directly on the patient's skin. This piece includes a flat diaphragm, optimized for picking up high-frequency sounds, and a concave bell for capturing lower-frequency sounds.

From the chest-piece, the captured acoustic vibrations travel through a pair of flexible acoustic tubes. These tubes are designed to transmit the sound waves with minimal loss or distortion. They connect the chest-piece to the binaural assembly, which is the metal framework that splits the sound path and leads to the earpieces.

The binaural assembly terminates in two angled earpieces fitted with soft, comfortable silicone tips. These are ergonomically designed to fit securely into the clinician's ear canals, creating an acoustic seal to block out ambient noise and deliver the body sounds clearly and simultaneously to both ears.

Functionally, the stethoscope operates in two main states: it is either idle when not in use, or in an active listening state when the chest-piece is positioned on a patient. The entire system is designed to perform effectively within the critical diagnostic frequency range of 20 to 2000 Hz and is built to be lightweight and comfortable for clinicians during extended use.
