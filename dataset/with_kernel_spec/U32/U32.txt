This model describes an Adams Clasp, a key component of a removable orthodontic appliance like a retainer, designed to securely anchor the appliance to a patient's tooth. The system is a mechanical retention device made from bent stainless steel wire that is partially embedded into the acrylic base of the appliance.

The core of the system is the Adams Clasp assembly itself. It consists of two arrowhead-shaped wire arms, one for the front-facing (mesial) side of the tooth and one for the back-facing (distal) side. These two arms are connected by a bridge wire that spans across the chewing surface of the tooth, maintaining a slight clearance.

The clasp functions by interacting with the natural anatomy of an anchor tooth, specifically the undercut areas below the widest part of the tooth's crown. The process of using the appliance involves several distinct states:
1.  **Insertion:** As the patient pushes the appliance onto the tooth, the flexible wire arms are forced to spread apart, or deflect, as they slide over the tooth's bulge.
2.  **Engagement:** Once the arms pass this widest point, their spring-like nature causes them to snap back inwards, fitting snugly into the tooth's undercuts.
3.  **Retention:** In this engaged state, the clasp exerts a continuous retentive force, securely holding the appliance in place and resisting accidental dislodgement during everyday activities like talking or chewing.
4.  **Removal:** The appliance can be intentionally removed when the patient applies enough force to once again flex the wire arms out of the undercuts.

The system is designed to balance providing sufficient retention force to be effective while ensuring the forces required for insertion and removal are manageable and comfortable for the patient.
