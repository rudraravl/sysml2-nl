This SysML model describes a portable, handheld Cancer Diagnostic Probe designed for real-time tissue analysis in a clinical setting. The device is intended to help clinicians quickly determine whether a tissue sample is benign, suspicious, or malignant without waiting for a traditional biopsy lab result.

The probe itself is a lightweight, battery-powered, and wireless instrument. Its core component is a biocompatible probe tip, designed for minimally invasive contact with tissue. This tip houses two critical sensors: an **Optical Biosensor** that uses red light to detect the concentration of specific cancer biomarkers, and a high-precision **Thermal Sensor** that measures tissue temperature to identify metabolic irregularities often associated with cancerous growths.

Internally, the probe contains a **Signal Processing Unit** which acts as its onboard computer. This unit takes the raw electrical signals from the optical and thermal sensors, filters out noise, and processes them into a usable format. This processed data is then fed to a **Diagnostic Algorithm**, which is the intelligent software that analyzes the biomarker levels and other signals. Based on pre-defined thresholds, this algorithm classifies the tissue and calculates a confidence level for its finding.

The entire device is powered by a high-capacity rechargeable **Battery Pack**, allowing for extended use during diagnostic procedures. A built-in **Wireless Transceiver**, likely using Bluetooth, transmits the final diagnostic data—including the tissue classification, confidence level, and a timestamp—to an external device like a tablet or a hospital's central monitoring system.

Operationally, the probe follows a clear, state-driven workflow. It starts in an **Idle** state. Upon command, it enters a **Calibrating** state to ensure its sensors are accurate before use. Once ready, a clinician can initiate a scan, putting the probe in the **Scanning** state where it actively gathers data from the tissue. It then moves to **Analyzing**, where the internal processor and algorithm work to classify the sample. If the analysis is completed with sufficient confidence, the result is transmitted, and the probe enters a **Complete** state. If the confidence level is too low, the probe is designed to automatically re-scan the tissue to gather more data, ensuring a reliable diagnosis. The system also includes a **Fault** state to handle any errors during its operation.
