This is a protective mechanical ventilation system designed for critically ill patients. Its primary purpose is to provide life-sustaining breathing support while actively preventing Ventilator-Associated Lung Injury (VALI), a type of lung damage caused by the ventilator itself.

The system is composed of three main functional components: a pneumatic delivery system, a sensor array, and a central controller.

The **Pneumatic Delivery System** is the physical mechanism responsible for delivering a controlled flow of breathable gas (like an air-oxygen mixture) to the patient. It manages the inhalation process by delivering a precise volume of gas at a specific flow rate and pressure, and also maintains a constant positive pressure (PEEP) in the lungs at the end of exhalation to prevent the air sacs from collapsing.

The **Sensor Array** is a suite of sensors that continuously monitors the patient's breathing circuit. It measures critical parameters in real-time, including the airway pressure, the volume of air delivered with each breath (tidal volume), and the rate of gas flow.

The **Ventilator Controller** acts as the brain of the system. It receives settings from a clinical user, such as the desired respiratory rate and oxygen concentration. The controller uses this information, along with the patient's predicted body weight, to execute a protective ventilation strategy. It continuously analyzes feedback from the sensor array and compares the measured pressures and volumes against strict, built-in safety limits.

In operation, the controller orchestrates a breathing cycle of inspiration and expiration. If the sensors detect that the pressure in the lungs is too high or the delivered volume exceeds the safe limit for the patient's size, the controller immediately triggers an alarm system to alert medical staff. This closed-loop control system ensures that ventilation is always within a lung-protective range, minimizing the risk of barotrauma (pressure injury) and volutrauma (volume injury).
