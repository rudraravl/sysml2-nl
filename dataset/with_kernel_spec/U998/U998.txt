This model describes a comprehensive **Bioethics Oversight System** designed to manage and enforce ethical standards for research involving human subjects. Its primary function is to ensure that all research activities are conducted ethically, protecting the rights, safety, and privacy of participants. The system formalizes and supports the entire lifecycle of ethical review, from the initial submission of a research plan to its ongoing monitoring and final reporting.

The system is composed of several specialized, interacting components:

*   The core of the system is an **Institutional Review Board (IRB)**. This is the central committee responsible for formally reviewing research proposals. Researchers submit their detailed research protocols and consent documents to the IRB. The board then conducts a structured review process, which includes assessing potential risks to participants, evaluating the study against fundamental ethical principles (like autonomy, beneficence, and justice), and validating that the informed consent process is clear and fair. Based on this evaluation, the IRB issues a formal decision, which can be an approval, a request for modifications, or a rejection.

*   A dedicated **Consent Management System** supports the IRB by handling the validation of informed consent forms. This component ensures that the documents provided to potential participants are easy to understand (by checking readability scores), contain all legally and ethically required information (such as disclosures of risks, benefits, and the right to withdraw), and are available in multiple languages to accommodate diverse participant populations.

*   Once a research study is approved, a **Compliance Monitoring System** takes over to ensure the study is conducted as planned. This system performs regular audits of active research projects. It verifies that researchers are adhering to the approved protocol, that the consent process is being followed correctly, that participant data is protected, and that any unexpected negative outcomes are reported promptly.

*   To meet strict data protection requirements, the system includes a **Data Privacy Module**. This component is responsible for safeguarding sensitive participant information. It employs techniques like de-identification (anonymization) to remove personal details, strong encryption to secure the data, and strict access controls to ensure only authorized personnel can view the information.

*   Finally, the system includes a **Participant Advocacy Office**, which serves as a direct point of contact and support resource for research participants. This office provides a channel, like a hotline, for participants to ask questions or raise concerns. It has dedicated advocates who investigate any issues to ensure the rights and well-being of participants are always protected.
