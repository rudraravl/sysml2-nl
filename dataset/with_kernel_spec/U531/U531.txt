This model represents a Knudsen Pump subsystem, a specialized gas pump designed to move gas from a low-pressure source to a higher-pressure destination. Crucially, it achieves this without any mechanical moving parts, relying instead on the physical principle of thermal transpiration.

The core of the system is a pumping unit that contains an array of microscopic channels. A significant temperature difference is intentionally created across these channels. One side is actively heated by an electric heating element, while the other is kept cool by a cooling element. When gas molecules within these tiny channels move from the cold side to the hot side, a net flow is generated, effectively "pumping" the gas. This process only works under specific low-pressure conditions where the gas behavior is dominated by molecular-level physics.

The complete subsystem is set up as a functional unit. It includes a source reservoir from which to draw gas and a destination reservoir to pump the gas into. A dedicated power supply provides the electricity needed for the heating element.

To monitor its performance and ensure proper operation, the system is equipped with a suite of sensors. Pressure sensors are placed at both the gas inlet and outlet to measure the pressure difference the pump creates. Temperature sensors monitor the hot and cold sides of the micro-channels, which is critical for maintaining the required temperature gradient. A flow meter is also included at the outlet to measure the rate at which gas is being pumped.

The system's operation follows a defined sequence. From an "off" state, it enters a "warmup" phase where the heating element is activated to establish the necessary temperature. Once the target temperature is reached, the pump enters its normal "operating" state, where it actively moves gas while continuously monitoring pressure, flow, and temperature to regulate its performance. When a stop command is given, it transitions to a "cooldown" phase to safely dissipate heat before shutting down completely. The system also includes a fault state to handle unexpected issues.
