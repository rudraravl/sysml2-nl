This model describes an industrial Brine Processing System designed to separate saline water into fresh, purified water and a highly concentrated brine solution. It is essentially a water treatment or desalination plant that uses a thermal evaporation process.

The system is composed of several key components that work in sequence. Raw brine is initially stored in a large **Feed Tank**. From there, a **Circulation Pump** moves the brine into a **Pre-Heater**, which raises its temperature to an optimal level for processing, around 85°C.

The hot brine then enters the core component, an **Evaporation Unit**. This unit operates under specific temperature and pressure conditions to boil the water, separating it as vapor from the dissolved salts. This water vapor is then condensed back into liquid form, producing fresh water with a very low salt concentration (less than 1 g/L), which is stored in a **Product Tank**. The remaining liquid, now a much more concentrated brine solution (with a salinity of at least 200 g/L), is collected and stored in a separate **Concentrate Tank**.

The entire operation is monitored and controlled by integrated systems. A **Temperature Controller** manages the Pre-Heater to maintain the target temperature, while a **Salinity Monitor** continuously checks the brine quality to ensure the process is effective.

The system operates through a defined lifecycle of states. It begins in a **standby** mode, progresses through **startup** and **heating** phases, and then enters a continuous **evaporating** and **concentrating** cycle during normal operation. A controlled **shutdown** sequence ensures the system cools down safely before returning to standby.
