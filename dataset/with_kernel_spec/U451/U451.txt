This model represents a Wearable Cardioverter Defibrillator (WCD), a life-saving medical device designed for patients at high risk of sudden cardiac arrest. It's a self-contained, automated system worn by the patient that continuously monitors their heart and can deliver an electrical shock to restore a normal rhythm if a life-threatening arrhythmia occurs.

The physical system consists of a comfortable, breathable garment, similar to a vest, which houses all the core components. This garment holds an array of four ECG electrodes that press against the patient's skin to continuously sense the heart's electrical activity. It also contains larger therapy electrodes designed to deliver a defibrillation shock.

At the heart of the system is a central control unit that acts as its brain. It receives a constant stream of ECG data from the sensing electrodes. This data is processed by a specialized rhythm analysis processor that can accurately detect specific dangerous heart rhythms, such as ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation.

The system operates through a carefully designed safety and therapy protocol. During normal operation, it is in a constant monitoring state. If the processor detects a life-threatening arrhythmia, it does not deliver a shock immediately. Instead, it first activates a patient alert module. This module creates strong vibrations and sounds a loud audible alarm to warn the patient and any bystanders that a shock is imminent. This critical step allows a conscious patient, who may not be in actual distress, to respond (likely by pressing a button) and prevent the shock.

If the patient is unresponsive and does not cancel the alert, the controller assumes a true medical emergency is underway. It then commands a high-voltage capacitor to rapidly charge to a pre-set energy level. Once charged, the system delivers a controlled defibrillation shock through the therapy electrodes to the patient's chest. Following the therapy, the system immediately resumes monitoring the heart to determine if the shock was successful in restoring a normal rhythm. The entire system is powered by a rechargeable battery pack designed to provide over 20 hours of continuous operation on a single charge.
