This model describes a single-use medical glove system designed to provide a physical barrier, protecting healthcare professionals and patients from cross-contamination during clinical procedures.

The glove is composed of three key components: a main barrier material, a textured surface for grip, and a cuff section for a secure fit. The core of the glove is its barrier material, which can be made from substances like nitrile, latex, or vinyl. This layer's effectiveness is defined by its thickness, its resistance to being permeated by fluids or pathogens, and its overall structural integrity.

To ensure the user can handle instruments effectively, the glove features a textured surface, typically on the fingers and palm, which improves grip in both wet and dry conditions. A cuff secures the glove around the wearer's wrist, designed with a specific length and resistance to prevent it from rolling down during use.

The system is designed to balance robust protection against contaminants like body fluids, pathogens, and chemical agents with the high degree of tactile sensitivity and dexterity required for medical tasks. Gloves are available in different sizes, can be sterile or non-sterile, and are often ambidextrous.

The glove has a distinct operational lifecycle. It begins in a sterile, packaged state. After being donned, it enters an "in-use" state where it actively provides barrier protection. The glove's condition is monitored, and if its integrity is breached (for example, by a tear or puncture), it is considered "compromised" and no longer offers effective protection. Following its use, whether compromised or not, the glove is safely removed and disposed of.
