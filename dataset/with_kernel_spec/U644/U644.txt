This model describes a Cylinder Head Temperature (CHT) Gauge System, a critical monitoring instrument for an engine, commonly found in aircraft or high-performance air-cooled vehicles. The system's primary function is to continuously measure the temperature of an engine's cylinder head and display it to an operator, providing clear warnings if the engine begins to overheat.

The system consists of four main components that work in sequence. First, a temperature sensor is placed in direct physical contact with the engine's cylinder head to absorb its thermal energy. As the engine's temperature changes, the sensor converts this heat into a proportional electrical signal (a specific voltage).

This electrical signal is then sent simultaneously to an indicator gauge and a separate warning light on an instrument panel. The indicator gauge interprets the incoming voltage, translates it into a temperature reading, and deflects a physical needle to provide a continuous visual display of the current temperature.

The gauge is designed with a built-in, multi-stage alert system. It operates in a "NORMAL" state during standard conditions. If the temperature surpasses a caution threshold (200°C), it transitions to a "CAUTION" state. If the temperature continues to rise and crosses a critical limit (250°C), it enters an "OVERHEAT" state. In parallel, the dedicated warning light illuminates specifically when this overheat threshold is reached, providing an immediate and unambiguous alert to the operator that the engine is in a dangerous thermal condition and requires immediate action.
