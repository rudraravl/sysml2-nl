This model describes a physical wedge system, a simple machine designed to split, separate, or lift materials. The system's primary function is to convert a linear input force, applied to the wedge's wide end, into much larger perpendicular forces that push outwards from its inclined sides.

The system consists of three main parts: the wedge itself, an applied load, and the target material it acts upon.

The **Wedge** is a physical object with specific geometric properties, most importantly its angle, length, and thickness. It is made of a durable material characterized by its hardness and strength, which must be sufficient to withstand the operational forces without breaking.

The **Applied Load** represents the input force acting on the wedge. This could be the impact from a sledgehammer, the consistent pressure from a hydraulic press, or the weight of an object.

The **Target Material** is the object that the wedge is intended to split or separate, such as a block of wood or stone. Its properties, like hardness and internal strength, determine how much force is required to split it.

In operation, the wedge is positioned against the target material. A force is then applied to its blunt end, driving it into the material. The wedge's angled surfaces amplify this input force, generating powerful lateral (sideways) splitting forces that push the material apart. The effectiveness of this force amplification is directly related to the wedge's angle—a smaller, sharper angle provides a greater mechanical advantage. The system is designed to operate with a wedge angle between 5 and 40 degrees for optimal performance and structural integrity.
