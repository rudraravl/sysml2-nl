This describes an autonomous robotic assembly system, designed for high-speed, precision manufacturing on a production line. The system's main function is to automate the assembly of products by identifying, picking up, precisely positioning, and securing individual components without direct human intervention.

At its core is a six-jointed robotic manipulator arm, which provides the reach and dexterity needed to perform complex assembly tasks. This arm is capable of handling components up to 15 kg within a 1.5-meter reach and can place them with an accuracy of 0.05mm. Attached to the arm's end is a specialized pneumatic gripper, which is used to securely grasp components with a controlled force and release them once they are in position.

The entire operation is orchestrated by a central assembly controller, which serves as the brain of the system. Components are introduced into the robot's workspace by a component feeder, typically a conveyor belt, which can hold a buffer of up to 50 parts.

A high-resolution vision system acts as the system's eyes. It constantly scans the feeder to identify incoming parts, determines their exact position and orientation, and performs critical quality inspections on the assembly both during and after the process.

A typical assembly cycle begins when the vision system identifies a valid component. The controller then plans an efficient, collision-free path for the robotic arm to move from its home position to the feeder. The arm acquires the component, transports it to the main assembly area, and carefully positions and secures it with a precise amount of force. After each placement, the vision system performs an inspection to verify quality and accuracy. The system operates through a series of defined states, including idle, scanning, acquiring, assembling, and inspecting. It also includes a dedicated fault state to safely handle errors or safety violations, ensuring the integrity of the product and the safety of the workspace.
