This model describes a Power-Line Communication (PLC) system, which is designed to transmit digital network data over standard electrical wiring, effectively turning any power outlet into a network port. The system allows for bidirectional communication, sending and receiving data without interfering with the normal delivery of electricity.

The overall system is composed of a transmitter and a receiver unit, connected by the building's existing power lines, which act as the communication channel. Each unit contains two key components: a **PLC Modem** and an **Analog Front End (AFE)**.

The **PLC Modem** is the core of the system, responsible for the digital processing. It takes standard data packets (like those from a computer or router) and converts them into a high-frequency analog signal suitable for transmission over power lines—a process called modulation. It also performs the reverse operation, demodulation, to convert incoming signals back into digital data. The modem is designed to operate in a specific frequency range (2-30 MHz) to avoid interfering with the standard 50/60 Hz electrical current and can support data rates up to 200 Mbps.

The **Analog Front End (AFE)** serves as the physical interface between the low-voltage modem and the high-voltage power line. It has a **signal coupler** that safely injects the data signal onto the live power wire and isolates the modem from the dangerous line voltage (up to 1000V). A **band-pass filter** cleans the signal, ensuring only the desired communication frequencies pass through while blocking electrical noise. For transmitting, a **line driver** amplifies the signal with enough power to travel effectively along the wiring, which can be up to 300 meters long.

In a typical operation, the transmitting modem modulates a data packet and sends the resulting signal to the AFE. The AFE amplifies, filters, and couples this signal onto the power line. The signal travels through the wiring to the receiving AFE, which safely extracts it from the power line, filters it again, and passes it to the receiving modem. This modem then demodulates the signal back into its original data packet form, completing the communication link.
