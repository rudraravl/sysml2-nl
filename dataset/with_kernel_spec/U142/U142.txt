This model describes an automated airlock system, designed to provide safe passage for people and equipment between a pressurized environment, such as a space station habitat, and the vacuum of space.

The core of the system is a sealed chamber with two doors: an inner door connecting to the habitat and an outer door opening to space. The system's primary function is to manage the atmospheric pressure within this chamber, allowing it to transition safely between being pressurized like the habitat or being in a vacuum like the outside environment.

The airlock is equipped with a pressure sensor to monitor the internal conditions of the chamber, as well as two main valves. One valve is used to vent the chamber's atmosphere to space (depressurization), while the other allows air from the habitat to fill the chamber (pressurization).

The system operates through automated cycles for "egress" (exiting the habitat) and "ingress" (entering the habitat).
- To exit (egress), a person enters the chamber from the habitat, and the inner door is sealed and locked. The system then vents the air from the chamber until its internal pressure matches the vacuum of space. Only after this pressure equalization is confirmed can the outer door be unlocked and opened.
- To enter (ingress), a person enters from outside, and the outer door is sealed and locked. The system then fills the chamber with air from the habitat until its pressure matches the internal habitat pressure. Once equalized, the inner door is permitted to open.

Critical safety features are fundamental to its operation. A door interlock system ensures that both doors can never be unlocked or open at the same time, preventing catastrophic depressurization of the habitat. Additionally, the doors are designed to remain locked and sealed if there is a significant pressure difference across them, ensuring structural integrity and user safety. The entire cycle is automated, receiving a simple command and executing the required sequence of valve and door operations.
