This model describes a neurally-controlled biorobotic prosthesis, essentially an advanced bionic limb designed to restore motor function for individuals with paralysis or limb loss. Its primary purpose is to read bioelectric signals directly from the user's body, interpret their intended movement, and translate that intent into fluid, coordinated motion in the robotic limb.

The system is composed of several key components working in a tightly integrated loop. It begins with a **Neural Sensor** that detects faint bioelectric signals from the user's residual nerves or muscles. This raw data is then sent to a dedicated **Signal Processor**, which filters out noise and intelligently decodes the signals to determine the user's desired action, such as grasping an object or flexing a joint.

This "motor intent" is passed to the main **Control Unit**, the brain of the prosthesis. The controller takes the user's intent, combines it with real-time sensory feedback from the limb itself, and calculates the precise, low-level commands for the motors. The system operates in several states, including an initial start-up, a standby mode, an active control mode for movement, and a fail-safe mode for safety.

The physical movement is carried out by a set of five **Bionic Actuators**, which act as the muscles of the limb. These actuators generate the necessary torque to move the joints and are equipped with sensors that provide proprioceptive feedback—information about the limb's joint angles, velocity, and the forces being applied.

A dedicated **Safety Monitor** constantly watches this feedback, ensuring that the limb's movements and forces stay within safe limits to prevent injury to the user or damage to objects. The entire system is powered by a central **Power Management** unit, which distributes electricity from a rechargeable battery to all the electronic components.

In operation, the system forms a continuous feedback loop: the user thinks of a movement, the sensor picks up the signal, the processor deciphers the intent, the controller commands the actuators, the actuators move and report their status back, and the safety monitor ensures everything is secure. This allows for intuitive, responsive, and safe control of the prosthetic limb.
