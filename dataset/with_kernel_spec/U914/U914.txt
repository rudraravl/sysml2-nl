This is a real-time monitoring and control system for thin-film deposition processes, typically used in high-tech manufacturing environments like vacuum chambers to precisely create coatings on surfaces such as optical lenses or semiconductor wafers.

The system's primary function is to measure the thickness of a material layer as it is being deposited and to automatically stop the process once a specific target thickness is reached.

At its core is a **Quartz Crystal Sensor**, which is placed inside the deposition chamber alongside the object being coated. As material accumulates on the object, it also accumulates on this crystal. The added mass causes the crystal's natural vibration frequency to decrease. A **Frequency Counter** precisely measures this change in frequency.

This frequency data is fed into a **Thickness Calculator**. Using the known physical properties of the material being deposited (such as its density and acoustic impedance), this unit calculates the exact thickness of the deposited film in real-time. It also determines the current deposition rate (how fast the film is growing).

A **Process Controller** acts as the system's brain. It constantly compares the live thickness measurement from the calculator to a pre-set target thickness. When the measured thickness reaches the target (within a specified tolerance), the controller automatically sends a command to close a physical shutter, which blocks the material source and precisely ends the deposition process.

For operator oversight, a **Display Unit** provides a continuous, live readout of the current film thickness and deposition rate, allowing a technician to monitor the process as it happens. The system is designed for high accuracy and can automatically control the creation of films with nanometer-level precision.
