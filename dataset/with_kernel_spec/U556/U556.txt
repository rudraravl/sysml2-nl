This is a Roundabout PlayPump water system, designed to harness the energy from children's play to pump groundwater for community use. The system integrates a playground roundabout with a mechanical water pump to provide a sustainable and engaging solution for accessing clean water.

The core of the system is the **Roundabout PlayPump** itself, which consists of two main parts: a children's roundabout and a pump mechanism. The **roundabout** is a heavy, 3.5-meter diameter spinning platform with eight seats. As children push and ride it, their kinetic energy generates rotational force. This rotation is mechanically transferred to a high-efficiency **positive displacement pump**.

The pump is engineered to lift water from a deep underground **water source**, specifically a 40-meter deep borehole. When the roundabout is spinning, the pump activates, drawing water up through a long **rising main pipe** made of PVC. From there, the water flows through a shorter **delivery pipe** and into a large, elevated **storage tank**.

This **storage tank** acts as a reservoir, holding up to 2,500 liters of water at a height of 7 meters, ensuring a consistent water supply is available for the community even when the roundabout is not in use. The tank has its own operational states, transitioning from empty to filling when the pump is active, becoming full, and then entering a dispensing state when community members draw water from it.

The entire system is designed to operate based on the presence of playing children. It is active and pumping water when the roundabout is in motion and remains in standby otherwise. The design ensures it is both functional and safe, meeting specific requirements for pump efficiency, water-lifting capability from significant depths, storage capacity, and the safe dimensions of the roundabout for children.
