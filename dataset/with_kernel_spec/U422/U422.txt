This model describes a medical patient transfer board system used by caregivers to safely move patients between two surfaces, such as from a hospital bed to a stretcher.

The core of the system is the transfer board itself, a rigid, flat sliding board. It is specifically designed with a low-friction top surface, which allows a patient to be slid across it with minimal effort, and a high-friction bottom surface that grips the bed and stretcher to prevent the board from slipping during the transfer. This dual-surface design is a key safety and functional feature. The board has defined physical properties, including its length, width, thickness, and a maximum weight capacity it can safely support.

The system also includes the two support surfaces involved in the transfer—an origin (e.g., a bed) and a destination (e.g., a gurney)—and a caregiver who performs the procedure. The entire transfer process is structured to ensure safety. It begins with the caregiver positioning and securing the board to create a stable bridge between the two surfaces. Before moving the patient, the system verifies that the patient's weight is within the board's load limits. The patient is then slid across the board. Once the transfer is complete, the board is removed.

The system operates through distinct phases or states: it starts as `idle` (not in use), moves to `positioning`, then `secured` (ready for transfer), `transferring` (when the patient is moving), and finally `removing` before returning to its idle state. This structured lifecycle ensures that all safety checks and steps are followed in the correct order.
