This model describes a Hot Filament Ionization Gauge, a highly sensitive instrument used for measuring ultra-high vacuum pressures in scientific and industrial applications. It operates by creating and measuring ions from the residual gas inside a vacuum chamber.

The system is composed of several key components that work in sequence. At its heart is a **Heated Filament**, typically made of tungsten, which is heated to a high temperature (around 2500 K) until it emits electrons. These electrons are then accelerated by a positively charged **Accelerating Grid**.

The high-energy electrons travel through an **Ionization Region**, which is open to the vacuum environment being measured. Here, they collide with and ionize the sparse gas molecules, stripping them of an electron and creating positive ions. These newly formed positive ions are then drawn towards a negatively charged **IonCollector**.

The flow of these ions to the collector creates a very small, measurable electrical current. A **Signal Processor** measures this ion current, and by using a pre-calibrated sensitivity factor, it converts the current reading into a precise vacuum pressure measurement. The entire system is powered by a dedicated **Power Supply Unit** that provides the specific, stable voltages required for the filament, grid, and collector.

For safety, a **Protection Circuit** continuously monitors the pressure. If the pressure rises above a safe threshold (e.g., due to a leak), it automatically shuts down the filament to prevent it from burning out in the presence of too much gas. The gauge operates in different states, including a warm-up phase, normal operation for active measurement, and an emergency shutdown mode for self-protection.
