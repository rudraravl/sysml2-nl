This model describes a gait belt system, which is a safety and mobility aid used by caregivers to assist patients with limited strength, balance, or endurance. The primary purpose of the system is to enable caregivers to safely guide and stabilize patients during transfers (like from a bed to a chair) and while walking, significantly reducing the risk of falls and preventing injury to both the patient and the caregiver.

The physical device is a durable gait belt made from reinforced nylon webbing. It features a strong, quick-release metal buckle for secure fastening around the patient's waist. The belt may also include one or two non-slip grip handles on the back to provide the caregiver with a firm, reliable point of contact.

The system's use involves a structured process that begins with the caregiver assessing the patient's mobility level to determine the type of transfer and the amount of assistance required. The caregiver then securely applies the belt around the patient's waist, ensuring a snug fit that still allows for two fingers to slip underneath.

Once the belt is on, the caregiver establishes a firm, underhand grip. During the transfer or ambulation, the caregiver walks with the patient, using the belt to provide physical support, apply a gentle upward lift to aid movement, and provide counterbalance to prevent instability. Throughout the process, the caregiver continuously monitors the patient for signs of fatigue, distress, or loss of balance.

A key aspect of this system is its defined response to instability. If the patient begins to wobble, the caregiver increases the stabilizing force through the belt. In the event of a significant loss of balance where a fall is imminent, the system's procedure is to use the belt to safely guide the patient down to the nearest surface, such as the floor or a chair, preventing an uncontrolled fall. Once the transfer is safely completed, the caregiver removes the belt.
