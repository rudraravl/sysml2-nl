This model describes a complete Global Positioning System (GPS) receiver, a device designed to determine a user's precise location, velocity, and time by processing signals from orbiting satellites.

The system is composed of several key functional components that work together in a sequence. It begins with a **GPS Antenna**, which captures the very faint radio frequency (RF) signals broadcast by multiple GPS satellites. These signals contain crucial navigation data, including the satellite's exact position and the precise time the signal was transmitted.

The captured signal is then passed to an **RF Receiver**. This component acts as the radio front-end, filtering out noise and converting the high-frequency satellite signal into a format suitable for digital processing.

Next, a **Correlation Engine** analyzes this processed signal. Its main job is to identify which specific satellites are being heard by matching the signal's unique code. In doing so, it calculates critical measurements for each satellite, including the signal's travel time (known as pseudorange) and its frequency change (Doppler shift), which relates to the satellite's velocity relative to the receiver.

These precise measurements from at least four different satellites are then fed into the **Navigation Processor**. This is the computational heart of the system. It uses the timing and satellite position data to solve a complex set of equations, effectively triangulating the receiver's own three-dimensional position (latitude, longitude, and altitude), its velocity vector (north, east, and up), and a highly accurate time estimate.

Finally, the calculated position, velocity, and time are sent to a **User Display**, which presents this information to the user in a clear and understandable format. The entire process runs continuously, acquiring signals and updating the navigation solution at least once per second to provide real-time tracking. The system is designed to achieve a position fix within 45 seconds from a cold start and to maintain a horizontal accuracy of 5 meters or better under normal conditions.
