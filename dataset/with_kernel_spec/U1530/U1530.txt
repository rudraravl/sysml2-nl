This model describes a traditional water-powered grain mill, or watermill. It is a mechanical system designed to use the energy from flowing water to grind grain into flour.

The system's operation begins with a water channel that directs a controlled flow of water toward a large water wheel. As the water fills the buckets on the wheel, its weight and momentum cause the wheel to rotate. This rotation is the primary source of power for the entire mill.

Connected to the water wheel is a thick, strong drive shaft. This shaft transfers the rotational mechanical power from the wheel into the main milling machinery. At the other end of the shaft is a large, heavy, circular millstone. This upper millstone is spun by the drive shaft directly above a second, stationary lower millstone.

While the water wheel provides power, a grain hopper stores raw grain (like wheat or corn) and steadily feeds it into a small opening at the center of the spinning upper millstone. The grain is pulled between the two millstones, where the immense weight and abrasive surfaces grind it into flour. The finished flour is then expelled from the outer edges of the millstones and gathered in a flour collector.

The entire system operates through different states. It remains idle until a sufficient flow of water is activated. Once water is flowing, the wheel begins to turn, and the system enters a starting phase, accelerating until it reaches a minimum operational speed. At this point, it is ready to begin its primary function of grinding grain. The mill will continue to operate as long as water and grain are supplied. If the water flow stops, the system has a built-in safety measure to automatically shut down, decelerating to a stop to prevent the millstones from being damaged by grinding against each other without grain.
