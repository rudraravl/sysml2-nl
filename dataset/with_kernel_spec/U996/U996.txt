This is a Barrett Technology WAM (Whole-Arm Manipulator) robotic system, designed for precise and safe object manipulation, particularly in environments where it may work collaboratively with humans.

The system's core is a highly flexible, seven-joint robotic arm. This arm structure, with joints for the shoulder, elbow, and a multi-axis wrist, mimics the dexterity of a human arm. It has a reach of one meter and can handle objects weighing up to 3 kilograms. A key feature of the arm's design is its use of cable-drive transmissions in each joint. This mechanism allows for extremely smooth, low-friction movement, making the arm "backdrivable" — it can be gently guided by hand, a crucial feature for both safety and for teaching the robot new tasks. Each joint is powered by a brushless motor and equipped with an electromagnetic brake to hold its position securely.

Attached to the arm is a sophisticated, three-fingered BarrettHand, which acts as the system's gripper. This is not a simple pincer; each finger has multiple links and is actuated by a tendon-like system, allowing it to conform around objects for an adaptive and secure grip. The fingers are also equipped with tactile sensors to provide feedback on touch and force. The hand can also spread its fingers to accommodate objects of different shapes and sizes.

The entire system is managed by an integrated control system. A motion planner calculates the optimal path for the arm to move, while specialized software handles the complex mathematics (kinematics) to translate a target hand position into the precise angles required for each of the seven joints. The system is rich with sensors, including position encoders and torque sensors in every joint, which provide constant, real-time feedback for precise control and force monitoring.

Safety is a fundamental aspect of the system's operation. A dedicated safety controller continuously monitors joint torques and velocities to detect potential collisions or excessive forces. If a problem is detected or an external emergency stop command is received, the system immediately halts all motion and engages its brakes.

Operationally, the system transitions through several states. It can be idle while waiting for a command, actively moving along a trajectory, performing a grasping or releasing action, or securely holding an object. Throughout its operation, it is constantly monitored to ensure it functions safely and effectively.
