This model describes an Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) system, a sophisticated medical imaging device used for generating non-invasive, high-resolution, cross-sectional images of biological tissue, similar to an "optical ultrasound." The system creates detailed, micrometer-scale images by measuring light that is scattered back from different depths within the tissue.

The core of the system is built around a **low-coherence light source**, typically a superluminescent diode, which produces a beam of light. This beam is sent into a **Michelson interferometer**, which splits it into two separate paths: a reference path and a sample path.

The sample beam is directed to a **scanning subsystem**, which uses a pair of fast-moving galvanometer mirrors to precisely steer and scan the beam across the surface of the tissue being imaged. Focusing optics ensure the beam is sharply focused at the desired depth. As the light penetrates the tissue, it scatters back from internal structures.

This backscattered light from the sample is collected and recombined with the light from the reference path. When the two beams are combined, they create an interference pattern. A highly sensitive **balanced photodetector** measures this pattern and converts the optical interference into an electrical signal.

This analog signal is then digitized by a high-speed **signal acquisition unit**. The resulting digital data is passed to a powerful **image processing unit**, which uses Fourier transforms to analyze the interference signal and calculate a depth profile of the tissue's structure at a single point (an A-scan). By assembling the depth profiles from all the points in the scan, the unit reconstructs a complete 2D cross-sectional (B-scan) or 3D volumetric image.

A central **control and display unit** manages the entire operation. It allows an operator to set imaging parameters, such as the scan depth and imaging mode (e.g., 2D or 3D), initiates the scanning process, and displays the final tomographic image on a monitor. The entire system cycles through distinct operational states, including idle, calibrating, scanning, processing, and fault, to ensure proper and safe operation.
