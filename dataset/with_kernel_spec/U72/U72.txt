This model describes a medical therapeutic hypothermia cap system, designed to prevent hair loss (alopecia) in patients undergoing chemotherapy.

The system consists of a wearable therapeutic cap connected to a central control and cooling console. The core of the system is a closed-loop coolant circuit. A circulation pump pushes a specialized coolant to a cooling unit, which chills the liquid to a precise therapeutic temperature. This cold coolant is then circulated through a network of small channels within the cap worn by the patient. As it flows through the cap, the coolant absorbs heat from the scalp, and the now-warmer liquid returns to the pump and cooling unit to be re-chilled and recirculated.

The entire process is managed by an integrated control module. A temperature sensor, placed on the patient's scalp, provides continuous real-time temperature feedback to this controller. The controller's job is to precisely manage the scalp's temperature according to a pre-set treatment plan.

The system operates through several distinct phases. It begins in an 'idle' state. When a treatment is started, it enters a 'cooling' phase, actively working to lower the scalp temperature to the target therapeutic range. Once this temperature is reached, the system transitions to a 'maintaining' phase, where it makes constant, small adjustments to the cooling power and flow rate to keep the temperature stable for the duration of the treatment session.

For patient safety, the system includes critical alarm functions. If the scalp temperature drops below a safe minimum threshold or another fault occurs, it will trigger an 'alarm' state to alert medical staff. The system is designed to maintain the scalp within a specific temperature window (e.g., 18°C to 22°C) for efficacy while also ensuring it never gets cold enough (e.g., below 15°C) to cause tissue damage. It also monitors coolant pressure to ensure the cap remains comfortable for the patient during long treatment sessions.
