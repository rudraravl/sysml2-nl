This is a Bakri Balloon Tamponade System, a medical device designed to control and manage severe postpartum hemorrhage (excessive bleeding after childbirth).

The system consists of a soft silicone balloon attached to the end of a long, flexible catheter. This catheter is designed with multiple internal channels (lumens) that serve different functions. One channel is an inflation port, used to fill the balloon with a sterile saline solution. This is typically done using a large 60 mL syringe.

In a clinical procedure, the catheter with the deflated balloon is inserted into the patient's uterus. The balloon is then gradually inflated with the saline solution, causing it to expand and apply direct physical pressure (tamponade) to the internal walls of the uterus. This pressure, carefully monitored by an external pressure gauge, helps to compress bleeding blood vessels and stop the hemorrhage. The balloon is designed to hold up to 500 mL of fluid to effectively fill the uterine cavity.

A key feature of the system is a separate, larger drainage channel that runs through the center of the catheter. This allows any blood from above the balloon to drain out of the body and be collected in a large drainage bag. This provides a direct way for medical staff to continuously monitor for ongoing blood loss and assess if the treatment is working. If the hemorrhage is controlled, drainage into the bag will cease.

The entire system works together to perform a life-saving procedure. After insertion, the balloon is inflated to create the tamponade effect. It is maintained at a safe but effective pressure while blood loss is carefully monitored via the drainage bag. If bleeding continues, the balloon's volume may be adjusted. Once the hemorrhage is confirmed to be under control, the device is left in place for a period before the balloon is deflated and the entire catheter is safely removed.
