This model describes a complete industrial fluid pumping system, designed to move a specific type of fluid from a source tank to a destination container. The core of the system is an electrically-driven, positive-displacement lobe pump.

The lobe pump itself consists of a chamber containing two synchronized, two-lobed rotors. An electric motor, connected through a gear set and driveshaft, turns one rotor (the driving rotor), which in turn drives the second rotor. As the rotors spin, they unmesh at the inlet port, creating expanding pockets that draw fluid into the pump. This captured fluid is then carried around the periphery of the chamber in the spaces between the lobes and the chamber wall. Finally, as the rotors mesh together again at the outlet, the fluid is squeezed out and discharged at a higher pressure. Precise synchronization and minimal clearance between the rotors are critical to prevent contact and ensure efficient operation.

The entire pumping subsystem includes the main lobe pump, a source reservoir to hold the fluid, and a destination receiver where the fluid is sent. To ensure safe and accurate operation, the system is equipped with pressure sensors at both the pump's inlet and outlet, a flow meter to measure the output, and a pressure relief valve as a critical safety feature to prevent over-pressurization.

The system's operation is managed through a series of states. It starts in an idle state, moves to a priming state to fill the pump and remove air, and then enters a normal operating state. During operation, it can intelligently respond to high-pressure events by entering a pressure-relief mode where it reduces its speed. The system also has defined states for shutting down properly and for handling faults. The electric motor is controlled by commanded speed signals and reports back its actual speed and operational status.
