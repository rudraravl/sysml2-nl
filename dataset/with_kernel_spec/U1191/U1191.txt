This model describes a robotic spacecraft designed for autonomous scientific exploration in space. The spacecraft is a complex system composed of several integrated subsystems that work together to carry out a mission, such as collecting science data and transmitting it back to Earth.

The core of the system is a central Command and Data Handling subsystem, which acts as the spacecraft's brain. It processes commands received from ground control and manages the flow of scientific and engineering data.

Key subsystems include:

*   **Power Subsystem:** This is the lifeblood of the spacecraft, featuring solar arrays and rechargeable batteries. It is responsible for generating, storing, and distributing electrical power to all other operational subsystems.
*   **Communication Subsystem:** Equipped with a high-gain antenna, this system manages all communications with Earth. It receives command signals and transmits telemetry, which includes system health updates and the valuable science data collected.
*   **Attitude Control Subsystem:** This system is crucial for orienting the spacecraft. It continuously determines the craft's current orientation (attitude) and adjusts it to meet specific targets, such as pointing the antenna towards Earth for communication, aiming the solar panels at the sun for power, or directing science instruments at an object of interest.
*   **Propulsion Subsystem:** Containing a specific amount of fuel, this system provides thrust to maneuver the spacecraft, allowing it to navigate to different orbits or target locations during its mission.
*   **Science Instrument Subsystem:** This is the primary payload, consisting of instruments designed to acquire scientific data about its environment or observation targets.
*   **Navigation Subsystem:** This component is responsible for determining the spacecraft's precise position and velocity as it travels through space.

The spacecraft operates in a continuous cycle to perform its mission. It generates power, listens for commands, determines and controls its attitude, navigates, acquires science data, and then transmits its findings back home. The entire system is designed to meet strict mission requirements, including maintaining a stable orientation, ensuring a continuous power supply, and adhering to a total mass limit of 5000 kg for launch compatibility.
