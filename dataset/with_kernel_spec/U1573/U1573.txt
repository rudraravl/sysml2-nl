This model describes a complete, real-time telepresence system designed for two-way audio and video communication between remote participants, similar to a high-end video conferencing setup.

The system consists of two identical endpoints, one for each location. Each endpoint is a comprehensive unit equipped with all the necessary hardware for a full communication session. This includes a camera to capture high-resolution video, a microphone for audio input, a display screen to show the remote participant's video feed, and a speaker to play their audio.

Functionally, the system works by capturing audio and video streams at one endpoint, compressing them for efficient transfer, and transmitting them over a network. The raw video from the camera and audio from the microphone are processed by dedicated video and audio encoders. These encoders package the media into data packets suitable for network transmission. A network interface then sends these packets to the remote endpoint.

Simultaneously, each endpoint receives data packets from the other. These packets are processed by video and audio decoders, which reconstruct the original media streams. The resulting video is then rendered on the display, and the audio is played through the speaker, creating the effect of a face-to-face conversation.

The system is designed to be robust, with a network connection that actively manages its state, moving between disconnected, connecting, connected, and reconnecting modes as needed. It also continuously monitors the quality of the connection, assessing factors like network bandwidth and latency. If the quality falls below an acceptable level, the session can be automatically ended. The system is built to meet specific high-performance standards, including a requirement for at least 720p video at 30 frames per second, low latency (under 150ms), and precise audio-video synchronization to ensure a natural and seamless user experience.
