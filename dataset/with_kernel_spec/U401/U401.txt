This is a model of a complete drinking straw system, encompassing the interaction between a person, a straw, and a beverage in a container. Its primary function is to transfer liquid from the container to the user's mouth through the application of suction.

The system is composed of three main parts: a human user, a beverage container, and the drinking straw that connects them. The beverage container holds a specific volume of liquid, which has physical properties like viscosity, density, and temperature. The user is capable of generating suction pressure with their mouth to initiate the drinking process and swallowing the liquid that is delivered.

The straw itself is a simple tube with defined physical characteristics, including its length, inner and outer diameters, and material. These properties determine its resistance to fluid flow. The system works by the user creating a low-pressure area at the top end of the straw. This pressure differential, relative to the ambient atmospheric pressure acting on the surface of the liquid in the container, pushes the beverage up through the straw and into the user's mouth.

The process of drinking is described through a series of states. The system is initially idle. When the user applies suction, it enters a "priming" state as the liquid is drawn up the straw. If the suction is strong enough to overcome the liquid's weight and flow resistance, it transitions to a continuous "flowing" state. The flow ceases when the user stops applying suction, returning the system to idle. The model also accounts for a "blocked" state, which could occur if the flow is obstructed.
