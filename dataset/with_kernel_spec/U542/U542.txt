This model describes a complete, high-pressure plunger pump subsystem, the kind used in industrial applications like high-pressure cleaning, water jet cutting, or fluid injection systems. Its primary function is to draw a fluid from a low-pressure supply tank, significantly increase its pressure, and deliver it at a steady, high-pressure flow.

The system is built around a central triplex plunger pump, which uses three separate plungers to ensure a smoother, less pulsating output. The entire subsystem is driven by a powerful electric motor. This motor turns a drive shaft, and a crank mechanism converts the shaft's rotation into a reciprocating (back-and-forth) motion for the three plungers.

Each plunger moves within its own chamber, operating in a two-stroke cycle. On the intake stroke, as the plunger pulls back, it creates a vacuum that draws fluid from a supply reservoir through a one-way inlet check valve. On the discharge stroke, the plunger moves forward, compressing the fluid. This pressure closes the inlet valve and forces the now high-pressure fluid out through a discharge check valve.

The fluid's journey begins in a supply reservoir. From there, it's drawn into the main pump. After being pressurized by the three plungers, the high-pressure fluid is delivered to a discharge pressure vessel. This vessel helps to dampen any remaining pressure pulses from the pump, ensuring a more stable output, and also acts as a safety component. The system's performance is actively monitored by pressure sensors at both the pump's inlet and outlet, as well as a flow meter on the discharge line.
