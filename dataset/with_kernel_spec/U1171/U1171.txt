This model describes a self-reconfiguring modular robotic system, also known as programmable matter. The system is designed to autonomously change its overall physical shape in response to digital commands. It functions as a collective of many small, identical robotic modules that work together to form larger, dynamic structures, making it suitable for applications like adaptive tooling, morphing devices, or reconfigurable structures.

The system is composed of three main parts: a large number of Modular Units, a central Control System, and a shared Energy System.

The **Modular Units** are the core building blocks of the system. Each unit is a small, self-contained robot with its own processor, sensors, and actuators. It is aware of its own position and orientation in 3D space. Each module has an actuation system to physically move itself and a sensing system to detect its proximity to other modules. A key feature is its bonding mechanism, which allows it to physically connect to and disconnect from its neighbors on any of its six faces, using methods that could be mechanical, magnetic, or electrostatic. These units are powered by a central source and are in constant communication with the control system and each other.

The **Control System** acts as the central brain for the entire collective. When given a target shape, its job is to orchestrate the transformation. It analyzes the desired final configuration, plans the most efficient movement paths for every individual module to avoid collisions, and coordinates their actions through a synchronization protocol. It sends specific movement and bonding commands to each module and receives status and sensor data back.

The **Energy System** is the central power supply that distributes electrical energy to all the individual modular units, ensuring they have enough power to move, sense, communicate, and bond with each other throughout the reconfiguration process.

In operation, a command to form a new shape initiates a complex, coordinated sequence. The Control System creates a plan, and the individual modules execute it. This involves a cycle where modules communicate their status, release bonds with their current neighbors, move short distances to their new designated positions, and then establish new bonds with their new neighbors. This step-by-step process of local movement and rebonding is repeated across the entire system until the new macroscopic shape is fully formed. The individual units transition between states such as idle, moving, bonding, unbonding, and sensing as they carry out their tasks.
