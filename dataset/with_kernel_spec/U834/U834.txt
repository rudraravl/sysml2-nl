This model describes a complete Proportional Counter System, a scientific instrument designed for the detection and energy measurement of ionizing radiation. Its key feature is that it not only counts radiation particles but also measures their individual energy, allowing for detailed analysis of radioactive sources.

The core of the system is the detector itself, which is a sealed cylindrical chamber filled with a special counter gas (like a mix of Argon and Methane, known as P10). Running down the exact center of this chamber is a very thin anode wire, while the outer wall of the chamber acts as the cathode.

The system's operation begins when an ionizing particle (such as an alpha particle or an X-ray) enters the detector chamber and collides with the gas atoms, creating a small trail of electron-ion pairs. A stable, high-voltage power supply creates a strong electric field between the central wire and the outer wall. This field causes the newly freed electrons to accelerate rapidly towards the central anode wire.

As these electrons gain speed, they collide with other gas atoms, knocking loose more electrons. This process repeats, creating a controlled "avalanche" or cascade of charge that is localized around the anode wire. This phenomenon, known as gas amplification, creates a much larger and easily detectable amount of charge. Crucially, the total amount of charge created in this avalanche is directly proportional to the energy of the single, original particle that entered the detector.

This collection of charge on the anode wire generates a small electrical pulse. This weak signal is then sent to a series of electronics, including a charge amplifier to boost its strength and a pulse shaper to optimize its form for measurement. Finally, a data acquisition system measures the height (amplitude) of this pulse, which corresponds to the energy of the detected particle, and counts the total number of pulses over time to determine the radiation intensity. The entire system is managed by a control unit that sets parameters like the operating voltage.
