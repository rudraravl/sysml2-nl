This model represents a medical device known as an inhaler spacer system, designed to improve the delivery of aerosolized medication to a patient's lungs from a standard Metered-Dose Inhaler (MDI), or "puffer." The primary purpose of the system is to overcome the difficulty many patients have in coordinating the act of pressing the inhaler and breathing in at the exact same time.

The system consists of several key physical components that work together:

*   **Metered-Dose Inhaler (MDI):** This is the standard canister containing the pressurized medication. When actuated, it releases a high-velocity spray of the drug.
*   **Spacer Chamber:** This is a hollow tube or chamber that attaches to the MDI. When the inhaler is activated, the medication aerosol is sprayed into this chamber instead of directly into the patient's mouth. The chamber's main jobs are to slow down the fast-moving aerosol particles and allow the liquid propellant to evaporate, leaving finer, more breathable drug particles suspended in the air.
*   **One-Way Inspiratory Valve:** Situated between the chamber and the mouthpiece, this valve opens only when the patient breathes in. This allows the suspended medication to be drawn from the chamber into the lungs.
*   **One-Way Expiratory Valve:** This valve, typically located on the mouthpiece, opens only when the patient breathes out, directing the exhaled air away from the device and preventing the patient from blowing the medication out of the chamber.
*   **Mouthpiece:** This is the part of the device that the patient puts in their mouth to form a seal and inhale the medication.

The operational flow for delivering a dose of medication is as follows: The user first actuates the MDI, filling the spacer chamber with an aerosol cloud. Because the chamber holds the medication in suspension, the patient can then take a moment to place the mouthpiece in their mouth and begin to inhale. As they breathe in, their inhalation pressure opens the inspiratory valve, allowing them to draw the cloud of medication into their lungs. After holding their breath to allow the drug to deposit, they can exhale through the device; the expiratory valve opens to release their breath, while the inspiratory valve closes to protect the remaining aerosol. This entire process ensures a more effective and efficient delivery of the medication to the respiratory tract.
