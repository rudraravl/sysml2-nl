This model describes a complete meteorological anemometer system designed to accurately measure and report both wind speed and direction.

The physical unit is a classic cup-and-vane anemometer. It features a set of three rotating cups that catch the wind, causing them to spin at a rate proportional to the wind's speed. Alongside the cups, a flat direction vane or "tail" aligns itself with the wind's flow to indicate its direction.

Internally, these mechanical movements are converted into electrical signals. A rotation sensor measures how fast the cups are spinning, and an angle sensor measures the precise orientation of the direction vane. Both sensors produce a voltage signal corresponding to their measurement.

These raw voltage signals are sent to an onboard data processor. The processor is the "brain" of the system, using pre-configured calibration parameters—such as a speed conversion factor and a direction offset—to translate the raw signals into precise, scientific measurements. It operates in a continuous cycle of acquiring sensor data, computing the results, and outputting them.

The final output is a timestamped wind measurement package containing the calculated speed and direction. This system is designed to meet specific performance standards, requiring high accuracy for both speed (within ±0.5 m/s) and direction (within ±5 degrees), and is built to operate reliably across a wide range of temperatures and wind conditions.
