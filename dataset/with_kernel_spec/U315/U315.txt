This is a Miller-Abbott tube system, a medical device designed to treat small bowel obstructions by decompressing the intestine. The system works by removing accumulated gas and fluid that are trapped behind a blockage, thereby relieving pressure and swelling.

The core of the system is the **Miller-Abbott tube**, a long, flexible, double-lumen tube. At its tip, it features two key components: a small, inflatable **distal balloon** and a **mercury-weighted tip**. The weight helps guide the tube through the stomach and into the small intestine. The second component is an **aspiration lumen**, an internal channel with multiple holes near the tip, which is used for suctioning.

This tube is connected to an external **suction apparatus**, a machine that generates a continuous, low-pressure vacuum to draw out intestinal contents. The system is designed to interact with the patient's **small intestine**, which may be obstructed, distended, and experiencing normal muscular contractions known as peristalsis.

The procedure involves several distinct phases. First, the tube is inserted through the patient's nose and advanced into the small intestine. Once in position, the distal balloon is inflated. The natural peristaltic waves of the intestine then grip the inflated balloon, propelling the tube further along until its tip is positioned at or near the obstruction. At this point, the suction apparatus is activated to continuously aspirate the trapped gas and fluid, decompressing the bowel. The system monitors the tube's position and the effectiveness of the decompression before the balloon is deflated and the tube is finally removed.
