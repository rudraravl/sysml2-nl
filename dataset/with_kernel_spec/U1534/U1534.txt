This model represents a mechanical wheel and axle lifting system, a type of simple machine designed to provide mechanical advantage for raising heavy objects. The system is composed of three main parts: a large wheel, a smaller-diameter axle fixed to its center, and the load to be lifted.

The core function of the system is to multiply force. An operator applies an input force to the outer edge of the large wheel, causing both the wheel and the attached central axle to rotate together. This rotation generates torque that is transmitted through the axle. A load, specified in this case as a 75 kg mass, is attached to the axle by a rope or cable. As the axle turns, it winds the cable, converting the rotational torque into a powerful linear lifting force that raises the load. Because the force is applied to the large radius of the wheel and the work is done by the small radius of the axle, the system makes it possible to lift a heavy weight with a much smaller effort.

The system's operation is managed through different states. It can be `idle` when not in use, `engaged` when an operator begins applying force, actively `lifting` the load, or `holding` the load stationary at a certain height. The system is designed with specific performance requirements, including the ability to magnify an input force by at least five times, lift loads up to 100 kilograms, and operate with at least 85% efficiency to minimize energy loss.
