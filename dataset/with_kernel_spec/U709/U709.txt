This model represents a Hook Gauge Evaporimeter, a scientific instrument designed to measure the rate of evaporation from an open water surface. The system is centered around a standardized, cylindrical Evaporation Pan which holds the water exposed to the atmosphere.

To ensure accurate readings, the pan is connected to a Stilling Well, a smaller vertical tube that dampens waves and ripples on the water's surface. A high-precision Hook Gauge, a mechanical device with a pointed hook, is used within this well to precisely determine the water level.

The system's operation involves taking an initial depth measurement, and then another after a specific time interval has passed, typically 24 hours. A Data Logger records these depth readings along with their timestamps. By comparing the change in water level over time, the system calculates the evaporation rate.

An integrated Weather Station complements the system by monitoring key environmental factors that influence evaporation, such as air temperature, relative humidity, and wind speed. The entire system operates in a cycle, moving from an idle state to measuring, allowing the water to stabilize, recording the data, and finally computing the rate before preparing for the next measurement cycle.
