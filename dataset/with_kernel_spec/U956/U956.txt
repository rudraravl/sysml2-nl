This model represents a complete Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) 3D printing system, designed to transform digital three-dimensional models into physical objects. The system fabricates parts by melting a thermoplastic filament and precisely depositing it onto a build platform in successive layers, one on top of the other, until the final object is formed.

The printer is composed of several key interconnected subsystems. A central **Controller Board** acts as the brain, processing the digital model file and orchestrating the entire printing process by sending commands to all other components. It also monitors the print's progress and status, providing feedback to the user.

The physical construction is handled by three primary systems:

1.  The **Motion System** is a three-axis gantry (X, Y, Z) that physically moves the print head around the build area with high precision. It is responsible for tracing the shape of each layer of the object.
2.  The **Extrusion System** manages the material flow. It pulls filament from a spool and pushes it into the hotend at a controlled rate.
3.  The **Hotend** is the print head itself, containing a heating element and a nozzle. It melts the incoming filament to a precise temperature, allowing it to be extruded as a thin bead onto the build platform.

For print quality and adhesion, a **Heated Bed** serves as the build platform, maintaining a constant warm temperature to prevent the object from warping as it cools. A dedicated **Cooling Fan** is also included to provide directed airflow, which helps solidify the freshly deposited plastic quickly for sharper details and better overhang performance.

The entire system is designed to work with various materials like PLA, ABS, and PETG, each requiring different temperature settings for the hotend and heated bed. The printing process follows a clear sequence: the system first initializes and homes its axes, then preheats the nozzle and bed to their target temperatures. Once ready, it begins printing layer by layer, with the controller coordinating the motion, extrusion, and cooling until the object is complete. Finally, the system cools down, allowing the finished part to be safely removed.
