This is a non-invasive, portable medical diagnostic system known as the Trimprob device, designed to detect potential pathological conditions in biological tissue. It operates by identifying electromagnetic anomalies, which can indicate abnormal cells, without the need for surgery or biopsies.

The core of the system is a handheld probe that emits very low-power electromagnetic signals and measures the tissue's response. The device works by sweeping through a range of frequencies, from 10 kHz to 500 kHz, and analyzing the tissue's unique resonance patterns at each step. Healthy and abnormal tissues respond differently to these signals, and the device is designed to detect these subtle variations.

Internally, the system is composed of several key components working in concert. A signal generator creates the precise, controlled electromagnetic waves, which are then transmitted by the probe head. The same probe head detects the return signal from the tissue. This response data is then sent to a response analyzer, which uses a "Resonance Pattern Recognition" algorithm to process the information and calculate an "anomaly score" and a confidence level for the diagnosis.

A central control processor manages the entire operation, from initiating the scan sequence to processing the results. The entire unit is powered by a rechargeable battery pack, allowing for at least four hours of continuous use in a clinical setting. The diagnostic results are presented to the operator in real-time on a built-in display screen.

The device follows a clear operational workflow. After being powered on, it enters a ready state. An operator can then initiate a scan, which causes the device to automatically sweep through its frequency range, collect data, analyze it, and display the final results, all within about two minutes. For safety, the device is strictly required to keep its energy emissions below 1 milliwatt.
