This model describes an actively controlled industrial rotor system, designed to spin a heavy rotor to a precise rotational speed and maintain it under strict performance and safety constraints. It's a self-contained unit that could be part of a larger machine, such as a flywheel energy storage device, a large-scale pump, or an industrial centrifuge.

The system consists of four primary components working together: a powerful electric motor, the physical rotor, a dedicated speed sensor, and a digital controller. The rotor is a significant mechanical part, specified with a mass of 15 kg and a radius of 0.25 meters.

In operation, the controller acts as the brain of the system. It continuously receives speed measurements from the sensor and compares this real-time data to a pre-set target speed of approximately 3000 RPM (314 rad/s). Based on any deviation, the controller calculates the precise amount of torque required and sends a command to the motor. The motor then draws electrical power and, with 92% efficiency, converts it into the mechanical force needed to accelerate or maintain the rotor's speed. This creates a persistent closed-loop control system that ensures operational accuracy.

The system's lifecycle is managed through distinct states. It begins in an `idle` state. Upon receiving a command, it enters a `starting` phase, accelerating until it reaches 95% of its target speed. It then transitions into a stable `operating` state, where it actively maintains its speed. The system is also designed for safety; it constantly monitors its own vibration levels and temperature. If vibrations exceed a critical threshold, it automatically triggers an emergency shutdown, spinning down to a safe stop to prevent mechanical failure.
