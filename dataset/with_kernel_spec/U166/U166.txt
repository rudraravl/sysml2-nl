This model describes a Biothesiometer, a medical device designed to quantitatively assess peripheral nerve function by measuring a patient's sensitivity to vibration. It is primarily used for the early detection and ongoing monitoring of peripheral neuropathy, a common complication of conditions like diabetes.

The system consists of a handheld probe with a vibrating tip that is placed against the patient's skin, typically on a location like the big toe. This probe is connected to a main control unit that also features a display panel and is powered by an internal power supply. The patient holds a separate response button to interact with the device during the test.

The core function of the device is to perform an automated threshold test. The test begins by applying a very faint, imperceptible vibration through the probe at a fixed frequency (typically 120 Hz). In a series of timed trials, the device's control module systematically increases the intensity (amplitude) of the vibration in small, precise steps. During each trial, the system monitors for a signal from the patient's response button.

When the patient first feels the vibration, they press the button. The control module registers this response and records the specific vibration amplitude at which it occurred. This value is the patient's "vibration perception threshold." Based on this threshold, potentially compared against normal values for the patient's age and the test location, the system calculates and displays a quantitative "neuropathy score." This provides a clinician with an objective measure of the patient's sensory nerve function.

The entire process is managed by the internal control module, which executes the test protocol, controls the vibration generator, processes the patient's response, and presents the current test status and final results on the display panel. The system is designed with safety limits to ensure the vibration amplitude never exceeds a predefined maximum level.
