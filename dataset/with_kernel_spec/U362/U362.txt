This is a Pulse Duplicator system, a specialized piece of laboratory equipment designed to simulate the human circulatory system for testing and evaluating prosthetic heart valves. Its primary function is to replicate the pulsating blood flow and pressure conditions of a human heart in a controlled, repeatable environment.

The system is built around a central fluid circuit. A **System Controller** acts as the brain, defining the test parameters like the desired heart rate, test duration, and a specific pressure waveform with systolic and diastolic phases. This controller sends commands to a **Pulsatile Pump**, which serves as the artificial heart, generating a rhythmic flow of fluid that mimics a heartbeat.

As the fluid leaves the pump, it enters a **Compliance Chamber**, a component that simulates the natural elasticity of the aorta and other major arteries. This helps to create a more realistic, less sharp pressure wave. The fluid then flows towards the **Test Valve** section, where the prosthetic heart valve being evaluated is installed.

To measure the valve's performance, a suite of sensors is used. An upstream **Pressure Sensor** measures the pressure just before the valve, and a downstream **Pressure Sensor** measures it just after. This allows the system to calculate the pressure drop, or gradient, across the valve—a critical performance metric. A **Flow Meter** is also integrated into the circuit to measure the volume of fluid passing through the valve over time.

Finally, a **Resistance Valve** at the end of the circuit simulates the "afterload," which is the resistance the heart has to pump against from the rest of the body's vascular system. This completes the realistic hemodynamic loop. During a test, the system runs for a set duration, continuously collecting pressure and flow data to provide a comprehensive performance analysis of the heart valve.
