This model describes an Antidote Treatment Nerve Agent Auto-injector (ATNAA). It is a portable, single-use medical device designed for emergency, on-the-spot treatment of nerve agent exposure, typically for military or first-responder personnel.

The core of the device is a powerful, spring-loaded mechanism. Its primary function is to automatically inject a pre-filled, two-drug antidote into a person's muscle tissue. The antidote consists of two separate medications, Atropine and Pralidoxime, which are stored within a special dual-chamber cartridge inside the injector.

To use the device, an individual first disengages a safety mechanism to prevent accidental activation. They then press the device firmly against their body, which triggers the injection process. This releases the compressed spring, which drives a plunger assembly forward. The plunger applies high pressure to the dual-chamber cartridge, forcing the two antidotes to flow out through an integrated needle assembly and deep into the muscle tissue.

The system includes an internal pressure sensor to monitor the injection and confirm that the full dose has been delivered. Once the injection is complete, a visual indicator window on the device changes to show the user that the administration was successful.
