This model describes a complete digital pH sensing system built around an Ion-Sensitive Field-Effect Transistor (ISFET). It is a solid-state sensor designed to precisely measure the acidity or alkalinity (pH) of water-based solutions by directly converting a chemical property into a digital electrical signal.

The core of the system is the ISFET sensor, which consists of several integrated components. The process begins at the **Ion-Sensitive Membrane**, the part that makes direct contact with the liquid being measured. This specialized membrane chemically reacts to the concentration of hydrogen ions in the solution, generating a small electrical surface voltage that is directly proportional to the solution's pH.

This surface voltage is used to control an **FET Transistor**. Essentially, the membrane acts as the gate of the transistor, modulating the flow of electrical current through it. A change in the solution's pH causes a corresponding change in the current flowing from the transistor. To provide a stable measurement, a **Reference Electrode** is also used to create a constant reference voltage.

Because the current from the transistor is very small, it is first sent to a **Transimpedance Amplifier**. This component converts the weak current signal into a stronger, more easily measurable voltage signal. This analog voltage is then fed into an **Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC)**, which translates the continuous voltage level into a discrete digital number.

Finally, a **Microcontroller** acts as the brain of the system. It takes the raw digital value from the ADC and applies calibration and temperature compensation algorithms. This processing corrects for environmental factors and sensor characteristics to calculate a final, highly accurate pH value. This processed digital pH reading is then provided as the system's main output.

The system is designed to meet stringent performance requirements, including a measurement range from pH 0 to 14, an accuracy of ±0.02 pH units, and a fast response time of under 10 seconds. It is built to be robust, operating in temperatures from 0°C to 100°C, and exhibits excellent long-term stability.
