This model describes a medical eyepatch system designed to protect and shield an injured or recovering eye. Its primary purpose is to block ambient light and prevent physical contact, creating an optimal environment for healing while ensuring the device is both secure and comfortable for the wearer.

The eyepatch is composed of three main physical components that work together:

1.  **Occlusive Pad:** This is the core component that fits directly over the eye. It is made from a soft, thick padding material designed to completely block at least 98% of incoming light. Its shape and size provide full coverage for the eye socket.

2.  **Cushion Layer:** Attached to the inner rim of the occlusive pad, this layer is made of a highly breathable and soft material. It serves as the direct interface with the wearer's skin, distributing the pressure of the eyepatch evenly to prevent discomfort and irritation, especially during long periods of use.

3.  **Adjustable Strap:** This is an elastic and durable strap that secures the eyepatch to the wearer's head. Its length can be adjusted to fit different head sizes, providing the necessary tension to hold the pad firmly in place without slipping during normal head movements, while also allowing the user to fine-tune the fit for maximum comfort.

The eyepatch operates through a clear sequence of states. It begins in an 'idle' state when not in use. When needed, a user goes through a 'fitting' process, which involves correctly positioning the pad over the eye and then adjusting the strap for a snug but comfortable fit. Once secured, the eyepatch enters its 'active' state, where it continuously performs its primary function of protecting the eye. During this active state, the system implicitly monitors for comfort; if the fit becomes too tight or loose, it requires readjustment. Finally, the eyepatch is removed, returning it to its idle state.
