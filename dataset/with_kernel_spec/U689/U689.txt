This model describes a galvanometer, an electromechanical instrument designed to detect and measure small electric currents. Its core function is to convert an electrical current into a proportional mechanical rotation, which is then displayed visually by a pointer on a calibrated scale.

The system is built around several key components that work together. A powerful permanent magnet creates a stable and uniform magnetic field. Suspended within this field is a movable coil assembly made of many turns of fine wire. When an electrical current is passed through this coil, it interacts with the magnetic field, generating a twisting force (torque) that causes the coil to rotate.

Attached to the coil is a spring mechanism. As the coil rotates, this spring tightens, producing a counter-torque that resists the rotation. The coil will continue to turn until the magnetic torque from the current is perfectly balanced by the restoring torque from the spring. A lightweight pointer needle is also attached to the coil, moving along with it. This needle points to a value on a calibrated scale, providing a direct visual reading of the current's magnitude.

To ensure a stable and quick reading, the system includes a damping mechanism. This prevents the pointer from oscillating or overshooting its final position, allowing it to settle smoothly and accurately.

The galvanometer operates through a clear sequence: it starts in an idle state with the pointer at zero. When a current is applied, the system becomes energized, causing the pointer to deflect. Once the magnetic and spring forces balance, the pointer settles at a final position to display the measurement. When the current is removed, the spring returns the coil and pointer back to the zero position, ready for the next measurement.
