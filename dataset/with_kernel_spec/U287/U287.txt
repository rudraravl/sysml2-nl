This is a specialized medical and safety device known as a Lung Counter System. Its primary purpose is to detect and measure the amount of gamma-ray-emitting radioactive material inside a person's chest, specifically within the lungs. This is crucial for monitoring workers in nuclear facilities or for assessing internal contamination after an accidental exposure.

The system is constructed as a heavily shielded chamber to ensure accurate measurements. The core of this is the **Lead Shielding**, which forms a small room or enclosure that drastically reduces natural background radiation from the environment. Inside this shielded room, a person sits in an adjustable **Positioning Chair**, which ensures that they are placed in the exact same, repeatable position relative to the sensors for every measurement.

An array of four sensitive scintillation detectors (**DetectorArray**) is strategically positioned around the person's thorax. When radioactive materials inside the lungs decay, they emit gamma photons, which are captured by these detectors. The detectors convert each photon into a small electrical pulse.

These pulses are fed into a **Multi-Channel Analyzer (MCA)**, a piece of electronics that sorts the pulses based on their energy level and counts them, creating a detailed energy spectrum. This spectrum is a unique fingerprint that can reveal which specific radioactive nuclides are present.

The spectrum data is then sent to an **Analysis Computer**. The computer's software performs a series of critical steps: it first subtracts a previously-measured background radiation spectrum, identifies the characteristic energy peaks in the person's net spectrum, and then uses calibration factors to calculate the precise activity (in Becquerels) of the identified radioactive materials.

An operator manages the entire process through a **Display Console**. The system operates through a clear sequence of states: it starts in an idle state, moves to positioning the subject, acquires a background count, acquires the subject's measurement, analyzes the data, and finally reports the results on the console before returning to idle. The final result indicates the total radiation counts, the calculated activity, and a list of the specific radioactive materials detected.
