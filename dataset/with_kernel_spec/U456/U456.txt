This is a clinical diagnostic system used in ophthalmology, known as the Worth 4 Dot Test. Its purpose is to evaluate a patient's binocular vision—the ability of their two eyes to work together to create a single, fused image. The system is used by clinicians to detect problems such as suppression (where the brain ignores the input from one eye) or diplopia (double vision).

The system consists of several key components that work in concert:

1.  **Illumination Unit:** This is a light source that projects a standardized pattern of four dots: one red, two green, and one white. It provides controlled lighting to ensure the test is consistent.

2.  **Filter Glasses:** The patient wears a pair of special red-green anaglyph glasses. These glasses are crucial as they separate what each eye sees. The right eye, looking through a red lens, can only perceive the red and white dots. The left eye, looking through a green lens, can only perceive the two green dots and the white dot.

3.  **Patient Interface:** This represents the means by which the patient reports their perception back to the clinician or system. The patient simply states how many dots they see in total.

4.  **Diagnostic Processor:** This is the analytical part of the system. It takes the patient's reported perception (the number of dots seen) and automatically determines a clinical diagnosis based on pre-defined rules.

The test procedure involves the patient wearing the filter glasses and looking at the illuminated four-dot pattern from a specific distance. The system then interprets their response:
*   Seeing **four dots** indicates normal binocular vision, as the brain has successfully fused the images from both eyes.
*   Seeing **two dots** (only the red ones) suggests the brain is suppressing the vision from the left eye.
*   Seeing **three dots** (only the green ones) suggests the brain is suppressing the vision from the right eye.
*   Seeing **five dots** (two red and three green) indicates double vision (diplopia), as the brain sees the input from both eyes but cannot merge them into a single image.
