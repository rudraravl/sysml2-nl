This is a Hydrocollator system, a thermostatically controlled water bath designed for use in physical therapy and clinical settings. Its primary purpose is to heat and maintain moist heat packs at a constant, safe, and therapeutic temperature, ensuring they are ready for patient treatment and pain relief.

The system is built around a main insulated tank that holds a large volume of water. Inside the tank is a high-power heating element responsible for raising the water temperature. A built-in rack or pack holder with multiple slots allows for several moist heat packs to be immersed and heated simultaneously.

The core of the system's operation is its thermostatic control loop. A temperature sensor continuously measures the water temperature inside the tank and feeds this reading to a controller. This controller compares the current temperature to a pre-defined therapeutic range (e.g., 71°C to 79°C). If the water temperature drops below the lower limit, the controller activates the heating element. Once the temperature reaches the upper limit, it deactivates the heater. This constant cycle ensures the water is always maintained within the precise temperature range required for effective therapy.

The system features a simple user interface, primarily an indicator light. This light typically shows red when the unit is in its initial heat-up phase after being turned on. Once the water reaches the target operating temperature, the light turns green, signaling to the user that the packs are ready for use. The system operates in several states: it is `powerOff` when inactive, goes into an `initialHeating` state upon startup, and then enters a `maintaining` state where it actively regulates the temperature for the remainder of its operation.
