This model describes an autonomous, two-wheeled mobile robot designed to navigate to a specific location and perform a physical task. The robot is a self-contained unit, weighing 30 kg and powered by an onboard battery.

Its core components work together to achieve its mission. A central control computer serves as the robot's brain, orchestrating all operations. For mobility, it uses a differential drive system with two independently controlled motorized wheels, allowing it to move forward, backward, and turn. To understand its surroundings, the robot is equipped with a sensor array that includes a position sensor to determine its location and proximity sensors on the front and rear to detect obstacles. A key safety requirement is that the robot must always maintain a distance of at least half a meter from any detected obstacle.

The robot's primary mission involves receiving a destination, navigating to it, and then using its manipulator arm to perform a task. This arm is equipped with a gripper capable of applying significant force to hold objects.

The robot's operational cycle is managed through different states. It begins in an `idle` state, and upon receiving a command, it enters a `navigating` state to plan a safe path. Once a path is calculated, it begins `moving`. If it detects an obstacle, it will stop and re-plan its route. Upon successfully reaching its destination, it transitions to an `executing` state to use its manipulator arm. After the task is finished, it enters a `completed` state before returning to `idle`, ready for a new assignment.
