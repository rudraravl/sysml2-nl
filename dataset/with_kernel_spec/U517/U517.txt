This model describes a complete hydraulic compressor system, commonly known as a Hydraulic Power Unit (HPU). Its purpose is to generate and supply high-pressure hydraulic fluid to power external machinery, such as actuators, presses, or motors.

The system is centered around an electric motor which drives a hydraulic pump. This motor-pump assembly is the core of the system, responsible for converting electrical energy into hydraulic power. The pump draws low-pressure hydraulic fluid from a large reservoir and pressurizes it to a high, specified level.

A sophisticated system controller serves as the brain, managing the entire operation. It continuously monitors the output pressure using a dedicated pressure sensor. Based on this feedback, the controller adjusts the speed of the electric motor to precisely maintain the desired system pressure, ensuring stable and consistent performance for the downstream equipment.

For safety and system protection, a mechanical pressure relief valve is integrated into the high-pressure circuit. If the pressure ever exceeds a safe maximum limit, this valve automatically opens and diverts excess fluid back to the reservoir, preventing damage to the components. The controller also has a protection function that can trigger an emergency shutdown in case of over-pressure or over-temperature conditions.

The system also includes an accumulator, which is a device that stores pressurized fluid. This helps to smooth out pressure fluctuations from the pump and can provide a temporary boost in flow rate when needed. A control valve is positioned at the system's output to regulate the final flow of pressurized fluid being delivered to the external machinery.

In operation, the system follows a controlled sequence. Upon receiving a start command, it performs initialization checks, such as verifying the fluid level. It then enters a continuous operating loop where it monitors and adjusts pressure. The system is designed to be shut down either through a normal, controlled sequence or via an immediate emergency stop if a critical fault is detected.
