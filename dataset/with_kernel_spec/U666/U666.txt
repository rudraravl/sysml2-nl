This model describes an electroscope, a scientific instrument designed to detect the presence and relative magnitude of an electric charge on an object.

The device is constructed from several key components. At the top, there is a conductive metal cap which serves as the primary interface for introducing an electric charge. This cap is connected to a conducting rod that extends downwards. Attached to the bottom end of this rod are two thin, lightweight metal leaves. The entire conductive assembly (cap, rod, and leaves) is mounted using an insulating support, which prevents the electric charge from leaking away. Finally, a transparent protective enclosure surrounds the delicate leaves to shield them from air currents and physical damage.

The electroscope's operation is based on electrostatic repulsion. When a charged object touches or is brought near the metal cap, charge is transferred and distributed down the rod to both metal leaves. Since both leaves receive the same type of charge (either positive or negative), they repel each other. This repulsive force causes the leaves to spread apart, or "diverge."

The angle of divergence between the leaves provides a visual indication of the amount of charge present—a larger charge results in a stronger repulsion and a wider angle. The system can be in an uncharged state with the leaves hanging collapsed, a charging state as charge is distributed, or a charged state where the leaves are diverged. The device can be returned to a neutral state by grounding it, which allows the charge to dissipate and the leaves to collapse.
