This is a structured-light 3D scanner system designed to create highly accurate, three-dimensional digital models of physical objects. The system works by projecting a series of light patterns onto an object and analyzing their deformation to calculate its surface geometry.

The scanner is built around several key components working in concert. A central, high-resolution **projector** beams specific, structured patterns of light, such as Gray codes or phase-shifted lines, onto the target object. A pair of high-framerate **stereo cameras** (a left and a right one), positioned at a fixed distance from each other, then capture images of how these light patterns bend and distort across the object's surface. The "brain" of the system is a central **processing unit** with significant memory and computing power.

The scanning process follows a precise sequence of operations. It begins with a calibration phase to ensure accuracy. Once calibrated, the system enters a scanning loop where the projector illuminates the object with a pattern, and both cameras simultaneously capture the result. The processing unit then analyzes the images from the two different perspectives. By using triangulation—comparing the distortion in the captured patterns against the original projected pattern—it calculates the precise 3D coordinates for millions of points on the object's surface. This cycle repeats until enough data is collected, at which point the system combines it to generate and output a final, dense point cloud representing the object's shape.

The system is designed to meet specific performance standards, including a depth accuracy of 0.5 millimeters and a resolution of 1920x1080. It can scan objects within a working volume of 30cm x 30cm x 50cm from a typical distance of about 80cm. The entire process, from initialization through calibration, projection, capture, and final processing, operates through a series of distinct states to ensure a reliable workflow.
