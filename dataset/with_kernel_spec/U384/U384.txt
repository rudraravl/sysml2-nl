This model describes a Shirley Drain System, a passive medical device designed for continuously draining unwanted serous fluid from a patient's body cavities, such as the chest or abdomen after surgery. The system operates using gravity rather than mechanical suction.

The core of the system is a flexible, perforated drain catheter made of medical-grade silicone. This tube is placed inside the patient's body cavity, where its multiple small perforations allow fluid to seep in.

Connected to the outside end of this catheter is a one-way anti-reflux valve. This crucial safety component ensures that once fluid leaves the body, it cannot flow back in, which helps prevent infection. The valve is designed to open only when there is sufficient fluid pressure from the patient's side.

The valve then leads to a graduated collection reservoir. This is a 400 mL container designed to accumulate the drained fluid. It features clear markings every 10 mL, allowing healthcare professionals to easily and accurately measure the volume of fluid being collected, which is an important indicator of the patient's recovery. The reservoir also includes a vent to equalize pressure.

In operation, the entire system relies on gravity. The collection reservoir is placed at a lower elevation than the patient's body, creating a gentle pressure gradient that passively pulls fluid from the body cavity, through the catheter, past the one-way valve, and into the collection chamber for measurement and eventual disposal.
