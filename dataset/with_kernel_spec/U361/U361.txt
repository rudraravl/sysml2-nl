This model describes the Prosorba Immunoadsorption System, a therapeutic medical device used to treat certain autoimmune disorders. Its purpose is to remove harmful antibodies and immune complexes directly from a patient's blood in an external circuit, a process similar to dialysis but for the immune system.

The system works by drawing whole blood from a patient and circulating it through a series of specialized components. First, a **Plasma Separator** uses a membrane to divide the incoming blood into two parts: plasma (the liquid component) and blood cells.

The separated plasma is then directed into the core therapeutic component, the **Prosorba Column**. This column contains a substance called Protein A, which has a high affinity for specific types of antibodies (IgG) that are often responsible for autoimmune disease. As the plasma flows through the column, these pathogenic antibodies bind to the Protein A and are effectively filtered out.

Simultaneously, the separated blood cells bypass the treatment column and are sent to a **Blood Recombiner**. After passing through the Prosorba Column, the purified plasma is also sent to this recombiner, where it is mixed back together with the blood cells to reconstitute whole blood.

This entire extracorporeal circuit is driven by a precise **Blood Pump**, which maintains a controlled flow rate. For patient safety, a **Safety Monitor** continuously checks critical parameters like blood pressure and flow within the circuit. If any readings go outside safe limits, it can trigger an alarm or place the system into a fault state.

The entire treatment is a continuous loop that runs for a set duration or until a target volume of plasma has been processed. The system operates through distinct phases, including an initial **priming** phase to prepare the circuit, the main **treating** phase where blood is processed, and a final **rinsing** and completion state. The end result is that the reconstituted, treated blood, now with a significantly reduced concentration of harmful antibodies, is returned to the patient.
