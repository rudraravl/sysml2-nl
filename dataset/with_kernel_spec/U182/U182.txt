This model describes a Cardiopulmonary Bypass (CPB) system, commonly known as a heart-lung machine. It is a critical medical life-support system used during open-heart surgery to temporarily take over the functions of a patient's heart and lungs, allowing surgeons to operate on a still, bloodless heart.

The system works by creating an external circuit for the patient's blood. The process begins with a **Venous Cannula**, a tube that drains deoxygenated blood from the patient's major veins. This blood flows into a **Venous Reservoir**, which collects the blood and acts as a buffer.

From the reservoir, a **Roller Pump**—the system's "heart"—propels the blood through the rest of the circuit. The blood is first pushed into a **Membrane Oxygenator**, which serves as the artificial lung. Here, it undergoes gas exchange: oxygen is added to the blood, and carbon dioxide is removed.

Immediately following oxygenation, the blood passes through a **Heat Exchanger**. This component precisely controls the blood's temperature, either cooling the patient to reduce metabolic demand during surgery or warming the blood back to a normal body temperature before it is returned.

As a final safety measure, the now oxygenated and temperature-controlled blood flows through an **Arterial Line Filter**. This filter is designed to capture and remove any microscopic air bubbles, clots, or other particulates. Finally, the clean, re-oxygenated blood is returned to the patient's arterial system through an **Arterial Cannula**.

The entire operation is managed by a **Bypass Controller** (representing a perfusionist's control console) and monitored by a suite of sensors. These include **flow meters**, **pressure monitors**, **temperature probes**, an **oxygen saturation monitor**, and a **level sensor** for the reservoir. This continuous monitoring ensures that critical parameters like blood flow rate, pressure, oxygen levels, and temperature are maintained within strict, safe limits, with alarms in place to alert the medical team to any potential issues.
