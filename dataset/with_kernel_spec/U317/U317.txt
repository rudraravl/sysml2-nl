This model describes a radiation therapy system featuring a multileaf collimator (MLC), a highly precise medical device used to shape radiation beams for cancer treatment. Its primary function is to conform the radiation beam to the exact shape of a tumor, delivering a therapeutic dose while protecting surrounding healthy tissue.

The core of the system is the multileaf collimator itself, which consists of 160 thin, individually controllable metal plates called "leaves." These leaves are arranged in two opposing banks of 80. By moving in and out of the radiation field, these leaves collectively create a custom-shaped aperture for the beam to pass through. This shape is dictated by a pre-loaded treatment plan, which specifies the exact target position for each of the 160 leaves.

Each leaf is driven by its own dedicated motor and has a high-precision position sensor. This allows for closed-loop control, ensuring each leaf reaches its target position with an accuracy of 0.1 millimeters, as verified by the sensor feedback. A central controller orchestrates this entire process. It receives the treatment plan, sends commands to the motor drives to position all the leaves, and continuously verifies their locations against the plan's specifications.

The system works in conjunction with a radiation source, which generates the raw, unshaped therapeutic beam. A critical component is the safety interlock system. This system acts as a fail-safe, preventing the radiation source from activating unless all conditions are met. These conditions include confirming that the treatment room door is closed, no emergency stop has been triggered, and, most importantly, that all 160 collimator leaves have been verified to be in their correct positions according to the plan.

In operation, the system starts in a standby mode. A treatment plan is loaded, and the controller commands the leaves to move into position. Once the leaves are correctly set and the safety system performs its final checks, it allows the radiation source to activate, delivering the precisely shaped beam to the patient. During treatment, the leaf positions are monitored continuously to ensure accuracy and safety.
