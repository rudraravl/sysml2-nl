This model represents a Taccola hydraulic system, an automated, self-regulating machine designed to pump water using only the power of gravity and water flow. It's essentially a clever, historical design for a hydraulic engine that requires no external power or manual intervention.

The core of the system is an upper reservoir that collects water from a source. Once the water in the reservoir reaches a specific, predetermined level, an automatic overflow siphon is triggered. This siphon rapidly releases the accumulated volume of water in a powerful, controlled flow.

This sudden rush of water is channeled to strike the buckets of a large undershot water wheel, causing it to rotate and generating mechanical power. This rotational energy is transferred through a sturdy oak axle shaft to a crank mechanism. The crank's function is to convert the wheel's continuous rotation into a reciprocating, back-and-forth linear motion. This motion, in turn, drives a set of pumping bellows. These bellows act as the system's pump, drawing water from a lower source and lifting it to an elevated destination.

The entire system operates in a continuous, automated cycle. It transitions through several operational states: it remains idle until a water supply is available, then enters a filling state as the reservoir collects water. When full, it transitions to an overflowing and operating state where the wheel turns and pumping occurs. As the reservoir empties through the siphon, the flow stops, and the system automatically returns to the filling state, beginning the cycle anew.
