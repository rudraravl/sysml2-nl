This is an automated condensate pumping subsystem, typically found in industrial settings like power plants or large manufacturing facilities. Its purpose is to collect liquid condensate (water formed from condensed steam) from a process and automatically transfer it to a main storage tank for reuse.

The system is built around several key components that work together in a sequence. It starts with a **Condensate Receiver**, a small tank that acts as a temporary collection point for the incoming condensate. This receiver is equipped with level sensors that monitor how full it is.

The heart of the system is a powerful, electrically-driven **Condensate Pump**. The operation of this pump is controlled automatically by the water level in the receiver. When the condensate level reaches a pre-set high point, a start signal is sent to the pump. The pump then turns on, drawing condensate from the receiver and pressurizing it. It runs through a sequence of states including starting, running, and stopping, and can enter a faulted state if an issue is detected.

Once pressurized, the condensate is pushed through a network of **Discharge Piping** that connects the pump to a large **Storage Tank**, which is the final destination for the fluid.

To ensure the system is working correctly, it is fitted with a suite of sensors. **Pressure transmitters** are placed at the pump's inlet and outlet to monitor the suction and discharge pressures, verifying the pump is performing as expected. A **flow transmitter** is installed in the discharge piping to measure the rate at which condensate is being transferred. This constant monitoring allows the system to operate safely and efficiently, ensuring a continuous and reliable transfer of condensate.
