This is a comprehensive underwater sound detection and recording system, commonly known as a hydrophone. It is designed to operate reliably in high-pressure, deep-sea environments, capturing acoustic signals, processing them, and storing them as digital audio data for analysis.

The entire system is housed within a durable, waterproof titanium casing designed to withstand significant underwater pressure. Its core component is a sensitive piezoelectric transducer, which acts as an underwater microphone. This transducer detects acoustic pressure waves in the water over a wide frequency range and converts them into a weak electrical signal.

This initial signal is immediately sent to a preamplifier, which boosts its strength. From there, an analog-to-digital converter (ADC) transforms the amplified analog signal into a high-resolution, high-sample-rate digital audio stream. This digital data is then processed by an onboard signal processor, which applies a bandpass filter to remove unwanted noise and isolate the frequencies of interest. The cleaned, processed digital audio is then stored on a local data recorder and can also be output directly from the system for real-time monitoring. A dedicated internal power supply provides the necessary voltage and capacity to run all the electronic components.

The hydrophone system operates in several states. It can idle in a low-power `standby` mode until it receives an activation signal. Once activated, it enters an `active` monitoring state, listening for sounds. Upon detecting a signal, it can automatically begin `recording` the data. When the recording is complete, it returns to the active monitoring state, ready to capture new events. A deactivation signal returns the system to standby.
