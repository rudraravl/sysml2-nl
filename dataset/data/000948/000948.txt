This model describes a human-powered treadle pump system, designed to lift water from a source, like a well, into a delivery tank for applications such as small-scale irrigation.

The core of the system is the treadle pump assembly, which features a dual-cylinder design for a more continuous water flow. An operator powers the pump by standing on two foot-pedals, or treadles, and shifting their weight in an alternating rhythm. Each treadle is mechanically linked by a connecting rod to a piston housed within a dedicated piston chamber.

The pumping action is a coordinated cycle. As the operator presses one treadle down, the linked piston descends. Simultaneously, the opposite treadle rises, pulling its piston upwards. This upward-moving piston creates suction, drawing water from the source through a one-way inlet check valve into its chamber. At the same time, the descending piston (which was filled on the previous stroke) compresses the water inside its chamber. This pressure forces the water out through a one-way outlet check valve and into the delivery pipe leading to the tank.

This dual-action mechanism, governed by four check valves (an inlet and outlet for each chamber), ensures that with each step, one chamber is drawing water in while the other is pushing water out. This prevents backflow and creates a steadier stream of delivered water compared to a single-piston pump.

The system has several operating states. It begins in an idle state when not in use. When an operator begins pumping, it enters a brief priming phase to fill the chambers and pipes with water. Once water is flowing, it transitions to a continuous operating state, cycling through the alternating up-and-down strokes of the left and right pistons until the operator stops.
