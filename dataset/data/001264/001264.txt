This model describes a high-precision strain measurement system designed for applications like structural health monitoring, material testing, and stress analysis. Its purpose is to accurately measure the tiny deformations (strain) that occur in an object when a mechanical force or load is applied.

The system is composed of several specialized electronic components working in sequence. At its core is a **Strain Gauge**, a sensitive resistor that is bonded to the surface of the object being tested. When the object stretches or compresses, the gauge deforms with it, causing a minute change in its electrical resistance.

This tiny resistance change is detected by a **Wheatstone Bridge**, an electrical circuit that converts the resistance shift into a very small, proportional voltage signal. This analog signal is typically weak and susceptible to noise.

To address this, the signal is passed to a **Signal Conditioner**. This component's job is to clean up and strengthen the signal by amplifying it and filtering out unwanted high and low-frequency electrical noise.

Next, an **Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC)** takes the clean, amplified analog voltage and converts it into a stream of digital numbers. This is a crucial step that allows a computer or microprocessor to read and interpret the sensor's output.

Finally, a **Data Processor** takes this raw digital data and performs a series of calculations. It applies calibration factors to ensure accuracy, corrects for any temperature fluctuations that might affect the reading, and converts the processed data into a final, precise strain measurement.

The entire system operates through different modes, including an idle state, a calibration phase to set a zero-point, and an active measuring state. It is designed to meet strict performance requirements for accuracy, speed, and stability across a wide temperature range.
