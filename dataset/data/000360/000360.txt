This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of physical quantities and units for the scientific field of thermodynamics. It is not a model of a specific machine or physical object, but rather a formal digital dictionary based on the international standard ISO 80000-5. Its purpose is to provide a clear, unambiguous, and machine-readable foundation for engineers and scientists to use when creating models of any system involving heat and energy.

The library defines a wide range of thermodynamic concepts, including:

*   **Fundamental Quantities:** It establishes core concepts like temperature (in both Kelvin and Celsius), energy, and heat. It distinguishes between different forms of energy critical to thermodynamics, such as internal energy, enthalpy, Gibbs energy, and Helmholtz energy.
*   **Material Properties:** It defines how materials behave in response to temperature changes. This includes properties like the linear and cubic expansion coefficients (how much a material expands when heated), thermal conductivity (how well it transfers heat), and specific heat capacity (how much energy is needed to raise its temperature).
*   **Heat Transfer Characteristics:** The library defines quantities that describe the movement of heat, such as heat flow rate, thermal conductance (how easily heat flows through an object), and its inverse, thermal resistance. It also includes concepts like the coefficient of heat transfer, which is crucial for analyzing insulation and cooling systems.
*   **Thermodynamic Processes and Efficiency:** It includes definitions for abstract but essential concepts like entropy, as well as practical metrics like thermal efficiency, which is used to measure the performance of heat engines.
*   **Properties of Gas and Vapor Mixtures:** A significant portion of the library is dedicated to describing mixtures, particularly moist air. It defines quantities like absolute and relative humidity, dew-point temperature, and various mass ratios of water vapor to dry gas, which are essential for applications in meteorology, HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning), and industrial drying processes.

Each quantity is precisely defined with its standard symbol, its relationship to other quantities, and its measurement units (e.g., Joules per Kelvin for entropy, or Watts per meter-Kelvin for thermal conductivity). In essence, this system provides the fundamental building blocks and rules for accurately describing and simulating the thermal behavior of any other real-world system.
