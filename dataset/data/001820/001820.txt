This is a nutating disc engine system, a type of mechanical motor designed to convert the energy from a pressurized fluid into continuous rotational power.

The core of the engine is a central disc housed within a specially shaped chamber. This "nutating disc" doesn't spin in a conventional way; instead, it wobbles in a predictable, circular pattern around a central sphere support. This unique wobbling, or nutating, motion is the key to its operation.

The process begins when a pressurized fluid, like compressed air or hydraulic fluid, is fed into the chamber through an intake valve. The pressure of the fluid pushes against the face of the disc, causing it to wobble. As the disc nutates, it's mechanically connected to an output shaft in such a way that its complex wobbling motion is translated into a smooth, continuous rotation of the shaft.

Simultaneously, the movement of the disc itself acts like a valve, partitioning the chamber and forcing the now lower-pressure, used fluid out through an exhaust valve. This continuous cycle of admitting high-pressure fluid, converting its energy into the disc's wobble, generating rotation, and expelling the low-pressure fluid produces a steady mechanical power output from the shaft.

The engine's operational lifecycle includes several states: it can be idle, go through a starting sequence to get up to speed, operate in a continuous running state, and then transition through a stopping phase. It also includes a fault state to handle operational errors. The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements for efficiency, torque, and operating speed.
