This model describes a Neil Robertson Stretcher system, a specialized piece of rescue equipment designed for evacuating injured individuals from confined and difficult-to-access spaces, such as engine rooms on ships, mines, or industrial sites. Its primary function is to completely immobilize a patient, especially one with suspected spinal injuries, to allow for safe extraction.

The stretcher is constructed like a rigid, wrap-around cocoon. It is built from a durable, heavy-duty canvas shell that encases a series of strong wooden slats, likely made of Ash wood. This semi-rigid design allows it to be wrapped securely around the patient while providing the necessary stiffness for full-body immobilization.

To secure the patient, the system uses a set of seven strong nylon webbing straps that are fastened across the body, along with specific provisions for securing the head and neck. This comprehensive restraint system prevents any movement during transport, which is critical for preventing secondary injuries.

A key feature of the system is its capability for vertical and horizontal extraction. It is equipped with a set of four lifting bridles that connect to multiple attachment points on the stretcher, enabling it to be safely hoisted by a crane or rope system. Its narrow and compact design is specifically intended for maneuvering through tight passages like ship hatches and narrow stairwells.

The operational use of the stretcher follows a clear sequence: it starts in a stowed state, is deployed to have a patient positioned and securely strapped in, and then enters an extraction phase where it can be lifted and navigated out of the confined space to complete the evacuation. The entire system is designed to be robust, capable of supporting a patient weighing up to 182 kg, and ensuring their safety throughout a complex rescue operation.
