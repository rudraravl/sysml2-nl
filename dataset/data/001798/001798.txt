This model describes a complete internal combustion engine system, the kind typically found in a vehicle. Its primary function is to convert the chemical energy stored in fuel into mechanical power, delivered as rotational force (torque) and speed through a crankshaft.

The system is composed of several critical subsystems working in concert. An **Air Intake System** draws in air, which is mixed with fuel from a **Fuel Injection System** to create a combustible mixture. This mixture is delivered to one or more **Combustion Chambers**, each containing a **Piston Assembly**.

The engine's operation follows a classic four-stroke cycle. An **Ignition System** provides a spark to ignite the compressed fuel-air mixture in the combustion chamber. The resulting explosion drives the piston, converting the combustion force into linear motion. This motion is then converted into rotational power by a **Crankshaft Assembly**. After combustion, an **Exhaust System** expels the resulting hot gases. A **Cooling System** works continuously to regulate the engine's operating temperature.

The engine's entire lifecycle is managed through different operational states. It begins in an 'off' state and transitions to 'cranking' upon receiving a start command. Once running, it can operate in various modes such as 'idle', 'accelerating', 'cruising', and 'decelerating', all depending on throttle commands. A stop command transitions the engine through a 'stopping' state before it returns to 'off'. The model specifically details an example of a four-cylinder, 2.0-liter engine configured for a vehicle.
