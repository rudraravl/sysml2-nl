This model describes a configurable passenger vehicle, focusing on its powertrain, control systems, and operational states. It represents a conventional automobile with an internal combustion engine designed to be adapted with different options, such as a four or six-cylinder engine, an automatic or manual transmission, and an optional sunroof.

The vehicle's core function is to generate and transmit power to its wheels. This process begins in the engine, which consumes fuel from a fuel tank to generate torque. This rotational power is then sent to the transmission, which modifies the torque before passing it along a driveshaft to the rear axle assembly. The rear axle assembly contains a differential that distributes the power to the two rear wheels, ultimately propelling the vehicle. The system also includes a front axle for steering. The vehicle's total mass is carefully tracked, accounting for the dry weight of all components, the fuel in the tank, and any additional cargo.

Overseeing the vehicle's operation is an electronic vehicle controller running dedicated software. This system manages the vehicle's different states. From an "Off" state, the vehicle transitions to "Starting" when it receives a start signal, but only if the brake pedal is depressed. Once running, it enters an "On" state where it performs a self-test and is ready to drive. When turned off, the system automatically applies the parking brake.

In addition to its primary operational states, the vehicle has a health monitoring system. It constantly senses its temperature, and if it exceeds a maximum threshold, it enters a "degraded" mode to protect itself. The system is also designed to enter a "maintenance" state at a pre-scheduled time, indicating service is required. These systems work together to ensure the vehicle operates safely and reliably while being evaluated against specific engineering requirements for mass, fuel economy, and power output.
