This model represents an automated fluid flow measurement and control system. It is designed to precisely manage the flow of a liquid, such as water, through a pipeline by applying the principles of fluid dynamics, specifically Bernoulli's principle.

The core of the system is a **Venturi tube**, a specially shaped pipe that narrows in the middle (the throat) before widening again. This design is crucial because as the fluid passes through the narrow throat, it speeds up, causing a predictable drop in pressure. By measuring this pressure difference between the wide inlet and the narrow throat, the system can accurately calculate the volume of fluid flowing through it per second.

The system's physical components work in a clear sequence. A **fluid reservoir** acts as the source tank. From there, a **pump** draws the liquid and pushes it into the system, providing the necessary pressure to drive the flow. The fluid then enters the Venturi tube, where an extensive suite of sensors is placed. **Pressure and velocity sensors** at both the inlet and throat continuously monitor the fluid's state.

After passing through the measurement section, the fluid flows into an adjustable **control valve**. This valve is the system's active control mechanism. Based on the real-time flow rate calculated from the sensor data, the system's logic automatically adjusts the opening of this valve. If the flow is too high, the valve closes slightly; if it's too low, it opens wider to maintain a pre-set target flow rate.

Operationally, the system functions through several distinct states, including standby, startup, normal operation, and shutdown. It also features a safety mechanism, transitioning to an alarm state if it detects an abnormal pressure drop, indicating a potential problem in the pipeline. In its normal operating state, the system is in a constant feedback loop: it measures the flow, compares it to the target, and adjusts the valve to minimize any difference, ensuring stable and precise fluid management.
