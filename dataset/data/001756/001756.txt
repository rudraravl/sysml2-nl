This model describes a capacitor-based energy storage system, a fundamental electronic circuit designed to temporarily store electrical energy and deliver it to a load on demand.

The system is composed of four main electrical components that work together. A **Voltage Source**, such as a 12V power supply, provides the initial energy. This energy is used to charge the central component, a **Capacitor**, which stores the energy in an electric field. To ensure the capacitor charges at a controlled rate and to limit the initial current, a **Charging Resistor** is placed between the power source and the capacitor. Finally, a **Load Resistor** represents an external device or circuit that consumes the energy once it's released from the capacitor.

In operation, the system is commanded to charge, causing the voltage source to supply power through the charging resistor to the capacitor. The capacitor's voltage gradually increases as it accumulates charge. The system's control logic actively monitors this voltage to ensure it does not exceed the capacitor's maximum safe rating (16V). Once charged, the system can be commanded to discharge, releasing its stored energy through the load resistor to power an external device. The system is designed to calculate key properties like the amount of charge and energy stored based on the capacitor's current voltage level.
