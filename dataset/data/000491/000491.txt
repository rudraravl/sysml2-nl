This system is a ptosis crutch, a specialized medical device designed to be mounted on an eyeglass frame to mechanically lift a drooping eyelid (a condition known as ptosis). Its primary purpose is to provide a non-surgical solution for clearing the user's field of vision by physically holding the eyelid in a more elevated position.

The device consists of a standard eyeglass frame, typically made of a durable titanium alloy, which serves as the mounting structure. For each affected eye, a small, adjustable support assembly is attached. This assembly features a thin "crutch bar" that extends from the frame towards the eye. At the end of this bar is a soft contact pad, made from medical-grade silicone, which gently rests against the skin of the upper eyelid.

The position and angle of the crutch bar can be precisely tuned using an adjustment screw. This allows a clinician or the user to set the exact amount of lift required to elevate the eyelid just enough to clear the pupil, thereby restoring an unobstructed line of sight.

The system is designed to operate through different phases. It begins with an evaluation phase where the degree of eyelid droop is assessed to calculate the necessary support force and optimal position. Following this, the device is adjusted to the calculated position. Once set, it enters a continuous support mode, applying a gentle, constant force to hold the eyelid up. The design also incorporates safety and comfort considerations, with limits on the maximum pressure applied to prevent discomfort or tissue damage. If any irritation is detected, the device can be readjusted.
