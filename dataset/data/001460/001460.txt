This model describes the **INTEGER Millennium House**, an advanced, sustainable demonstration home designed for ultra-low energy consumption and minimal environmental impact. The primary purpose of this house is to provide a comfortable, healthy, and secure living space while achieving near-zero annual energy usage. It accomplishes this by integrating high-performance passive building techniques with intelligent, active renewable energy systems.

At its core, the house is built with a **Passive House Envelope**, which is a highly insulated and airtight shell with significant thermal mass. This foundation drastically reduces the energy required for heating and cooling by minimizing heat loss in the winter and heat gain in the summer. High-performance, **triple-glazed windows** are a key part of this envelope, providing excellent insulation while allowing for controlled solar heat gain.

The house generates its own electricity using a rooftop **photovoltaic array** (solar panels). This renewable energy powers the home's active systems, primarily a highly-efficient **ground-source heat pump** that provides both heating and cooling by exchanging heat with the stable temperature of the earth.

To maintain excellent indoor air quality in the airtight structure, a **mechanical ventilation system with heat recovery (MVHR)** is used. This system continuously supplies fresh, filtered air while extracting stale air. Crucially, it recovers over 90% of the heat from the outgoing air and transfers it to the incoming fresh air, preventing energy waste.

Energy management is a key feature. Excess heat generated by the heat pump or recovered by the ventilation system can be stored in a **thermal energy storage tank** (essentially a large, insulated water tank) for later use. This acts as a thermal battery, ensuring heating is available even when the heat pump isn't running. The house also features an energy-efficient **LED lighting system** and a **rainwater harvesting system** that collects and stores rainwater for non-potable uses.

The entire house is orchestrated by a central **Building Management System (BMS)**, which acts as the system's brain. Using data from a network of **occupancy sensors** and environmental monitors (for temperature, CO2, etc.), the BMS continuously optimizes the performance of all active systems. It intelligently controls the heat pump, ventilation rate, and lighting to ensure occupant comfort while minimizing energy consumption, making decisions based on factors like occupancy, time of day, and available solar power.
