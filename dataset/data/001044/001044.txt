This is an Engine Knock Control System for an internal combustion engine, designed to detect and prevent a harmful condition called "engine knock." Its primary purpose is to protect the engine from damage while optimizing performance and fuel efficiency.

The system is built around a central computer, the Engine Control Unit (ECU), which acts as the brain. It receives information from one or more Knock Sensors mounted on the engine block. These sensors are essentially tiny microphones tuned to listen for the specific high-frequency vibrations produced by abnormal combustion (knock).

When a Knock Sensor detects these vibrations, it sends a signal to the ECU. The ECU analyzes this signal to determine the intensity and severity of the knock, classifying it as light, moderate, or severe. Based on this analysis, the ECU immediately takes corrective action.

For light knock, the ECU sends a command to the Ignition System to slightly delay the spark timing. This is the primary method of control. For moderate or severe knock, the ECU will not only delay the spark but also send a command to the Fuel Injection System to temporarily enrich the fuel mixture (add more fuel). The extra fuel has a cooling effect inside the combustion chamber, which helps to stop the knock.

The system operates in a continuous closed loop. After making an adjustment, the ECU continues to monitor the Knock Sensors to confirm that the knock has been eliminated. The system has several operational states, including a normal monitoring state, an active mitigation state when it's fighting a knock, and a more aggressive protection mode for severe, persistent knocking to prevent catastrophic engine failure.
