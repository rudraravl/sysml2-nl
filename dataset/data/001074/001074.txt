This model describes a complete Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR) system designed for subsurface imaging. Its primary purpose is to see through the ground to detect and create images of buried objects, geological layers, voids, or infrastructure like pipes and cables. The system is intended for use in fields such as archaeology, utility detection, geological surveying, and infrastructure assessment.

The GPR system is composed of several key components that work together to perform a scan. It has a **Pulse Generator** that creates short, high-frequency electromagnetic pulses. These pulses are sent to a **Transmit Antenna**, which directs them down into the ground. As the pulses travel through the subsurface, they reflect off of any objects or changes in material they encounter.

A separate **Receive Antenna**, which is highly sensitive, detects the faint returning echoes. Because these echoes are very weak, they are first passed through a **Signal Amplifier** to boost their strength. The amplified signal is then sent to a **Digital Signal Processor (DSP)**, which converts the analog echo into digital data that a computer can analyze.

This digital data is processed by two main units. A **Depth Calculator** uses the time it took for the echo to travel down and back to precisely calculate the depth of the object that caused the reflection. Then, an **Image Generator** takes a continuous stream of these depth measurements as the GPR is moved across the surface and assembles them into a two-dimensional cross-section image of the ground beneath. This final image is shown to the operator on a **Display Monitor**. The entire scanning process, including the scan speed and mode, is managed by a central **Control Unit**.

Operationally, the system has several states. It rests in a **standby** mode when powered on but not in use. When a scan is initiated, it first goes through an **initializing** and calibration phase. The main work happens in the **scanning** state, where it continuously cycles through transmitting a pulse, receiving the echo, processing the data, and updating the image in real-time. Once the scan is finished, it returns to standby. The system also includes an **error** state to handle malfunctions, from which it can be reset.

Key performance characteristics of this system include the ability to scan to a maximum depth of 15 meters and to distinguish between objects that are as close as 5 centimeters apart vertically. It operates over a frequency range of 100 MHz to 1000 MHz, which provides a balance between deeper ground penetration and high-resolution imaging.
