This model represents a comprehensive, standardized digital library of "characteristic numbers" as defined by the international standard ISO 80000-11. It's not a physical object, but rather a foundational reference catalog for engineers and scientists to use when modeling and analyzing real-world physical systems.

The library provides the precise definitions for a wide range of dimensionless quantities that are crucial for understanding complex phenomena. These numbers help compare the relative importance of different physical forces or effects.

For example, the catalog includes:
*   **Numbers for Fluid Dynamics:** Definitions like the **Reynolds number**, used to predict whether fluid flow in a pipe will be smooth or turbulent; the **Mach number**, which describes an object's speed relative to the speed of sound; and the **lift and drag coefficients**, essential for designing aircraft wings and vehicle bodies.
*   **Numbers for Heat and Mass Transfer:** It defines quantities like the **Nusselt number** to analyze convective heat transfer, the **Prandtl number** relating momentum and thermal diffusion, and the **Schmidt number** for fluid flows involving mass diffusion, which are critical in designing heat exchangers and chemical reactors.
*   **Numbers for Electromagnetism:** It also includes specialized quantities like the **Hartmann number** for studying the flow of electrically conducting fluids in magnetic fields.

In essence, this is a universal toolkit of scientific principles. An engineer designing a new airplane, a chemical processing plant, or a plasma reactor would use the definitions from this library to ensure their calculations are based on universally accepted physical relationships, allowing for accurate analysis and prediction of system behavior.
