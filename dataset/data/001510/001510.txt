This describes a nanorobotic system for targeted medical therapy, designed to operate autonomously within a biological environment like the human body. The system's primary goal is to locate specific diseased cells, such as cancer cells, and deliver a therapeutic drug directly to them, minimizing harm to surrounding healthy tissue.

The core of the system is an individual therapeutic nanorobot, a microscopic device less than 500 nanometers in size, allowing it to navigate safely through blood vessels and cellular spaces. Each nanorobot is a self-contained unit composed of several key components:

*   **A Biosensor Array:** This acts as the robot's detection system, constantly scanning the immediate environment for unique molecular biomarkers present on the surface of diseased cells. It's highly sensitive and must achieve a confidence level of over 95% before identifying a cell as a target.
*   **A Navigation Controller:** This is the robot's guidance system. Once the biosensors identify a target, the navigation controller calculates the best path and propels the nanorobot toward the cell's location with extreme precision, aiming to arrive within 10 nanometers of its target.
*   **A Payload Module:** This component stores and dispenses the therapeutic drug. It is a critical safety feature, as it will only release its payload after receiving a definitive confirmation that the nanorobot has reached a verified diseased cell. The robot carries enough payload to treat several target cells during a single mission.
*   **A Power Management Unit:** This is the onboard battery, providing the energy for all the robot's functions—sensing, navigation, and drug delivery—for a mission lasting at least 60 minutes.
*   **A Communication Module:** This allows the nanorobot to transmit status updates and data about its environment, such as temperature and pH levels, and potentially receive commands.

The nanorobot operates through a clear mission sequence. It starts in a "Searching" state, actively looking for targets. When a potential target is found, it transitions to a "Navigating" state to travel to the cell. Upon arrival, it enters a "Verifying" state for a final, close-range confirmation. If the target is confirmed, it proceeds to the "Delivering" state to release the drug; otherwise, it returns to searching. After delivery, it may enter a monitoring phase before seeking its next target, continuing this cycle until its mission is complete or its payload is depleted.
