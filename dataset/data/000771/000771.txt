This model represents a digital, automatic sphygmomanometer, commonly known as a home blood pressure monitor. It is designed to non-invasively measure a person's arterial blood pressure (systolic and diastolic), mean arterial pressure, and pulse rate.

The system's core components work together to perform the measurement using the oscillometric method. It includes an inflatable cuff that is wrapped around the user's arm. When a user presses the start button, a central microprocessor unit orchestrates the measurement process. First, an electric pneumatic pump inflates the cuff with compressed air to a pressure high enough to temporarily stop blood flow in the artery.

Once inflated, a controlled exhaust valve begins to slowly and steadily release the air pressure from the cuff. During this deflation phase, a sensitive pressure transducer continuously measures the overall pressure inside the cuff. Simultaneously, an oscillation detector identifies the small, rhythmic pressure fluctuations caused by blood pulsing through the artery as the pressure is released.

The microprocessor records this stream of pressure and oscillation data. It analyzes the pattern of the oscillations, specifically identifying the cuff pressure at which the oscillations are strongest, which corresponds to the mean arterial pressure. From this peak and the points where oscillations start and fade, it calculates the systolic and diastolic blood pressure values. The final results, along with the calculated pulse rate, are then sent to an LCD display for the user to read.

The entire system is designed with patient safety and accuracy in mind, incorporating a maximum safe pressure limit for the cuff and specific accuracy requirements for the pressure readings. The measurement cycle—from inflation to displaying the result—is designed to be completed quickly, typically within a minute.
