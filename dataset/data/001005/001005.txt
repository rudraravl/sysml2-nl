This describes a carbon paste electrode system used for quantitative chemical analysis. Its primary function is to accurately measure the concentration of specific, electroactive substances (analytes) within a liquid solution by converting chemical information into a measurable electrical signal.

The system is composed of an electronic instrument called a **Potentiostat** and an **Electrochemical Cell**. The cell is a container that holds the liquid sample and a three-electrode setup.

The core of the system is the **Working Electrode**, the actual sensor, which is made from a carefully prepared paste of graphite powder and a binder material. This is the surface where the key chemical reactions happen. The other two electrodes are a **Reference Electrode**, which provides a stable voltage baseline for the measurement, and a **Counter Electrode**, which completes the electrical circuit through the solution.

In operation, the Potentiostat precisely controls the electrical potential applied to the Working Electrode, sweeping it across a predefined range. This changing potential causes the target chemical in the solution to react at the electrode's surface, either losing electrons (oxidation) or gaining them (reduction). This electron transfer generates a tiny electrical current, which the Potentiostat measures.

The result of a scan is a data plot of current versus potential, called a voltammogram. The system analyzes this data to find a "peak current," the point of maximum reaction, which is directly related to the concentration of the chemical. The overall process involves preparing the electrode surface, performing the voltage scan, analyzing the resulting redox reactions, extracting the peak data, and calculating the final concentration. The system is designed to be highly sensitive, stable over long periods, and provide reproducible results for reliable chemical detection.
