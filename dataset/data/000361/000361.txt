This model represents the International System of Units (SI), the globally standardized framework for scientific and commercial measurement. It is the modern form of the metric system, designed to provide a consistent and coherent language for quantifying the physical world. This system is fundamental to science, engineering, technology, and trade, ensuring that measurements are unambiguous and universally understood.

The system is built upon a foundation of seven base units which are considered dimensionally independent: the metre for length, the kilogram for mass, the second for time, the ampere for electric current, the kelvin for thermodynamic temperature, the mole for the amount of substance, and the candela for luminous intensity.

From these base units, a comprehensive set of derived units is constructed to measure more complex quantities. For example, speed is defined as metres per second, and force as newtons (which are fundamentally kilogram-metres per second squared). Many of these derived units, such as the watt (for power), volt (for electric potential), and pascal (for pressure), are given special names for convenience.

The framework includes a standardized set of prefixes, such as 'kilo' for thousands and 'milli' for thousandths, allowing it to elegantly handle measurements across vast scales, from the subatomic to the astronomical. The system also formally recognizes other commonly used units by defining their precise conversion to SI units, such as the hour, the day, the litre, and the ångström. It even handles complex cases like temperature, carefully defining the relationship between the absolute Kelvin scale and the common Celsius scale, including the offset for their zero points.
