This is a command and control system designed to manage a large fleet of autonomous machines, such as ground robots or vehicles, executing a coordinated march across terrain. The system's primary goal is to make a fleet of one hundred machines move together in precise, pre-defined formations toward a destination.

The system has a three-tiered control architecture. At the top, a **Mission Controller** acts as the strategic planner. It defines the overall objective of the mission, plans it in phases, and monitors the fleet's progress toward the final goal.

The Mission Controller gives high-level directives to a **Formation Coordinator**, which acts as the tactical manager for the entire fleet. This coordinator is responsible for maintaining the shape and integrity of the formation (e.g., a "wedge" pattern). It continuously assesses the position of every machine, calculates the ideal spot for each one based on the active formation pattern, and issues specific commands to the machines to correct their positions if they deviate beyond a set tolerance (e.g., 2 meters).

The core of the system is the fleet of **Autonomous Machines**. Each machine is an independent, battery-powered unit capable of navigating on its own. It constantly tracks its own position, speed, and battery level. Machines receive marching orders—including their assigned position, speed, and destination—from the Formation Coordinator. They also communicate directly with their neighbors, sharing status updates to help maintain local spacing and coherence.

An individual machine operates through a lifecycle of different states. It can be in `standby` awaiting orders, `marching` toward a waypoint, actively `correcting` its path to maintain formation, or returning to base for `recharging` when its battery runs low. If a machine deviates from its assigned spot, it will automatically calculate a correction to its heading and speed to get back in line. This entire operation is designed for endurance, with each machine capable of operating for extended periods before needing to recharge.
