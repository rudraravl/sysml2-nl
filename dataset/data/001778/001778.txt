This model describes a generalized heat engine, a system designed to convert thermal energy into useful mechanical work. It operates based on the fundamental principles of thermodynamics, specifically the conservation of energy.

The system consists of three main components interacting with the engine core. A high-temperature reservoir acts as the heat source, like a boiler or a combustion chamber, supplying thermal energy. A low-temperature reservoir serves as a heat sink, like a cooling tower or the surrounding atmosphere, to absorb waste heat. Finally, a mechanical load represents the device that consumes the work produced by the engine, such as a generator or a piston driving a wheel.

The engine's operation is a continuous, cyclical process. It begins by absorbing a packet of heat from the hot reservoir, which increases the internal energy, temperature, and pressure of a working substance inside the engine. This high-energy substance then expands, performing mechanical work on the load. To prepare for the next cycle, the engine expels the remaining unusable or "waste" heat to the cold reservoir. The substance is then compressed back to its initial state, ready to absorb heat and start the cycle over again.

The entire process is governed by the First Law of Thermodynamics, ensuring that the initial heat energy supplied by the hot reservoir is precisely accounted for, splitting into the useful work performed and the waste heat rejected to the cold reservoir.
