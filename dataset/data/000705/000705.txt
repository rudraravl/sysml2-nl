This model describes a Nasopharyngeal Airway (NPA) system, a medical device used by first responders and healthcare professionals to establish and maintain an open airway in a patient who is unconscious or semiconscious. Its primary function is to bypass an airway obstruction in the upper throat, which is often caused by the tongue falling back.

The physical device itself, the Nasopharyngeal Airway, consists of two main parts. The core component is a soft, flexible, hollow tube, typically made of medical-grade silicone. It features a beveled tip designed to make insertion into the patient's nasal passage smoother and less traumatic. The other part is a flared flange at the external end of the tube. This flange is a critical safety feature that rests against the nostril, preventing the device from being inserted too far into the airway and helping to secure it in position.

The system's operation follows a standard medical procedure. A caregiver first assesses the patient to determine if the device is needed and to select the appropriate size. The chosen airway tube is then prepared by applying a water-based lubricant to its surface. It is then carefully inserted through the patient's nostril until the tip rests in the pharynx, just behind the tongue, creating a clear channel for air to travel from the outside directly to the lungs.

Throughout its use, the device's state changes from stored, to prepared, inserted, and finally to a functioning state where it is actively keeping the airway patent (open). The patient's condition, particularly their oxygen saturation, is continuously monitored to ensure the device is working effectively. If issues arise, the device might be adjusted or removed entirely. After its use is no longer required, the device is removed from the patient.
