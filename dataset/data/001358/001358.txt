This model describes an Automated Teller Machine (ATM) system, designed to provide customers with secure, 24/7 access to automated banking services like cash withdrawals, balance inquiries, deposits, and funds transfers.

The physical ATM is composed of several key hardware components that work together under the management of a central controller. A customer initiates a session by inserting their bank card into a card reader, which can handle both magnetic stripe and chip cards. The customer then interacts with the system using a physical keypad to enter their Personal Identification Number (PIN) and make selections from menus shown on a display screen.

Internally, the ATM is managed by a central controller that directs the entire user session. A critical security module handles the encryption of sensitive data, like PINs and account numbers, and monitors for physical tampering. For all banking operations, a network interface establishes a secure, encrypted connection to the central banking system to authenticate the user and authorize transactions.

The core function of dispensing cash is handled by a cash dispenser, which maintains an inventory of different bill denominations ($20, $50, $100). It only dispenses cash after receiving approval from the bank and ensures it has sufficient funds to complete the transaction. After each transaction, a receipt printer generates a physical record for the customer, detailing the transaction type, amount, and account balance.

The system operates through a well-defined sequence of states, starting from an idle state waiting for a customer. Once a card is inserted, it moves through states for reading the card, awaiting a PIN, authenticating with the bank, processing the selected transaction, dispensing cash, and printing a receipt. For security, the system has built-in rules, such as limiting PIN entry to three attempts and automatically ending a session after 60 seconds of inactivity to protect the user's account.
