This model describes a complete steam turbine power generation system, a type of thermal power plant designed to convert the heat energy in steam into electrical energy for a power grid. The system is comprised of four main interconnected components that work together in a continuous thermodynamic cycle.

The process begins with the **Steam Boiler**, which acts as the heat source. It contains a combustion chamber to generate intense heat, which is used to boil water in a drum and then further heat it in a superheater. This produces a continuous flow of high-pressure, high-temperature superheated steam.

This steam is then piped to the **Steam Turbine**. The turbine consists of a rotor with multiple stages of precisely shaped blades, all housed within a sturdy stator casing. The high-pressure steam expands and pushes against these blades, causing the entire rotor assembly to spin at a very high, controlled speed. A governor system ensures this speed remains stable. As the steam passes through the turbine, its pressure and temperature drop significantly.

The turbine's rotating shaft is directly coupled to an **Electrical Generator**. The generator converts the mechanical rotational energy from the turbine into electrical power. It does this using a rotor field with an excitation current and a stationary stator winding. The system includes a cooling mechanism to manage the immense heat generated during this conversion. The final output is high-voltage electrical power, synchronized to a specific frequency (like 50 Hz) for grid compatibility.

Finally, the low-pressure, low-temperature steam that exits the turbine enters a **Steam Condenser**. The condenser's job is to cool this steam and turn it back into liquid water. It accomplishes this by passing the steam over a large bundle of tubes through which cool water flows. A vacuum system is used to maintain very low pressure inside the condenser, which increases the overall efficiency of the power plant. The resulting liquid water, called condensate, collects in a reservoir called a hotwell, from where it is pumped back to the boiler to begin the cycle again.
