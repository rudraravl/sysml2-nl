This model describes an automated Fire Pumping Subsystem, the kind of critical equipment found in large buildings or industrial facilities for fire suppression. Its primary function is to draw water from a dedicated source and deliver it at a significantly increased pressure and flow rate to fire sprinklers or hydrants.

The heart of the system is a powerful, electrically-driven Fire Pump, which consists of a motor, a shaft, and an impeller housed within a durable casing. This pump draws water from a large Water Supply Source through its suction port. Once activated, the pump's motor spins the impeller at high speed, pressurizing the water and forcing it out through a discharge port.

The pressurized water is then directed into a Discharge Manifold, a component designed to distribute the high-pressure water flow to multiple outlets, which would connect to the building's firefighting network.

The entire operation is managed and monitored by a dedicated Pump Controller. This controller receives real-time data from a suite of sensors: one pressure sensor monitors the incoming (suction) pressure, another monitors the outgoing (discharge) pressure, and a flow meter measures the rate of water moving through the system. Based on these readings and external commands, the controller starts, stops, and regulates the pump to maintain a target pressure and flow.

The system operates through several distinct states. It remains in an idle or off state until it receives a start command. It then enters a priming state to ensure the pump is ready, after which it transitions to its main running state. The controller continuously monitors for unsafe conditions, such as dangerously low suction pressure or excessive discharge pressure, and can trigger an alarm or initiate a shutdown to protect the equipment. When a stop command is received, it executes a controlled shutdown procedure. The system is designed to meet strict performance and reliability requirements, ensuring it can start up quickly and operate continuously when needed.
