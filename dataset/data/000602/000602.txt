This model describes a medical infusion pump, a system designed to deliver therapeutic fluids like medications, nutrients, or other treatments to a patient in a highly controlled and safe manner. Its primary function is to administer these fluids at a precise flow rate and for a specific volume as programmed by a healthcare professional.

The system is built around a core fluid delivery path. It starts with a **fluid reservoir**, which holds the liquid to be administered. From the reservoir, the fluid is drawn into a **pumping mechanism** (such as a peristaltic or syringe pump) that precisely controls the movement of the fluid. As the fluid is pushed towards the patient, it passes through a series of critical safety sensors. A **pressure sensor** monitors for blockages (occlusions) in the line, a **flow sensor** verifies that the fluid is moving at the correct rate, and an **air detector** scans for dangerous air bubbles. A separate sensor also monitors the fluid level in the reservoir to prevent it from running empty.

The "brain" of the device is a central **control processor**. This unit receives all the data from the various sensors and constantly compares it against the settings entered by the user through an interface. Based on this comparison, the controller adjusts the speed of the pumping mechanism to maintain the target flow rate. It also sends status updates, such as the total volume delivered and any active alarms, back to the user interface.

For safety, a dedicated **alarm subsystem** provides both audible and visual alerts for conditions like occlusions, air in the line, an empty reservoir, or a low battery. The entire device is powered by a **power management unit** that can use mains electricity and also includes a rechargeable battery for backup and portability, ensuring continuous operation for several hours without an external power source.

The infusion pump operates through a well-defined set of states. After being powered on, it performs a self-test. Once passed, it enters a standby mode. A user can then program the delivery and initiate a priming sequence to fill the tubing and remove air. During delivery, the pump is in an active state, which can be paused and resumed. If any safety issue is detected, the pump will enter an alarm state, halting the delivery and alerting the user to the problem.
