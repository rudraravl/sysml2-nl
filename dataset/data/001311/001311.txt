This model describes a Voltage Detector System, a safety and monitoring device for electrical equipment. Its primary purpose is to continuously watch the voltage of an electrical signal and automatically trigger an alarm if the voltage goes outside of a predefined safe range. This helps prevent equipment damage and ensures operational safety by alerting operators to over-voltage or under-voltage conditions.

The system is composed of three main functional components that work in sequence:

1.  **Voltage Sensor:** This is the input unit of the system. It connects to an electrical line and continuously measures the incoming voltage level, converting the raw electrical signal into a precise voltage reading.

2.  **Threshold Comparator:** The voltage reading from the sensor is fed into this central processing unit. The comparator constantly checks if the measured voltage is within a safe operational window, which is defined by configurable upper and lower voltage thresholds. If the voltage drops below the lower limit or exceeds the upper limit, the comparator identifies a violation.

3.  **Alarm Controller:** When the comparator detects a voltage violation, it signals the alarm controller. This unit is responsible for activating an external alarm system, sending out a signal to indicate that a problem has been detected and specifying the type of violation (e.g., too high or too low).

The system's operation is managed through a control interface that allows a user to set the upper and lower voltage thresholds and to enable or disable the monitoring function. The system operates in several distinct states: it starts in an **initialization** state, moves to a **standby** mode when ready, and enters **active monitoring** when commanded. If a voltage fault is detected during active monitoring, it transitions to an **alarm state** until the condition is resolved and the alarm is cleared, at which point it returns to monitoring.
