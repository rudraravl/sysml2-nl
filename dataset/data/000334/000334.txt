This model describes the fundamental principles of how information, energy, or physical items are exchanged between different parts of any system. It isn't a single physical object, but rather a foundational set of rules and concepts for defining communication and transfer.

At its core, the model defines a **"Flow"** as a directed transfer of a "payload" from a source component to a target destination. These flows are not just simple lines on a diagram; they are tied to the specific timing and behavior of the components they connect.

The system breaks down this concept into increasingly specific types of interaction:

*   The most basic interaction is a **Message**, which represents a transfer of something between a source and a target that is linked to specific events in time. For example, a message might be sent when the source component starts a task and received just before the target component ends its task.

*   A more defined version is the **Flow**, which specifies that the transfer happens between a designated *output point* on the source and a designated *input point* on the target. This ensures the connection is precise, like plugging a cable from a specific output port on a computer to an input port on a monitor.

*   Finally, a **Succession Flow** represents a strictly sequential hand-off. In this case, the source component must fully complete its action *before* the flow begins, and the flow must be fully completed *before* the target component begins its own action. This is like an assembly line, where one station must finish its work entirely before passing the item to the next station in the sequence.

In essence, this is a blueprint for defining how parts of a system talk to each other, ensuring that transfers are clearly defined, properly timed, and connected to the correct points.
