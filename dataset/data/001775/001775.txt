This model describes a complete external combustion engine system, specifically a closed-loop steam engine designed to convert thermal energy into mechanical power. The system operates on a continuous cycle, using water as its working fluid.

The core of the system consists of four main components working in a cycle: a Boiler, a Steam Expander, a Condenser, and a Feedwater Pump.

The process begins in the **Boiler**, which acts as a furnace and heat exchanger. It takes in fuel and water, combusts the fuel externally to generate heat, and uses that heat to convert the water into high-pressure, high-temperature steam.

This high-pressure steam is then piped to the **Steam Expander** (such as a piston engine or turbine). Here, the steam expands and pushes against mechanical components, converting the steam's thermal energy into rotational force (torque). This mechanical output is the primary power generated by the system. As the steam does its work, it loses pressure and temperature.

The resulting low-pressure exhaust steam flows from the expander into the **Condenser**. The condenser uses an external source of cooling water to cool the steam, causing it to change back into liquid water.

Finally, this condensed water is collected and sent to the **Feedwater Pump**. The pump pressurizes the water and forces it back into the boiler, completing the closed loop and allowing the cycle to repeat continuously.

The entire engine system has several operational states, including an **idle** state when it's off, a **startup** phase for building steam pressure, a normal **operating** state where it produces power, and a **shutdown** procedure. The system is designed with specific safety and performance requirements, such as maintaining boiler pressure within safe limits, ensuring a minimum water level, and achieving a target thermal efficiency of at least 15%.
