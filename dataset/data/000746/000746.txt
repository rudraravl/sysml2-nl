This is a medical diagnostic system for performing and assessing deep tendon reflex examinations. The system involves a clinician, a patient, and a specialized reflex hammer tool used to conduct the test.

The core component is the reflex hammer, which is composed of two main parts: a head and a handle. The head is made of medical-grade rubber with a specific hardness to ensure a safe but effective impact on the patient's tendon. The handle is ergonomically designed with a textured grip and specific dimensions to provide the clinician with optimal control and minimize fatigue during examinations. The hammer's overall mass and length are carefully balanced for precise handling.

The system models the entire clinical procedure. A clinician holds the hammer and uses it to deliver a controlled mechanical strike to one of the patient's specific tendons (such as the patellar, Achilles, or biceps). This strike acts as a stimulus, designed to elicit an involuntary muscle contraction, or reflex.

The clinician then observes and assesses the patient's reflex response, considering its latency (the time it takes to react) and magnitude. The response is then graded on a standard five-point clinical scale, ranging from absent to hyperactive, which helps in diagnosing the condition of the patient's nervous system. The entire process, from positioning the hammer to assessing the final response, is part of this system.
