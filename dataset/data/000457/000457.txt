This model describes a Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation (HNS) system, an implantable medical device designed to treat obstructive sleep apnea. The system works by actively monitoring a patient's breathing during sleep and intervening to keep their airway open.

The core of the system is an Implantable Pulse Generator (IPG), a small, battery-powered device surgically placed under the skin in the upper chest. The IPG acts as the brain of the system, connected to two other implanted components: a respiratory sensor and a stimulation lead. The respiratory sensor, placed near the ribs, continuously tracks the patient's breathing patterns, including airflow and breathing rate.

When the sensor detects that the patient has stopped breathing (an apnea event), it signals the IPG. The IPG then generates a series of gentle, precisely controlled electrical pulses. These pulses are sent through the stimulation lead, a thin, flexible wire that runs up to the hypoglossal nerve in the neck.

The lead connects to a nerve cuff electrode, which is carefully placed around the hypoglossal nerve—the nerve that controls the movement of the tongue. The electrical stimulation from the electrode activates this nerve, causing the genioglossal muscle at the base of the tongue to contract. This contraction gently pushes the tongue forward, opening up the upper airway and allowing the patient to breathe normally again.

The entire process is a closed-loop therapy that operates automatically throughout the night. The patient uses an external, handheld remote control to turn the system on before going to sleep and off after waking up. This allows for a responsive and adaptive treatment that prevents airway collapse without waking the patient, thereby reducing sleep apnea events and improving overall sleep quality and oxygenation.
