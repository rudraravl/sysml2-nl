This model describes an autonomous docking system for spacecraft, designed to connect two separate modules in orbit. The system's purpose is to create a secure, rigid, and pressurized passageway between them, allowing for the transfer of crew or resources.

The system consists of two main parts installed on the respective spacecraft: an **Active Docking Mechanism** and a **Passive Docking Adapter**.

The **Active Docking Mechanism** is the "smart" and powered half of the system. It is equipped with laser rangefinder sensors to precisely measure its distance, angle, and velocity relative to the passive target. It features a 1.2-meter diameter docking ring and a set of 16 powerful mechanical latches that extend to capture and secure the other module. Once a hard physical connection is made, it activates a silicone elastomer pressure seal to create an airtight connection. This entire active unit is mounted to its spacecraft via a robust structural interface capable of withstanding significant loads.

The **Passive Docking Adapter** serves as the stationary target. It has a matching docking ring and seal for the active mechanism to connect with. It also features guide petals, which help to funnel the active mechanism into the correct position during the final stages of approach.

The docking process is a carefully managed sequence. The active mechanism first uses its sensors to measure alignment. If it's not perfectly aligned, it makes corrective maneuvers. Once it is within the required tolerances for distance and angle, it receives a command to initiate the capture. The capture latches engage, creating a solid mechanical link. Following this "soft capture," the system pulls the two modules together to achieve a "hard capture," activates the pressure seal, and verifies that the connection is secure and airtight. The system includes critical safety features, allowing it to abort the sequence and safely disengage if alignment is lost or the connection fails verification at any point.
