This model describes an Airtraq Video Laryngoscope, a specialized medical device used for endotracheal intubation, which is the process of inserting a breathing tube into a patient's trachea. It is designed to provide a clear, indirect view of the patient's airway, making it easier for clinicians to guide the tube accurately and safely.

The device is a single, handheld unit composed of several integrated components. The physical structure features an anatomically-shaped blade designed for insertion into the patient's mouth to expose the larynx. A key feature is a dedicated tube guiding channel built alongside the blade, which holds the endotracheal tube and directs it at a precise angle toward the vocal cords.

At the core of the system is its visualization technology. A battery module provides power to all electronic components. An LED illumination system at the tip of the blade provides bright, cool light to illuminate the airway. The view from the tip is captured through an optical prism system and relayed to a micro-camera module. This camera sends a live video feed to an internal video processing unit, which enhances the image for better clarity, brightness, and contrast. The final, enhanced video is displayed in real-time on an integrated display screen, giving the operator a clear, magnified view without needing to directly align their eye with the patient's airway.

The system's operation follows a clear procedural flow. After being powered on, the clinician inserts the blade and uses the screen to navigate the patient's anatomy. The device allows for repositioning until a clear view of the glottis (the opening to the trachea) is achieved. Once the target is visualized, the clinician advances the endotracheal tube through the guiding channel, watching its progress on the screen to ensure it passes through the vocal cords correctly. After placement is verified visually, the procedure is complete, and the device is removed.
