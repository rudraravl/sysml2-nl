This model describes a fundamental conceptual framework for defining the properties of any system, rather than a physical object itself. It establishes the universal concept of an "attribute"—a way to specify a quality, characteristic, or measurable fact about a system or one of its parts.

Think of it as the blueprint for creating labels or specification fields on any design. For example, when designing a car, attributes would be things like its 'color', 'horsepower', 'weight', or 'fuel capacity'. For a software application, they might be 'version number', 'memory usage', or 'license type'.

The primary purpose of this framework is to provide a standardized and consistent way for engineers to attach these specific data values to the components they are designing. It ensures that when a characteristic like the 'voltage' of a battery or the 'material' of a screw is defined, that piece of information is understood and handled uniformly throughout the entire system model.

In essence, this is not a system you can build or touch, but a foundational rulebook. It provides the basic vocabulary needed to describe the concrete details of any other system, from a simple toaster to a complex satellite.
