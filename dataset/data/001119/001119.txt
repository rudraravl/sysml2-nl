This model describes a Light-Addressable Potentiometric Sensor (LAPS), a sophisticated chemical sensing system designed to measure the concentration of specific ions (like pH) in a liquid sample. Instead of providing a single reading, this system can create a two-dimensional map of the chemical properties across its sensor surface, offering high spatial resolution.

The heart of the system is a multi-layered sensor chip composed of a semiconductor substrate, an insulating layer, and a specialized ion-sensitive membrane. When this chip is exposed to a chemical solution (the analyte), the membrane's surface potential changes in response to the concentration of target ions.

The system works by scanning a focused, modulated beam of light across the back of the semiconductor chip. At each point the light strikes, it generates a small electrical current, known as a photocurrent. The magnitude of this photocurrent is directly dependent on the local surface potential on the other side of the chip. By precisely measuring this photocurrent as the light beam is scanned, the system can build a detailed, point-by-point map of the chemical potential across the entire sensor surface.

This process involves several key components working in concert:
*   A **Light Source** and **Optical System** generate and focus the modulated light beam.
*   A **Scanning Controller** meticulously directs the light beam across the sensor to create the 2D scan.
*   A **Reference Electrode** provides a stable voltage baseline, which is essential for any accurate potentiometric measurement.
*   A sensitive **Photodetection Circuit** captures the tiny photocurrent generated by the light.
*   A **Lock-In Amplifier** is used to extract the weak photocurrent signal from background noise with high precision, using the light's modulation as a reference.
*   Finally, a **Data Processor** takes the series of measurements from the scanner and lock-in amplifier, applies calibration data, and constructs the final surface potential map and calculates the overall analyte concentration.

The entire unit operates through distinct states, including initialization, calibration, active measurement, data processing, and a ready state, ensuring a controlled and repeatable measurement process.
