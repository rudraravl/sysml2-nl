This model represents a system for analyzing the one-dimensional, straight-line motion of a vehicle. It's a physics-based simulation designed to predict the vehicle's position, velocity, and acceleration over a period of time.

The system's core subject is a generic vehicle, which is defined by a single key property: its mass. The analysis works by taking a set of initial conditions for this vehicle, including its starting position and speed.

The primary driver for the simulation is a "power profile," which is a pre-defined sequence of power values applied to the vehicle over time. You can think of this as a script for how the vehicle's throttle or engine power is adjusted moment by moment.

The simulation progresses in discrete time steps. At each step, it calculates the vehicle's current acceleration based on the power being applied at that instant, the vehicle's mass, and its current speed. It then uses this acceleration to compute the vehicle's new speed and new position for the next moment in time. By repeating this process for the entire duration of the power profile, the system generates a complete history of the vehicle's movement. The final output is a detailed profile of the vehicle's acceleration at every step of the journey.
