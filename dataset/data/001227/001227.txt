This model describes an advanced, self-monitoring road surface system, essentially a "smart road," designed to actively maintain safe driving conditions. The system is comprised of a physical section of road pavement which has integrated technology to sense and react to its environment and physical condition.

The core of the system is the road surface itself, constructed from a material like asphalt concrete with a specific design lifespan. Embedded within this surface are four sensor units that continuously measure critical properties such as friction, temperature, roughness, and the level of wetness.

This smart road features several active subsystems to manage its condition. It includes a built-in drainage subsystem to efficiently clear water from the surface during rain, and a heating subsystem to raise the pavement temperature and prevent ice from forming in cold weather.

The entire system is managed by a maintenance controller. This controller acts as the brain, processing data from the embedded sensors along with external information about weather and traffic loads. Based on this information, the controller can autonomously activate the heating or drainage systems. For example, if the sensors detect a temperature below freezing, the heating is turned on. If the surface becomes too rough or its friction drops to unsafe levels from wear and tear, the controller evaluates the degradation and can automatically schedule and issue commands for maintenance work.

The road system operates through various states in its lifecycle, beginning in an "optimal" condition, progressing to "acceptable" and then "degraded" as it wears down. It can enter an "under maintenance" state for repairs and eventually reaches a "retired" state at the end of its service life.
