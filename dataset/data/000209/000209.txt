This model describes the control system for a vehicle, focusing on its operational states and health monitoring. The system consists of the main vehicle body and an integrated electronic controller that work together to manage its functions safely.

The vehicle's primary purpose is to provide power, a function that is directly managed by an external fuel command. Its behavior is governed by two parallel processes: its operational status and its health status.

The operational process manages the vehicle's main states: off, starting, and on. To start the vehicle, two conditions must be met: a "start" signal must be received, and a safety interlock requires the brake pedal to be depressed. Once these conditions are met, the vehicle signals its internal controller to activate and enters a "starting" phase. Upon receiving a final "on" signal, the vehicle becomes fully operational. When entering the "on" state, it first performs a self-test. While it remains on, it continuously provides power. When signaled to shut down, it automatically applies the parking brake before powering off completely.

Simultaneously, the vehicle's health monitoring system runs continuously. It actively senses its internal temperature. If the temperature exceeds a predefined maximum, the system enters a "degraded" state and sends an "overheating" alert to its controller. The vehicle is also programmed to enter a "maintenance" state when a specific, pre-scheduled time is reached. The system can be returned to its "normal" health status from either the degraded or maintenance states after an external "return to normal" command is received, indicating that the issue has been resolved or maintenance is complete.
