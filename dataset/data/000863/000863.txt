This model represents a reaction turbine power generation system, designed to efficiently convert the thermal and pressure energy of a working fluid, such as steam or gas, into electrical power.

The core of the system is a single turbine stage, which consists of a matched pair of a stationary component (the stator) and a rotating component (the rotor). The stator is a set of fixed nozzles that takes the high-pressure fluid and accelerates it, converting the fluid's pressure into high velocity. This accelerated fluid is then directed onto the blades of the rotor.

As the fluid flows across the rotor blades, it imparts force, causing the rotor and its central shaft to spin at high speed. The rotor is designed to extract energy not just from the fluid's velocity but also from a continued pressure drop, a key characteristic defined by the system's "degree of reaction." This parameter determines how the total energy extraction is split between the stationary stator and the moving rotor, and it is a critical factor for the turbine's overall efficiency.

The mechanical power from the turbine's rotating shaft is used to drive a generator, which converts this rotational energy into electricity.

An integrated controller actively manages the entire process. It continuously monitors the turbine's performance, particularly its efficiency. If the efficiency falls below a target threshold, the controller can adjust operating parameters to optimize the degree of reaction and restore peak performance. The system is designed to operate within a specific optimal range, ensuring balanced energy extraction and requiring it to deliver a minimum amount of power while adhering to fundamental energy conservation principles.
