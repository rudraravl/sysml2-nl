This model represents a comprehensive, formal library of standard physical quantities used to describe light and radiation. It is not a physical object itself, but rather a digital catalog based on the international standard ISO 80000-7, which provides a foundational language for science and engineering. The system's purpose is to define, in an unambiguous way, all the essential concepts for measuring and analyzing electromagnetic waves and their interaction with matter.

The library is organized into several key domains:

*   **Radiometry:** This section deals with the measurement of electromagnetic radiation in terms of absolute physical power. It defines fundamental concepts like **radiant energy** (the total energy of light waves), **radiant flux** (the rate of energy flow, or power, measured in Watts), **irradiance** (the power of radiation received by a surface), and **radiance** (the power emitted from a surface in a specific direction).

*   **Photometry:** This covers quantities related to how light is perceived by the human eye. These are analogous to radiometric quantities but are weighted based on a standard model of human vision sensitivity. Key concepts include **luminous flux** (perceived power, measured in lumens), **illuminance** (the amount of light illuminating a surface, in lux), and **luminance** (the perceived brightness of an object). It also defines the **luminous efficacy** of a source, which describes how efficiently it converts power (Watts) into visible light (lumens).

*   **Photon Quantities:** This section describes light at the quantum level, defining properties based on the number of photons rather than energy. It includes concepts such as **photon flux** (the number of photons per second) and **photon exposure** (the total number of photons that hit a surface area).

*   **Colorimetry:** This part of the library provides the foundation for the science of color measurement. It formally defines the **CIE standard color-matching functions** (which model the response of the three types of color-sensitive cones in the human eye), **tristimulus values** (the basis for color spaces like CIE XYZ), and **chromaticity coordinates**, which specify a color independent of its brightness. It also includes definitions for **color temperature**, used to describe the hue of a light source.

*   **Material Interaction Properties:** Finally, the library defines how materials interact with light. This includes properties like **absorptance**, **reflectance**, and **transmittance**—the fractions of light that are absorbed, reflected, or transmitted by a material. It also defines related concepts such as **emissivity** (a material's ability to emit thermal radiation) and various **attenuation coefficients** that describe how light is diminished as it passes through a medium.
