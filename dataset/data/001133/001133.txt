This is a Magnetic Anomaly Detector (MAD) system designed to identify localized disturbances in the Earth's magnetic field. Its primary purpose is to detect and locate ferromagnetic objects such as submarines, unexploded ordnance, or mineral deposits by sensing the variations they cause in the surrounding magnetic environment.

The system is composed of several key components that work together in a continuous operational cycle. At its core is a high-sensitivity **Magnetometer**, which constantly measures the ambient magnetic field strength. The raw data from this sensor is immediately sent to a **Signal Conditioner**, which filters out environmental noise and amplifies the signal to ensure clarity and precision.

Simultaneously, a **Position Tracker**, likely a GPS unit, continuously provides the system's precise geographic coordinates, including latitude, longitude, and altitude.

The cleaned magnetic field data is then passed to an **Anomaly Analyzer**. This component compares the incoming measurement against a pre-established baseline of the normal magnetic field for the area. If the measured field deviates from this baseline by more than a configurable threshold, the system flags it as a potential anomaly.

When an anomaly is confirmed, a **Data Recorder** logs a complete record of the event. This record includes the measured magnetic field strength, the magnitude of the deviation, the precise geographic position, and a timestamp. The system continues this cycle of measuring, analyzing, and recording until it receives a command to deactivate, at which point it can provide a complete log of all detected anomalies.
