This model represents a standardized digital library of physical quantities and concepts related to space and time, based on the international standard ISO 80000-3. It isn't a physical object, but rather a foundational vocabulary designed to be used in engineering and scientific models to ensure that physical concepts are described precisely and consistently.

The library defines a comprehensive set of fundamental measurements and their relationships. It starts with basic spatial dimensions like length, width, height, diameter, and radius, and builds upon these to define more complex concepts like area and volume.

For describing motion and location, the library provides a robust framework. It defines different types of three-dimensional coordinate systems for locating objects in space, including the familiar Cartesian (x, y, z), Cylindrical (radial distance, angle, height), Spherical (radial distance, two angles), and a specialized Planetary system (latitude, longitude, altitude). Within these frameworks, it defines vector quantities like position, displacement, velocity, and acceleration.

The library also covers concepts related to rotation and periodic phenomena. It includes definitions for angles, angular velocity (how fast something is spinning), and angular acceleration (the rate of change of spin). For waves and oscillations, it defines key properties such as frequency, period (the duration of one cycle), wavelength, and damping coefficients.

In essence, this system acts as a universal rulebook for other models. By providing a common, unambiguous, and machine-readable definition for everything from "distance" to "angular acceleration," it allows complex engineering systems (like vehicles, satellites, or power plants) to be modeled with a high degree of precision and clarity.
