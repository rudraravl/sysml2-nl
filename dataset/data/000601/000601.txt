This model describes a quantitative esthesiometer system, a medical or research device designed for the precise assessment of a person's sense of touch. Its primary functions are to measure the minimum force a person can feel (their sensory threshold) and their ability to distinguish between two distinct points of contact on their skin (spatial resolution or two-point discrimination).

The core of the system is a mechanical **Probe Head** assembly that directly interacts with the patient. This unit features two tactile probes made of stainless steel. It contains a sophisticated **Force Regulator** that can apply very specific, computer-controlled amounts of force, ranging from a barely perceptible 0.01 Newtons up to 5 Newtons, with high accuracy. The Probe Head also includes a **Spacing Mechanism** that can precisely adjust the distance between the two probes, from 1mm to 50mm apart, which is essential for two-point discrimination tests.

To conduct a test, a clinician uses the **Control Module** to initiate a test sequence. The system follows an automated protocol, such as the "ascending method of limits," where it starts by applying a very low force with the probes and gradually increases it. The patient uses a simple **Patient Interface**, typically pressing a response button the moment they feel the sensation. This interface also provides audio and visual cues to guide the patient during the test.

The patient's response and the corresponding force level are sent to the **Data Processor**. This internal component acts as the system's brain for analysis, using a "staircase" algorithm to calculate the exact sensory threshold based on the series of stimuli and responses. It then logs these findings, along with other test parameters like the location on the body and the timestamp, into a final test results report.

An operator interacts with the system through a **Display Module**, which features an LCD screen and status indicator lights. This display shows the system's current status, guides the operator through the test procedure in real-time, and presents the final, calculated results.

The entire device operates through a structured lifecycle. When powered on, it performs a self-test and a calibration routine to ensure accuracy. It then enters a "ready" state, awaiting a command to begin a test. Once a test is complete, it analyzes the data, displays the results, and, after the operator acknowledges them, returns to the ready state for the next assessment. The system also includes an error state to safely handle any malfunctions.
