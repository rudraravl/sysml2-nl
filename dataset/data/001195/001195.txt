This is a Pitometer Log, a marine navigation instrument designed to measure a vessel's speed through the water and the total distance it has traveled. It provides real-time speed and distance information that is essential for navigation and voyage planning.

The system's operation begins with a Pitot tube, a hydrodynamic sensor that extends through the ship's hull. This tube measures the difference between the dynamic pressure of the water flowing past the hull and the surrounding static water pressure. This raw pressure difference is then sent to a pressure transducer, which converts it into a calibrated electrical signal.

This signal is processed by a velocity computer. Using the principles of fluid dynamics (specifically Bernoulli's principle), the computer calculates the vessel's instantaneous speed through the water. The system is designed to be calibrated by adjusting factors like water density to maintain accuracy.

The calculated speed is then used in two ways. First, it is sent to a display unit for the crew to monitor the ship's current speed. Second, it is fed into a distance integrator. This component continuously calculates the distance traveled over small time intervals and adds it to a running total, effectively acting as the ship's odometer. This accumulated distance is also sent to the display unit. The entire system works in a continuous loop to provide constant, up-to-the-minute updates on the vessel's speed and total distance logged.
