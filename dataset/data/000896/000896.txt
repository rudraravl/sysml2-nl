This model describes a complete hydraulic pumping subsystem, designed to generate and supply pressurized hydraulic fluid to power external machinery. It functions as a self-contained hydraulic power unit.

The system's core consists of an electric motor driving a hydraulic pump. The pump draws low-pressure hydraulic fluid from a reservoir, which acts as a storage tank for the fluid. The motor's rotational energy is converted by the pump into hydraulic pressure, which then delivers a high-pressure flow of fluid to a "fluid consumer"—this represents the external equipment, such as an actuator or cylinder, that the subsystem is powering. Once the consumer has used the fluid's energy, the now low-pressure fluid is routed back to the reservoir, completing the circuit.

To ensure safe and reliable operation, the system incorporates a critical safety feature: a pressure relief valve. This valve is connected to the high-pressure line and is designed to automatically open if the system pressure exceeds a predefined safety limit, diverting excess fluid back to the reservoir to prevent component damage.

The subsystem also features an active control loop to maintain performance. A pressure sensor continuously monitors the output pressure of the pump. This real-time measurement is used by a control system to adjust the speed of the electric motor. If the pressure drops below what the consumer requires, the motor's speed is increased to compensate, ensuring a steady and correct operating pressure. The system is also equipped with a flow sensor to monitor the volume of fluid being delivered. The pump itself has a defined operational lifecycle, moving through states such as idle, starting, running, and faulted.
