This model describes an automated gasoline additive system designed to enhance the quality of base gasoline by precisely injecting and mixing chemical additives. The system's primary function is to increase the fuel's octane rating and introduce cleaning agents to optimize combustion performance, operating as a self-contained unit in a fuel processing line.

The system is built around several key physical components. It includes a main **Fuel Tank** that holds the untreated base gasoline and a smaller **Additive Reservoir** containing a concentrated chemical, such as a detergent cleaner and octane booster.

The entire process is managed by a central **System Controller**. When fuel starts flowing from the main tank, a **Flow Sensor** detects it, and the system transitions from an idle state to a monitoring state. A **Quality Sensor** then analyzes the incoming base gasoline to measure its initial octane rating.

If the controller determines that the fuel's quality is below the target threshold (e.g., an octane rating of 93), it initiates the treatment cycle. The controller calculates the exact amount of additive required and sends a command to a high-precision **Dosing Pump**. This pump draws the additive from its reservoir and injects it into a **Mixing Chamber**, where it combines with the flowing gasoline. The mixing chamber is designed to ensure the additive is thoroughly and efficiently blended into the fuel, creating a homogeneous mixture.

After treatment, the final blended fuel passes by the quality sensor again. This allows the controller to verify that the fuel now meets the required specifications for both octane rating and additive concentration. The system operates in a continuous feedback loop; if the quality is still not met, it can adjust the dosage. Once the fuel quality is acceptable, the system returns to monitoring the flow until the process needs to be repeated or the fuel flow stops.
