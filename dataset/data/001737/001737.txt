This model describes an eductor-jet pumping system, a type of pump with no moving parts that uses a high-pressure fluid to pump and mix a second, lower-pressure fluid. The system is designed for applications where simplicity, reliability, and the absence of mechanical components are critical.

The core of the system is the eductor-jet pump itself, which operates on the Venturi principle. It is composed of four main internal components that work in sequence:

1.  **Converging Nozzle:** A high-pressure "motive" fluid from an external source is fed into this nozzle. The nozzle's shape accelerates the fluid to a very high velocity, which simultaneously causes a sharp drop in its pressure.
2.  **Suction Chamber:** The high-velocity, low-pressure jet created by the nozzle passes through this chamber. The low pressure acts as a vacuum, drawing in a "suction" fluid from a separate reservoir.
3.  **Mixing Throat:** In this section, the motive fluid and the entrained suction fluid are thoroughly mixed. The momentum from the high-speed motive fluid is transferred to the suction fluid, propelling the combined stream forward.
4.  **Diverging Diffuser:** The mixed fluid then enters the diffuser, which gradually widens. This shape slows the fluid down, converting its kinetic energy (velocity) back into potential energy (pressure).

The complete pumping system includes the eductor pump, a high-pressure source to supply the motive fluid, a reservoir for the suction fluid, and a destination for the final discharged fluid. The process results in the suction fluid being pumped to a pressure higher than its starting pressure, allowing it to be moved to the desired destination.

The system's operation follows a defined lifecycle, moving from an `idle` state to `starting` as motive flow is established. It then enters an `operating` state, which is monitored for steady or fluctuating flow conditions, and finally proceeds to a `stopping` phase for a controlled shutdown.
