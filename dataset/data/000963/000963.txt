This is a medical or research-grade actigraphy system used to monitor a person's rest and activity cycles, with a primary focus on analyzing sleep patterns. The system consists of two main components: a wrist-worn wearable device and a separate computer workstation for data analysis.

The wearable device, an "Actigraphy Watch," is designed for continuous, long-term use. It is worn on the wrist and secured with a comfortable, medical-grade silicone strap. At its core is a tri-axial accelerometer that constantly measures the user's motion. An internal processor converts these raw motion readings into "activity counts" for set time intervals, called epochs (e.g., every 60 seconds). Based on the level of activity and the time of day, the processor also classifies each epoch into a sleep phase, such as awake, light, deep, or REM sleep.

This processed activity and sleep data is stored in the device's internal memory, which can hold several weeks of continuous recordings. The device is powered by a long-lasting rechargeable battery, designed to operate for at least two weeks on a single charge. The watch operates in several modes: it can be actively recording data, idle, in the process of downloading its stored data, or charging.

The second part of the system is the Analysis Workstation, which is a computer running specialized software. After a monitoring period is complete, the data stored on the watch is transferred to this workstation. The software then performs an in-depth analysis of the activity and sleep data. It identifies sleep and wake periods, calculates key clinical metrics like total sleep time, sleep efficiency (the percentage of time in bed actually spent asleep), and the amount of time spent in different sleep stages. The final output is a comprehensive Sleep Analysis Report, providing objective insights into the user's sleep quality and patterns for clinical assessment or research.
