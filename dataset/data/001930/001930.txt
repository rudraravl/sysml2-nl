This model describes a modular spacecraft system designed for autonomous operation on a scientific mission.

The spacecraft is composed of five primary, interconnected subsystems. A central **Power Subsystem** generates electricity using solar panels and manages stored battery power, distributing it to all other components. The **Attitude Control Subsystem (ADCS)** is responsible for sensing and maintaining the spacecraft's orientation in space, ensuring its solar panels, antennas, and instruments are correctly pointed. For movement, a **Propulsion Subsystem** uses fuel to generate thrust, allowing the spacecraft to perform orbital maneuvers.

The core purpose of the mission is carried out by the **Payload Subsystem**, which contains the scientific instruments used for data collection. All communication with ground control is handled by the **Communication Subsystem**, which transmits the collected science data and vehicle telemetry back to Earth, while also receiving new commands.

In operation, the spacecraft executes a continuous mission loop: it monitors and adjusts its attitude, performs maneuvers to alter its trajectory, collects data with its payload, and communicates with the ground. This entire system is designed with specific constraints in mind, including a maximum total mass for launch, a minimum battery charge level for survivability, and a required data rate for effective communication.
