This model describes an automated irrigation pumping system designed specifically for the A.M. Bohnert Rice Plantation. Its primary function is to draw water from a source and deliver it to an irrigation field to meet the crop's water requirements.

The core of the system is a heavy-duty, cast iron centrifugal pump manufactured by A.M. Bohnert. This pump uses a rotating six-vaned impeller, driven by a stainless steel shaft, to pressurize and move large volumes of water. The shaft is supported by grease-lubricated ball bearings and sealed with a durable silicon carbide mechanical seal to prevent leaks, ensuring reliability throughout the long irrigation season.

Powering the pump is a 7.5kW, 480-volt electric motor. The motor is directly coupled to the pump's drive shaft, spinning it at a nominal speed of 1750 RPM to achieve the required flow and pressure.

The entire system is connected through a network of PVC piping. A suction pipe draws water from the source, passing it through a strainer to remove debris and a foot valve to keep the pump primed. After being pressurized by the pump, the water is pushed through a long discharge pipe equipped with a check valve to prevent backflow and a gate valve for manual flow control. This pipe delivers the water to the rice field's distribution network.

Automation is handled by a dedicated control panel. This panel houses a motor starter, overload protection relays, and a pressure switch. The pressure switch continuously monitors the water pressure at the pump's outlet. It automatically starts the motor when the system pressure drops below a set point and stops it when the pressure reaches the upper limit, ensuring the irrigation field receives water on demand without manual intervention. The pump itself operates through a clear cycle of states: off, starting, running, and stopping, all managed by this control system.
