This model describes a conductive electrode gel system used in medical applications to measure bioelectric signals from the body, such as in an electrocardiogram (ECG) or electroencephalogram (EEG). The system's primary function is to create a reliable electrical connection between the human skin and a measurement electrode, which is essential for capturing clear and accurate signals.

The core of the system is the gel itself, a specialized formulation with key properties for performance and safety. It has high electrical conductivity, which allows it to efficiently transmit the body's faint electrical signals. The gel's viscosity (or thickness) is carefully balanced so that it's easy to apply, stays in place without running, and maintains a stable contact layer. For patient safety, the gel is biocompatible with a neutral pH to avoid irritating the skin.

In practice, the system works by applying a thin, uniform layer of the gel to the skin's surface. This gel layer overcomes the high electrical resistance (impedance) of the dry outer layer of skin, acting as an "impedance reducer." By creating this low-resistance path, the gel effectively couples the bioelectric signal from the skin to the electrode, ensuring it can be measured with high quality and a good signal-to-noise ratio.

The gel's performance follows a predictable lifecycle. It starts in a stable, stored state inside its container. Once applied, it enters an active state where it effectively conducts signals. Over time, as it is exposed to air and skin, it will begin to degrade (for example, by drying out), eventually becoming depleted and losing its conductive properties, at which point it must be cleaned off and replaced.
