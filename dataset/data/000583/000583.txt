This model describes a high-speed, air-driven dental handpiece system, commonly known as a dental drill. It is designed for precise dental procedures, such as removing decay or shaping teeth, by using a rapidly rotating cutting tool.

The system is composed of several key components that work together. The core of the handpiece is an **Air Turbine**, which acts as a miniature motor powered by compressed air. This turbine can achieve very high rotational speeds, up to 400,000 RPM, to enable efficient cutting of hard tooth structures. The speed of the turbine is controlled by the dentist via a **Foot Pedal**. The more the pedal is pressed, the more air is supplied to the turbine, and the faster it spins.

To prevent overheating of the tooth and surrounding tissue during high-speed operation, the system features an integrated **Water Delivery System**. This component provides a continuous spray of cooling water directly onto the cutting area. This cooling function is critical for patient safety and is automatically activated when the drill's speed exceeds a certain threshold.

The rotational power generated by the turbine is transferred through a precision **Bearing Assembly**, which ensures the rotation is smooth and stable, to a **Burr Chuck** at the tip of the handpiece. The chuck is a specialized clamp that securely holds the small cutting tool, or "burr."

A central **Control Module** manages the handpiece's operation. It interprets signals from the foot pedal to regulate the airflow for speed control and to command the water delivery system. The entire system operates in different states, including a `standby` mode when idle, an `operating` mode when the pedal is pressed, and a `shutdown` sequence when the pedal is released, ensuring the tool stops rotating quickly and safely. The system also includes safety features, such as an interlock that prevents operation if the cooling water supply is insufficient.
