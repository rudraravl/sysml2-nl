This describes a standard medical syringe system designed for the precise aspiration and injection of fluids.

The system is composed of three primary physical components: a barrel, a plunger, and a needle. The `BarrelChamber` acts as the main cylindrical container that holds the medical fluid, characterized by its maximum capacity and internal diameter. The `PlungerAssembly` is a piston with a seal that fits snugly inside the barrel. By applying force to this plunger, an operator can move it back and forth to either draw fluid into the barrel or expel it. The `NeedleTip` is the sharp, hollow point attached to the end of the barrel, providing a narrow channel for the fluid to pass through during aspiration or injection.

The syringe operates through a clear lifecycle of states. It begins in a `ready` (empty) state. When the plunger is pulled back, it enters a `drawing` state to aspirate fluid, becoming `loaded` once the target volume is reached. When the plunger is pushed forward, it transitions to the `injecting` state, delivering the fluid until it is `depleted`.

Key design considerations for this system include high accuracy in fluid delivery, structural integrity to withstand operational pressures without leaking, and the maintenance of sterility throughout the fluid path. The system is designed to handle medical fluids with specific properties and operate within defined force and pressure limits.
