This model describes an audio-animatronics system, the kind of robotic character you might see in a theme park attraction. Its purpose is to perform lifelike, entertaining shows for an audience by perfectly synchronizing pre-programmed physical movements with a corresponding audio track.

The core of the system is the **Animatronic Character**, a physical robot built with several articulated joints that allow it to move. This specific character has joints for panning and tilting its head, opening its jaw, rotating its torso, and pitching its left and right shoulders.

Each joint is a self-contained mechanical assembly driven by a **Servo Actuator**, which is the motor responsible for creating the physical motion. These actuators are not just simple motors; they provide feedback on their current angle and the amount of force (torque) they are exerting, allowing for precise control and monitoring.

The character's movements are managed by a set of dedicated **Motion Controllers**. There is a controller for each major joint or limb. These controllers act as the bridge between the high-level show commands and the low-level motor operations. They receive instructions like "move the head to a 45-degree angle" and translate them into the specific control voltages needed to drive the servo actuators to that exact position. They also process sensor feedback from the actuators to ensure the commands are being followed correctly.

The entire performance is orchestrated by a central **Show Sequencer**. This unit is the brain of the operation. It loads a complete **Show Program**, which contains both the audio track and a detailed, time-coded script of every single movement for every joint. During a performance, the sequencer reads this program and sends out a continuous stream of synchronized commands: audio data is sent to an **Audio Playback Unit** for the speakers, while motion commands are sent to the appropriate motion controllers on the animatronic character.

The system operates through several distinct states. It starts in a `standby` mode, powers up to a `ready` state when a show is loaded, and then enters a `performing` state to run the show. A key feature is its safety protocol; if sensors detect an issue like excessive force or an out-of-position joint, the system immediately triggers a `safeModeHalt`, stopping all motion and sound to prevent damage or injury.
