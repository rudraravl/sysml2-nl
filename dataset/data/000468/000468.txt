This model describes the Matibabu Diagnostic System, a portable, non-invasive medical device designed for the rapid screening of malaria. It is engineered for use in resource-limited settings, providing a point-of-care diagnosis without needing a blood sample or complex laboratory equipment. The system's primary function is to detect malaria parasites by analyzing the optical and magnetic properties of blood directly through a patient's finger.

The device is a self-contained, battery-powered unit. Its core component is a specialized **Sensing Probe** where the patient places their fingertip for the test. This probe integrates several key technologies: an **LED Emitter** shines light of a specific wavelength into the finger, a **Photodetector Array** captures the light patterns reflected from the blood cells, and a **Magnetometer Sensor** measures the distinct magnetic signature of hemozoin crystals—a byproduct of the malaria parasite.

The data gathered by the probe is processed by an internal **Signal Processor**. This unit runs a sophisticated algorithm that analyzes the combined optical and magnetic signatures to determine if malaria is present. It can distinguish between a positive, negative, or inconclusive result and is also capable of identifying the specific type of malaria parasite, such as *Plasmodium falciparum* or *Plasmodium vivax*.

The entire diagnostic process is designed to be quick and user-friendly. The device operates through a clear workflow: after being powered on, it enters a `ready` state. Once a test is started, it guides the user to position the finger correctly before beginning the `scanning` phase. After `analyzing` the data, the final result is compiled into a diagnostic report and shown on the built-in **Display Module**. The system is designed to complete a full diagnosis in under two minutes, making it highly effective for large-scale screening.
