This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of "characteristic numbers" as defined by the international standard ISO 80000-11. It is not a model of a single physical object, but rather a foundational, digital dictionary of dimensionless quantities used across various fields of science and engineering.

The purpose of this library is to provide a formal and consistent set of definitions for analyzing complex physical phenomena. The components are the individual characteristic numbers themselves, each representing a specific ratio of physical forces or properties.

For example, the library includes definitions for:
*   **Fluid Dynamics:** Quantities like the **Reynolds number** to predict fluid flow patterns (laminar vs. turbulent), the **Mach number** to understand the effects of compressibility in high-speed flows, and the **lift and drag coefficients** to analyze aerodynamic performance.
*   **Heat and Mass Transfer:** Numbers such as the **Fourier number** for analyzing transient heat conduction, the **Nusselt number** for characterizing convective heat transfer, and the **Schmidt number** for describing fluid flows where both momentum and mass diffusion are important.
*   **Magnetohydrodynamics:** Values like the **Hartmann number** and **magnetic Reynolds number** for describing the behavior of electrically conductive fluids (like plasmas or liquid metals) in the presence of magnetic fields.

In essence, this system is a reusable, authoritative reference that engineers and scientists can incorporate into their own models. By using this standard library, they can ensure their calculations and simulations are based on universally accepted definitions for fundamental physical relationships.
