This model describes a system for defining and calculating the mass of physical objects, particularly for complex assemblies. It represents objects that can be either simple, single-piece parts or complex structures made up of multiple subcomponents.

The system is built on two types of physical items:

1.  **Simple Things:** These are fundamental, indivisible components. Think of a single bolt, a metal plate, or a resistor. Such an object has a straightforward mass, and its total mass is simply its own weight.

2.  **Composite Things:** These represent assemblies or more complex objects built from other parts. A composite object has its own intrinsic mass (like the weight of an empty computer case or a car chassis) and also contains a collection of other components. These subcomponents can be either simple things or other, smaller composite things.

The key function of this system is to "roll up" the mass hierarchically. The total mass of a composite object is calculated by taking the mass of its own structure and adding the total masses of all the individual parts contained within it. This allows for an accurate mass calculation of a highly complex product, like a satellite, by breaking it down into its systems, subsystems, and individual components.
