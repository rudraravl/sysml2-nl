This model describes a Sengstaken-Blakemore tube system, a specialized, multi-lumen medical device used in emergency medicine to control severe, life-threatening bleeding from enlarged veins (varices) in the esophagus and stomach.

The core of the system is a flexible, 1-meter-long tube made of medical-grade silicone. This main tube body houses several critical components that work together to stop hemorrhage. It features two inflatable balloons: a larger **gastric balloon** (up to 500 mL) intended for the stomach and a smaller **esophageal balloon** (up to 200 mL) for the esophagus. When a patient is experiencing severe bleeding, this tube is inserted, and these balloons are inflated to specific pressures. The inflated balloons apply direct pressure against the internal walls of the stomach and esophagus, physically compressing the bleeding veins to stop the blood flow—a procedure known as mechanical tamponade.

In addition to the balloons, the device contains two separate aspiration channels, or lumens. The **gastric aspiration lumen** is used to suction blood and other fluids directly from the stomach, while the **esophageal aspiration lumen** clears the esophagus. This is crucial for two reasons: it allows medical staff to monitor if the bleeding has stopped and it prevents the patient from inhaling fluids into their lungs.

The operation of the device follows a strict procedure to ensure both safety and effectiveness. The gastric balloon is always inflated first and anchored in place with gentle traction before the esophageal balloon is inflated. The pressures in the balloons are carefully managed, with safety limits of 45 mmHg for the esophageal and 60 mmHg for the gastric balloon to prevent tissue damage. The system's operational states include deflated, inflating, maintaining pressure on the bleed, and deflating, all managed through external control ports where syringes and suction lines are connected.
