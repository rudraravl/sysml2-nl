This model represents a Nellcor pulse oximeter, a clinical-grade medical device used for the continuous, non-invasive monitoring of a patient's arterial oxygen saturation (SpO2) and pulse rate. Its primary function is to provide real-time vital sign data to clinicians, enabling the early detection of low blood oxygen levels (hypoxemia) and supporting patient safety.

The system is composed of several integrated components. The core of the system is an optical finger sensor that clips onto a patient. This sensor contains two light-emitting diodes (LEDs)—one red and one infrared—that shine light through the patient's finger tissue. A photodetector on the opposite side of the sensor measures how much of this light is absorbed.

The raw light signal from the sensor is fed into a central signal processing unit. This processor is the "brain" of the oximeter; it filters the signal to remove noise and then performs a critical calculation. By analyzing the different absorption rates of red and infrared light, which vary between oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, it accurately determines the percentage of oxygen saturation. The processor also analyzes the rhythmic pulsations in the light signal to extract the patient's pulse rate.

The calculated SpO2 and pulse rate are then sent to a display module, which presents the information clearly on an LCD screen for clinicians to read. The display typically shows the numeric values for oxygen saturation and heart rate, and often a plethysmographic waveform that visually represents the patient's pulse.

A crucial part of the system is the integrated alarm subsystem. It constantly compares the patient's live readings against preset safety thresholds. If the SpO2 level drops below a critical point or the pulse rate becomes too high or too low, the system activates both an audible alarm (a beeping sound) and a visual alarm (a flashing light) to immediately alert medical staff. The system can differentiate between alarm severities, such as a "critical" alarm for low oxygen versus a "warning" for an abnormal pulse.

The entire device is powered by a self-contained unit with a rechargeable battery, designed to operate for several hours on a single charge, making it suitable for continuous bedside or portable monitoring. The system as a whole is engineered to meet strict medical device requirements for measurement accuracy, patient safety, and operational reliability.
