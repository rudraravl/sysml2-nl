This is a Fabry-Perot Interferometer, a high-precision optical system designed for high-resolution spectral analysis and wavelength filtering. It uses the principles of optical interference to selectively transmit light of very specific wavelengths.

The system's core is a resonant optical cavity formed by two highly reflective, parallel mirrors separated by a precise distance. A coherent laser source provides an input beam of light that is directed into this cavity. Due to the multiple reflections between the mirrors, only light of specific wavelengths that satisfies a resonance condition (where waves interfere constructively) can build up in intensity and pass through. All other wavelengths are effectively filtered out.

A photodetector is placed at the output of the cavity to measure the intensity of the transmitted light. The critical distance between the two mirrors is controlled with extreme precision by a piezo actuator, which can make adjustments on the nanometer scale in response to a control voltage.

To ensure stable operation, the system employs a stabilization controller that forms a feedback loop. This controller monitors the intensity of the transmitted light measured by the photodetector and sends correction signals to the piezo actuator. This process actively maintains the perfect mirror spacing to keep the cavity locked on a resonance peak, compensating for any disturbances like temperature fluctuations or vibrations.

The interferometer can be operated in two primary ways: it can perform a spectral analysis by systematically scanning the mirror spacing to measure the transmission spectrum of the input light, or it can be locked to a single resonance peak to function as a highly stable and narrow-band optical filter.
