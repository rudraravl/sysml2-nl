This model describes a Claytronics system, a form of programmable matter designed to dynamically form and change three-dimensional shapes. The system is composed of a massive swarm of thousands of tiny, self-contained, cylindrical robotic units called "catoms" that are managed by a central "Ensemble Controller."

The primary function of this system is to physically create objects on demand. The entire collection of catoms can work together to reconfigure itself from one shape (e.g., a cube) into another (e.g., a wrench) by having each individual unit move to a new position.

Each catom is an independent robot equipped with its own processor, sensors, power supply, and an actuator. It doesn't have wheels or legs; instead, it moves by latching onto its neighbors. A catom features six electromagnetic ports, which allow it to form strong bonds with adjacent catoms, creating a solid, stable structure. These ports are also used for communication.

The process of changing shape begins when the Ensemble Controller is given a target shape. The controller calculates the final destination for every single catom and transmits these commands to the swarm. Each catom then operates in a cycle: it senses its local environment, detaches its electromagnetic bonds, pivots around a neighboring catom to move into an adjacent empty space, and then re-establishes new bonds. This process is repeated across the entire swarm in a coordinated fashion to prevent collisions until the new target shape is achieved and verified by the controller.

During operation, a catom can be in several states: idle while waiting for commands, actively reconfiguring by moving and re-bonding, or stable and bonded to maintain the final object's shape.
