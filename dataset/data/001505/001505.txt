This system models the entire semiconductor technology ecosystem to forecast the evolution of computer processors based on the principles of Moore's Law. Its primary function is to support strategic planning, investment decisions, and manufacturing optimization within the semiconductor industry by simulating technological and economic progression over time.

The system is composed of three main, interacting components:

1.  **Semiconductor Processor:** This represents the actual computer chip. It is defined by key technical specifications, including its transistor count, feature size (e.g., 3-nanometer), power consumption, and clock frequency. The model tracks a processor through its entire lifecycle, from the initial research and design phase, through development and prototyping, into mass production, and eventually to its end-of-life.

2.  **Technology Forecaster:** This component is responsible for creating long-term technology roadmaps. It uses the core principle of Moore's Law—that the number of transistors on a chip doubles approximately every two years—to project the specifications for future generations of processors. It takes into account market demands and generates a multi-year plan, predicting how performance metrics will evolve.

3.  **Fabrication Facility:** This represents the physical manufacturing plant (or "fab") where the chips are produced. It assesses the practical and economic feasibility of manufacturing the advanced chip designs projected by the forecaster. This component considers critical real-world factors like the current manufacturing technology node, production capacity, and the manufacturing yield, which directly impacts the cost and viability of producing a new chip. It can also simulate processes for optimizing manufacturing to improve yield and reduce costs.

These components work together in an integrated cycle. The Technology Forecaster generates a roadmap of future chip designs. These designs are then fed as input to the Fabrication Facility to determine if they can be built effectively and at a reasonable cost. The successful output of the manufacturing process defines the specifications for the next generation of Semiconductor Processors, completing the cycle of forecasting, manufacturing, and product evolution.
