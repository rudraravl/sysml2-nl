This model describes a controlled ember system designed to generate continuous and stable radiant heat through a flameless combustion process. Its primary function is to convert the chemical energy stored in solid fuel into thermal energy by carefully managing a slow, glowing burn, much like a charcoal briquette in a high-tech grill or a specialized heating unit.

The system is built around four key components that work in concert. A **fuel bed** serves as the reservoir for the solid, carbon-based fuel. This fuel is supplied to a central **combustion core**, which is the heart of the system where the actual burning occurs. To sustain this reaction, an **air supply system** delivers a regulated flow of oxygen. The intense heat generated within the core is then transferred to a **thermal radiator**, a component specifically designed to efficiently release this energy into the surroundings.

Operationally, the system has a distinct lifecycle. It begins in a **dormant** state before being ignited. Once lit, it enters a stable **glowing** phase, where it actively monitors its temperature and regulates the airflow to maintain a steady heat output. If the reaction starts to weaken due to low fuel or oxygen, it transitions to a **fading** state. From here, it can attempt to revive itself by increasing the air supply to return to a glowing state, or it will eventually become **extinguished** once the fuel is spent. This self-regulating behavior makes it a reliable source for persistent, long-duration, flameless heat.
