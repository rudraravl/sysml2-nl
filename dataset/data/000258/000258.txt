This model describes a basic internet networking system, outlining the common electronic devices and connection types found in a home or small office. The system is designed to facilitate the flow of data between various pieces of hardware.

At its core, the system consists of a general category of powered devices. Every device requires an electrical supply, characterized by its voltage and current, to operate. They are all designed to handle data through one or more connections.

The system defines several specific types of these devices:
*   A **Wi-Fi Router** acts as a network hub. It has multiple physical cable ports (Ethernet) for wired connections and also broadcasts one or two wireless networks (e.g., 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands) for other devices to connect to wirelessly.
*   A **DSL Router** is another type of hub that connects to the internet via a specific wired DSL phone line. It then distributes that internet access to other local hardware through multiple physical Ethernet cable ports.
*   A **Mobile Device**, such as a smartphone or tablet, is an end-user device. Its key feature is having multiple wireless connection capabilities, allowing it to connect to networks like Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and 4G/5G cellular.

The connections that link these devices are also defined. They are all two-way paths for sending and receiving data. These connections are categorized as either **Wireless Connections**, which are defined by their operating frequency, or **Cable Connections**, which are defined by the number of physical wires they contain.
