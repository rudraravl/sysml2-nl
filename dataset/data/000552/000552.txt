This model describes a Blind Insertion Airway Device (BIAD) system, a piece of emergency medical equipment used to quickly establish a breathable airway for a patient who is unconscious or unable to breathe on their own. The system is designed for rapid deployment by paramedics or other medical personnel without needing to directly see the patient's vocal cords, which is often difficult in emergency situations.

The core of the system is a specialized airway tube that is inserted into the patient's throat. This tube features two inflatable cuffs: a larger one that seals the upper part of the throat (pharynx) and a smaller one that blocks the entrance to the esophagus. When inflated, these cuffs ensure that any air pushed into the tube is directed into the lungs and prevents stomach contents from being aspirated. The tube itself comes in different sizes to fit various patients.

To support the airway tube, the system includes several key components. An inflation syringe is used to fill the cuffs with a specific volume of air, and a pressure manometer (gauge) connects to the cuffs to ensure they are inflated to a safe pressure—enough to create a seal but not so much as to cause tissue damage.

Once the tube is in place and sealed, a manual ventilation bag (like a bag-valve mask) is attached to the tube's external port. An oxygen source can be connected to this bag to supply high-concentration oxygen. The operator then squeezes the bag to deliver breaths to the patient. The entire process is monitored using a patient monitor, which measures the patient's oxygen saturation and the level of carbon dioxide in their exhaled breath to confirm the tube is correctly placed and ventilation is effective. The system's operational lifecycle involves distinct phases: inserting the device, inflating the cuffs, verifying correct placement, and then continuously ventilating and monitoring the patient.
