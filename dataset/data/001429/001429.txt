This model describes an integrated Building Management System (BMS) for a single floor of a commercial building. Its primary purpose is to automate and intelligently manage the floor's environment, security, and energy consumption to ensure occupant comfort and safety while maximizing efficiency.

The system is designed for a typical office floor layout, which includes individual offices, a conference room, a server room, a break room, a restroom, and a main hallway connecting all these spaces. Each of these areas is equipped with a suite of sensors and actuators.

The floor's operations are handled by four interconnected core subsystems:

1.  **HVAC System:** This is the central climate control unit for the entire floor. It receives real-time temperature, humidity, and air quality data from sensors in each room. Based on this information and occupancy levels, it dynamically adjusts heating, cooling, and ventilation to maintain a comfortable and healthy environment where people are present, while saving energy in empty rooms.

2.  **Lighting System:** This is a smart lighting network that manages all the lights on the floor. It uses occupancy sensors to automatically turn lights on when someone enters a room and off when it's empty. It also uses ambient light sensors to dim the artificial lights when there is sufficient natural daylight, further reducing energy use.

3.  **Access Control System:** This system manages security and physical access to various rooms. It controls the door locks for offices, the server room, and other secure areas. It can grant or deny access based on security credentials and pre-defined schedules, and it maintains a log of all access events for security audits.

4.  **Energy Management System:** This system acts as the central power controller, monitoring the total electricity consumption of the floor. It distributes power to the HVAC, lighting, and access control systems. During periods of high demand, it can intelligently reduce power to non-essential areas (a process called load shedding) to prevent overloads and minimize energy costs.

The entire system operates in different modes. During normal business hours, it's in a **Normal Operation** mode, prioritizing occupant comfort. When it detects that the floor is mostly empty (e.g., overnight), it can switch to an **Energy Saving Mode**, reducing lighting and relaxing temperature controls. In case of a fire alarm or other emergency, it enters an **Emergency Mode**, which can automatically unlock all doors for evacuation and turn on all lights to aid in a safe exit.
