This is an automotive Blind Spot Monitoring System designed to enhance driver safety by alerting them to vehicles in adjacent lanes that may not be visible in the side mirrors.

The system is composed of several key components working together: two radar sensors, a central control module, and a pair of visual indicators. The radar sensors are mounted on the left and right rear sides of the vehicle and are responsible for continuously scanning the blind spot zones. Each sensor can detect other vehicles, measuring their distance, relative speed, and angle.

The data from both radar sensors is fed into a central control module, which acts as the brain of the system. This module also receives real-time information from the vehicle itself, including its current speed and whether the left or right turn signals are active. The system is designed to operate only when the vehicle is moving above a minimum speed (e.g., 10 km/h); below this speed, it remains inactive.

When the control module detects a vehicle in a blind spot, it triggers a visual indicator, typically an icon or light located in the corresponding side mirror. This serves as a standard warning to the driver. The system provides a more urgent, escalated alert if the driver activates their turn signal to change lanes while a vehicle is present in that blind spot. This escalated warning might involve the indicator flashing or becoming brighter to more effectively capture the driver's attention and prevent a potential collision. The entire process, from detection to warning, is designed to be very fast, updating within milliseconds to provide timely feedback.
