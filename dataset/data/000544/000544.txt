This is a Bárány Chair system, a medical device designed for assessing a patient's vestibular system, which controls balance and spatial orientation. It works by subjecting a patient to precise, controlled rotational movements to stimulate the inner ear and observe the resulting physiological responses, such as involuntary eye movements (nystagmus) and feelings of dizziness.

The core of the system is a motorized, rotating platform upon which a patient is securely seated. The seat assembly includes a headrest and a safety harness to ensure the patient remains safely and correctly positioned throughout the test. A powerful drive motor provides the torque to rotate the platform, capable of operating in both clockwise and counter-clockwise directions at various speeds.

The entire operation is governed by a central control system. An operator inputs test parameters, such as the target speed, duration, and direction of rotation. The control system then commands the motor to smoothly accelerate the chair, maintain a constant rotational speed for the specified time, and then smoothly decelerate to a stop. The chair operates through several distinct states, including idle, accelerating, constant rotation, decelerating, and a stopped state.

While the test is underway, an integrated observation system monitors the patient for vestibular responses. This system is designed to detect and record key indicators like nystagmus, which are crucial for diagnosis. A comprehensive safety system runs in parallel, ensuring that patient restraints are engaged, the chair's speed stays within safe limits, and an emergency stop can be activated at any time to bring the chair to a quick and safe halt.
