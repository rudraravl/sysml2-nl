This model describes a portable, manually-operated stirrup pump system, specifically configured for firefighting services. It's a self-contained unit designed to draw water from a local source and deliver it at high pressure to extinguish fires.

The system is composed of three primary physical components: a fluid reservoir, the pump mechanism, and a delivery hose. The reservoir is a container, likely a portable tank or bladder, that holds the water supply for the pump. The delivery hose connects to the pump's outlet to direct the pressurized water stream onto a target.

The core of the system is the stirrup pump itself. It is a manually operated, positive displacement pump. An operator provides stability by standing on a footplate at the base and pumps a T-handle up and down. This handle is connected to a piston that moves inside a cylinder.

The pump's operation is based on a simple two-stroke cycle controlled by a pair of one-way check valves. On the upward "intake" stroke, the piston creates a vacuum in the cylinder, which opens an inlet valve and draws water in from the reservoir. On the downward "discharge" stroke, the piston compresses the water, forcing the inlet valve shut and opening an outlet valve, which expels the water under pressure into the delivery hose. The system is designed to repeat this cycle continuously to generate a steady flow of water.
