This model describes a system for analyzing the fuel economy of a ground vehicle. The analysis is designed to simulate the vehicle's performance and determine if it can meet a specific efficiency requirement.

The vehicle itself is defined by its key physical characteristics, including its base mass, the mass of its cargo, the diameter of its wheels, and the efficiency of its drivetrain.

The analysis works by running a virtual test of the vehicle over a specific driving scenario. This scenario is laid out as a series of waypoints, with each point defining a target position, speed, and time. In essence, this creates a detailed driving route with required acceleration and deceleration phases.

The simulation proceeds in two main steps. First, based on the vehicle's mass and the demands of the driving scenario, it calculates the moment-by-moment engine power needed to follow the route precisely. Second, it uses this calculated power profile to determine how much fuel the engine would burn to complete the trip.

The final output is the vehicle's overall fuel economy for that specific journey, which is then compared against a performance requirement (in this case, achieving better than 30 miles per gallon) to pass or fail the analysis.
