This model describes a portable digital breathalyzer, a device designed to measure a person's Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) by analyzing a breath sample. Its main purpose is to determine if an individual's alcohol level is above the legal limit.

The system is composed of several key components working together. A **Breath Sensor** is responsible for capturing a breath sample from the user. This sensor has specific sensitivity and calibration settings to ensure accuracy. Once a sample is taken, it's passed to an **Analysis Module**. This internal processor converts the measured breath alcohol concentration into an equivalent blood alcohol concentration using a standard conversion factor. The module then compares this calculated BAC against a pre-set legal limit to determine if the user is in violation.

The result is then sent to a **Display Unit**, which shows the user their exact BAC level and provides a clear, color-coded alarm status: 'safe', 'warning' (if approaching the limit), or 'violation' (if over the limit). The entire operation is managed by a central **Control Unit**, which guides the device through its various states: from 'idle' to 'ready' when a test is started, through 'sampling' and 'analyzing', to 'complete' when the result is displayed. The whole device is powered by an internal **Power Supply**, likely a battery.

In a typical use case, a user initiates a test, causing the device to become ready. They then blow into the sensor. The device analyzes the sample, calculates the BAC, and displays the result within a few seconds, along with a clear visual or audible alarm indicating their level of intoxication relative to the legal limit before resetting for the next use.
