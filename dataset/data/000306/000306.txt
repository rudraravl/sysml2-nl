This model represents a digital library of standardized quantities and units for the fields of physical chemistry and molecular physics. It is not a physical object, but rather a formal, computer-readable version of the international standard ISO 80000-9. The purpose of this system is to serve as a foundational knowledge base for scientific and engineering software, simulations, and other complex models, ensuring that calculations and data exchange are consistent and accurate.

The library defines a comprehensive set of concepts used to describe chemical systems and their behavior. These include:

*   **Fundamental Quantities:** Basic concepts like the amount of a substance (moles), the number of entities (atoms, molecules), relative atomic mass, and molar mass.
*   **Molar Properties:** Quantities expressed on a per-mole basis, such as molar volume, molar internal energy, molar enthalpy, and molar heat capacity. These are crucial for thermodynamic analysis of chemical substances.
*   **Mixtures and Solutions:** A wide range of properties for describing mixtures, including different types of concentration (mass concentration, mole fraction, molality), partial pressure, and volume fraction. It also defines concepts for non-ideal solutions, such as chemical potential, activity, fugacity, and osmotic pressure.
*   **Chemical Reactions:** Key parameters that govern chemical reactions, including the stoichiometric number (for balancing equations), the extent of reaction, reaction affinity (the driving force), and various equilibrium constants that predict the final state of a reaction.
*   **Transport and Electrochemistry:** Properties related to the movement of particles and charge, such as diffusion coefficients, ionic strength, and electrolytic and molar conductivity.
*   **Statistical Mechanics:** Concepts that connect the microscopic behavior of molecules to macroscopic properties, such as various partition functions and the statistical weight of quantum states.

In essence, this system functions as a definitive, standardized dictionary for the language of physical chemistry. By providing precise, unambiguous definitions for these quantities and their associated units (like Joules per mole or moles per liter), it allows different engineering models and software tools to work together seamlessly, preventing errors and misinterpretations in complex scientific and technical applications.
