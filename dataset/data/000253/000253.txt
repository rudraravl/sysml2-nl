This model describes an Electrical Impedance Tomography (EIT) system, a non-invasive medical imaging device used to monitor physiological functions, such as lung ventilation or blood flow, in real-time.

The process involves several key participants and steps. It begins with an engineer or technician who prepares the system by performing calibrations to ensure accuracy. This technician is also responsible for physically applying a specialized belt, which contains multiple electrodes, to the patient.

Once the patient is prepared, a doctor initiates the monitoring session. The EIT system then automatically performs its core functions: it collects electrical impedance data from the patient's body via the electrode belt and processes this raw data to generate cross-sectional images. These images visualize the internal conductivity changes within the body.

Finally, the generated images are presented to the doctor, who analyzes them to assess the patient's condition, aid in diagnosis, or guide treatment. The system represents a complete clinical workflow, from technical setup and patient interaction to automated data processing and expert medical analysis.
