This model describes a Perineometer, a medical device designed to measure and assess the strength of pelvic floor muscles. It is used for rehabilitation and therapeutic exercises by providing real-time biofeedback to the user or a clinician.

The system consists of several integrated components. The primary sensing element is an `InflatableProbe` that is inserted to take measurements. Inside this probe is a sensitive `PressureSensor` that detects changes in intravaginal pressure caused by muscle contractions.

The data from the sensor is sent to a central `ProcessingUnit`. This unit acts as the brain of the device, filtering the raw pressure signals and analyzing them to calculate key performance metrics. These results include a general strength index, the peak pressure achieved during a contraction, and the duration a contraction can be sustained.

The analyzed results are then sent to a `DisplayUnit`, which features a screen to provide immediate visual feedback to the user. This allows them to see their performance in real-time. A `ControlUnit` manages the device's overall operation, allowing a user or therapist to select different measurement modes like "quick," "extended," or "endurance" tests, and to start or stop a session.

The Perineometer operates through a clear sequence of states. After being powered on, it enters an idle state. Before a measurement begins, it runs a calibration routine. It then proceeds to the measuring, analyzing, and finally displaying states, guiding the user through a complete assessment session before returning to idle.
