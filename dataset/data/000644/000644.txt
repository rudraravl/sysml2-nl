This model describes a comprehensive, integrated cardiology instrument system, likely found in a hospital's critical care unit or emergency room. It's a patient-side device designed for continuous cardiac monitoring, automated diagnosis, and therapeutic intervention.

The system is composed of several key modules working together. An **ECG Monitor** continuously captures the heart's electrical activity using a 12-lead setup, producing a detailed waveform. Simultaneously, a **Vital Signs Monitor** measures the patient's heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation at regular intervals.

This data from both monitors is sent in real-time to two places: a high-resolution **Display Console** for medical staff to view the live ECG waveform and numerical vital signs, and a central **Diagnostic Processor**. This processor acts as the system's brain, analyzing the incoming data to automatically detect dangerous conditions such as cardiac arrhythmias, ST-segment elevation (a key indicator of a heart attack), abnormal blood pressure, and low oxygen levels.

Based on its analysis, the processor triggers an **Alarm Module**, which can issue different levels of alerts. Minor abnormalities might trigger a standard alert, while life-threatening conditions activate a more urgent, critical alarm to summon immediate medical help.

Crucially, the system also includes a built-in **Defibrillator** for therapeutic intervention. When the diagnostic processor detects a condition requiring electrical shock, it can command the defibrillator to begin charging. The defibrillator then progresses through its states from idle to charging, signals when it is ready, and delivers a controlled electrical shock to the patient upon authorization from a clinician. This integration allows the system to not only monitor and diagnose but also provide immediate, life-saving treatment.
