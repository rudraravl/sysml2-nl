This is an autonomous, solar-powered fluid pumping system designed to automatically transfer a fluid, such as water, from a source reservoir to a destination tank. The entire system is self-contained and operates without a connection to an external power grid.

The system is comprised of several key components that work together to manage power and fluid flow. A **solar panel array** serves as the primary power source, converting sunlight into electrical energy. This generated power is managed by a **charge controller**, a critical component that regulates the flow of electricity. The charge controller intelligently decides whether to send power directly to the pump, use it to charge the system's **battery bank**, or do both simultaneously. It also protects the battery from overcharging.

The **battery bank** stores excess energy generated by the solar panels, allowing the system to operate during the night or on overcast days when solar power is unavailable.

A central **pump controller** acts as the brain of the system. It continuously monitors the battery's charge level as well as the fluid levels in both the source reservoir and the destination tank. Based on pre-set rules, this controller decides when it is safe and appropriate to operate the pump. For instance, it will prevent the pump from starting if the battery is too low, if the source reservoir is nearly empty (to prevent the pump from running dry), or if the destination tank is almost full (to prevent overflow).

The heart of the fluid transfer operation is a **centrifugal pump**. When activated by the pump controller, it draws fluid from the **source reservoir** and pushes it into the **destination storage tank**. The pump's operation is state-driven, transitioning from off to starting, running, and stopping based on commands from the controller and the availability of power. This ensures a controlled and reliable fluid transfer process.
