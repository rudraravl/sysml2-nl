This model describes a manually operated water pumping system, specifically a "Bush Pump," designed to lift water from deep underground sources like a well or borehole to the surface.

The system is comprised of several key mechanical components working together. A long pump rod connects a surface-level operating handle to a submerged pump cylinder. This cylinder, located deep within the water source, contains a piston that moves up and down. The pump's operation relies on two critical one-way valves: a foot valve at the bottom of the cylinder that lets water in from the source, and a piston valve located on the piston itself.

The pumping action is a two-stroke cycle:

1.  **Downstroke:** When the user pushes the handle down, the piston moves downwards. This pressure closes the foot valve and forces the piston valve to open, allowing water already in the cylinder to flow to the area above the piston.
2.  **Upstroke:** When the user pulls the handle up, the piston rises. The piston valve closes, trapping the water above it. The piston then lifts this entire column of water up through a rising pipe towards the surface. This upward movement also creates a suction effect below the piston, which opens the foot valve and draws a new supply of water into the cylinder from the well.

This repeated cycle creates a continuous flow of water, which exits through a discharge spout at the top and can be collected in a storage tank. The entire system is designed to be robust and operate efficiently at significant depths, with requirements ensuring it can function up to 40 meters deep and deliver a minimum flow rate of 12 liters per minute.
