This model represents a business analysis and decision-support framework for a futuristic space mining corporation. It's designed to evaluate the risk-adjusted profitability of deploying mining frigates to different regions of space, each with varying levels of security and resource value.

The core of the system is a profitability calculation that helps the corporation decide where to mine. It does this by balancing the potential revenue against operational costs and the significant risk of losing ships. The analysis considers three distinct operational scenarios:

*   **High-Security Space:** A relatively safe region where the probability of survival is very high (98%), but the available ore is less valuable and operational costs are low.
*   **Low-Security Space:** A more dangerous area with a moderate chance of survival (75%). The risk is higher, but so is the value of the ore and the cost of operating.
*   **Null-Security Space:** A lawless, high-risk, high-reward frontier. It offers the most valuable ores but comes with high operational costs and a low probability of a ship surviving its mission (only 50%).

For each scenario, the framework calculates the expected profit by factoring in the mining ship's ore collection rate, the market price for that region's ore, and the statistical chance of being destroyed. Essentially, it's a strategic tool used to determine if the potential rewards of mining in dangerous space outweigh the expected financial losses from destroyed ships.

The system also defines a broader set of calculations to measure the overall effectiveness of the mining fleet, including its operational uptime, ore delivery efficiency, and survivability, which are influenced by factors like pilot skill, ship cargo capacity, and situational awareness.
