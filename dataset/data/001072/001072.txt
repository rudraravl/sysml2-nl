This is a Golay Cell Detector System, a highly sensitive thermal instrument designed to detect infrared radiation and convert it into a measurable electrical signal. It is particularly useful for detecting a broad spectrum of infrared wavelengths.

The system is composed of four main components that work together in a sequence. First, an **Absorption Window** at the front of the detector takes in the incident infrared radiation. This window is designed to efficiently absorb the radiation's energy and convert it into heat.

This heat is transferred to a sealed **Gas Chamber** located directly behind the window. The chamber contains a fixed amount of gas which, when heated, expands and increases in pressure.

One wall of this gas chamber is a very thin, flexible, and reflective **Flexible Membrane**. As the pressure inside the chamber rises, it pushes against this membrane, causing it to bulge or deflect outwards. The amount of this deflection is directly proportional to the intensity of the original infrared radiation.

Finally, an **Optical Readout** system continuously monitors the reflective surface of the membrane. It typically uses a light source (like a laser) and a photodetector. By measuring changes in the reflected light, this system can precisely determine the extent of the membrane's deflection. It then converts this physical displacement measurement into a proportional electrical voltage signal, which serves as the detector's final output.

The detector operates in a continuous cycle. It starts in a stable, equilibrium state. When radiation enters, it triggers the process of absorption, gas expansion, and membrane deflection, leading to an electrical signal. Once the radiation source is removed, the gas cools, the pressure drops, and the membrane returns to its resting state, ready to make another measurement.
