This model describes a powered exoskeleton system, a wearable robotic suit designed to augment a human user's physical strength and endurance. Its primary purpose is to assist with physically demanding tasks, such as lifting and carrying heavy objects in industrial or logistical environments, by amplifying the user's natural movements.

The system is built around a lightweight but strong structural frame, likely made from a material like carbon fiber composite, that the user wears. This frame is articulated with a series of powered joints that mirror the user's own, including joints for the hips, knees, ankles, shoulders, elbows, and even lumbar (back) support.

Each of these joints is a complex module containing the core components of the system. A powerful motor actuator acts as the "muscle," providing the assistive force. A set of integrated sensors, including force sensors and angle sensors, acts as the "nerves," detecting the user's applied force and the joint's current position and velocity.

The "brain" of the exoskeleton is a central control processor. It continuously gathers data from all the sensors across the various joints to interpret the user's intended movement in real-time. Based on this detected intent and a configured assistance level, the processor calculates the precise amount of torque needed from each motor. It then sends commands to the motor actuators, which apply a supportive force that works in concert with the user's own effort, making the task feel significantly easier. This entire sense-compute-actuate cycle happens extremely quickly—within milliseconds—to ensure the assistance feels natural and responsive.

A high-capacity rechargeable battery pack powers the entire system, providing electricity to the control processor and all the motor actuators. It is designed to last for an extended period, such as a full 6-hour work shift, on a single charge.

The exoskeleton operates in several modes, including a standby state, an active mode where it provides assistance, and an emergency mode that can be triggered if a safety-critical issue is detected. The system continuously monitors its own status, including battery level and joint states, to ensure safe operation. If it detects a problem, it can quickly transition to a safe state to protect the user.
