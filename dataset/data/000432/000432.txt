This is the Anne Chamney System, an integrated medical monitoring and response platform designed for continuous patient surveillance in a clinical setting. Its main purpose is to automatically detect adverse changes in a patient's condition, alert medical staff, and help coordinate the delivery of care, creating a closed-loop system from monitoring to intervention.

The system is composed of several key components that work together:

*   A **Biomedical Sensor Array** is attached to the patient and continuously measures critical vital signs, including heart rate, blood pressure (both systolic and diastolic), oxygen saturation, and body temperature.

*   This stream of live data is fed into a **Clinical Analysis Processor**. This processor acts as an intelligent watchdog, constantly evaluating the patient's vitals against pre-defined safety thresholds. It analyzes the data to calculate a risk score and determines if the patient's condition is stable, requires attention, or is critical.

*   If the processor detects a dangerous trend or a breach of a safety threshold, it automatically generates a **Clinical Alert**. This alert, which includes the patient's ID, the specific vital sign of concern, and a severity level, is sent to the **Clinical Response Team**—the doctors and nurses responsible for the patient.

*   Upon receiving an alert, the medical team assesses the situation, formulates an appropriate treatment plan, and can issue commands to an **Intervention Controller**. This controller is a system that manages the execution of treatment, such as administering medication or adjusting life support. It operates in distinct states, moving from standby to preparing and executing a command, ensuring the intervention is validated and properly delivered.

*   Throughout this entire process, a **Data Archive System** logs all collected vital signs and any alerts that were generated. This creates a complete and timestamped medical record for the patient, which can be used by the response team to review a patient's history and support their clinical decisions.

In essence, the system connects continuous, automated monitoring with rapid, coordinated clinical action, aiming to improve patient safety and treatment outcomes in a critical care environment.
