This model represents a standardized digital library of physical quantities, units, and definitions for the field of condensed matter physics. It essentially serves as a formal, machine-readable version of the international standard ISO 80000-12, providing a common and unambiguous language for scientists, engineers, and software systems that deal with the properties of solid materials.

The library provides a comprehensive vocabulary to describe the fundamental characteristics of materials at the atomic and electronic levels. This includes:

*   **Crystal Structure:** It defines quantities like `lattice vectors` and `lattice plane spacing` to describe the geometric arrangement of atoms in crystalline solids, as well as concepts like the `Bragg angle`, which is essential for analyzing these structures using diffraction techniques.
*   **Thermal and Vibrational Properties:** It includes concepts related to how atoms vibrate within a solid, such as the `Debye temperature` and `Debye angular frequency`, which model the collective vibrations (phonons) that determine a material's heat capacity and thermal conductivity.
*   **Electronic Properties:** The library defines the key parameters that govern a material's electronic behavior, distinguishing metals, semiconductors, and insulators. These include `Fermi energy` (the energy of the highest-occupied electron state), `gap energy` (the energy required to excite an electron into a conducting state), and the `work function` (the energy needed to remove an electron from the surface).
*   **Transport Phenomena:** It specifies quantities that describe how electrons and heat move through a material, such as `electrical resistivity`, the `Hall coefficient` (related to charge carrier density), and thermoelectric properties like the `Seebeck` and `Peltier` coefficients.
*   **Specialized Phenomena:** The system also defines critical parameters for magnetism and superconductivity. This includes the `Curie` and `Néel` temperatures, which mark phase transitions for magnetic materials, and quantities like the `superconduction transition temperature`, `London penetration depth`, and `critical magnetic flux density` for superconductors.

By organizing these diverse physical concepts into a structured and interconnected framework, the library serves as a foundational knowledge base for modeling, simulation, and data exchange in materials science and solid-state physics.
