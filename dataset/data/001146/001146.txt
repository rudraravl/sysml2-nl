This model describes a microwave chemistry sensing system, a scientific instrument designed to determine the composition and concentration of chemical samples. It works by analyzing how a sample's unique dielectric properties—its ability to store and dissipate electric energy—affect a microwave signal passing through it.

The core of the system is a precisely engineered **Resonant Cavity**, a chamber designed to resonate with microwaves at a specific frequency. A chemical sample is placed inside this cavity for analysis. To ensure accurate and repeatable measurements, a **Temperature Controller** first brings the sample to a stable, predefined temperature.

The measurement process begins when a **Microwave Source** generates a highly stable microwave signal and directs it into the resonant cavity. As the microwaves interact with the chemical sample, the sample's molecular structure alters the signal's characteristics. These changes, such as the amount of signal reflected, the amount transmitted, and shifts in the cavity's resonant frequency, are unique to the sample's chemical makeup.

Two highly sensitive **Microwave Detectors** are positioned to capture these changes: one measures the reflected signal and the other measures the transmitted signal. The raw data from these detectors is then passed to a **Signal Analyzer**, which processes the information to calculate the sample's fundamental electrical properties, specifically its permittivity and conductivity.

Finally, a **Concentration Calculator** uses these calculated properties, along with the sample's temperature, to compute the final chemical concentration. This calculation relies on a pre-established calibration model that correlates the measured electrical properties with known concentrations. The system then outputs the final concentration value, a confirmation of the measurement's validity, and an estimate of its uncertainty.

The entire unit operates through a series of states, beginning with initialization and self-calibration when powered on. Once ready, it waits for a measurement command, then proceeds through temperature stabilization, data acquisition, and processing before returning to a ready state for the next sample.
