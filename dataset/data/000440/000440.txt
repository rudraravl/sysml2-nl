This model represents a Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) therapy system, a sophisticated medical device designed to treat the motor symptoms of neurological conditions like Parkinson's disease. The system works by implanting components into a patient to deliver controlled electrical pulses to specific, targeted areas of the brain, thereby modulating abnormal neural activity.

The complete system consists of several key components working in concert. The central piece is the **Implantable Pulse Generator (IPG)**, a small, battery-powered device surgically placed in the patient's chest. This unit acts as the brain of the system, containing the power source, processing circuits, and telemetry for wireless communication.

Connected to the IPG are one or more **Lead Assemblies**. These are thin, insulated wires with multiple electrodes at their tips. The leads are carefully guided through the brain to a specific target, such as the subthalamic nucleus. These electrodes are the interface with the neural tissue, responsible for both delivering the therapeutic stimulation and sensing the brain's electrical signals.

An **External Clinician Programmer** completes the system. This is a handheld device used by doctors to communicate wirelessly with the implanted IPG. It allows them to set and fine-tune the stimulation parameters (like pulse amplitude and frequency), monitor the device's battery life, and check the overall system integrity without needing further surgery.

In operation, the system functions as an adaptive, closed-loop therapy. The electrodes on the leads continuously sense the neural activity in the target brain region. The IPG analyzes these signals to detect pathological patterns. Based on this analysis, it calculates and generates a precise electrical stimulation pulse, which is then delivered back to the brain tissue to normalize its activity. This cycle of sensing, calculating, and stimulating happens continuously.

The system operates in various states, including being off, in a standby mode where it can be programmed, and in an active therapy mode. It also incorporates critical safety features, such as constantly monitoring the electrical impedance of the leads and the charge being delivered to ensure patient safety, and includes alerts for conditions like a low battery.
