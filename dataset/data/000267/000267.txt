This model describes a corporate, space-based asteroid mining operation designed for the large-scale extraction, processing, and transportation of ore for profit. The system is centered around a **Mining Corporation** which manages fleets of specialized spaceships and measures its success through various performance metrics like profitability, fleet utilization, and operational efficiency.

The core of the operation consists of a fleet of ships, primarily **Mining Frigates**, which are the workhorses responsible for direct ore extraction. These are supported by larger capital ships, such as the **Rorqual**, which act as mobile industrial command centers capable of compressing raw ore to make it more compact for transport. All ships, regardless of type, share common characteristics like cargo capacity, shield strength, and the ability to travel at faster-than-light "warp" speeds.

Each spaceship is controlled by a **Pilot** housed within a life-sustaining **Pilot Pod**. From a control console, the pilot executes all key operations: plotting courses, navigating through asteroid belts, targeting asteroids for mining, deploying defensive drones, and docking with stations.

The system operates in a dynamic and often dangerous space environment. The primary resources are found in **Asteroid Belts**, which contain various asteroids with finite amounts of ore. However, these areas are also patrolled by **Hostile Ships** that can attack the mining fleet, making defense a critical component of any operation. Friendly **Stations** serve as the main logistical hubs where ships can dock to safely offload their cargo, resupply, and perform maintenance.

A typical operational cycle involves a pilot navigating a mining frigate to an asteroid belt, mining until its cargo hold is full, and then warping to a station to offload the ore. Throughout this process, the pilot may deploy drones to defend against threats. The overall success of the corporation depends on effectively coordinating its fleet to maximize ore extraction while minimizing ship losses and logistical downtime.
