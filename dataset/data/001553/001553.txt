This is a biomimetic robotic fin system designed for underwater propulsion. It functions much like a fish's tail, generating thrust by performing controlled oscillatory motions in the water. The system is a self-contained unit intended for integration into larger underwater vehicles.

The system is built around four main components that work together in a continuous feedback loop:

1.  A **Fin Blade**: This is the physical surface that interacts with the water. Its shape, size, and rigidity are designed to efficiently convert motion into forward thrust.
2.  An electric **Motor**: This is the muscle of the system, receiving power and generating the necessary torque to pivot the fin blade back and forth.
3.  An **Angle Sensor**: This component precisely measures the fin's current angle and speed of movement at all times, providing real-time feedback.
4.  A **Fin Controller**: This is the brain of the system. It receives external commands specifying a target angle, velocity, or oscillation frequency.

In operation, the controller compares the desired target motion with the actual motion measured by the angle sensor. Based on any difference, it calculates the exact command—how much torque and in which direction—to send to the motor. The motor then adjusts the fin blade's position accordingly. This cycle repeats continuously at a high rate, allowing the fin to achieve smooth, accurate, and powerful oscillations.

The system has distinct operational states, including an idle state, a brief initialization and calibration phase upon startup, and an active state for propulsion. It is designed with specific performance requirements in mind, including generating a minimum amount of thrust, maintaining a high degree of control accuracy, and adhering to strict weight limits for use in aquatic drones or other vehicles.
