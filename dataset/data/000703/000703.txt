This is a Nasal Expiratory Positive Airway Pressure (EPAP) system, a medical device designed to treat conditions like obstructive sleep apnea. Its primary function is to maintain a gentle, positive pressure in the user's airway specifically during exhalation to prevent it from collapsing.

The system consists of the main therapeutic device and a nasal interface, such as a mask or nasal prongs, which connects to the patient. The core of the device is an intelligent valve and sensor system that works in sync with the patient's natural breathing.

Here's how its components work together:

A **Respiratory Monitor** continuously senses the patient's airflow to detect whether they are inhaling or exhaling and to measure the current pressure in the airway. Based on the breathing phase, a **Pressure Regulator** controls an **Expiratory Valve**.

During inhalation, the valve opens completely, providing minimal resistance so the patient can breathe in comfortably and naturally. When the monitor detects that the patient is starting to exhale, the regulator signals the valve to partially close, creating a controlled resistance. This resistance generates the positive pressure that serves as a pneumatic "splint" to keep the airway open. The regulator constantly adjusts the valve to maintain the prescribed therapeutic pressure level.

The system also includes a critical **Safety Monitor**. This component constantly checks the airway pressure against predefined safety limits. If the pressure becomes too high or too low, it can trigger an alarm or initiate an emergency shutdown to ensure patient safety. The entire system is designed to deliver effective respiratory therapy that is responsive, comfortable, and safe for the user.
