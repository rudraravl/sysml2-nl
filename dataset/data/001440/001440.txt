This model describes an automated HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning) system designed to regulate the climate within a building or a specific room, referred to as the "conditioned space." The primary goal of the system is to maintain a comfortable indoor temperature and air quality by automatically heating, cooling, or circulating air as needed.

The system operates as a closed-loop feedback system. A **Temperature Sensor** located within the conditioned space continuously measures the ambient temperature and humidity. This real-time data is sent to a central **Thermostat**, which acts as the brain of the operation. The Thermostat compares the current room temperature to a user-defined setpoint.

Based on this comparison, the Thermostat makes a decision:
*   If the room is too cold, it commands the system to enter heating mode.
*   If the room is too hot, it switches to cooling mode.
*   If the temperature is within an acceptable range, it may simply ventilate the air at a low speed or remain idle.

These commands are sent to the core functional components. For heating, a **Furnace** is activated to generate heat. For cooling, a **Compressor** (the heart of the air conditioning unit) is switched on.

Regardless of whether it's heating or cooling, air movement is managed by an **Air Handler** unit, which contains a powerful **Blower Fan**. This fan pulls return air from the room through a **Duct System**, passes it across the furnace's heat exchanger or the compressor's cooling coils, and then pushes the newly conditioned (heated or cooled) air back into the room through a separate set of supply ducts. The Thermostat also controls the fan's speed (low, medium, or high) to manage the rate of airflow.

In essence, the system continuously circulates air, senses the environmental conditions, and intelligently activates its heating or cooling functions to maintain the desired temperature set by the user.
