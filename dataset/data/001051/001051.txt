This is a scientific fisheries acoustic system designed for the quantitative assessment and mapping of fish populations in marine and freshwater environments. Typically mounted on a research vessel, its primary purpose is to provide data for sustainable fisheries management and marine ecosystem analysis.

The system operates using active sonar. A core component, the **Split-Beam Transducer**, transmits controlled acoustic pulses (pings) into the water and listens for the returning echoes. These echoes, which bounce off fish and other objects, are captured as raw data.

This raw echo data is then handled by an **Echosounder Processor**. This digital signal processing unit filters out background noise and analyzes the acoustic signals to detect potential targets, such as individual fish or large schools.

Once targets are identified, a **Biomass Analyzer** performs the key scientific calculations. It uses the strength of the returning echoes to estimate critical information like the density of fish within a school, the size of individual fish, and ultimately, the total biomass (weight) of the fish population in the surveyed area.

To ensure the data is geographically accurate, a **GPS Navigation System** is integrated into the system. It continuously tracks the vessel's position and speed, allowing every acoustic detection to be precisely georeferenced. All the final, processed survey data, which includes maps of fish school locations and their estimated biomass, is managed and stored by a **Data Recorder**.

The system operates through several distinct phases: it starts in a standby mode, undergoes calibration to ensure accuracy, enters an active surveying state to collect data, and finally moves to an analysis state to process the collected information before returning to standby. This entire process allows scientists to conduct comprehensive acoustic surveys to monitor the health and abundance of fish stocks.
