This is a hydraulic plunger system designed to convert mechanical force into fluid pressure and displacement. It functions like a basic positive-displacement pump, using a reciprocating (back-and-forth) motion to move a fluid.

The system consists of a central **plunger assembly** that moves inside a stationary **cylinder**. The plunger itself is made up of a **head**, which directly pushes the fluid; a **shaft**, which connects the head to the external force mechanism; and a **seal**, which prevents the pressurized fluid from leaking back past the plunger head.

In operation, an external force is applied to the plunger, causing it to slide forward within the cylinder. This movement, known as the "extending" stroke, displaces the working fluid, forcing it out of a port at high pressure. The pressure generated is directly proportional to the applied force and inversely proportional to the surface area of the plunger head.

The system operates in a defined cycle: it begins at an **idle** or rest position, moves through an **extending** phase to push fluid, reaches a fully **extended** position at the end of its stroke, and then **retracts** back to the start, ready for the next cycle. The plunger's movement is constrained by a maximum stroke length to prevent damage. The entire system is designed to operate safely below a specified maximum pressure limit.
