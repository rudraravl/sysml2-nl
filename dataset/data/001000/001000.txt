This describes a non-contact capacitance probe system designed for measuring the physical properties of materials. Its primary purpose is to determine a material's thickness, dielectric properties, or its proximity to the sensor without making physical contact.

The system is composed of several interconnected electronic modules. At its core is a **Probe Sensor**, which generates an electric field. When this probe is brought near a target material, the material's properties alter the capacitance measured by the probe. This measurement process is driven by an **Oscillator Circuit**, which produces a stable, high-frequency AC electrical signal that energizes the probe.

A dedicated **Power Supply Unit** provides the necessary voltage and current to operate both the oscillator and the processing electronics.

The raw capacitance reading from the probe is sent to a **Signal Processor**. This component is the "brains" of the system; it filters the incoming signal, applies gain, and performs calculations to convert the raw capacitance value into useful, processed data, such as the material's thickness or dielectric constant. This final, calculated result is then sent to a **Display Interface** for an operator to view.

In operation, the system functions in a continuous cycle: it generates the signal, measures the capacitance, validates that the reading is reliable, and then processes the data. If a reading is deemed invalid, it can trigger a recalibration process. The entire system operates through different states, including being powered off, initializing upon startup, waiting in a standby mode, and entering an active state during continuous measurement.
