This model represents a complete electrical power generation and distribution system, from the power plant to the local substation. The core of the system is a thermal power plant that converts the chemical energy in fuel into electricity.

The process begins in a boiler, which burns fuel to create high-pressure, high-temperature steam. This steam is then directed to a multi-stage steam turbine, causing it to spin at high speed. The turbine is mechanically coupled to an electrical generator, which converts this rotational energy into electrical power.

Once generated, the electricity must be transported over long distances. A step-up transformer increases the voltage to a high level (345 kV) to make transmission more efficient. This high-voltage power travels along a transmission line to a substation closer to consumers. Here, a step-down transformer reduces the voltage to a medium level. Finally, a distribution substation further processes the power, lowering the voltage again and splitting it into multiple feeder lines for delivery to homes and businesses.

A central control system monitors the electrical demand from consumers and adjusts the power plant's generation output to match it precisely. The entire system operates through distinct states, including startup, synchronizing with the electrical grid, steady power generation, load-following to handle demand changes, and controlled shutdown. This specific system has a rated capacity of 650 MW and is designed to maintain a stable grid frequency of 60 Hz.
