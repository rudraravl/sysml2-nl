This model represents a surgical instrumentation system designed for precision-guided orthopedic procedures, such as fixing bone fractures. The system provides a stable and accurate pathway for drilling holes and installing fasteners, like bone screws, at a predetermined angle and depth.

The core of the system is a long, hollow cylindrical bar, known as a **cannulated bar**, which is made from surgical-grade materials like stainless steel or titanium alloy. This bar is attached to an **ergonomic handle** that allows a surgeon to securely grip and position the entire assembly. At the opposite end of the bar is a **drill guide**, a precision sleeve that rests against the target site (e.g., a bone) to ensure the drilling operation starts at the correct point and angle. An adjustable **depth stop** mechanism is also part of the system to prevent the tools from penetrating too far, enhancing the procedure's safety.

The system is used in a multi-step procedure. First, the surgeon aligns the assembly with the target. A thin **guide wire** is then inserted through the handle, down the hollow channel of the cannulated bar, and into the bone to the required depth. This guide wire acts as a precise track for all subsequent steps.

Next, a cannulated **cutting tool** (a drill bit with a hole through its center) is slid over the guide wire and used to drill a hole. The guide wire ensures the drill bit follows the exact established path. After drilling, a cannulated **fastener** (a screw) is inserted over the same guide wire, ensuring it is placed accurately within the prepared hole. Once the fastener is secured, the guide wire and the rest of the instrumentation are removed.

The system operates through several distinct phases, including an initial idle state, a positioning phase for alignment, followed by guide wire insertion, drilling, and finally fastening. This guided, step-by-step process ensures a high degree of accuracy and repeatability in complex surgical or mechanical applications.
