This model describes a conceptual framework for defining and evaluating rules or conditions within a system. It's not a physical object, but rather a logical system for ensuring that a design or process adheres to a specific set of requirements and prohibitions.

At its core is the fundamental concept of a "Constraint Check," which is a single, specific rule that can be evaluated as either true or false. For example, a rule could be "the temperature must be less than 50°C" or "the user must be an administrator."

This framework allows for a collection of these individual checks to be managed together. This collection is then divided into two distinct categories:

1.  **Asserted Constraints:** These are the rules that are required to be true. They represent the mandatory conditions and requirements for the system to be considered valid or operating correctly.
2.  **Negated Constraints:** These are conditions that are explicitly forbidden and must be false. They define what the system must *not* do or what states it must not be in.

In essence, this is a system for formally expressing and verifying the operational boundaries of another system, ensuring it meets all its necessary conditions while avoiding all forbidden ones.
