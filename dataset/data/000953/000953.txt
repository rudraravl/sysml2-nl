This model represents an integrated infrastructure management system designed for a small village. Its primary function is to provide reliable, continuous water and electricity to essential community facilities—specifically a health clinic and a community center—and to ensure the long-term operation of this infrastructure through proactive monitoring and maintenance.

The system is composed of several key components that work together:

A **Water Supply System** provides potable water for the village. It draws water from a well using a hand pump and holds it in a large storage tank to ensure a consistent supply, even if the well's recharge rate varies. This water is then piped to the health clinic's sanitation facilities and the community center.

A hybrid **Power Generation System** supplies electricity. It primarily relies on a set of solar panels for clean energy generation. The power is stored in a large battery bank to provide electricity during the night or on cloudy days. For backup and to handle peak loads, a diesel generator is also part of the system. This combined system powers the medical equipment and lighting in the clinic, as well as the lights and communication equipment in the community center.

To ensure reliability, a **Monitoring Station** continuously tracks the status of both the water and power systems using a network of sensors. It collects data on metrics like water levels, power output, battery charge, and equipment health.

If the monitoring station detects a problem—such as a failing pump, low generator fuel, or a drop in power output—it sends out a **Maintenance Alert**. These alerts are handled by a dedicated **Maintenance Team** of technicians who are equipped with tools and spare parts to perform routine checks and emergency repairs on any part of the infrastructure.

In essence, this is a self-sufficient village utility system that not only provides essential services but also includes the necessary oversight and repair capabilities to keep them running smoothly and efficiently.
