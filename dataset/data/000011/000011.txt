This SysML model represents a simple vehicle's operational state machine.  The system is a vehicle (could be a car, a boat, a piece of heavy machinery, etc.) that can be in one of several states: stopped, ready, running, and paused.

The vehicle starts in a stopped state.  When a "VehicleStartSignal" is received, it transitions to the "ready" state, indicating it's prepared to operate. A subsequent "VehicleStartSignal" moves it from ready to "running," signifying active operation.

While running, receiving a "VehicleOnSignal" (perhaps mistakenly named, as it signifies stopping) transitions the vehicle to the "stopped" state.  Alternatively, a "VehicleOffSignal" (appropriately named) will put the vehicle into a "paused" state.

From the paused state, another "VehicleOffSignal" (meaning resuming operation in this context) will return it to the running state, while a second "VehicleOffSignal"  will move it to stopped state.  Finally, from the stopped state, a "VehicleOffSignal" (again, potentially a naming issue) transitions it to a "done" state, implicitly indicating a completion of a task or session.  The model doesn't explicitly define what "done" entails, beyond being a terminal state after completing a session in stopped state.  The inconsistency in signal interpretation from the paused state to the stopped state needs clarification in the real-world system design.
