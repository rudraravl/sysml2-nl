This model represents a foundational digital library, not a physical object. Specifically, it is a standardized catalog of physical quantities and units used in the field of classical mechanics, based on the international standard ISO 80000-4.

The purpose of this library is to provide a formal, unambiguous, and reusable set of definitions for engineers and scientists. It contains a comprehensive list of mechanical concepts, including fundamental properties like mass, density, and moment of inertia, as well as concepts related to motion and interaction, such as force, momentum, torque, pressure, and power. It also defines material properties like stress, strain, viscosity, and the modulus of elasticity.

For each physical quantity, the library specifies its precise scientific definition, its standard symbol (e.g., `F` for force), its relationship to fundamental dimensions (mass, length, time), and its associated measurement units (e.g., Newtons for force, Pascals for pressure). The system is sophisticated enough to distinguish between simple scalar magnitudes (a single value), vectors (quantities with a direction, like velocity or force), and tensors (for more complex properties like stress within a material).

Ultimately, this library acts as a foundational toolkit for modeling and simulation. By importing it into other models of real-world systems like vehicles, buildings, or fluid pumps, designers can ensure that all their calculations are based on a consistent and universally understood set of physical principles.
