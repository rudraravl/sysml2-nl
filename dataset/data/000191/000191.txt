This model represents a conceptual framework for conducting system safety and reliability analysis. It is not a physical object, but rather a structured vocabulary or toolkit used by engineers to describe, analyze, and document how a system might fail.

The framework consists of several key concepts that work together to build a complete picture of a potential problem:

*   **Situation:** Defines the specific operational context or conditions in which an event occurs, such as "operating during a thunderstorm" or "under heavy load."
*   **Cause:** Represents the root reason or trigger for an event, like "component wear" or "software bug."
*   **Failure:** Describes the specific malfunction or deviation from expected behavior, such as "engine overheating" or "data transmission error."
*   **Causation:** The direct link or relationship that connects a specific cause to a resulting failure.
*   **Scenario:** A complete narrative that ties all these elements together, describing a full sequence of events from an initial situation and cause, through the chain of causation, to the ultimate failure.

In practice, an engineer would use this framework to formally map out potential risks in a system, ensuring a thorough and consistent approach to identifying and mitigating failures.
