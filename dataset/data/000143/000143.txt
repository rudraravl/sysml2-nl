This model describes the startup and shutdown sequence for a modern vehicle, managed by an electronic control system. The system is designed with a strong emphasis on safety interlocks.

The system consists of the main vehicle, which has a sensor to detect if the brake pedal is depressed, and a separate vehicle controller, which acts as the brain for managing the vehicle's operational state.

The vehicle's lifecycle starts in an 'off' state. When a driver initiates the startup process (e.g., by pressing a start button), the system moves into a 'starting' phase. It will only transition to the fully 'on' state after it confirms two conditions are met: it receives a final 'on' signal and verifies that the brake pedal is being pressed. This "press brake to start" feature is a critical safety requirement.

Once the conditions are met, the system signals the main vehicle controller to activate and enters its 'on', or running, state. As soon as it becomes active, it performs a diagnostic self-test. While in this state, it continuously provides power to the vehicle's systems.

To shut the vehicle down, the driver sends an 'off' signal. As a final safety measure upon exiting the 'on' state, the system automatically applies the parking brake before transitioning back to the fully 'off' state.
