This is a closed-loop, electrically-powered pulser pumping subsystem designed to transfer fluid from a supply tank to a discharge point while precisely controlling the output pressure.

The system's core component is a **Pulser Pump**, which uses a flexible diaphragm moving back and forth within a pulsation chamber to create pressure pulses that move the fluid. This pump is powered by an electrical **Power Supply** and managed by an electronic **Controller**.

The process begins as fluid is drawn from a **Supply Tank**. It first passes through a one-way **Check Valve** to prevent backflow before entering the Pulser Pump. After being pressurized by the pump, the now-pulsating fluid flows through a second Check Valve and into an **Accumulator**. The accumulator is a key component that absorbs the pressure spikes from the pump, smoothing out the pulses to create a more stable and continuous stream of fluid.

After leaving the accumulator, the fluid passes through a **Flow Meter** that measures the flow rate and total volume transferred. Finally, it exits the system via a **Discharge Manifold**.

A **Pressure Sensor** is located at the discharge manifold to constantly monitor the output pressure. This measurement is sent as feedback to the Controller. The Controller compares the actual pressure to a desired setpoint and sends command signals to the Pulser Pump, adjusting its operation (likely its speed or frequency) to maintain the target pressure.

The system operates through several distinct states. It can be **idle**, in a **priming** state to prepare the pump and lines, or in a **running** state for normal operation. During operation, it monitors for abnormal conditions such as high pressure or low flow. If a critical issue is detected, the system can enter a **fault** state for safety.
