This is a comprehensive management system for serial publications like journals, magazines, and newspapers, which are identified by a unique International Standard Serial Number (ISSN). The system is designed to manage the entire lifecycle of these publications, from their initial registration and creation to their distribution, discovery, and long-term preservation.

The system is composed of several distinct, yet interconnected, components that work together:

*   An **ISSN Registration Authority** acts as the official registry. When a new journal or serial is created, it submits an application to this authority, which validates the request, verifies its uniqueness, and assigns a globally unique ISSN code. This ensures that every publication has a standardized and distinct identifier.

*   An **Editorial Management System** handles the content production for each publication. It manages the workflow for individual issues, moving them from a draft state through review and approval processes. Once an issue is finalized and approved, this system marks it as published. Each issue contains specific articles, identified by volume/issue numbers, publication dates, and other metadata.

*   A **Subscription Management System** manages the publication's audience. It maintains a database of subscribers, tracking their information and subscription status (e.g., individual, institutional, or trial). When the editorial system publishes a new issue, this component is responsible for distributing it to all currently active subscribers.

*   A **Catalog System** functions as a searchable public-facing database. It contains metadata for all registered publications, allowing users to find journals or magazines by searching for their title, publisher, or ISSN. This component makes publications discoverable.

*   An **Archive System** is responsible for the long-term storage and preservation of published content. After an issue has been published and distributed, it is sent to the archive, ensuring that past issues remain accessible for future retrieval.

Together, these components create a seamless workflow. A new publication is first registered to get its ISSN. Then, its individual issues are created and approved through the editorial system. Upon publication, the issue is simultaneously distributed to subscribers, indexed in the public catalog, and preserved in the archive.
