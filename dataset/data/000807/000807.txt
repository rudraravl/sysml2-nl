This describes the "Tubex" system, a pneumatic tube transport network designed to rapidly move physical documents and other small items between different locations within a building, such as an office.

The system uses cylindrical transport capsules to carry payloads, which can be documents or items weighing up to 1.5 kg. These capsules are propelled through a sealed network of tubes using controlled air pressure, achieving speeds of up to 8 meters per second.

The core components of the system include:
*   **Loading and Receiving Stations:** Designated points where users can send and receive items. A typical setup involves a central sending station (like a mailroom) and multiple receiving stations (such as specific office floors). At a loading station, a document is placed in a capsule, which is then sealed and launched into the network. At a receiving station, the arrived capsule is collected, and its contents are extracted.
*   **Tube Network:** A series of interconnected pipes that form the pathways for the capsules. The model shows a main tube that branches off to connect the single sending station to two different receiving stations.
*   **Pressure Generator:** This is the engine of the system, acting as a powerful air pump. It pressurizes the tube network to create the air differential needed to push the capsules from their origin to their destination.
*   **System Controller:** The central brain that manages all operations. It processes transport requests, coordinates the pressurization and launch sequence, and monitors the overall health of the system.
*   **Safety Sensors:** These are placed throughout the network to monitor for unsafe conditions, such as excessive pressure or physical obstructions within the tubes, allowing the system to halt and enter a safe state if a fault is detected.

The system operates through a distinct cycle of states: it begins in **standby**, moves to **loading** a capsule, then **pressurizing** the tubes, **transporting** the capsule, **receiving** it at the destination, and finally **depressurizing** the network before returning to standby. This entire process is automated and managed by the system controller to ensure reliable and safe delivery.
