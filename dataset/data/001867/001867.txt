This model represents an electromechanical solenoid actuator system, a common device used to convert electrical energy into precise, linear mechanical motion. It's designed for applications like controlling valves, operating locking mechanisms, or actuating automated switches.

The system's core components work in concert to produce this motion. It features an **electromagnetic coil** that, when supplied with a 24-volt electrical current, generates a powerful magnetic field. This field acts on a movable metal rod called a **plunger**, causing it to slide a specific distance—in this case, a 12 mm stroke. This movement generates the system's output force, which is strong enough to perform mechanical work.

To ensure the system can reset, a **return spring** is included. When the electrical power to the coil is turned off, the magnetic field collapses, and the spring pushes the plunger back to its original resting position. All these internal components are encased in a protective and durable **ferromagnetic steel housing**.

The solenoid's operation is controlled by simple "activate" and "deactivate" commands. Upon activation, the plunger moves to its extended position in under 40 milliseconds. When deactivated, the spring returns it to its starting point. This system is designed for high reliability and a long operational life, capable of performing at least 2 million actuation cycles while consuming minimal power.
