This model describes a Denis Browne bar, an orthopedic brace system designed for infants to maintain the correction of clubfoot after initial treatment like serial casting. Its primary purpose is to prevent the foot deformity from relapsing by holding the feet in a specific, outwardly rotated position.

The system consists of three main components that work together. At the center is a rigid metal bar, sized to match the infant's shoulder width. Attached to each end of this bar is an adjustable footplate. These plates are the key to the treatment, as they can be set and locked at a specific angle of external rotation, typically around 70 degrees. Finally, a pair of specialized orthopedic shoes are mounted onto these footplates.

In use, the infant's feet are placed into the shoes, and the entire assembly—bar, plates, and shoes—securely holds both feet apart and turned outwards. This continuous, gentle positioning maintains the corrected alignment of the foot's bones and ligaments. The system is used over an extended period, following a treatment plan that progresses through stages: an initial fitting, an active correction phase where the brace is worn nearly full-time, a maintenance phase, and finally, a weaning period as the correction becomes stable.
