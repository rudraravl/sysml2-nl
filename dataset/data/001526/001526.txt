This model describes a high-precision Permanent Magnet Motor Control (PMC) system, the kind often used in industrial automation, robotics, or CNC machinery where exact mechanical motion is critical. Its primary function is to take electrical power and precisely control an electric motor's position, speed, and torque based on external commands.

The system is a complete, self-contained unit built around several key components working together in a closed-loop fashion. At its heart is a **Permanent Magnet Motor**, which does the physical work of converting electrical energy into rotational motion. This motor is powered by a dedicated **Power Supply** that provides the required voltage and current.

The "brain" of the system is the **Motor Controller**. This unit receives commands, such as a target position or speed, from an external source. To know what the motor is actually doing, the controller constantly receives feedback from an **Encoder Sensor**, which measures the motor's exact rotational position and speed, and a **Current Sensor**, which monitors the electrical current being used.

The controller continuously compares the commanded position with the actual position from the sensors. Based on this comparison, it calculates the necessary adjustments and sends a control signal to a **Power Stage**. The power stage acts as an amplifier, taking the low-power signal from the controller and modulating the high-power electricity from the power supply to drive the motor. This entire cycle of sensing, calculating, and adjusting happens thousands of times per second, allowing the system to follow commands with extreme accuracy and responsiveness.

The system's operation is managed through different states. On startup, it initializes and calibrates its sensors. It then waits in an idle state for a command. When active, it continuously runs its control loop. The system is designed for safety and reliability, featuring a dedicated fault state that it enters if it detects a problem like an over-current condition. When a stop command is received, it executes a controlled deceleration to bring the motor to a safe halt before returning to idle.
