This model describes the power delivery system, or powertrain, of a vehicle. Its primary function is to convert a fuel command into rotational force (torque) and deliver it to two of the vehicle's wheels.

The system is defined by a sequence of functional steps that work together. It starts with a function that generates torque, much like an engine, based on the amount of fuel commanded. This initial torque is then passed to a second function that amplifies it, representing the action of a transmission or gearbox.

Following amplification, the torque is transferred by another function, analogous to a driveshaft, to the final stage. This last function acts as a differential, distributing the torque to two separate outputs, which would correspond to the left and right wheels.

The entire power delivery process is governed by a simple control system. It will not generate or transfer any torque until it receives an "Engine Start" signal. Once started, the powertrain functions are enabled. The process is deactivated upon receiving an "Engine Off" signal, at which point all torque-related functions are disabled.
