This model describes an active vapor lock prevention system, designed to ensure a continuous and reliable supply of liquid fuel to an engine, particularly under high-temperature conditions. The system is built around a standard fuel delivery architecture consisting of a fuel tank, a fuel pump, a fuel line, and the engine itself.

The core of the system is its ability to detect and counteract the conditions that lead to vapor lock—where fuel turns into vapor within the fuel line, blocking the flow and starving the engine. To do this, it is equipped with dedicated temperature and pressure sensors placed strategically on the fuel line near the engine intake. These sensors constantly monitor the fuel's physical state.

The system's control logic continuously analyzes the sensor readings to assess the risk of vapor lock. If the fuel temperature rises or its pressure drops to a critical point where it might begin to vaporize, the system automatically initiates a coordinated set of countermeasures. Simultaneously, it activates a cooling system to reduce the fuel's temperature, commands the fuel pump to increase its output pressure (which raises the fuel's boiling point), and opens a special vent valve to release any vapor that has already formed in the line.

By actively managing fuel temperature and pressure and venting excess vapor, the system maintains the fuel in a liquid state, guaranteeing an uninterrupted flow to the engine and ensuring its smooth operation across a wide range of environmental conditions.
