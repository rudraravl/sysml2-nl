This model describes a Stereotaxis Magnetic Navigation System, a sophisticated medical device used for performing minimally invasive procedures within a patient's cardiovascular system. The system's primary function is to remotely and precisely steer a flexible, magnetic-tipped catheter through complex blood vessels to a specific target area, such as the heart.

The system is composed of several key, interconnected components that work in concert. At its core is an **External Magnet System**, consisting of two large, powerful magnets positioned on either side of the patient. These magnets generate a controlled magnetic field in the operational area. By precisely adjusting the strength and orientation of this field, the system can manipulate the magnetic tip of the catheter inside the patient's body.

A live view of the procedure is provided by a **Fluoroscopy Imaging System**, which uses X-rays to create real-time images of the patient's internal anatomy and the catheter's location. This visual feedback is crucial for navigation.

The entire operation is orchestrated by a central **Navigation Workstation**. This is the system's "brain," where a physician plans the catheter's route on a user interface using the images from the fluoroscopy system. The workstation's software then calculates the exact magnetic field adjustments needed to guide the catheter along this planned path. It continuously receives position data from the imaging system and sends corresponding commands to the external magnets, creating a closed-loop control system for highly accurate navigation. The system can operate in manual, semi-automated, or fully automated modes, offering flexibility to the physician.

To ensure patient safety, a dedicated **Safety Monitoring System** runs continuously. It monitors critical parameters like the force being exerted by the catheter's tip against vessel walls and the total duration of the procedure. If these parameters exceed pre-set safety thresholds, the system can automatically issue a warning or trigger an emergency stop.

The overall procedure follows a structured sequence of phases, starting with planning the navigation path, followed by the active navigation of the catheter to the target. Once the target is reached, the intervention (the actual medical treatment) is performed, after which the catheter is safely retracted, and the procedure is completed.
