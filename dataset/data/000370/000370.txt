This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of physical quantities and units for the domain of electromagnetism. It is not a physical object, but rather a foundational knowledge base designed to provide a precise and unambiguous set of definitions for modeling and analyzing electrical and magnetic systems, based on the international standard IEC-80000-6.

The library systematically defines the core concepts of electromagnetism. It starts with fundamental properties like electric charge and electric current and builds upon them to describe field quantities such as electric field strength, magnetic flux density, and the forces they exert. It also includes all the key quantities used in circuit theory, including voltage (electric potential difference), resistance, capacitance, and inductance.

Crucially, the library doesn't just list these quantities; it formally defines the physical laws and mathematical relationships that connect them. For example, capacitance is defined by the relationship between stored charge and voltage, while impedance in AC circuits is described as the complex ratio of voltage to current, incorporating both resistance and reactance. It also captures concepts like the Poynting vector, which describes the flow of energy in an electromagnetic field based on the electric and magnetic fields.

Properties of materials are also defined, such as permittivity (how a material stores electric energy), permeability (how a material supports the formation of a magnetic field), and conductivity (how easily current flows through it). Each quantity is rigorously defined with its corresponding standard unit (like Volts, Amperes, Farads, Ohms) and its fundamental dimensionality in terms of base SI units like mass, length, time, and electric current.

In essence, this is a formal "dictionary" of electromagnetism. It serves as a foundational layer, ensuring that any engineering model built using it—such as a model of a motor, an antenna, or an integrated circuit—is based on a consistent, standardized, and physically correct understanding of the underlying principles.
