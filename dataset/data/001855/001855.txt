This model describes a CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing system, a biotechnology tool designed to make precise changes to the DNA sequence of a living organism. The system functions like a "search and replace" tool for genetics.

The core of the system relies on three main biological components: a target **DNA strand** that contains the genetic information to be modified, a **Guide RNA** molecule that acts as a programmable guide, and a **Cas9 protein** that functions as a pair of molecular scissors.

The editing process works in a sequence of steps. First, the Guide RNA and the Cas9 protein are combined to form an active unit called a **Ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complex**. This complex is the central tool that performs the editing.

Next, this RNP complex is introduced to the target DNA. The complex scans along the DNA strand until the Guide RNA finds and binds to a specific sequence that perfectly matches its own genetic code. Once this target site is recognized, the Cas9 protein component of the complex cuts both strands of the DNA.

After the DNA has been cut, the system's final module takes over to repair the break. Using a provided DNA repair template, it introduces the desired genetic modifications at the cut site. The final output is a new, **edited DNA strand** with the intended changes incorporated, and the system's overall success is measured by its editing efficiency.
