This model describes a six-axis industrial robotic arm system designed for precise, automated pick-and-place operations in settings like manufacturing or logistics.

The system is composed of a main articulated arm with six individual joints: a shoulder, upper arm, elbow, forearm, and a two-axis wrist. This six-joint structure gives the arm the flexibility to position and orient its end tool in any direction within its reach. At the end of the arm is an end-effector equipped with a controllable gripper, which is used to grasp, hold, and release objects with a specified amount of force.

A central controller serves as the brain of the robot. It receives high-level commands, such as a target 3D coordinate for the arm to reach. The controller processes this command by performing complex calculations (inverse kinematics) to determine the exact angle each of the six joints must move to. It then sends specific angle and torque commands to each joint's motor.

Each joint is an independent, powered unit that can rotate to a commanded angle while applying a specific torque. The joints continuously send sensor feedback, including their current angle and torque, back to the controller. This closed-loop system allows the controller to monitor the arm's status and ensure it moves accurately.

The entire system, including all joints and the gripper, is electrically powered. Its primary function is to execute complex sequences of movement, such as picking up an object from one location and precisely placing it in another. The system is designed with specific performance requirements, including the ability to position its gripper within 0.5mm of a target, handle payloads up to 5 kg, and operate safely by limiting the forces it exerts.
