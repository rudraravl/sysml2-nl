This model represents a physics-based simulation of a ground vehicle's straight-line motion, such as a car or a train. It defines the core dynamics that govern how the vehicle accelerates, moves, and changes position over time.

The system works by calculating the net power available to accelerate the vehicle. It starts with the total power delivered to the wheels, but then accounts for forces that resist motion. These include aerodynamic drag, which depends on the vehicle's speed, and rolling friction, which is related to the vehicle's mass.

By subtracting the power lost to drag and friction from the total wheel power, the model determines the actual power that contributes to acceleration. This net power, combined with the vehicle's mass and current speed, is used to calculate its new acceleration. Subsequently, this acceleration is used to update the vehicle's velocity and, ultimately, its new position over a small time step.
