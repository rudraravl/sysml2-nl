This model describes a residential electric water heater system designed to automatically heat and maintain a supply of hot water for household use.

The core of the system is a 200-liter insulated water tank, which receives cold water and stores it for heating. The heating is performed by two separate electrical heating elements: a powerful 4500-watt primary heater for rapid heating and a smaller 2000-watt secondary heater, likely for maintaining temperature efficiently.

The system's operation is managed by a central control unit, which acts as the brain. It constantly monitors the water temperature using an internal temperature sensor. This information is processed by a thermostat, which compares the current temperature to the user-defined target (e.g., 60°C). If the temperature drops below a certain threshold, the control unit activates the heating elements to bring the water back up to the desired temperature.

The system operates in several modes. It can be actively `heating` when the water is cold, `maintaining` the temperature once it's hot, or in `standby` when there's no demand for hot water. A circulation pump is included to ensure even temperature distribution within the tank and potentially to support a hot water recirculation loop in the home.

Safety is a critical aspect of the design. The control unit will shut down the heaters if the temperature exceeds a maximum safe limit of 85°C to prevent scalding. Additionally, a mechanical safety valve is built in to automatically release pressure if it builds up to a dangerous level inside the tank, preventing a potential rupture. The system is designed to be highly efficient, ensuring minimal energy loss while continuously providing hot water on demand.
