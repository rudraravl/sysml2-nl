This model describes a precision tweezer system, a hand tool designed for the delicate task of grasping, holding, and manipulating small objects.

The system is a mechanical assembly consisting of two primary components: a left arm and a right arm. These arms are connected at a central pivot joint, which allows them to open and close in a controlled manner. Each arm is designed with a specific length and material stiffness, which determines the mechanical advantage, or lever ratio, that translates the user's squeeze into a precise force at the tips. The ends of the arms are fitted with specialized grip tips, which are engineered with a specific radius and a smooth surface to ensure a secure, non-damaging contact with the object being handled.

The tweezers operate through a clear sequence of actions to manipulate an object. The process begins by measuring the target object's size to determine the necessary tip gap. The tips are then positioned and aligned to approach the object correctly. A controlled grip force is applied by the user, which is amplified by the arms to secure the item. The system is designed to verify that this grasp is both firm enough to hold the object's mass and gentle enough to avoid damaging it based on its fragility. Once a safe and secure grasp is confirmed, the object can be transferred to a new location. Upon reaching the destination, the user releases the pressure, and the tips open to gently release the object.

Operationally, the tweezers move through several states. They begin in an `idle` state with the tips open. When a task begins, they transition to `approaching` and `positioning` to align with the target. The core action occurs in the `gripping` state; if the hold isn't perfect, it can enter an `adjusting` state to correct the force. Once the object is securely held, the tweezers are in a `holding` state, ready to be moved. The `transferring` state covers the movement of the object, followed by the `releasing` state at the destination, after which the tool returns to `idle`.

Key design requirements for this system include the ability to handle objects within a specific size range (0.5 mm to 8 mm), apply a controlled and precise grip force (between 0.5 N and 5.0 N), and ensure the safe handling of delicate components through smooth tips and force limitations. The symmetrical design of the arms is critical for maintaining precision alignment and a balanced grip.
