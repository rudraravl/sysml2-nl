This model describes an electrically powered chain pump system, a type of water-lifting device typically used for irrigation or moving water from a low-lying source to a higher elevation.

The core of the system is a long, inclined housing tube, inside of which an endless chain loop operates. Attached to this chain at regular intervals is a set of tightly-fitting disks, made of a material like rubber. The bottom of this tube is submerged in a lower reservoir that holds the water supply.

Power for the system comes from an electric drive unit. This unit consists of an electric motor connected to a gear reducer, which slows the rotation and increases the torque. The gear reducer then turns a drive sprocket that engages with the chain, pulling it through the tube in a continuous loop.

The pumping action occurs as the disks on the moving chain enter the submerged end of the housing tube. As each disk enters, it traps a column of water between itself and the disk ahead of it. The chain's upward movement then lifts these successive columns of water through the tube. Because the disks fit snugly inside the tube, very little water leaks back down. When the water reaches the top, it is released from an upper discharge spout, from where it can flow to its destination.

The system is designed to meet specific performance targets, such as lifting water to a height of at least 10 meters and delivering a flow rate of at least 5 liters per second. It's intended for continuous, reliable operation, capable of handling fluids with low viscosity, like water.
