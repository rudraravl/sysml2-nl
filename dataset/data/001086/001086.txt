This is a Hydrocarbon Dew Point Control System, an automated industrial plant designed to process raw natural gas to meet quality standards for pipeline transportation. The primary purpose of the system is to remove heavier hydrocarbon components (like propane, butane, and pentanes) by chilling the gas, which causes these components to condense into a liquid. This process is crucial for preventing liquids from forming in downstream pipelines, which could cause blockages, corrosion, and operational hazards.

The system is composed of several key pieces of equipment that work together. Raw natural gas first enters the system where its temperature, pressure, and hydrocarbon dew point are measured by a suite of sensors, including a specialized **DewPoint Analyzer**. The gas then flows into a **Chiller Unit**, which is a heat exchanger powered by a dedicated **Refrigeration System**. The chiller cools the gas to a precise, low temperature (e.g., below -5°C).

This chilling process causes the heavier hydrocarbons to condense into a liquid. The resulting cold mixture of gas and liquid flows into a **Separation Vessel**. Inside this vessel, the liquid condensate is separated from the gas stream. The system produces two outputs: the primary product is the processed, dry "sales gas" that now meets the required dew point specification, and the secondary product is the liquid condensate, which is rich in valuable natural gas liquids (NGLs) and is piped out for collection.

The entire operation is managed by an automated **Process Controller**. This controller continuously receives data from the dew point analyzer and temperature sensors. It compares the current dew point of the processed gas to a pre-defined setpoint. If the dew point is too high, the controller automatically signals the chiller to increase its cooling power, ensuring the gas is always treated correctly. This automated control allows the system to adapt to variations in the incoming gas flow, temperature, and composition without needing manual intervention.
