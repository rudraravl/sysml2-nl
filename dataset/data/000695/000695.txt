This is a Military Anti-Shock Trousers (MAST) system, an automated medical device designed for treating traumatic hemorrhagic shock in field conditions. Its primary function is to stabilize a patient suffering from severe blood loss by applying external pressure to the lower body, which helps redirect blood from the limbs and abdomen to the vital organs like the heart, lungs, and brain.

The system is composed of several key components that work together to provide automated therapy. The physical trousers contain three separate **Inflatable Compartments**: one for the left leg, one for the right leg, and one for the abdomen. These compartments are the core of the treatment, applying graduated pressure to the patient.

A central **Therapy Controller** acts as the brain of the system. It receives continuous data from a **Patient Monitoring Unit**, which measures the patient's blood pressure, heart rate, and perfusion status. The controller analyzes these vital signs to automatically detect if the patient is in shock and to determine its severity.

Based on its assessment, the controller commands a **Pneumatic Control Unit**—the system's air pump and valve manager—to begin treatment. The process is sequential: first, the two leg compartments are inflated to a target pressure. The system then re-evaluates the patient's vital signs. If blood pressure has not improved sufficiently, the controller will then direct the inflation of the abdominal compartment.

Throughout the treatment, dedicated **Pressure Sensors** in each compartment provide real-time feedback, allowing the system to precisely maintain the required pressure levels. The system operates through distinct phases, including an initial assessment, deployment of compression, a maintenance phase to sustain hemodynamic support, and a carefully controlled "weaning" phase where the pressure is gradually released as the patient stabilizes to prevent a sudden drop in blood pressure. The entire system is built with safety in mind, featuring maximum pressure limits and an emergency rapid deflation capability to prevent injury and allow for immediate medical intervention if required.
