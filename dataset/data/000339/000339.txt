This model describes the fundamental concept of a universal connection point, or "port," which is a standardized interface designed to connect different parts of any system. Its core purpose is to enable interaction and exchange between components, allowing for the transfer of things like data, power, or physical materials.

These ports can be simple, single-function connectors, or they can be complex, hierarchical structures. A single, large port can contain multiple smaller "sub-ports" within it, each dedicated to a specific function. For example, a single advanced docking connector on a laptop is a main port, but it internally houses distinct sub-ports for video output, USB data, and charging power.

The fundamental behavior of a port is to connect to other compatible ports. When two ports are linked, they form a direct interface. This connection ensures that anything sent out from one port is properly received by the other, establishing a reliable pathway for communication or transfer between the system components they belong to.
