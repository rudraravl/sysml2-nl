This model represents a foundational scientific framework: the **International System of Quantities (ISQ)**. It's not a physical object, but rather a formal, digital definition of the seven base quantities that form the bedrock of all modern science and engineering measurements, as defined in the ISO 80000 standard.

Think of it as a universal dictionary for physical properties. The system's core components are the seven fundamental quantities:

*   **Length:** The measure of distance in space.
*   **Mass:** A measure of an object's inertia and its gravitational attraction.
*   **Duration (Time):** The measure of the interval between two events.
*   **Thermodynamic Temperature:** A measure of the average kinetic energy of particles in a system.
*   **Electric Current:** The rate of flow of electric charge.
*   **Luminous Intensity:** The perceived power emitted by a light source in a particular direction.
*   **Amount of Substance:** A measure of the number of elementary entities (like atoms or molecules) in an object or sample.

For each of these base quantities, the system provides a complete and unambiguous definition, including its name, standard scientific symbols (e.g., `m` for mass), and its relationship to its corresponding base measurement unit (e.g., kilograms for Mass, seconds for Duration).

These seven quantities work together as a complete set of building blocks. By combining them, any other physical quantity—such as velocity (length/time) or force (mass * length / time²)—can be precisely derived. The purpose of this system is to provide a standard, reliable foundation for any other engineering or scientific model, ensuring that physical properties are described consistently and correctly everywhere.
