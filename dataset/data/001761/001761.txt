This model describes a digital clock system designed to accurately track and display the current time. The system is built from four primary electronic components that work together to perform the timekeeping function.

At its core is a **Power Supply** that provides a steady 5-volt electrical current to all other parts of the clock, enabling them to operate. The timekeeping mechanism is driven by an **Oscillator**, which acts as the clock's pacemaker. It generates a stable, precise signal at a frequency of 1 Hz, producing one electronic "tick" every second.

This tick signal is fed into a **Time Counter** module. The counter's job is to receive each tick from the oscillator and, in response, increment its stored time value—advancing the seconds, which in turn advance the minutes and hours. This component effectively translates the oscillator's rhythmic pulses into a human-understandable time format.

Finally, the updated time from the counter is sent to a **Display Unit**. This component takes the time data (hours, minutes, and seconds) and presents it in a human-readable format.

In operation, the entire system functions as a continuous loop: the power supply enables the components, the oscillator generates a constant rhythm, the counter tracks the passage of time based on that rhythm, and the display continuously shows the result. The system is designed for accuracy, with a requirement to not lose or gain more than two seconds over a 24-hour period.
