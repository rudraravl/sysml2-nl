This model describes a complete medical stent system designed to treat vascular stenosis, which is the narrowing or blockage of a blood vessel. The system's primary function is to reopen the affected vessel and restore adequate blood flow.

The system consists of two main physical components: a permanent implant and a disposable delivery device. The implant is a **stent**, a small, tubular mesh scaffold made from a biocompatible material like a Cobalt-Chromium alloy. It is designed to be expanded from a small, collapsed diameter to a larger one that matches the healthy diameter of the target blood vessel.

The delivery device is a **delivery catheter**, a long, thin, flexible tube that carries the stent into the patient's vascular system. Mounted on the tip of this catheter is an inflatable **balloon**, with the collapsed stent crimped securely over it.

The procedure involves guiding the delivery catheter through the patient's blood vessels to the location of the blockage or lesion. Once precisely positioned, the balloon is inflated by applying a specific amount of pressure. This inflation expands the stent, pressing it firmly against the inner wall of the blood vessel. The stent exerts a continuous outward radial force, acting as a scaffold to hold the vessel open. After the stent is fully expanded, the balloon is deflated, and the entire delivery catheter is withdrawn from the body, leaving the stent permanently in place to maintain the vessel's patency.
