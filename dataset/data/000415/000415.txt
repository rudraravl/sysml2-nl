This model describes a Xenobot, an autonomous, microscopic biological organism designed for targeted drug delivery within a biological environment. It is a programmable living machine constructed entirely from a collection of specialized biological cells.

The Xenobot is composed of two primary types of cells that enable its function: contractile cells, which allow it to change shape and pulse, and ciliated cells, which use tiny hair-like structures to propel it through a fluid environment. The system integrates three main functional components: a sensor, a motor, and a payload carrier.

Its core navigation system is based on a chemotaxis sensor, which can detect chemical gradients in its surroundings. This allows the Xenobot to "smell" and locate specific targets, such as disease sites or tumors, which release particular chemical signals. The information from this sensor is directly linked to its locomotion module—the collection of contractile and ciliated cells. When a chemical gradient is detected, the sensor sends a command to these cells, which then work in concert to generate a propulsive force, moving the organism toward the source of the signal.

The Xenobot is also equipped with a payload carrier capable of holding a specific mass of a therapeutic drug. Its mission is to transport this payload to the target location it finds via chemotaxis. Upon arrival, a signal triggers the release of the drug directly at the site, ensuring precise and targeted treatment.

The organism operates through a clear lifecycle of states. It begins by `searching` for a chemical signal. Once a strong enough signal is detected, it transitions to `navigating` toward the target. Upon reaching the destination, it enters a `delivering` state to release its payload. After delivery, it can return to searching. The Xenobot has a finite internal energy supply, and if this runs out, it enters a final, `depleted` state. The entire organism is designed to be biocompatible and biodegradable, meaning it can operate safely within a living system and will break down naturally after its mission is complete.
