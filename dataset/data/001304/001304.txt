This is a Variable Reluctance Sensing Unit designed to measure the rotational speed of a mechanical component, such as a spinning shaft or gear.

The system is composed of two primary parts: a variable reluctance sensor and a signal conditioning unit. The sensor itself works by placing a permanent magnet and a wire pickup coil near a rotating target made of a magnetic material (like iron or steel) that has teeth, similar to a gear.

Here's how it functions: The permanent magnet creates a steady magnetic field that passes through the pickup coil. As the toothed target rotates, each tooth that passes by the sensor alters this magnetic field. This periodic change in the magnetic field induces a small, analog AC voltage in the pickup coil. The frequency of this voltage signal is directly proportional to how fast the teeth are passing by, and therefore, to the rotational speed of the target.

This raw analog signal from the sensor is then fed into the signal conditioner. This electronic module amplifies the weak signal, filters out any electrical noise, and converts it into a clean, crisp digital square-wave signal. This final digital output is robust and can be easily read by a microcontroller or engine control unit to calculate the precise rotational speed.

The entire unit operates in different states. It remains in an 'idle' state when powered on but no motion is detected. When rotation begins, it transitions to an active 'sensing' state. If the rotation stops, it returns to idle. It is also designed to enter a 'fault' state if it detects a signal anomaly, and it can be reset to return to normal operation. The system is engineered to meet specific performance requirements, including measuring speeds up to 6000 RPM and operating within a precise air gap between the sensor and the rotating target.
