This model describes a complete Urban Air Mobility network centered around a fleet of electric Vertical Takeoff and Landing (eVTOL) aircraft. The system's purpose is to provide rapid, on-demand, point-to-point air transportation within a city, functioning as an emission-free air taxi service to bypass ground traffic congestion.

The network consists of three main parts: a fleet of eVTOL aircraft, numerous ground-based Vertiport stations, and a central Network Operations Center.

The eVTOL aircraft is a sophisticated, multi-rotor vehicle designed to carry four passengers. Its key feature is a propulsion system with eight tilt-rotors, each powered by an individual electric motor. This design allows the aircraft to take off and land vertically like a helicopter from compact spaces, and then tilt its rotors forward to fly efficiently like a conventional airplane during the cruise portion of a flight. The aircraft is powered entirely by four large, rechargeable battery packs that provide redundant power for safety.

For autonomous operation, the aircraft is equipped with a triple-redundant flight computer that acts as its brain. This computer processes data from a comprehensive sensor suite, which includes LiDAR for short-range obstacle detection, radar for long-range weather and traffic awareness, and high-resolution cameras for visual navigation.

A typical mission involves a passenger booking a flight between two Vertiports. The aircraft performs a pre-flight check, calculates the optimal flight path, and receives clearance. It then executes a vertical takeoff, transitions to high-speed forward flight, cruises along its designated route, and finally transitions back to vertical flight for a precise landing at the destination Vertiport. The entire flight is a sequence of distinct phases, including preflight, takeoff, transition, cruise, approach, and landing, all managed by its autonomous systems in communication with the operations center.
