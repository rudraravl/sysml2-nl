This model describes a Noria, a traditional mechanical system designed to lift water from a low-lying source to a higher elevation, typically for irrigation.

The core of the system is a large, vertically-mounted wheel, eight meters in diameter, which is held in place by a nine-meter-tall reinforced timber support structure. Attached to the rim of this wheel is a series of 24 buckets, each capable of holding 15 liters of water.

The system's operation is a continuous cycle powered by a drive system that applies torque to rotate the wheel. As the wheel turns, the buckets at the bottom dip into a water source, such as a river or well, and scoop up water. The rotation then lifts these filled buckets upward. When each bucket reaches the top of the wheel's arc, its orientation causes it to tilt and pour its contents into a discharge channel positioned at a higher elevation. The now-empty bucket continues its rotation back down to the water source to repeat the process.

This entire assembly works together to create a continuous flow of water from the source to the elevated channel. The system is designed for steady, ongoing operation as long as the drive system is active and the water source has sufficient water. The structural components are engineered to safely support the combined weight of the wheel, the buckets, and the water being lifted.
