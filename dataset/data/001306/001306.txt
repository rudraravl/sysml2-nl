This model describes a high-precision velocity receiver system, designed to be used in applications like vehicle navigation, guidance, or motion tracking. Its primary function is to receive a raw signal from an external source, process it to determine an object's velocity, and provide this accurate velocity data to a larger system, such as a navigation computer.

The system is comprised of several specialized components that work in sequence. It begins with a **Signal Acquisition Unit**, which is responsible for searching for and locking onto the incoming velocity signal. This unit operates through distinct states, moving from searching for a signal, to attempting to lock onto it, and finally to actively tracking it. If the signal is lost, it returns to the searching state.

Once a raw signal is acquired, it is passed to a **Signal Processor**. This digital processing unit first validates the signal's integrity. If the signal is valid, it applies filters to remove noise and interference and then performs calculations to compensate for known errors. This "cleans up" the signal to ensure high quality.

The cleaned signal is then fed into a **Velocity Estimator**. This component uses the refined signal data to calculate the final, precise three-dimensional velocity vector. In addition to the velocity, it also generates a confidence score, indicating the reliability of the measurement, and provides the overall status of the receiver (e.g., `idle`, `acquiring`, `tracking`).

The entire operation is managed by a **Receiver Controller**. This central unit oversees the system's operating mode, handles external commands, and manages internal processes like calibration to maintain accuracy. The processed, high-fidelity velocity data is then sent out to a consuming system, like a navigation computer, which uses this information for its own calculations. The receiver is designed for continuous, real-time operation, required to provide frequent and highly accurate velocity updates.
