This is a Bair Hugger forced-air warming system, a medical device designed to prevent and treat hypothermia in patients, typically during surgical procedures. The system works by delivering a continuous flow of temperature-controlled warm air to a specialized, disposable blanket that is placed over the patient.

The core of the system is a warming unit that houses several key components. It draws in ambient air and passes it through a high-efficiency filter to ensure it is clean. A blower then propels this filtered air across a heating element, warming it to a precise temperature. This heated air is transported through a flexible delivery hose to the warming blanket, which is designed with a specific perforation pattern to distribute the warm air evenly over the patient's body.

The system's operation is managed by a central control module. This controller receives input from a clinician via a user interface, where a target temperature can be set. It continuously receives feedback from two temperature sensors: one measuring the temperature of the air leaving the heater and another monitoring the patient's actual body temperature. Based on this data, the controller creates a feedback loop, automatically adjusting the power of the heating element and the speed of the blower to maintain the patient at the desired temperature.

For patient safety, a dedicated safety monitor operates independently. It constantly checks for dangerous conditions, such as the air becoming too hot. If a safety threshold is crossed, it can trigger an immediate system shutdown to prevent patient injury. The entire device is powered by an internal power supply that converts standard wall outlet power for use by the various components.

The system operates through several states, including `power off`, `standby` for initialization and self-testing, `ready` for operation, and the active `warming` state. While warming, it can also enter an alarm state to alert medical staff to any issues, ensuring safe and effective patient temperature management.
