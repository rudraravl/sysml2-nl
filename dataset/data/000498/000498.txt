This is a model of a digital scoliometer, a portable medical device used by clinicians to screen for scoliosis. Its primary function is to measure spinal asymmetry by determining a patient's Angle of Trunk Rotation (ATR) during a standard forward bend test.

The device is composed of several integrated components. The core of the system is an inclinometer sensor, which is placed on the patient's back to physically measure the angle of trunk rotation. This sensor sends a raw angle measurement to an internal signal processor. The processor is the brain of the device; it takes the raw data, applies calibration corrections, and filters out noise to calculate a precise and accurate ATR value.

Beyond just providing a number, the processor automatically assesses the severity of the measurement. Based on pre-defined clinical thresholds, it classifies the result as normal, requiring monitoring, or requiring a referral to a specialist. Both the final ATR angle and this severity classification are then shown on a clear digital display for the clinician to read immediately.

To support patient tracking over time, the scoliometer includes a data logger. This component stores a history of measurements, with each record including the angle, a timestamp, the specific vertebral level where the measurement was taken, and the patient's position. The entire handheld unit is powered by an internal battery, allowing for portable use in a clinical setting.

Operationally, the device follows a simple workflow. After being powered on and initializing, it enters a ready state. The clinician then begins the screening, which moves the device through states for actively measuring, processing the data, and finally displaying the result. Once the result is acknowledged, the device returns to the ready state, prepared for the next measurement.
