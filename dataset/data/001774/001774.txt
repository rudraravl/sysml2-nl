This model represents a system for simulating biological evolution within a population of organisms. It demonstrates how a population adapts and changes over many generations in response to environmental pressures through the core mechanisms of natural selection.

The system is centered around a population of unique individuals. Each individual has a distinct genetic makeup (its genotype) which determines its observable physical and behavioral traits (its phenotype), such as body size, metabolic rate, reproductive rate, and overall survival probability.

These individuals exist within a dynamic habitat, which is defined by a set of environmental factors including temperature, resource density, and the number of predators. This environment has a limited carrying capacity, meaning it can only support a certain number of individuals.

The simulation unfolds over a series of generations, with each generation representing a complete life cycle. The process works as follows:

1.  **Fitness Evaluation:** Each individual's traits are assessed against the current environmental conditions to calculate a "fitness score," which represents its likelihood of surviving and reproducing.
2.  **Natural Selection:** A selection mechanism then acts on the population, favoring individuals with higher fitness. These individuals are more likely to survive, while less-fit individuals are eliminated.
3.  **Reproduction:** The surviving individuals reproduce, passing their genetic material to a new generation of offspring. This process introduces new genetic diversity through mechanisms like random mutation and the shuffling of genes (recombination).
4.  **Environmental Change:** The environment itself can change between generations, creating new challenges and selection pressures for the population.

This entire cycle repeats for hundreds or thousands of generations. Over time, traits that are advantageous in the given environment become more common, causing the population as a whole to become better adapted. The system can model the entire lifecycle of a population, from its founding and growth through periods of adaptation and stability, or even its potential extinction if it fails to adapt to severe environmental changes.
