This is an Electronic Tongue system, a sophisticated analytical instrument designed to mimic the human sense of taste by evaluating liquid samples. Its primary function is to identify and quantify the five basic taste categories—sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami—to generate a comprehensive taste profile.

The system is composed of several key integrated components. A **Fluid Delivery System** first draws in a liquid sample, pumps it into a measurement chamber, and then disposes of it as waste after analysis. This component also includes a cleaning unit to automatically rinse the chamber, ensuring there is no cross-contamination between samples.

At the heart of the system is a **SensorArray**, which acts as the artificial tongue. This array consists of five distinct chemical sensors, each specifically engineered and calibrated to detect one of the five basic tastes. When the liquid sample flows over this array, each sensor produces a unique electrical response—in terms of voltage and resistance—based on the chemical composition of the liquid.

These raw electrical signals are then sent to a **Signal Processor**. This unit amplifies the weak signals from the sensors, filters out any electrical noise, and normalizes the data to prepare it for analysis. The clean, processed data is then passed to a **Pattern Analyzer**, which is the brain of the system. Using a pre-trained neural network, this analyzer interprets the complex patterns from the combined sensor data to classify the tastes and calculate their respective intensities, confidence levels, and an overall quality score.

The entire process is managed by a central **Control Unit**, which sequences the analysis through distinct operational states such as idle, sampling, measuring, processing, and complete. A **Display Interface** provides a user-friendly way to interact with the system, allowing an operator to start an analysis and view the final taste profile and system status on a high-resolution screen.
