This model describes a self-contained Occupancy Sensing Unit, an electronic device designed to monitor and report the number of people within a specific area, such as a conference room or an office space. Its primary function is to provide accurate, real-time data about how a space is being used.

The unit is built from three main components that work in sequence. First, an **Occupancy Sensor** serves as the primary detector. This sensor is designed to monitor a specific room with a known area and capacity, and it has a defined detection range (e.g., 8 meters) and a fast response time (1.5 seconds). It continuously scans the area at a set frequency (2 times per second). For each measurement, it generates data that includes a timestamp, the number of occupants counted, the occupancy level (as a percentage of capacity), and a confidence score for that reading.

The raw data from this sensor is immediately passed to an internal **Signal Processor**. This component acts as the brains of the unit, filtering the sensor's raw readings to improve accuracy, remove noise, and reduce the chance of false positives.

Once processed, the refined measurement data is sent to an onboard **Data Storage** unit. This component logs the history of occupancy measurements, capable of holding thousands of records which are saved at regular intervals (e.g., every second).

The entire system operates in a continuous cycle. After being initialized, the unit enters an "active" state to perform a measurement, moves to a "processing" state to analyze the data, and then enters a "reporting" state before returning to an "idle" mode to conserve power. It is designed to meet strict performance requirements, demanding at least 95% accuracy and a response time of under two seconds, all while operating on a standard low-voltage power supply.
