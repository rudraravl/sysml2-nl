This model describes an Automated External Defibrillator (AED), a portable medical device designed to treat sudden cardiac arrest by analyzing the heart's rhythm and delivering a controlled electrical shock.

The system is comprised of several integrated components that work together to provide automated, life-saving therapy. The core of the device is a central **Control Processor** that manages the overall state of the system, transitioning from monitoring and analysis to charging and shock delivery.

Interaction with the patient begins with a pair of disposable **Electrode Pads** placed on the chest. These pads serve a dual purpose: they sense the heart's faint electrical signals (the ECG) and, when commanded, deliver the high-energy therapeutic shock.

The signals from the pads are fed into an **ECG Analysis Module**. This component is the diagnostic brain of the AED, using a sophisticated algorithm to analyze the heart's rhythm in real-time. It can distinguish between a shockable rhythm (like ventricular fibrillation) and a non-shockable one, providing a "shock advised" or "no shock advised" recommendation.

Power for the entire system is provided by a self-contained **Battery Pack**. This battery powers an **Energy Storage Unit**, which contains a high-voltage capacitor. When a shockable rhythm is detected, the controller instructs this unit to charge the capacitor to a specific energy level, a process that happens very quickly.

Once charged, the energy is delivered via a specialized **Shock Delivery Circuit**. This circuit shapes the raw electrical energy into a precise, biphasic waveform—a pulse that travels in two directions—which is more effective and safer for the heart muscle. The circuit also compensates for the patient's body resistance (impedance) to ensure the correct amount of energy is delivered.

A critical **Safety Interlock System** acts as a safeguard, preventing accidental shocks. It continuously verifies that the electrode pads have good contact, that the patient's impedance is within a safe range, that the capacitor is fully charged, and most importantly, that the human operator has pressed the shock button to confirm they are ready. A shock cannot be delivered unless all these conditions are met.

Finally, an **Operator Interface**, consisting of a screen and a speaker, guides the user through the entire process. It provides clear, calm voice prompts and visual instructions, such as "Apply pads to patient's bare chest," "Analyzing heart rhythm, do not touch the patient," and "Shock advised, press the flashing shock button."

In operation, the AED creates a continuous cycle: it analyzes the heart rhythm, advises the operator, delivers a shock if needed, and then immediately re-analyzes the rhythm to determine the next step, providing a seamless protocol for emergency resuscitation.
