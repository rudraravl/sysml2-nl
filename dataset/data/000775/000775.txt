This model describes a therapeutic standing frame system, a piece of medical equipment designed to safely assist patients with limited mobility in moving from a seated to a standing position. Its primary purpose is to provide weight-bearing support for therapeutic benefits, such as improving circulation, bone density, and muscle tone.

The system is built on a robust structural frame with a wide, stable base and vertical supports, engineered to safely handle patients weighing up to 150 kg.

The core of the system is an electrically powered tilt mechanism. This component uses an actuator to smoothly and slowly lift the entire patient support platform from a horizontal (0 degrees) to a near-vertical standing position (up to 85 degrees). To ensure patient safety and comfort, the tilt speed is controlled, and a mechanical locking system secures the frame at the desired angle.

To ensure proper and secure positioning, the patient is supported by several components:
*   A main **Support Platform** that includes a footplate for the feet, padded supports for the knees, and a chest pad for upper body stability.
*   A comprehensive **Restraint System** featuring adjustable straps for the pelvis, chest, and feet. These straps are secured before any movement begins to hold the patient safely in place.

The entire operation is managed by a central **Control System**, which includes a simple user interface for a caregiver to operate the device. A critical safety feature is an integrated safety controller with an emergency stop function that can halt all motion immediately. The system is powered by a rechargeable battery, allowing for mobility and use away from a direct power outlet.

A typical use involves a clear sequence of operations: The patient is first positioned and secured with all restraints while the frame is in the seated position. The caregiver then uses the controls to begin the slow upward tilt. Once the target standing angle is reached, the system locks in place for the duration of the therapy. Finally, the frame is smoothly returned to the seated position, and the patient can be safely released.
