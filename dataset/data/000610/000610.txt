This model represents an automated **Flow Injection Analysis (FIA) system**, a laboratory instrument designed for rapid and efficient chemical analysis of liquid samples. Its primary function is to determine the concentration of a specific substance (the analyte) within a sample by reacting it with a chemical reagent and measuring the resulting product.

The system operates by precisely manipulating small volumes of liquids through a network of tubing. The core of the system consists of several interconnected components working in sequence. Two **peristaltic pumps** continuously propel a carrier solution and a reagent solution at constant, controlled flow rates.

The analysis cycle begins at the **injection valve**. This valve has a small, fixed-volume sample loop (e.g., 200 microliters) which is first filled with the liquid sample to be analyzed. On command, the valve switches, precisely injecting this small "plug" of sample into the continuously moving carrier stream without stopping the flow.

This carrier stream, now containing the sample plug, travels to a **mixing tee**, where it merges with the stream from the reagent pump. The sample and reagent are combined and then flow into a **reaction coil**. This is a length of coiled tubing that provides sufficient time—known as residence time—for the chemical reaction between the sample's analyte and the reagent to complete. This reaction typically produces a colored compound.

From the coil, the stream containing the now-colored reaction product passes through a **flow-through optical detector**. This device shines a beam of light (at a specific wavelength, like 540 nm) through the liquid as it flows. It measures the amount of light absorbed by the colored product, generating a signal proportional to the analyte's concentration.

This electrical signal is sent to a **data acquisition system**. This unit processes the raw absorbance signal, often identifying the peak height or area, and uses a pre-established calibration curve to calculate the final concentration of the substance in the original sample.

Finally, after passing through the detector, the entire liquid stream is collected in a **waste reservoir**. The system is designed for high throughput, capable of completing a full analysis in under a minute while consuming minimal amounts of sample and reagent, making it highly efficient for laboratories that need to process many samples.
