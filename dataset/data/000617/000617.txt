This model describes the Global Harmonization Task Force (GHTF), an international system designed to align medical device regulatory practices across different countries and regions. The primary goal is to create consistent, harmonized standards that ensure patient safety while making it easier for medical devices to be approved and sold globally.

The system is a collaboration between several key entities:

*   **Regulatory Authorities:** These are the national or regional health agencies (like the FDA or EMA) from various parts of the world, including North America, Europe, and Asia. They are the core members who provide input and vote on final guidance.
*   **Study Groups:** These are specialized technical committees, each focusing on a specific area of medical device regulation. They are responsible for the detailed work of drafting and revising the guidance documents.
*   **Secretariat:** This is the central administrative body, based in Brussels, that manages the overall process. It handles document distribution, tracks the consensus-building progress, and formally publishes the final guidance.
*   **Stakeholders:** These are other interested parties, such as industry associations or patient advocacy groups, who can provide feedback and input into the process.

The main output of this system is a **Regulatory Guidance Document**. Each document goes through a structured lifecycle, beginning as a draft, moving through review and revision, and ultimately being published once a high level of agreement is reached. The system operates under a key rule that a guidance document cannot be published until it achieves at least a 75% consensus among the participating regulatory authorities.

The process for creating a new harmonized standard follows a clear workflow. It begins by collecting initial proposals and feedback from the regional authorities and stakeholders. A dedicated study group then uses this information, along with existing standards, to develop a draft document. This draft is circulated for review, and the system evaluates the level of consensus. If agreement is insufficient, the document is revised based on the feedback and re-circulated. Once the required consensus is achieved, the Secretariat publishes the final document, which is then distributed to all member authorities for adoption into their local regulations.
