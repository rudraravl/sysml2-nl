This model describes a soft robotic gripper system designed for carefully handling delicate or irregularly shaped objects. The system is built around a flexible, three-fingered gripper that uses compressed air to actuate its movements.

The core of the system is a set of three soft pneumatic actuators, which function as the gripper's fingers. These fingers are made from a compliant, flexible material that inflates with air, causing them to bend and curl to conform to the shape of an object. This soft-touch approach allows the gripper to securely hold items without crushing or damaging them.

The entire system is powered by an air compressor that generates the necessary compressed air. A pressure regulator takes this high-pressure air and precisely controls the flow and pressure delivered to each of the three fingers independently. This fine-tuned control is managed by a central control unit, which acts as the brain of the system.

To perform a grip, the control unit receives a command specifying not only the desired force but also the desired "compliance" or stiffness of the grip. It then orchestrates the inflation of the fingers. As the fingers make contact with an object, integrated force sensors provide real-time feedback on how hard they are squeezing. The control unit uses this feedback to continuously adjust the air pressure, ensuring the grip force is accurate and stable. When commanded to release, the system simply deflates the fingers, causing them to open.

The system operates through distinct states: it can be idle, actively performing a grasping maneuver, or in the process of releasing an object. This design creates an adaptive and precise robotic tool ideal for applications where rigid, traditional grippers are not suitable, such as handling food, assembling fragile components, or interacting safely alongside humans.
