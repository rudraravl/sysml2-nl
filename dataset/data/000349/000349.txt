This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of fundamental quantities, units, and concepts used in the scientific and engineering field of acoustics. It is not a physical object, but rather a formal definition of the language and mathematics needed to describe and analyze sound, based on the international standard ISO 80000-8.

The system provides a complete framework for understanding acoustic phenomena. It begins with the most basic concepts, such as **sound pressure**, which is the small, rapid change in pressure caused by a sound wave, and the resulting motion of particles in the air or another medium, described by their **displacement**, **velocity**, and **acceleration**.

Building on these basics, the library defines concepts related to the energy of sound. This includes **sound energy density** (the amount of sound energy in a given volume), **sound power** (the total energy radiated by a sound source over time, like from a speaker), and **sound intensity** (the sound power passing through a specific area). It also defines **sound exposure**, which measures the total acoustic energy a point receives over a period of time.

Furthermore, the system defines properties of the medium through which sound travels, such as its **characteristic impedance**, which describes how much the medium resists the propagation of sound waves. It also defines concepts for specific situations, like the **reverberation time**, which describes how long it takes for a sound to fade away in an enclosed space like a room or concert hall.

Finally, it formalizes the common logarithmic scales used in acoustics, most notably the decibel (dB). It provides precise definitions for widely used measurements like **Sound Pressure Level (SPL)**, **Sound Power Level**, and **Sound Exposure Level**, all of which compare a measured value to a standard reference value.

In essence, this system is a foundational dictionary for acoustics, providing the standard building blocks needed to model, measure, and analyze any system involving sound.
