This model describes a formal framework for system verification, designed to manage the process of confirming that a product meets its specified requirements. It's not a physical object, but rather a structured methodology for organizing and tracking engineering validation and testing activities.

At its core, the system is built around the concept of a "Verification Case," which represents a specific verification activity, such as a test, inspection, or analysis. Each Verification Case clearly identifies the "subject"—the part or system being checked—and its "objective," which is to confirm compliance with one or more requirements. The outcome of any verification activity is a standardized "verdict," which can be "pass," "fail," "inconclusive" (if the result is ambiguous), or "error" (if the test itself failed to run correctly).

The framework is hierarchical, allowing a large, complex verification effort—like qualifying a new aircraft wing—to be broken down into a series of smaller, more manageable sub-cases. For example, the overall wing verification could include sub-cases for structural load testing, aerodynamic analysis, and material inspection.

To provide clarity and consistency, each verification activity is categorized by its method. The standard methods include "inspection" (visual checks), "analysis" (simulations or calculations), "demonstration" (showing a capability), and "test" (operating the system and measuring its performance). Essentially, this system provides the structure and vocabulary needed to plan, execute, and document the complete verification and validation lifecycle for any complex engineering product.
