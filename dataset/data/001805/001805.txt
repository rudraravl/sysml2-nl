This model describes a mechanical four-bar linkage system, designed to convert continuous rotary motion into a controlled, reciprocating linear motion. It is a common mechanism found in various machines, such as engine pistons, pump jacks, or manufacturing automation equipment.

The system consists of four main rigid bodies, or "links," connected in a loop. A stationary ground frame provides the fixed base for the mechanism, with two pivot points. A rotary actuator, such as an electric motor, provides the input power by spinning a link known as the crank. The crank is connected to a coupler link, which translates the crank's rotation into a more complex motion. The other end of the coupler is attached to a rocker link, which is also pivoted to the ground frame and is forced to swing back and forth in an arc.

In operation, the actuator continuously rotates the crank. This motion, transmitted through the coupler, causes the rocker to oscillate. An output mechanism is attached to the rocker, converting its arcing swing into a purely linear, back-and-forth movement.

The system is designed with specific physical dimensions for its links to produce a precise output stroke, required to be between 5 cm and 40 cm. It must operate smoothly without jamming and is engineered to be efficient, converting at least 82% of the input motor torque into a usable linear force at the output.
