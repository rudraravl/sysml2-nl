This model describes a high-performance thermal management system that uses liquid metal to cool a powerful heat source. The system is designed for applications where conventional air or water cooling is insufficient, such as in high-performance computing, advanced electronics, or compact reactors.

The core of the system is a closed-loop circuit filled with a Sodium-Potassium alloy (NaK), a liquid metal chosen for its exceptional ability to transfer heat. This coolant is continuously circulated to absorb heat from a component, transport it away, and then dissipate it into the surrounding environment.

The system is composed of several key components that work in concert:

*   **Heat Source:** This is the component being cooled, which generates a significant amount of thermal energy during its operation.
*   **Cold Plate:** This acts as the thermal interface. It is mounted directly to the heat source, and as the liquid metal flows through internal channels within the plate, it efficiently absorbs the heat.
*   **Electromagnetic Pump:** A specialized, no-moving-parts pump that uses electromagnetic forces to circulate the highly conductive liquid metal. This design enhances reliability and is well-suited for handling corrosive coolants.
*   **Radiator:** After absorbing heat at the cold plate, the hot liquid metal is pumped to the radiator. Here, the heat is transferred from the liquid metal to the ambient environment, often through fins that increase the surface area for cooling. The now-cooled liquid metal then returns to the pump.
*   **Expansion Tank:** This is a reservoir that safely accommodates the expansion and contraction of the liquid metal as its temperature changes, preventing dangerous pressure buildup in the sealed loop.
*   **System Controller & Sensor:** A temperature sensor constantly monitors the coolant after it leaves the cold plate. This data is fed to the system controller, which acts as the brain. The controller compares the actual temperature to a target setpoint and automatically adjusts the speed of the electromagnetic pump to maintain the desired temperature, creating a responsive feedback control loop.

The system operates through several distinct states, including a standby mode when idle, a startup sequence to begin circulation, and a normal operation mode where it actively regulates temperature. It also features a high-load mode where the pump runs at full capacity to handle thermal spikes, and an emergency state to protect the system if temperatures exceed safe limits, before finally entering a controlled shutdown.
