This is a medical device for iontophoretic drug delivery, a system designed to deliver medication through a patient's skin using a controlled, low-level electric current. This non-invasive technique allows for localized or systemic therapy without the need for injections.

The core of the system is a portable device that consists of a main controller, a power supply, and two electrodes: an active electrode and a return electrode. The active electrode contains a drug reservoir pre-filled with a specific ionized therapeutic drug. To begin treatment, both electrodes are placed on the patient's skin.

The system's operation is managed by a central treatment controller. It starts in an idle state, moves to a preparation phase, and then begins active delivery. During active delivery, the power supply provides electricity, which is precisely managed by a current regulator to maintain a constant, safe therapeutic current (e.g., 2-4 milliamps). This current flows from the active electrode, through the patient's skin to the return electrode, completing the circuit. The electric field created by this current drives the charged drug molecules from the reservoir through the outer layers of the skin and into the underlying tissue.

To ensure patient safety, the device incorporates a comprehensive safety monitor and an impedance sensor. The sensor continuously measures the electrical impedance of the skin, which helps ensure good electrode contact and can indicate potential skin irritation. The safety monitor constantly checks that the current level and total treatment time do not exceed established safety limits. If an unsafe condition is detected, such as a sudden drop in impedance or excessive current, the system will enter a fault state and immediately terminate the treatment.

The treatment controller monitors the progress of the drug delivery against a target dosage, and once the prescribed amount has been administered, the device automatically stops the current and signals that the treatment is complete.
