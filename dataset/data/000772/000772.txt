This model represents a Spica splint system, a medical device designed to immobilize a patient's thumb and wrist. Its primary purpose is to hold the limb in a stable, therapeutic position to facilitate healing from conditions like fractures, ligament injuries, or post-operative recovery.

The system is constructed from several integrated components. The main structure is a rigid, lightweight outer shell made from a thermoplastic composite, which provides the necessary support to prevent unwanted movement. Inside this shell is a soft padding layer of closed-cell foam, designed to ensure patient comfort, distribute pressure evenly to avoid sores, and wick away moisture from the skin.

The splint features two specialized support structures: a thumb component that positions the thumb at a specific angle away from the palm, and a wrist component that holds the wrist in a slightly extended position. These precise angles are critical for promoting proper anatomical healing. The entire device is secured to the patient's limb with a system of four adjustable fastening straps, which allow for a secure fit and can be tightened or loosened to accommodate changes in swelling throughout the healing process.

The use of the splint follows a clinical lifecycle. It begins with an initial fitting and evaluation on the patient. Once applied, it actively immobilizes the thumb and wrist. The patient's condition is then monitored for comfort and proper circulation, with the splint's compression being adjusted as needed. This process continues until the injury has healed, at which point the splint is removed.
