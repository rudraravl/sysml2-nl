This model describes a comprehensive societal impact analysis system for the deployment of self-driving cars. It's not the self-driving car itself, but rather a high-level monitoring and reporting platform designed for city planners, transportation authorities, and policymakers to evaluate the real-world effects of a large-scale autonomous vehicle fleet.

The system's core function is to continuously collect data from multiple sources and analyze it to measure the success of the autonomous vehicle program against key societal goals. It is built around several interconnected components that work together to create a holistic picture of the technology's impact.

Key components of this system include:

*   An **Autonomous Vehicle Fleet** and the **Transportation Infrastructure** it operates on. These represent the real-world elements generating data, such as the number of cars on the road, their operational hours, traffic flow, accidents, and energy consumption. The infrastructure includes smart technology that allows vehicles to communicate with it.
*   A series of specialized monitoring and analysis subsystems. A **Traffic Management System** works to optimize traffic flow, while dedicated **Safety, Environmental, and Economic Monitoring Systems** process raw data in their respective areas. For example, the Safety System tracks accident rates, and the Economic System analyzes job creation or displacement.
*   A central **Impact Analysis Engine**. This is the brain of the system, which gathers the processed metrics from all the other subsystems. It analyzes the combined data to assess the overarching impact on safety, traffic efficiency, the environment, the economy, and accessibility for populations like the elderly and disabled.

The system operates through different assessment stages, recognizing that the impact of self-driving cars will change as their adoption rate grows from an initial deployment to full market saturation. The final output is a detailed **Impact Report** that quantifies performance in each category—for instance, showing the percentage reduction in fatalities, improvement in commute times, or decrease in emissions—providing a clear, data-driven basis for making strategic decisions about the future of transportation.
