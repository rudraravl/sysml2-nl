This model describes an automated HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning) system for a building, designed to maintain a comfortable indoor climate. The system's core function is to automatically regulate temperature and air circulation by controlling various pieces of equipment.

The brain of the system is a central **Thermostat**, which continuously receives data from indoor and outdoor **Temperature Sensors** as well as an indoor **Humidity Sensor**. It compares this real-time climate information against a desired temperature setpoint, which includes a target temperature and a tolerance range (deadband).

Based on this comparison, the thermostat sends control signals to the appropriate climate-control hardware:
*   A **Furnace** provides heating when the temperature drops below the setpoint.
*   An **Air Conditioner** provides cooling when the temperature rises above the setpoint.
*   A **Blower** (or fan) works with the furnace or air conditioner to circulate the conditioned air throughout the building via a network of **Ductwork**.

The system operates in several distinct modes, such as **Idle**, **Heating**, **Cooling**, and **Ventilating**. It transitions between these states automatically. For example, if the system is idle and detects the temperature has fallen below the target, it will switch to the heating state, activating the furnace and blower until the desired temperature is reached.

Key design requirements for the system include maintaining the indoor temperature within a tight range of the setpoint, operating with energy efficiency by limiting total power consumption, and ensuring safety by preventing the heating and cooling units from running at the same time.
