This model represents a complete implantable cardiac pacemaker system, designed to monitor a patient's heart and deliver electrical therapy to treat abnormally slow or irregular heart rhythms. The system consists of the main pacemaker device, which is implanted in the patient, and the leads that connect it to the heart muscle.

The core of the system is the **Pacemaker**, an electronic device that houses all the control and power components. It is powered by a long-life **Battery**, which is designed to last for many years. The pacemaker is connected to the patient's heart via flexible, insulated wires called **Leads**; the model shows distinct leads for the atrium and the ventricle, allowing for sophisticated, dual-chamber therapy.

These leads serve a dual purpose: they sense the heart's natural electrical signals and deliver therapeutic pacing pulses.

The pacemaker's operation is a continuous cycle of sensing and responding:

1.  A **Sensing Amplifier** continuously "listens" to the heart's intrinsic electrical activity through the leads. It detects the timing and strength of each natural heartbeat.

2.  This information is passed to the **Control Logic**, which is the "brain" of the device. This unit compares the heart's actual rhythm against programmed settings, such as the desired minimum and maximum heart rates and the specific therapy mode (e.g., DDD, which allows for sensing and pacing in both heart chambers).

3.  A dedicated **Timing Circuit** works with the control logic to manage the precise intervals required for a healthy heart rhythm, such as the delay between the contraction of the upper and lower chambers (atrioventricular delay).

4.  If the Control Logic determines that the heart's natural rate is too slow, it commands the **Pulse Generator** to create a small, precisely-timed electrical pulse.

5.  This therapeutic pulse is then delivered through the appropriate lead to stimulate the heart muscle, causing it to contract and produce a heartbeat. If the heart is beating adequately on its own, the pacemaker remains in a monitoring state and does not deliver a pulse.

The entire system also includes a **Telemetry** function, which allows doctors to communicate with the device wirelessly to program its settings and retrieve diagnostic data about the patient's heart rhythm and the device's performance.
