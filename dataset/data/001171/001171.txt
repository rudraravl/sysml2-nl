This model describes a complete digital pH measurement system, a scientific instrument used to determine the acidity or alkalinity of a liquid solution. The core of the system is a combination glass electrode probe that generates an electrical voltage proportional to the pH of the solution it is immersed in.

The sensing component consists of three main parts working in concert. A specialized, ion-selective **glass membrane** is the primary sensor; it develops an electrical potential on its surface that changes based on the hydrogen ion concentration in the test sample. An **internal reference element**, containing a stable buffer solution (typically pH 7), provides a constant, known potential on the inside of the glass membrane. Finally, an **external reference electrode** provides a second stable potential in the solution, serving as a baseline for the measurement. The voltage difference between the glass membrane and this external reference is the raw signal that corresponds to the solution's pH.

Because this raw voltage signal is very weak and comes from a high-resistance source, it is first fed into a **high-impedance amplifier**. This special amplifier measures the voltage without drawing significant current, which would otherwise disrupt the sensitive chemical balance at the electrode and produce an inaccurate reading.

The electrode's voltage response is also dependent on temperature. To correct for this, a **temperature compensator**, which includes a temperature sensor, adjusts the amplified voltage signal based on the actual temperature of the solution being measured. This ensures the pH reading remains accurate across different operating temperatures.

The corrected analog voltage is then converted into a digital value by an **analog-to-digital converter (ADC)**. This digital signal is sent to a central **signal processor**, which acts as the brain of the system. The processor uses stored calibration data (a slope and intercept) to mathematically convert the final voltage reading into a precise pH value, which is then presented as the final output.

The system's operation follows a defined lifecycle. When not in use, the electrode is kept in a **storage** state, typically in a special solution to keep the glass membrane hydrated. Before use, it enters a **preparation** phase where it is rinsed. It must then be **calibrated** using standard buffer solutions of known pH (e.g., pH 4.01 and 7.00) to establish its unique response characteristics. Once calibrated, the system is in a **ready** state. During a **measurement**, it waits for the electrical signal to stabilize before taking a final reading. The system can also enter states for **maintenance**, such as cleaning, or a **fault** state if a malfunction is detected.
