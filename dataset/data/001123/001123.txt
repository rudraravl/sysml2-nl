This is a high-precision liquid capacitive inclinometer, an electronic device designed to measure the tilt or inclination angle with high accuracy and stability. Its primary application is in fields requiring precise leveling and angle measurement, such as in construction, industrial machinery, and geotechnical monitoring.

The core of the sensor is a sealed chamber, made from anodized aluminum, which is partially filled with a special dielectric fluid. Inside this chamber are two parallel, gold-plated copper plates that act as a differential capacitor. The fundamental principle is that as the device tilts, gravity causes the fluid to redistribute, covering a different area of each plate. This change in the fluid level alters the capacitance between the plates, and this change is directly proportional to the angle of inclination.

The system's electronics work together to translate this capacitance change into a precise angle reading. An excitation circuit first applies a stable, high-frequency AC voltage to the capacitive plates. A sensitive capacitance bridge then measures the very small difference in capacitance between the upper and lower plates, converting this difference into a weak analog voltage signal. This signal is then passed to a signal conditioner, which amplifies it and filters out any electrical noise.

Next, a high-resolution (16-bit) analog-to-digital converter samples this cleaned-up signal, turning it into a digital value. To ensure accuracy across its operating temperature range of -20°C to +85°C, an integrated temperature sensor continuously monitors the device's temperature. A central processing unit takes both the digital signal and the temperature reading, applies complex calibration and temperature compensation algorithms, and calculates the final, highly accurate tilt angle.

Finally, a communication interface transmits this angle data to an external computer or control system using a standard RS485 industrial protocol. The entire system is designed for low power consumption and has several operational states, including initialization with self-tests, a main loop for continuous measurement, a dedicated calibration mode, and a fault state for error handling.
