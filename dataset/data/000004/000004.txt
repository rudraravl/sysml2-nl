This model represents a conventional ground vehicle, such as a car or truck, and a virtual analysis framework designed to evaluate its fuel economy.

The physical vehicle is defined with key components including four wheels of a uniform diameter and a fuel tank that holds a specific volume of fuel. The vehicle's performance characteristics are determined by properties like its overall mass, the additional mass of any cargo it carries, and the efficiency of its drivetrain.

The primary purpose of this system is to simulate the vehicle's performance to verify that it meets specific fuel efficiency targets. The analysis is conducted against two distinct requirements: achieving at least 25 miles per gallon (mpg) in city driving conditions and at least 30 mpg on the highway. Both of these evaluations assume the vehicle is carrying a cargo load of 1000 pounds.

To perform the evaluation, the vehicle is subjected to two pre-defined driving scenarios—one simulating typical city driving and another for highway driving. These scenarios dictate the precise velocity the vehicle must achieve over a timeline. A physics-based dynamics simulation calculates the moment-by-moment engine power needed for the vehicle to follow these speed profiles, factoring in its total mass. From the required power, the system then calculates the total fuel consumed during the scenario. This allows for a final fuel economy figure to be determined for both city and highway conditions, which is then checked against the requirements to verify the design.
