This model describes an autonomous developmental robotics system, designed to learn physical skills through self-exploration and interaction with its environment, much like a human infant learns to control its body. The system is not pre-programmed with specific tasks but is built to discover and master skills on its own.

The physical robot consists of a multi-jointed robotic manipulator (an arm) equipped with a comprehensive sensor suite. This includes a vision system for sight, proprioceptive sensors within its joints to know the position and speed of its own limbs, and a tactile sensor array that acts like a sensitive skin to feel contact, pressure, and texture.

The robot's "brain" or control system is composed of several interconnected cognitive modules. A **Predictive Engine** serves as the robot's imagination; it takes in all the sensory information and uses an internal "World Model" to constantly predict the sensory consequences of potential actions. Based on these predictions, a **Motor Planner** decides what to do next. This planner intelligently balances exploiting skills it has already learned with exploring new, unknown actions to see what happens.

The core of the system is the **Developmental Learning Module**. After the robot performs an action, this module compares the actual sensory feedback with what the Predictive Engine forecasted. Any difference, or "prediction error," is used as a learning opportunity to refine and improve the internal World Model. This model is a rich, internal representation of not only external objects but also the robot's own body (a "body schema") and the cause-and-effect rules of interaction.

The robot's learning journey progresses through distinct developmental phases. It begins in a **reflexive** state, engaging in "motor babbling" or random movements to build a foundational understanding. As it gains a basic level of competence, it enters a **coordinative** phase, where it starts forming coherent sensorimotor patterns. With further learning, it becomes **goal-directed**, capable of planning actions to achieve specific outcomes. Finally, it reaches a **skilled** phase, where it can execute complex tasks efficiently and adaptively. This entire process is driven by an intrinsic cycle of sensing, predicting, acting, and learning from experience.
