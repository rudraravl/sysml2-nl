This model represents a Bonanno Suprapubic Catheter system, a medical device designed for continuous urinary drainage directly from the bladder. It is inserted through the abdominal wall (a suprapubic approach) rather than the urethra.

The core of the system is a flexible catheter with several key components. At the tip that resides inside the bladder, an inflatable balloon is used to anchor the catheter in place. This balloon is filled with a saline solution to a specific volume, preventing the catheter from being accidentally dislodged.

A critical feature of this catheter is its dual-lumen design, meaning it has two independent internal channels. The primary, larger channel is the drainage lumen, which allows urine to flow from the bladder, through the catheter, and into an external collection system. The second, smaller channel is the irrigation lumen. This allows a medical professional to introduce a saline solution into the bladder to flush it or, more commonly, to clear any sediment or clots that might be blocking the drainage lumen, ensuring it remains open and functional.

The catheter tip itself has multiple openings (eyelets) to ensure efficient collection of urine from the bladder.

The complete system includes the catheter connected to a large-capacity (2-liter) urine collection bag. This bag features an anti-reflux valve to prevent urine from flowing back towards the bladder, reducing the risk of infection.

The system operates through a clear lifecycle: it is inserted, positioned, and then secured by inflating the balloon. In its normal operational state, it provides continuous drainage. If the flow stops, the irrigation function can be used to restore it. For removal, the balloon is deflated first, allowing the catheter to be safely withdrawn.
