This model describes a digital residential water meter system designed to accurately measure, display, and record water consumption for purposes such as utility billing and resource monitoring.

The system is a physical device installed in a water line, with a water inlet and a water outlet. As water passes through, its core function is to measure the volume consumed. The device is built from several integrated components that work together to perform this task.

At its heart is a **flow sensor** that measures the instantaneous rate at which water is passing through the meter. This raw flow rate data is then sent to a **volume integrator**. The integrator acts as the system's calculator, continuously processing the flow rate over time to calculate and accumulate the total volume of water that has been used.

The processed information is then used by two other components. A **display module** provides a user-facing screen that shows both the current flow rate and, more importantly, the cumulative volume of water consumed—the essential reading for a water meter. In parallel, a **data logger** periodically captures and stores a complete measurement record, which includes the total volume, the flow rate at that moment, and a timestamp. This creates a historical log of water usage.

The meter operates intelligently through different states. It remains in a low-power **idle** state when no water is being used. As soon as it detects water flow, it transitions to an **active** state and begins its continuous measurement and recording process. If it detects a problem, such as a sensor malfunction, it will enter an **error** state. Once the water flow stops or a fault is resolved, the meter returns to its idle state. The system is designed to meet strict requirements, including a measurement accuracy of within ±2% and reliable operation across a specific range of water pressures and flow rates typical of a residential environment.
