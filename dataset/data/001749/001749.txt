This model represents a complete automobile system, designed for the primary purpose of transporting passengers. It details the vehicle's mechanical and electronic components and how they interact to enable driving.

At its core, the system is a passenger car operated by a driver. The driver interacts with the vehicle through an accelerator pedal, a brake pedal, and a steering wheel. These inputs are translated into commands that control the car's movement.

The vehicle's powertrain consists of an engine, a transmission, and a fuel tank. The engine, which can be in an off, idling, or running state, generates power by consuming fuel. The amount of fuel supplied to the engine is precisely managed by an Engine Control Unit (ECU). This ECU acts as the car's brain, receiving the driver's throttle command as well as real-time feedback on the vehicle's speed and the engine's RPM. Based on this data, it calculates the optimal fuel flow to achieve the desired acceleration. The power and torque from the engine are then passed through a transmission, which adjusts them using gear ratios before sending the force to the wheels.

Braking and steering are handled by dedicated subsystems. When the driver presses the brake pedal, the brake system applies a braking force to all four wheels, slowing the car down. The steering wheel controls the steering system, which adjusts the angle of the front wheels to direct the vehicle.

The system includes several key sensors for feedback and control. A speedometer calculates the vehicle's speed by measuring the rotational speed of the wheels, feeding this crucial information back to the ECU. The fuel tank also monitors its own level, transitioning through states like full, normal, low, and empty.

The automobile as a whole operates through a clear lifecycle of states, including parked, starting, idling, driving, emergency braking, and shutting down. This describes its journey from being stationary to moving and back to a parked and off state. The design is bound by specific performance and safety requirements, such as a total mass under 2000 kg, a maximum speed of at least 180 km/h, a minimum braking force, and a fuel capacity sufficient for long-distance travel.
