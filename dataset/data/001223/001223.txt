This model describes a laboratory-grade electrochemical system designed for precisely controlling and studying oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions. It is a standard three-electrode electrochemical cell, a common setup in chemistry and materials science research.

The system's core consists of three distinct electrodes immersed in a chemical solution called an electrolyte, which is held in a reservoir. The primary electrode, known as the "working electrode," is where the chemical reaction of interest occurs. It has an active surface with specific catalytic properties designed to facilitate the transfer of electrons with chemical species in the electrolyte.

To complete the electrical circuit and allow current to flow, a "counter electrode" is also placed in the solution. The third electrode, a "reference electrode," provides a stable, constant electrical potential. This stable reference is crucial because it acts as a fixed point against which the potential of the working electrode can be accurately measured and controlled.

The entire process is managed by an electronic instrument called a "potentiostat." The potentiostat is electrically connected to all three electrodes. It works by applying a desired voltage between the working and reference electrodes and then measures the resulting electrical current that flows between the working and counter electrodes. This allows an experimenter to precisely drive chemical reactions (either oxidation or reduction) at the working electrode's surface.

The system operates in different states, such as being idle, actively causing oxidation, or actively causing reduction, depending on the electrical potential applied by the potentiostat. It can perform a full redox cycle, switching between oxidizing and reducing chemical species in the electrolyte. The system is designed to meet strict performance and stability requirements, ensuring that the electrode's potential is precisely controlled and remains stable over time, and that the resulting current is measured with high resolution. This setup is fundamental for applications like battery research, corrosion studies, developing sensors, and electroplating.
