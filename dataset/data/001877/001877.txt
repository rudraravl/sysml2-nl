This model represents a Savery Steam Pump, an early type of steam-powered engine designed specifically to remove water from mines or other low-lying areas. It operates without any pistons or complex moving parts, relying instead on the direct force of steam pressure and the power of a vacuum.

The core of the system consists of a boiler, which generates high-pressure steam, and a central receiving vessel where the main pumping action occurs. This vessel is connected to a series of pipes and valves that control the flow of steam and water. The system includes a supply pipe to draw water from a source (like a flooded mine), a delivery pipe to discharge the water at a higher elevation, and a source of cold water for cooling.

The pump works in a continuous, four-stage cycle:

1.  **Water Expulsion:** The cycle begins when the receiving vessel is full of water. A valve opens, admitting high-pressure steam from the boiler directly into the top of the vessel. This steam pressure pushes down on the water, forcing it out through a one-way valve and up the delivery pipe.

2.  **Steam Condensation:** Once the vessel is empty of water and full of steam, the steam valve is closed. A different valve then opens briefly, spraying a small amount of cold water into the vessel. This instantly condenses the hot steam back into a small amount of water, creating a powerful vacuum inside the vessel.

3.  **Water Intake:** This vacuum sucks water up from the mine through the supply pipe, refilling the receiving vessel for the next cycle.

4.  **Repeat:** With the vessel refilled, the system is ready to admit steam again, repeating the entire process to create a continuous, albeit pulsed, flow of pumped water.
