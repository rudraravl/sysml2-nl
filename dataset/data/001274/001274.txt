This model represents a digital tachometer system, designed to measure and display the rotational speed of a mechanical shaft, such as a vehicle's engine crankshaft. Its primary purpose is to provide real-time monitoring of speed in Revolutions Per Minute (RPM).

The system consists of three main electronic components that work in sequence: a magnetic sensor, a signal processor, and a digital display.

1.  **Magnetic Sensor**: This component is the primary input device. It is physically coupled with the rotating shaft and generates a series of electrical pulses corresponding to the shaft's rotation. The sensor is calibrated with information on how many pulses represent one full revolution.

2.  **Signal Processor**: The processor acts as the brain of the system. It receives the raw pulse signal from the sensor and performs calculations. By counting the number of pulses within a specific time window and applying a calibration correction factor, it accurately computes the shaft's speed in RPM.

3.  **Digital Display**: This is the user interface, which receives the calculated RPM value from the processor and shows it on a screen. The display has several operational states: it is 'off' without power, 'idle' when the vehicle is on but the engine isn't running, and 'active' when displaying the live RPM. It also features a 'warning' state to alert the user if the rotational speed exceeds a safe maximum limit, such as an engine redline.

These three components are integrated into a cohesive unit, all powered by a single electrical source. The system is designed to meet specific requirements for accuracy, reliability, and responsiveness, ensuring the displayed RPM is a timely and trustworthy reflection of the shaft's actual speed.
