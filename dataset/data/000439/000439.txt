This describes a biomedical system for HIV prevention, centered around a Dapivirine Vaginal Ring. The system is designed for user-controlled, long-term administration of an antiretroviral drug to prevent the sexual transmission of HIV.

The core of the system is the vaginal ring itself, a flexible, circular device made from a biocompatible silicone polymer. Embedded within this silicone structure is a drug reservoir containing an initial dose of Dapivirine, the active antiretroviral compound.

When a user inserts the ring, it is designed to provide a continuous and controlled release of Dapivirine directly into the vaginal environment over an extended period, typically 28 days. The released drug then diffuses into the surrounding tissues, aiming to achieve and maintain a therapeutic concentration that is sufficient to prevent HIV infection.

The system's operation follows a distinct lifecycle. It begins in a packaged, sterile state. Once inserted by the user, it enters an active state where it continuously releases the drug. After its effective period, the drug becomes depleted, and the ring is then removed. The overall effectiveness of this prevention method relies on proper insertion and the user's adherence to wearing the ring continuously for the entire 28-day cycle.
