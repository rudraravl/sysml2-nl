This model describes an airlift pumping system designed to move a fluid, like water, from a lower source reservoir to a higher destination tank. Unlike conventional pumps with mechanical moving parts, this system leverages the principles of buoyancy and density difference to lift the fluid.

The system is composed of several key components that work in sequence. It starts with an air compressor that generates high-pressure air. This air is then sent through a pressure regulator, which precisely controls and sets the air pressure to an optimal level for pumping.

The core of the system is the airlift pump itself, which consists mainly of a long vertical pipe, called a riser, submerged in the source reservoir. The regulated compressed air is injected at the bottom of this submerged pipe. This injection creates a mixture of air bubbles and fluid within the pipe, significantly reducing the overall density of the mixture compared to the fluid outside it. Due to this density difference, the buoyant air-fluid mixture naturally rises up the pipe, effectively lifting the fluid to a higher elevation.

Once the mixture reaches the top, it flows into an air-fluid separator. This component separates the air, which is vented off, from the now-lifted fluid. The separated fluid then passes through a flow meter, which measures its flow rate for monitoring purposes, before being discharged into the final destination tank.

The entire system operates through a defined lifecycle, including an idle state, a starting sequence to build air pressure, a continuous operating state for active pumping, and a controlled shutdown process. It also includes a fault state to manage potential system errors.
