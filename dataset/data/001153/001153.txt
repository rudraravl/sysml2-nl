This model describes a high-sensitivity optical nanosensor system designed for the real-time detection and quantification of specific target molecules in biological or chemical samples. The system's primary mission is to enable advanced monitoring for applications in medical diagnostics, environmental sensing, and biochemical research.

The sensor works by using a specially prepared nanoparticle surface, which is coated with a functional layer (such as Gold-Antibody) designed to selectively capture specific target molecules from a sample. When a molecule binds to this surface, an optical transducer converts this physical binding event into a faint optical signal by exciting the surface with light of one wavelength and measuring the light emitted at another.

This initial optical signal is then passed to an internal signal amplifier to increase its strength and ensure it can be measured accurately. Following amplification, a dedicated data processor analyzes the signal, applying calibration data to convert the light intensity into a precise concentration value for the target molecule.

The entire system is controlled through an external interface that allows an operator to start, stop, and calibrate the device. It operates through several distinct states, including an idle state, a calibration mode, an active sensing and analyzing cycle, and a low-power standby mode. The system is designed for continuous operation, allowing it to monitor and report molecular concentrations over extended periods with very high sensitivity and a fast response time.
