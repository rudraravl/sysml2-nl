This model describes a non-invasive Impedance Cardiography (ICG) system, a medical device designed for the continuous, real-time monitoring of a patient's cardiac performance. The system assesses how well the heart is pumping blood without requiring invasive procedures like catheterization.

The core of the system's operation involves an **Electrode Set**, typically consisting of four electrodes placed on the patient's thorax. A **High-Frequency Current Source** generates a very small, safe, and imperceptible alternating electrical current. This current is passed through the patient's chest via two of the electrodes.

As the heart beats, the volume of blood in the major arteries of the chest changes, which in turn causes slight changes in the chest's electrical impedance (resistance). The other two electrodes are used to measure the resulting voltage changes. This raw voltage signal is then sent to a **Signal Acquisition Unit**, which amplifies the very weak signal, filters out noise, and calculates the baseline impedance of the chest as well as the tiny, rhythmic changes caused by blood flow.

This processed impedance data is fed into the system's "brain," the **Hemodynamic Processor**. This unit analyzes the impedance waveform to identify key events in the cardiac cycle. Using this data and patient-specific information (like the length of their thorax), it calculates critical hemodynamic parameters such as Stroke Volume (the amount of blood pumped with each heartbeat), Heart Rate, and Cardiac Output (the total volume of blood pumped per minute).

All this vital information is then sent to a **Display and Alarm Unit**. This component provides a user interface for clinicians, showing the calculated values and real-time waveforms. It also features a critical alarm function that alerts medical staff if the patient's cardiac output falls outside a pre-set safe range.

To ensure patient safety, a dedicated **Safety Monitoring Unit** continuously checks that the applied electrical current remains at a safe low level and verifies that the electrodes have good contact with the patient's skin. If any safety issue is detected, it can trigger an alarm or initiate a shutdown of the system. The entire system operates in a continuous cycle of calibrating, measuring, analyzing, and displaying data to provide an up-to-the-second view of the patient's cardiovascular status.
