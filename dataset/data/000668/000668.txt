This model describes an extracorporeal liver support system, a medical device designed to perform blood purification for patients with acute or chronic liver failure. It functions as an artificial liver, cleaning the patient's blood outside of their body.

The system's core is a blood processing circuit. A **Blood Pump** draws blood from the patient and pushes it through the system at a controlled rate. The blood then enters a **Dialysis Filter**, which is the primary cleaning component. Inside the filter, toxins and metabolic waste are removed from the blood into a separate waste fluid. After being filtered, the blood passes through a **Temperature Regulator** to ensure it is warmed back to a safe, precise body temperature before being returned to the patient.

The entire process is managed by a central **Control Unit**. This unit oversees the system's operation and is supported by a suite of sensors. A **Toxin Sensor** measures the level of impurities in the blood, while a **Pressure Monitor** continuously checks the pressure within the circuit to prevent dangerous conditions. If any readings go outside of safe limits, the control unit can trigger an alarm and halt the procedure.

The system operates through several distinct phases: it starts in an **idle** state, moves to a **priming** phase to prepare the circuit, then enters the main **treating** phase. After the session is complete, it performs a **rinsing** cycle. In case of a malfunction, it enters a protective **alarm** state. The system is designed for continuous treatment sessions lasting several hours, with built-in safety requirements for blood flow, pressure, and temperature.
