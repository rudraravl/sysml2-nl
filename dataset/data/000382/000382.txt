This model describes a sophisticated solar array system for a spacecraft, which is part of a mission designated as the "Eagle Eye Mission." The system is designed to generate up to 1000W of electrical power for the spacecraft by continuously tracking the sun.

The system features a complex, multi-stage deployment and operation process. For launch, the solar array, which consists of multiple panels, is folded and rigidly secured to the spacecraft's structure by a Hold-Down and Release Mechanism (HDRM). This is a critical safety feature to prevent the array from being damaged or causing damage to other equipment due to the intense vibrations during launch.

Once the spacecraft is in orbit, the system undergoes a precise deployment sequence. First, the HDRM releases the stowed solar array assembly. A deployment arm then extends to push the entire array away from the main body of the spacecraft. This is carefully engineered to position the array's center of gravity directly on its rotation axis, which prevents the spacecraft from wobbling when the array rotates. Following this, the individual solar panels unfold in a specific order, with lateral panels releasing and deploying from a central panel.

In its operational state, a Solar Array Drive Mechanism (SADM) and its associated electronics rotate the fully deployed array to keep it pointed at the sun. This rotation occurs at a rate of about one turn per day. The system is also capable of making fine adjustments to its pointing throughout the year to account for changes in the sun's apparent position, ensuring maximum power generation. The power collected by the panels is then transferred through the rotating SADM joint to the main spacecraft.
