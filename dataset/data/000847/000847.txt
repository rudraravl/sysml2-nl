This model represents the **Artificio de Juanelo**, a famous and complex 16th-century hydraulic machine. It was designed to lift a substantial amount of water from the Tagus River up to the Alcázar, a hilltop palace in Toledo, Spain.

The system is powered entirely by the natural current of the **Tagus River**. A large **water wheel** sits in the river, and its rotation, driven by the water flow, serves as the engine for the entire contraption.

The rotational power from the water wheel is mechanically transferred to drive a long, continuous **bucket chain**. This chain is equipped with a large number of buckets that, at the bottom of their cycle, dip into the river to fill with water. The powered chain then lifts these filled buckets vertically over a great distance—nearly 100 meters.

Once a bucket reaches the top of the machine, its contents are discharged into the **Alcázar's reservoir**, providing a steady water supply to the palace. The empty bucket then continues its journey down the chain to be refilled, creating a continuous water-lifting cycle. The machine's operation follows a clear sequence for each bucket: filling at the river, being lifted to the top, discharging its water, and then returning to the bottom.
