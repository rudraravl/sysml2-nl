This is a multi-stage effluent decontamination system designed to treat raw, contaminated wastewater and render it safe for discharge. It is an automated, sequential processing plant that cleans water by taking it through a series of physical, chemical, and biological treatments.

The process begins when an **Intake Pump** draws raw effluent into the system. This wastewater first passes through a **Pre-Treatment Filter** to remove large solid particles. From there, the filtered water flows into a **Chemical Reactor**, where a specific chemical reagent is introduced to neutralize certain types of contaminants.

Following chemical treatment, the water enters a **Biological Treatment Unit**. This stage uses concentrated biomass (microorganisms) to digest and break down organic waste over a set period. The final treatment step is a **UV Sterilizer**, which uses high-intensity ultraviolet light to disinfect the water, killing any remaining bacteria and viruses.

A critical part of the system is its continuous quality control. After sterilization, the now-treated effluent is sampled by a **Quality Monitor** which measures its final contaminant concentration and pH levels. A central **Control System** uses this data to ensure the treatment is effective. If the water quality meets the required safety standards (a contaminant level of 10 ppm or less), it is approved for final discharge. If the monitor detects that the water is still too contaminated, the control system raises an alarm to alert operators of a system failure. The entire system is designed to process a minimum of 1000 liters of wastewater per minute.
