This model represents a large-scale, automated system for mitigating the societal and economic impacts of technological unemployment. Its core purpose is to manage the transition of workers who are displaced from their jobs by automation and artificial intelligence, aiming to maintain economic stability and provide a social safety net.

The system is composed of several interconnected components that work together in a continuous cycle. An **Economic Monitor** constantly tracks key metrics like the national employment rate and the growth of automation across different industry sectors. This data is fed to a **Displacement Analyzer**, which identifies specific workers who have lost their jobs or are at high risk of displacement due to new technologies.

Once a displaced worker is identified, a central **Policy Coordinator** oversees the intervention strategy. The worker is assessed and directed into one or more support pathways. The **Retraining Center** offers various training programs designed to upskill workers for new jobs that are in high demand. Simultaneously, a **Benefit Distributor** manages and disburses financial aid to eligible workers, providing them with economic support while they are in transition.

After completing a program, the **Job Matching Engine** uses a sophisticated algorithm to connect these newly retrained workers with suitable job openings in the market. This entire process defines a clear lifecycle for an individual, moving them from an "employed" status, through "displaced," into "retraining" or "receiving benefits," with the ultimate goal of becoming "reemployed."

In essence, this is a blueprint for a national or regional socio-economic infrastructure designed to make the workforce more resilient to technological change. It acts as an integrated pipeline for identifying at-risk individuals, providing them with new skills and financial support, and actively guiding them back into stable employment.
