This model describes a physical system for moving a heavy object using a simple machine: an inclined plane, or ramp. The system consists of three main components: a load to be moved, the inclined plane itself, and an operator who provides the necessary force.

The load is an object with a specific mass and weight. Its state is tracked by its current position on the ramp and its velocity. The inclined plane is a flat supporting surface tilted at an angle, characterized by its length, height, and the frictional properties of its surface. The operator represents the person or mechanism applying an effort force to push or pull the load up the plane. This operator has a defined maximum strength or force they can safely exert.

The system operates to lift the load to a higher elevation with less effort than lifting it vertically. The operator applies a force, and if this force is sufficient to overcome the combined effects of gravity (pulling the load down the slope) and friction, the load will move up the plane. The system's operation follows a clear lifecycle: it begins with the load at rest at the bottom, moves into an active state when sufficient force is applied, and concludes when the load reaches the top of the ramp.

The design is governed by practical requirements. It must provide a mechanical advantage, meaning it must demonstrably reduce the force required. It also has efficiency goals, which aim to minimize energy loss from friction. Finally, safety constraints ensure that the operator is not required to apply an unsafe amount of force and that the ramp's angle is stable enough to prevent the load from sliding back down uncontrollably.
