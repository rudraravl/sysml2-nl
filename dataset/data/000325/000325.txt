This model describes a conceptual framework for managing and tracing engineering requirements. It's not a physical object, but rather a methodology used in complex projects to ensure that every detailed design choice can be linked back to an original goal or need.

The system establishes a clear hierarchy for requirements. It defines three key concepts:

1.  **Original Requirement:** This is the high-level, foundational requirement that often comes from a customer or a set of top-level goals. For example, an original requirement for a new car might be "The vehicle must be safe for its occupants."

2.  **Derived Requirement:** This is a more detailed, specific, and often technical requirement that is created to help satisfy an original requirement. Using the car example, derived requirements from the main safety goal could be "The car must include front and side airbags" or "The braking system must be able to stop the car from 60 mph in under 150 feet."

3.  **Derivation Link:** This is the explicit, traceable connection between an original requirement and the derived ones. It serves as the logical justification, showing *why* a specific derived requirement exists. It formally documents that the need for airbags, for instance, is a direct consequence of the overarching need for vehicle safety.

In essence, this system provides a structured method for engineers to break down complex goals into manageable, testable requirements while maintaining a clear and auditable trail of how and why each specific requirement was created.
