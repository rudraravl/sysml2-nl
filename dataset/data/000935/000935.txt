This model represents a Saqiyah, a traditional, animal-powered water-lifting system designed to elevate water from a source like a well to a higher-level irrigation channel.

The system's power is generated by an animal (such as an ox or donkey) walking in a circle, which turns a large, horizontal gear wheel. This horizontal wheel is mechanically linked to a vertical water wheel, transferring the rotational power and causing the vertical wheel to turn.

A continuous bucket chain, with numerous buckets attached, is looped over this vertical wheel. As the wheel rotates, it drives the chain in a continuous cycle. On their downward path, the buckets dip into a water well, filling with water. They are then lifted upwards as the chain ascends. Upon reaching the top of the cycle, the buckets tilt and discharge their water into an irrigation channel positioned at a higher elevation. The empty buckets then travel back down to the well to repeat the process.

The entire system is designed for continuous, low-speed operation. It can be in one of two main states: idle when the animal is not engaged, or active when it is powered and lifting water. The active state involves a constant, repeating cycle of buckets filling, ascending, and discharging their contents. The system is engineered to lift water to a specific height and deliver a consistent flow rate, governed by the speed of the animal and the capacity of the buckets.
