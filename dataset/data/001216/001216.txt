This model describes a handheld radar gun system, the kind used for measuring the speed of moving objects like vehicles. It operates by transmitting a radio frequency pulse and analyzing the change in frequency (the Doppler shift) of the signal that reflects off the target.

The system is composed of several key electronic modules that work together to perform a measurement. A **Control Unit** manages the overall operation, responding to user inputs like pressing the trigger or turning the power on and off. The entire device is powered by a self-contained **Power Supply**, which is a battery pack that provides stable voltage and current to all the components.

When a user pulls the trigger, the **Transmitter Module** generates a precise, high-frequency radar pulse (operating around 24.15 GHz) and sends it out through an antenna. When this pulse hits a moving target, it reflects back toward the gun as an echo. A sensitive **Receiver Module** captures this faint echo, filters out unwanted background noise, and amplifies the signal.

The core of the system is the **Signal Processing Unit**. This unit takes the original transmitted pulse data and the clean echo signal and compares them. It calculates the Doppler shift to determine the target's speed and also estimates the target's distance by measuring the time delay between sending the pulse and receiving the echo. The processor also validates the measurement to ensure it is a reliable reading.

Finally, the calculated speed and distance are packaged into a speed reading and sent to the **Display Module**, which shows the final speed to the user on a screen. If the measurement is not valid, the display can show an error code instead. The system is designed for high performance, with a detection range of up to 500 meters and the ability to accurately measure speeds from as low as 1.4 m/s to as high as 111 m/s.
