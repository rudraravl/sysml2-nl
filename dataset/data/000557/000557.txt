This model describes a Bronchial Blocker System, a specialized medical device used during thoracic (chest) surgery. Its primary purpose is to enable single-lung ventilation, where one lung is intentionally collapsed to provide surgeons with a clear, still operating field, while the patient is kept ventilated through the other lung.

The core of the system is a flexible `BlockerCatheter` that is inserted into the patient's airway. This catheter houses several key components that work together to safely isolate a lung. At its tip is an `InflatableCuff`, a small balloon that, when inflated, creates a seal within the targeted bronchus (the main airway of a lung), preventing air from entering.

To ensure the device is placed correctly, it includes an integrated `FiberopticChannel`. This allows a clinician to pass a small camera through the device to get a direct visual feed from inside the airway, confirming the blocker's tip is in the precise location before the cuff is inflated.

The system is equipped with critical safety and monitoring features. An integrated `PressureSensor` continuously measures the pressure within the inflatable cuff to ensure it remains within a safe range, preventing potential tissue damage to the airway lining from over-inflation. An `OxygenSensor` monitors the patient's oxygen saturation levels, providing real-time feedback on how well the single-lung ventilation is working.

The operational lifecycle of the device involves several distinct phases. It begins with insertion and positioning, where the catheter is guided to the correct depth and its placement is visually verified. If misplaced, it can be adjusted. Once confirmed, the cuff is inflated to isolate the lung. During the surgical procedure, the system continuously monitors the patient's status and cuff pressure. If pressure becomes too high or the procedure is complete, the device is commanded to deflate the cuff, allowing normal two-lung ventilation to be restored.
