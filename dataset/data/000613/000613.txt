This describes an obstetrical forceps system, a medical instrument used by a clinician to assist in childbirth. The system's purpose is to safely grasp the fetal head and apply controlled pulling force (traction) and rotation to guide the baby through the birth canal, particularly when maternal pushing is insufficient or the baby's descent has stopped.

The core of the system is the forceps instrument, which consists of two interlocking components. Each component includes a curved blade, designed to fit around the fetal head, and a handle for the clinician to grip. A central lock mechanism allows the two halves to be securely articulated after they have been individually positioned.

The entire system operates as part of a structured medical procedure, which involves several distinct phases:
1.  **Assessment:** The clinician first evaluates the position, orientation, and size of the fetal head relative to the birth canal to determine if the use of forceps is appropriate.
2.  **Application:** The clinician carefully applies the left and right blades one at a time to the sides of the fetal head.
3.  **Locking:** The two halves of the instrument are brought together and the lock is engaged, creating a secure and stable grasp.
4.  **Rotation and Traction:** If the head is mispositioned, the clinician performs a controlled rotation. The primary action is then applying gentle, controlled traction in sync with maternal efforts to guide the baby's descent. A critical aspect of this phase is the constant monitoring of the pressure applied by the blades to the fetal head to ensure it remains within safe limits to prevent trauma.
5.  **Release:** Once the head is delivered, the lock is disengaged, and the blades are removed individually to complete the assisted delivery.
