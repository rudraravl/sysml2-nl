This model describes a soft robotic work cell designed for automated material handling tasks. The system is distinguished by its use of non-silicon-based materials, such as organic polymers and biocompatible components, making it suitable for environments where traditional electronics are not ideal.

The core of the system is a robot with a multi-jointed arm and a gripper. Instead of electric motors and a computer processor, this robot is actuated and controlled entirely by a fluidic system. An external pump supplies pressurized fluid to the robot, and an onboard fluidic controller directs this fluid to move the arm and operate the gripper. This design results in soft, compliant movements rather than the rigid motions of a conventional industrial robot.

The robot is equipped with four organic sensors to perceive its surroundings, providing feedback on its position and the forces it is applying. Its primary function is to perform a complete pick-and-place operation: it senses the location of a workpiece, calculates a path, moves to the object, grasps it with a controlled force, transports it to a new destination, and then releases it.

The system operates through a clear lifecycle, starting in a powered-off state, moving through initialization to a ready state, and then entering an operating mode to perform its task. It includes error handling to manage task failures and can be reset to a ready state. The entire work cell includes the robot, its fluidic pump, and a dedicated power supply. A key design feature is its inherent safety, with strict limits on the maximum force the arm and gripper can exert.
