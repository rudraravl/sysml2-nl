This model describes a digital inclinometer system, a device designed for the precise measurement of tilt, pitch, and roll angles relative to Earth's gravitational field. It's intended for applications like construction leveling, vehicle stability monitoring, and industrial platform alignment where accurate angle information is crucial.

The system is composed of four main electronic components working together. At its core is a three-axis MEMS accelerometer, which acts as the primary sensor by measuring the components of Earth's gravity vector. This raw acceleration data is then fed into a signal processor, the brain of the device. The processor filters out noise from the sensor readings, applies calibration corrections, and performs the trigonometric calculations needed to convert the acceleration data into final pitch and roll angles.

The calculated tilt measurements are sent to a display controller, which manages the visual output for the user. A central power regulator takes in external power and provides stable, regulated voltage to the accelerometer, processor, and display, ensuring reliable operation of all components.

In operation, the inclinometer continuously measures acceleration, processes it, and updates the displayed tilt angles at a high rate for real-time feedback. The system supports field calibration, allowing users to correct for any mounting offsets and ensure high accuracy. It also has several operational states, including a powered-off mode, an initialization sequence on startup, a calibration mode, and the primary operational mode for continuous measurement. An error state handles sensor failures or other faults, and the device can be reset or powered down in response to specific commands. The final tilt measurement, including its validity and a confidence score, is available both on the display and as a data output for other systems to use.
