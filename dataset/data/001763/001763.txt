This model describes a complete hydraulic concrete pumping subsystem, the kind of heavy machinery used on construction sites to transport wet concrete from a mixer to the specific location where it's needed.

The system is composed of several key components working together. It starts with a **concrete hopper**, which acts as a reservoir to hold the freshly mixed concrete. From the hopper, the concrete flows into the main **concrete pump**. This pump is the core of the system, a powerful hydraulic unit responsible for generating the high pressure needed to move the heavy, viscous concrete mixture. The pump is powered by a dedicated **hydraulic power pack**, which converts electrical energy into hydraulic power.

Once pressurized by the pump, the concrete is forced through a long, durable **delivery hose** to its final **discharge point**, such as a building foundation or a high-rise floor. The entire operation is managed by a **pump controller**, which serves as the system's brain. It receives commands from an operator to start, stop, and control the pumping speed. For safety and performance, the system is equipped with **pressure and flow sensors** that continuously monitor the concrete moving through the pump and hose.

The pump operates through a distinct lifecycle. It begins in a stopped state and, upon receiving a start command, enters a priming phase to prepare the system. Once primed, it begins normal operation, actively pumping concrete while the controller continuously monitors for potential faults like excessive pressure. If a fault is detected, the system will enter a safe, faulted state. After the job is complete, it goes through a shutdown sequence that can include a necessary cleaning cycle before returning to a stopped state. The system is specifically designed to handle concrete with defined properties, including aggregate size and slump (a measure of its workability).
