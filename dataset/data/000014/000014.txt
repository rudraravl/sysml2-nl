This model represents a system for assessing the profitability of mining operations in a space-based context, considering varying levels of risk.  The system analyzes the profitability of ore mining within three distinct security zones: high-security, low-security, and null-security space.  Profitability is calculated by considering the ore yield, the market price of the ore, operational costs (including fuel, maintenance, and crew), and the probability of survival (the risk of losing the mining vessel to pirates or other threats).

The core calculation involves computing the expected revenue from ore sales, determining a risk factor based on survival probability, and then calculating a risk-adjusted profitability.  Higher-risk zones (low and null security) have higher ore prices but also significantly higher operational costs and lower survival probabilities, thus impacting the final profitability calculation.  The model incorporates different assumptions for ore values and operational costs depending on the security level. The input parameters, such as ore yield and survival probability, are based on factors like the mining ship's capabilities, pilot skill, and the inherent dangers of the mining location. The final output is a risk-adjusted profitability figure in ISK per hour for each security zone, allowing a mining corporation to make informed decisions about where to deploy its resources for optimal profit.
