This model represents a Gerotor-type hydraulic pump system. It is a mechanical device designed to convert rotational energy from a motor or engine into hydraulic power by pressurizing and moving fluid.

The core of the system is the Gerotor pump itself, which consists of several key mechanical components working together inside a main housing. A central drive shaft receives torque and speed from an external power source. This shaft directly turns an inner rotor, which has a specific number of lobes (e.g., six). This inner rotor is mounted off-center (eccentrically) inside a larger outer rotor, which has one more internal cavity than the inner rotor has lobes (e.g., seven).

The system's operation is based on the interaction between these two rotors. As the drive shaft spins the inner rotor, the eccentric meshing causes the outer rotor to turn as well. This coordinated rotation continuously creates expanding chambers between the rotors on the pump's inlet side. This expansion creates a vacuum, drawing hydraulic fluid into the pump. As the rotors continue to turn, these fluid-filled chambers are sealed, carried around to the outlet side, and then compressed as the rotor lobes and cavities mesh more tightly. This compression forces the fluid out of the outlet port at a high pressure. This mechanism results in a smooth, continuous flow of pressurized fluid, making it suitable for applications like lubrication systems or hydraulic power units.

The pump's lifecycle is managed through several operational states. It begins in a `stopped` state and transitions to `starting` upon receiving a start command, where it accelerates to its target speed. Once at speed, it enters the `operational` state, actively pumping fluid. It can be brought to a controlled stop through a `stopping` state or shut down instantly in an `emergency` state. The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, including delivering a rated flow rate, withstanding a maximum operating pressure, and maintaining high volumetric efficiency.
