This is a millimeter-wave security scanner system, the kind often used for personnel screening in airports and other secure locations. Its primary purpose is to detect concealed objects, such as weapons or contraband, hidden beneath a person's clothing without physical contact.

The system is composed of several key components that work in a coordinated sequence. An **Antenna Array**, connected to a **Transmitter** and a **Receiver**, sends out very low-power millimeter-wave signals toward the person being scanned. These signals penetrate clothing but reflect off the skin and any objects carried on the body. The Antenna Array then captures these reflected signals.

The raw reflection data is sent to a **Signal Processing Unit**, which acts as the system's image-creation engine. It processes the complex data—including the amplitude and phase shift of the reflected waves—to construct a detailed, three-dimensional image of the person and any items they are carrying.

This generated image is then analyzed by an automated **Threat Detection Module**. This software uses specific algorithms to identify shapes and densities that do not correspond to the human body, flagging them as potential threats. Simultaneously, the image and the threat assessment are sent to an **Operator Console**, where a security officer can view the results.

The entire process follows a distinct operational flow. The scanner begins in an idle, ready state. When an operator initiates a scan, it transitions to an active scanning state. Once the scan is complete, it processes the data, analyzes the resulting image for threats, and finally displays the results to the operator before returning to the idle state, ready for the next person.
