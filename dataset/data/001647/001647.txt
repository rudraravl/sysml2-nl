This model represents a Daintree Networks wireless lighting control system, an intelligent automation solution for commercial buildings designed to provide energy-efficient lighting. The system operates on a self-organizing wireless mesh network, which eliminates the need for dedicated control wiring between devices.

The core components of the system are:

*   **Lighting Fixtures:** These are the actual lights within the building. Each fixture is individually addressable on the network and contains a controllable ballast that allows its brightness to be dimmed precisely. They also include a power meter to report their energy consumption.
*   **Occupancy Sensors:** These devices are placed throughout a space to detect human presence. When they detect motion, they broadcast an "occupied" status over the network; after a period of no motion, they signal that the space is "vacant."
*   **Daylight Sensors:** These sensors measure the amount of natural, ambient light entering a space (e.g., from windows). They periodically report this light level to the system.
*   **Wireless Area Controller:** This is the local "brain" for a specific zone or room. It receives data from both occupancy and daylight sensors within its area. Based on this information, it executes control logic to maintain optimal lighting. For example, if a room is occupied and there is plenty of natural daylight, the controller will command the fixtures to dim down, saving energy. If it's dark outside, it will command them to a brighter level. If the room is vacant, it will turn the lights off.
*   **Network Gateway:** This device acts as the central coordinator for the entire mesh network. It manages communication traffic between all the devices and often serves as the bridge between the local lighting network and a central building management system or a cloud-based platform for configuration and monitoring.

In operation, the sensors continuously provide real-time data to the Area Controller. The controller processes this data and sends specific dimming commands to the lighting fixtures to adjust their output. This creates an automated environment that responds to both occupancy and available natural light, significantly reducing energy consumption while ensuring spaces are always appropriately lit for occupants.
