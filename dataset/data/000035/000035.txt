This SysML model describes a system for managing legal adoptions.  The core functionality centers around the agreement and certification of adoptions between involved parties: a judge, prospective adoptive parents, a child, and potentially biological parents.  The system models the interactions and communications between these individuals, ensuring compliance with relevant laws.

The system includes various actors: a judge responsible for overseeing the legal process, a woman and a man (potentially biological parents or not), and two adoptive parents.  Each person has attributes like name, age, and weight, and importantly, a state indicating whether they are awake or asleep, affecting their ability to respond to communication.  Communication occurs through a "VerbalExchange" mechanism, representing verbal interactions.  These interactions are formalized as messages, such as statements of applicable law, agreements, and assertions of the adoption certificate.  The system uses these messages to track the progression of the adoption process.

A crucial part is the "Adoption_Certificate," a legal document recording the adoption.  The certificate connects the adoptive parents and the child and potentially includes biological parents.  The system defines different types of adoption certificates to handle various scenarios.  The model includes constraints, such as a minimum age requirement for adoptive parents (18 years old), which must be satisfied for a valid adoption.  The system also includes a mechanism to represent time, using snapshots (like the birth and adoption dates) and timeslices, to model the adoption as it progresses through stages.  Finally, the model incorporates requirements that formalize the legal requirements for an adoption to be valid.  Essentially, the system is a structured representation of the legal processes and relationships involved in an adoption, with a focus on information flow and the verification of legal requirements.
