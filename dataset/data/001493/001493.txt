This model describes an automated, modular industrial system designed for purifying raw materials. Its primary purpose is to take in a continuous flow of raw, contaminated matter and process it to produce a high-purity, refined product ready for storage or downstream use.

The system is composed of several key physical units that work in a coordinated sequence. Raw material first enters and is held in an **Input Storage Vessel**. From there, it is sent to an **Inspection Unit**, which acts as a quality control sensor, analyzing the material to determine its current level of contamination.

Based on the data from the inspection unit, a central **Control System** makes decisions. If the material's contaminant level is too high, it is routed to a **Separation Unit**. This is the core processing component, which actively removes contaminants from the matter with a specific efficiency. The system is designed to operate cyclically; matter may be re-inspected and re-processed by the separation unit until it meets the required purity standard.

Once the material is certified as sufficiently pure by the inspection unit, it is passed to a **Consolidation Unit**. This unit likely compresses or otherwise prepares the purified matter for dense storage. Finally, the finished, high-purity product is transferred to an **Output Storage Vessel**, ready for distribution.

The entire process is managed by the central Control System, which monitors all units, compares real-time purity readings against a target setpoint (e.g., 97.5% purity), and sends commands to the various units to adjust their operation. The system is engineered to meet specific performance requirements, including maintaining a nominal processing speed (throughput) and adhering to strict safety constraints, such as not exceeding the capacity of its storage vessels.
