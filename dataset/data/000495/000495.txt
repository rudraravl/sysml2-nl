This is a retinal prosthesis system designed to restore a degree of functional vision to individuals with retinal degenerative diseases. The system works by capturing images of the outside world and translating them into electrical signals that directly stimulate the user's remaining retinal neurons, bypassing the damaged parts of the eye.

The system is composed of two main assemblies: an external, wearable unit and a surgically implanted unit.

The external unit, typically mounted on a pair of eyeglasses, includes:
*   A **miniature camera** that captures a live video feed of the user's surroundings.
*   An **image processing unit** that receives the video and converts it into a simplified digital pattern. It uses adaptive algorithms to enhance important visual information like edges, contrast, and motion.
*   A **radio frequency transmitter** and an **inductive power coil** that wirelessly send the processed visual data and power across the skin to the implanted components.
*   A **rechargeable battery** that powers all the external electronics, designed to last for a full day of use.

The implanted unit is a small, biocompatible device placed inside the eye during surgery. It consists of:
*   A **radio frequency receiver** that picks up the wireless power and data signals from the external unit.
*   A **signal decoder and stimulation controller** that interprets the incoming data and generates precise electrical pulses.
*   A **microelectrode array**, a grid of 60 tiny electrodes, which is placed in direct contact with the retina.

In operation, the camera captures a scene, the processor simplifies it into a stimulation pattern, and this pattern is transmitted wirelessly to the implant. The implant then generates electrical pulses according to the pattern and delivers them through the electrode array to the retinal neurons. These neurons, in turn, send signals to the brain via the optic nerve, allowing the brain to perceive patterns of light, shapes, and movement. The entire process, from capturing an image to stimulating the retina, is designed to happen in real-time to provide a fluid visual experience.

The system can operate in different states, including a fully operational mode for active vision, a low-power standby mode, and a diagnostic mode for system checks. It is engineered with strict safety features to prevent tissue damage, with constraints on the maximum electrical current and the mass of both the wearable and implanted components.
