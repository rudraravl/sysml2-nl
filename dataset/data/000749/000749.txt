This model describes a complete medical rhinoscope system, an endoscopic instrument designed for the detailed visual examination of a patient's nasal cavity and nasopharynx. Its primary purpose is to provide doctors with a brightly lit, high-definition, magnified view inside the nasal passages for diagnosis and to assist with minor therapeutic procedures.

The system is composed of several integrated components working together. The core is a slender, flexible **Insertion Tube**, which is designed to be safely inserted into the patient. This tube acts as a conduit, housing a fiber optic bundle, an image sensor with a lens, and channels for both irrigation and suction.

Visualization is achieved through a sophisticated optical and video chain. A powerful, external **Light Source** generates high-intensity, daylight-quality illumination. This light is channeled through a **Fiber Optic Bundle** that runs the length of the insertion tube to its tip, illuminating the area being examined. A miniature **Lens Assembly** at the tip captures the reflected light and focuses a clear, magnified image onto a high-definition (1920x1080) **Image Sensor**. The raw video from the sensor is then sent to a dedicated **Video Processor** which enhances the image with minimal delay, ensuring a smooth, real-time feed. Finally, the processed video is displayed on a large **Display Monitor** for the operator to view.

To ensure a clear field of view during an examination, the system includes two supporting subsystems. An **Irrigation System** can pump a sterile fluid from a reservoir through a channel in the insertion tube to wash away mucus or blood. Complementing this, a **Suction System** uses vacuum pressure to remove these fluids and other debris through a separate channel in the tube, collecting them in a container.

A central **Control Unit** serves as the brain of the system, managing the power distribution to the light source, irrigation, and suction pumps based on the operator's commands. The entire system operates through various states, such as initializing, ready for use, actively examining, and specific modes for when irrigation or suction is engaged.
