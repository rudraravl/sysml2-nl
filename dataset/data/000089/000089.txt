This describes a configurable wheeled vehicle, such as a car or truck, with a focus on its chassis and powertrain assembly. The system is defined through a series of increasingly detailed configurations.

The basic vehicle is a four-wheeled system with distinct front and rear axle assemblies. The front assembly uses two "narrow-rim" wheels, each attached with four lug bolts, while the rear assembly uses two heavier-duty "wide-rim" wheels, each attached with six lug bolts. A key engineering detail is the specific tightening torque required for these bolts, with the front wheels requiring 10 Newton-meters and the rear wheels requiring a higher 20 Newton-meters.

A more detailed configuration specifies the physical mounting of each individual wheel. It shows how the hub of each wheel (left-front, right-front, etc.) is physically connected to a corresponding mounting point on its axle.

The most advanced configuration details the vehicle's powertrain. It introduces a transmission and shows how it connects to the rear axle via a driveshaft. This connection is responsible for transferring drive torque from the engine (through the transmission) to the rear wheels, indicating that this is a rear-wheel-drive vehicle.
