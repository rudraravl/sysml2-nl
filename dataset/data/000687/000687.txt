This model describes the Meril Myval Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR) System, a comprehensive medical device suite designed for minimally invasive heart valve replacement. Its primary purpose is to replace a patient's diseased aortic valve, typically due to severe aortic stenosis, without the need for open-heart surgery.

The system consists of several key components that work together during the procedure. The central element is the **Myval Bioprosthetic Valve**, an artificial heart valve featuring leaflets made from bovine pericardium. This valve is designed to be crimped to a very small diameter so it can be delivered through the body's blood vessels. Once in position, it expands with a specific radial force to anchor itself within the patient's old, diseased valve.

To deliver the valve, the system uses the **Navigator Delivery Catheter**. This is a long, flexible tube that is inserted into the patient, typically through the femoral artery in the leg. It securely holds the crimped valve at its tip, and a physician uses its release handle and a control console to navigate it through the vascular system to the heart and precisely deploy the valve.

The entire procedure is guided by sophisticated imaging and monitoring equipment. A **Fluoroscopy System** provides live X-ray imaging, allowing the medical team to see the catheter and valve as they move through the body. An **Echocardiography System**, using a transesophageal probe, offers detailed, real-time ultrasound images of the heart to ensure perfect positioning and assess the new valve's function. Simultaneously, a **Hemodynamic Monitor** continuously measures critical data like blood pressure gradients and flow to confirm that the new valve is working correctly and to check for any leaks.

All of this information is fed into a central **Control Console**, which serves as the operational hub for the medical team, displaying integrated imaging and vital signs. The procedure follows a series of well-defined phases: preparation of the valve and catheter, gaining vascular access, navigating to the heart, precise positioning of the new valve, controlled deployment, and a final assessment of the valve's performance before the delivery system is withdrawn.
