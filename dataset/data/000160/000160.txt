This model represents a computational simulation of a vehicle's dynamics, designed to calculate its speed and position as it moves in a straight line. It's a digital representation of the physics governing a vehicle's motion.

The simulation is configured with key physical properties of a real-world vehicle, including its total mass, the power being delivered to its wheels, a coefficient for aerodynamic drag, and another for rolling friction.

The system operates by taking the vehicle's current speed and position as a starting point. It then simulates a small step forward in time by first calculating the vehicle's acceleration. This calculation balances the driving force generated by the wheel power against the opposing forces of air resistance and friction. Based on the resulting acceleration, the model calculates the vehicle's new speed. Finally, it determines the new forward position of the vehicle, completing the simulation for that time step. This process can be repeated to model the vehicle's performance over a longer journey.
