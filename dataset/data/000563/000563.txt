This model describes a medical cannula delivery system designed for the precise and controlled administration of medical fluids directly into a patient's biological tissue.

The system is composed of several key components working in unison. A fluid reservoir holds the medical liquid, which is then managed by a flow controller that regulates the delivery rate and maintains safe pressure levels. The fluid path includes a dedicated pressure sensor that continuously monitors the system to prevent tissue damage by triggering an alarm if pressure becomes too high.

The core of the system is the cannula itself, a sterile, needle-like device. It is constructed from medical-grade stainless steel and consists of three main parts: a sharp, angled tip designed for penetrating tissue; a hollow shaft of a specific length and diameter that serves as the fluid conduit; and a standardized "Luer Lock" hub for securely connecting to the fluid lines.

The system operates through a well-defined procedure with distinct phases. It begins in an idle, sterile state. The first phase is preparation, where sterility is verified and the fluid path is primed. Next, the cannula is inserted into the patient's target tissue to a specific depth. Once correctly placed, the delivery phase begins, with the flow controller administering the fluid at a preset rate. The system can enter an alarm state if unsafe pressure is detected, pausing the delivery until the issue is resolved. After the full dose is administered, the procedure concludes with the safe removal and disposal of the cannula. The entire system is designed to meet strict requirements for accuracy, safety, and performance, ensuring the correct volume of fluid is delivered without harming the patient.
