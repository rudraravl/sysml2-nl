This model describes an autonomous deep space probe designed for long-duration scientific missions to explore distant celestial bodies. The probe is engineered to operate independently for extended periods, managing all its critical functions without direct real-time control from Earth.

The probe is composed of several integrated subsystems that work together to achieve the mission objectives:

*   A **Power Subsystem** serves as the spacecraft's heart, generating electricity using large solar panels and storing it in rechargeable batteries. It manages and distributes power to all other onboard systems.
*   The **Communication Subsystem** is its link to Earth. It uses a high-gain antenna to receive commands from mission control and to transmit valuable scientific findings and health status (telemetry) back home.
*   An **Attitude Control Subsystem** is responsible for orientation. Using a combination of reaction wheels and thrusters, it precisely points the probe's scientific instruments at observation targets or aims the communication antenna toward Earth.
*   The **Science Instrument Subsystem** carries the primary payload, in this case, a multispectral imaging spectrometer. This instrument collects detailed scientific data from the target celestial body.
*   A **Data Storage Subsystem** acts as an onboard memory buffer. It stores the large amounts of data collected by the instruments, holding it until a communication window with Earth opens for transmission.
*   The **Navigation Subsystem** functions as the probe's internal GPS, determining its precise position and velocity in space by observing stars and using onboard sensors. It also plans and executes trajectory maneuvers.
*   A **Thermal Control Subsystem** acts as the probe's heating and cooling system, using radiators and heaters to maintain all electronics within their safe operating temperatures in the extreme environment of space.

In its normal operational sequence, the probe autonomously points its instruments at a target, collects scientific data, and stores it. When a communication opportunity arises, it reorients itself to transmit the stored data back to Earth. The system is also designed for resilience; if it detects a critical issue like low power or a system malfunction, it will automatically enter a "safe mode" to conserve energy, stabilize its orientation, and await further instructions from mission control.
