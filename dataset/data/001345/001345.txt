This is an industrial articulated robotic system designed for automated pick-and-place operations within a dedicated work cell.

The core of the system is a six-axis articulated robot arm, constructed from a series of connected links and motorized joints, including a base, shoulder, elbow, and a three-jointed wrist. This structure gives the robot a long reach (1200 mm) and flexible movement. At the end of the arm is a gripper, which can be commanded to open, close, and apply a specific amount of force to handle objects. The robot is engineered for precision, with a positioning accuracy of 0.3 mm, and is capable of lifting payloads up to 12 kg.

This robot arm operates within a physical work cell, which includes a work table and is enclosed by a tall safety fence to protect human workers. A vision system, essentially a camera, is used to scan the workspace, identify workpieces, and determine their precise location and orientation.

A central robot controller acts as the brain of the system. It processes data from the vision system, plans the robot's movements, and sends commands to the arm's joints and the gripper. The controller manages the entire operational sequence, which typically involves moving the arm above a target workpiece, lowering to pick it up, lifting it, moving it to a destination, placing it down, and retracting to a ready position.

The system operates through distinct states, including being idle while waiting for a task, executing a task, and indicating when a task is completed or if an error has occurred. The entire system is designed to be highly efficient, capable of completing a full pick-and-place cycle in 28 seconds.
