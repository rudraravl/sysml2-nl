This model describes an Endobronchial Valve System, a medical device designed for treating severe emphysema by reducing air-trapping (hyperinflation) in diseased portions of the lung.

The core of the system is a small, one-way valve that is implanted into a patient's bronchial airway. This valve is composed of a biocompatible body, a flexible internal membrane that controls airflow, and a self-expanding anchoring mechanism that secures it firmly within the bronchus after placement.

The system's function is tied directly to the patient's natural breathing cycle. During exhalation, the pressure from the diseased lung segment pushes the valve's membrane open, allowing trapped air and secretions to escape. Conversely, during inhalation, the valve closes to create a complete seal, blocking new air from entering and re-inflating that specific lung segment.

By continuously performing this one-way regulation, the system progressively deflates the targeted lung segment. This reduction in volume alleviates pressure on the diaphragm and allows healthier parts of the lung to function more effectively, ultimately improving the patient's overall breathing capacity. The device is designed to achieve a specific therapeutic goal of significant lung volume reduction while meeting strict safety requirements for biocompatibility and structural stability within the airway.
