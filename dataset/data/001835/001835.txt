This model describes a gravitational energy storage system, essentially a mechanical battery designed for grid-scale energy management. The system's core function is to store energy by lifting a heavy mass and then generating electricity by letting it descend in a controlled manner.

The system is composed of four primary components working together. A large, 1000 kg **mass block** serves as the energy storage medium. A powerful **lifting mechanism**, like a crane or winch, is used to hoist this block to a maximum height of 50 meters. This process converts electrical energy from the grid into stored gravitational potential energy in the elevated block.

When electricity is needed, a **release controller** sends a signal to begin the energy generation phase. The mass block is then allowed to descend under gravity. This controlled descent drives a **power converter**, which functions like a generator, transforming the mechanical energy of the falling block back into electrical power. This generated electricity is then fed back into the grid.

The system operates in a clear cycle of states: it is **idle** when the block is at its lowest point, **charging** as the block is being lifted, **charged** when the block is held at its maximum height, and **discharging** as it descends to generate power. This entire process is managed based on operator commands and system status, allowing it to absorb surplus energy and release it on demand.
