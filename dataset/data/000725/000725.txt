This model describes a Palatal Obturator System, a custom-made maxillofacial prosthetic designed for patients with a defect or opening in the roof of their mouth (the palate). This condition, which can be present from birth or result from surgery or trauma, creates an unwanted connection between the oral (mouth) and nasal cavities, severely impacting speech, swallowing, and chewing.

The core of the system is the **Palatal Obturator**, a removable prosthetic device. It is composed of three main parts that work together:
*   An **Obturator Body**, which is the main structural component, custom-shaped to physically fill the specific dimensions of the patient's palatal defect.
*   An **Obturator Bulb**, which is a compressible extension that projects into the defect, creating a tight yet comfortable seal to prevent air, liquids, and food from passing from the mouth into the nasal cavity.
*   A set of **Retention Clasps**, which are small mechanical hooks that securely anchor the entire device to the patient's remaining teeth, ensuring it stays firmly in place during normal functions like talking and eating.

The system's primary purpose is to restore the natural separation between the mouth and nose. The overall process begins with a clinical assessment of the patient's defect. Based on these precise measurements, a custom obturator is fabricated from medical-grade, biocompatible materials. The device is then fitted into the patient's mouth. This is often an iterative process where the seal's effectiveness is evaluated and the device is adjusted until an optimal fit is achieved.

Once properly fitted, the device allows the patient to speak clearly, swallow without nasal regurgitation, and chew food effectively. The system is designed for long-term use, with a lifecycle that includes the initial fabrication and fitting, a functional in-use period, and provisions for periodic maintenance or adjustments if the fit changes over time.
