This model represents a Gardon Gauge, a specialized sensor system designed to precisely measure high-intensity thermal radiation. It is typically used in demanding environments like fire testing, furnace analysis, or aerospace applications where accurate heat flux data is critical.

The core of the system is a sensor assembly that converts the energy from incident heat radiation into a measurable electrical signal. The process begins when a thin, circular metal foil, called the **Absorber Foil**, is exposed to a heat source. This foil absorbs the radiative energy, causing its center to heat up significantly.

The foil is mounted on a **Water-Cooled Heat Sink**, which maintains a constant, cool temperature around the foil's outer edge. This setup creates a sharp and stable temperature difference, or gradient, between the hot center of the foil and its cool perimeter. The flow of heat is controlled by a **Thermal Resistance Element** between these hot and cold points.

This temperature difference is the key to the measurement. A **Thermocouple Junction** is strategically placed to measure this gradient, generating a very small voltage that is directly proportional to the incident heat flux. Because this voltage is so low, a **Signal Amplifier** boosts it to a more usable level. The amplified voltage is then processed by a **Calibration Module**, which uses a specific calibration factor to convert the electrical reading into a precise heat flux value, typically in Watts per square meter. Finally, a **Data Acquisition Unit** digitizes this calibrated signal, timestamps it, and outputs it as a formal measurement.

The entire system operates through a series of states. After an **initialization** period where the water cooling stabilizes, the gauge enters a **standby** mode, ready for a command. When triggered, it moves into a **measuring** state, actively performing the energy conversion and signal processing. If a critical failure occurs, such as a loss of coolant flow, the system will enter a **fault** state to prevent damage.
