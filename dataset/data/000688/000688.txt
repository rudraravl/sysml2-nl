This model describes an intelligent, automated metal corset system designed for therapeutic and structural purposes, such as providing torso support and actively correcting posture.

The core of the system is a physical corset built with a rigid structural frame. This frame contains multiple metal boning elements, made of materials like spring steel, which are distributed around the torso to provide firm support. The corset is fitted and adjusted using an automated lacing mechanism that can tighten or loosen to apply varying levels of compression.

Unlike a traditional passive corset, this is an active, responsive system. It is equipped with a posture sensor that continuously monitors the wearer's spinal alignment and the current circumference of their torso. This information is sent to a central control unit, which acts as the system's brain.

The control unit constantly compares the wearer's live posture data against a pre-set ideal posture angle. If the system detects that the wearer is slouching or their posture deviates from the target, the control unit calculates the necessary correction. It then sends a command to a compression actuator, which automatically adjusts the tension in the lacing mechanism. This change in compression provides physical support and feedback, guiding the wearer back to the correct posture. This entire process of sensing, calculating, and adjusting runs in a continuous loop, ensuring dynamic and responsive postural support.

The system is designed to be both safe and adaptable, with configurable limits on the maximum compression force and an adjustment range to accommodate different body types and therapeutic requirements.
