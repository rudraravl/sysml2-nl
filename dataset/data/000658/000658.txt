This describes a reusable kidney dish system designed for use in medical and surgical settings. The central component is the kidney dish itself, a shallow, kidney-shaped basin made from a material like medical-grade stainless steel, which is designed to hold a specific volume of contents without spilling.

The primary purpose of the system is to provide a sterile, temporary container for medical instruments, biological fluids, and tissue specimens during a procedure. It operates in conjunction with a medical procedure station (like an operating table), which is the source of the instruments and biological materials placed into the dish.

The kidney dish follows a distinct lifecycle to ensure safety and sterility. It begins in a sterile state, ready for use. During a procedure, it enters an "in-use" state where it actively receives and holds materials. The system monitors its volume to ensure it doesn't get overfilled. Once the procedure is complete, the dish and its contents are considered contaminated. The contents are then transferred to a dedicated waste disposal unit.

To be reused, the contaminated dish undergoes a cleaning and sterilization process. It is processed by a sterilization unit, such as an autoclave, which uses high temperature over a set duration to ensure it is completely sterile before it can be used in another procedure. This cyclical process of use, disposal of contents, and sterilization allows the kidney dish to be safely reused while maintaining aseptic conditions for patient care.
