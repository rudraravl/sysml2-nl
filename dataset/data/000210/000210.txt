This model describes a vehicle system, such as a modern car, which includes an integrated electronic control unit. The system's behavior is managed through two parallel processes: one for its operational status and another for its health monitoring.

The vehicle's operational cycle consists of "off," "starting," and "on" states. To start the vehicle, a start signal must be received while the brake pedal is depressed. This action triggers a "start" signal to be sent to the internal controller. Once fully on, the vehicle performs a self-test and then enters its main function of providing power, a process that is influenced by an external fuel command. When the vehicle is turned off, it automatically applies the parking brake before shutting down.

Simultaneously, the system continuously monitors its health. It is always sensing its temperature. Under normal conditions, it operates as expected. However, if the temperature rises above a predefined maximum limit, the system enters a "degraded" state and sends an "over temperature" alert to its internal controller. The system is designed to return to normal operation once the issue is resolved. Additionally, the system is programmed to enter a "maintenance" state at a specific, scheduled time, requiring a "return to normal" signal to resume standard operation.
