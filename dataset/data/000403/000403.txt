This model describes a pneumatically-powered, articulated soft robotic arm system designed for safely handling and manipulating delicate objects. Its core purpose is to perform "pick and place" tasks in environments where rigid, traditional robots might cause damage.

The system's main component is a flexible, multi-segmented arm. This arm is constructed from three distinct soft actuator segments: a base, a middle segment, and a tip. Unlike a traditional robot with rigid joints, these segments are made of a compliant, elastic material that bends and flexes when inflated with compressed air. By precisely controlling the air pressure in each segment, the arm can be steered and positioned with a high degree of freedom. Each segment is equipped with internal pressure and bending sensors to provide feedback on its current state and position.

At the end of the arm is a three-fingered soft gripper. Similar to the arm segments, the fingers are soft actuators that curl inward to grasp an object when inflated. This design allows for a gentle, compliant grip that conforms to the shape of the object. A force sensor is integrated into the gripper to monitor the gripping force, ensuring it does not crush the item being held. The gripper can operate in several states, including open, closing, gripping, and releasing.

The entire system is powered and controlled by two main units. A Pneumatic Control Unit houses an air compressor and a series of valves to generate and precisely regulate the airflow sent to the arm and gripper. This unit manages the inflation, deflation, and pressure-holding states for all the soft actuators. A high-level Motion Controller acts as the brain of the system. It calculates the complex movements required to reach specific points in space (inverse kinematics) and translates these calculations into commands for the pneumatic unit and the gripper, orchestrating the entire pick and place operation from start to finish.
