This model describes a liquid-propellant propulsion system for a spacecraft, essentially a rocket engine designed for in-space maneuvers. Its primary purpose is to generate controlled thrust to adjust the spacecraft's orbit, correct its trajectory, or maintain its position over time.

The system is composed of several key physical components that work together to produce thrust. At its heart is a **Propellant Tank**, which stores 800 kg of propellant under pressure. When the engine needs to fire, a **Propellant Feed System** draws fuel from the tank and pumps it at a high, regulated pressure into the **Combustion Chamber**.

Inside the combustion chamber, the propellant is ignited and burned at extremely high temperatures (around 3400°C) and pressures. This process converts the liquid propellant into a high-energy exhaust gas. This gas is then directed into a specially shaped **Nozzle**, which is designed to accelerate the gas to very high speeds (over 4,200 meters per second). According to Newton's third law, this rapid expulsion of gas out the back of the nozzle creates an equal and opposite force—thrust—that pushes the spacecraft forward.

The entire operation is managed by a **Thrust Controller**. This is the "brain" of the engine, receiving commands from the main spacecraft computer that specify the desired amount of thrust and how long to fire. The controller manages the engine's ignition, regulates the flow of propellant to maintain a steady thrust output, and monitors critical performance data like chamber pressure, temperature, and the amount of propellant remaining. It sends this information back to the spacecraft as telemetry data.

The system's operational lifecycle includes safely starting the engine, maintaining steady thrust, and performing a controlled shutdown. It is also designed with emergency shutdown procedures in case of an anomaly. Overall, this propulsion system is engineered for reliability and efficiency, providing the spacecraft with the necessary force to successfully carry out its mission objectives.
