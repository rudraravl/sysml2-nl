This model represents a foundational framework for defining and organizing physical quantities, much like the International System of Quantities (ISQ) used in science and engineering. Its purpose is to create a consistent and logical structure for all types of measurements, ensuring that concepts like length, mass, time, and more complex ones like velocity and acceleration are understood and related to each other correctly.

At its core, the system is built upon a set of fundamental or "base" quantities, such as Length (L), Mass (M), and Time (T). These are the irreducible building blocks of measurement.

From these base quantities, more complex "derived" quantities can be constructed. For instance, acceleration is not a fundamental quantity but is derived by combining Length and Time (specifically, length divided by time squared, or L¹⋅T⁻²). This method of construction gives every quantity a unique "dimension," which is essentially its recipe of base quantities and their powers.

The system is also sophisticated enough to handle "dimensionless" quantities, which are ratios where the fundamental dimensions cancel out. This allows it to distinguish between different kinds of dimensionless values, such as an angle (a ratio of lengths) and a Mach number (a ratio of speeds), even though both might appear as simple numbers.

While this framework is modeled after the standard ISQ, it is flexible, allowing for the creation of custom systems of quantities for specialized domains or different physical models.
