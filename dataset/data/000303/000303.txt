This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of physical quantities and units specifically for the domain of atomic and nuclear physics. It functions as a formal, digital dictionary based on the international standard ISO 80000-10, providing precise, unambiguous definitions for use in scientific and engineering models and simulations.

The library contains a wide array of fundamental concepts needed to describe the subatomic world. This includes basic properties of atoms and nuclei, such as atomic number (number of protons), nucleon number (protons plus neutrons), atomic mass, and rest energy. It defines key quantum mechanical properties like spin, various quantum numbers (principal, orbital, magnetic), and total angular momentum.

Furthermore, the library covers the physics of radioactivity and radiation. It provides formal definitions for quantities like decay constant, half-life, nuclear activity (measured in becquerels), and the different energies associated with alpha and beta disintegration. It also details how radiation interacts with matter, defining concepts like cross-section (the effective target area for a nuclear reaction), particle fluence, linear energy transfer (LET), and the mean free path of particles.

For applications in radiation protection and dosimetry, the system defines critical quantities such as absorbed dose (measured in grays), kerma, dose equivalent (measured in sieverts), and the quality factor used to weigh the biological effectiveness of different types of radiation. Each quantity is defined with its scientific symbol, its relationship to other quantities, its fundamental dimensions (in terms of mass, length, time, etc.), and its standard measurement units.
