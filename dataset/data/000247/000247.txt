This model represents a structured library of engineering materials, designed to classify and define substances based on their composition and physical properties.

The system establishes a basic classification that distinguishes between pure metals and more complex alloys. Pure metals, such as Iron, Carbon, and Manganese, are characterized by their fundamental atomic mass. Alloys, on the other hand, are defined as composite materials, and their exact makeup is specified by the mass fraction of each constituent element.

A specific, practical example is provided for a high-tensile strength steel, "Steel_980". This material is defined as an alloy composed primarily of Iron (98.6%), with small but critical amounts of Manganese (1.2%) and Carbon (0.18%). A key performance characteristic is also captured: this specific steel has a defined tensile strength of 980 Newtons per square millimeter, which measures its resistance to being pulled apart.

Overall, this represents a knowledge base for materials, allowing for the precise definition of substances by their type, elemental composition, and key mechanical properties.
