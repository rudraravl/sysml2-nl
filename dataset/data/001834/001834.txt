This model describes a fully autonomous, solar-powered ocean cleanup vessel designed for long-duration missions at sea. The system operates as a self-sufficient floating platform that actively patrols designated areas of the ocean to find, collect, and process plastic pollution without human intervention.

The vessel's operation is powered entirely by a large array of solar panels, which charge an onboard battery system. This ensures continuous functionality, allowing it to operate through the night and during periods of low sunlight. The stored energy powers all onboard systems, including propulsion, sensors, and processing equipment.

To locate plastic waste, the vessel is equipped with a sophisticated detection system that uses a combination of optical sensors and sonar arrays to identify debris on the surface and just below it. When a significant concentration of plastic is found, the vessel's navigation controller autonomously plots a course to intercept it.

A wide collection mechanism at the front of the vessel, featuring conveyor belts and filtering screens, scoops up the plastic debris from the water. This collected waste is then fed into an internal processing unit, which first shreds the plastic into smaller pieces and then compresses it into dense, space-efficient blocks. These blocks are stored in a high-capacity onboard container.

The vessel operates through a series of intelligent states. It patrols until plastic is detected, at which point it enters a collection and processing mode. It constantly monitors its power levels and will enter a power-conservation state if necessary. Once its storage is full, it autonomously navigates to a designated location to offload the compressed plastic before returning to its mission. A communications module allows the vessel to regularly transmit its status, location, and the amount of plastic collected back to a central command.
