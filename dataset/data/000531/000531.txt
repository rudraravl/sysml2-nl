This model describes the Argus Retinal Prosthesis System, a "bionic eye" designed to restore a degree of functional vision to people who have become blind due to degenerative retinal diseases like retinitis pigmentosa. The system works by bypassing the damaged photoreceptor cells in the eye and directly stimulating the remaining retinal nerve cells with electrical pulses, which the brain interprets as light.

The system is comprised of two main subsystems: external components worn by the patient and internal components that are surgically implanted.

The external portion consists of a pair of eyeglasses equipped with a miniature video camera. This camera captures a live video feed of the user's surroundings. The feed is sent via a cable to a small, wearable Video Processing Unit (VPU). This VPU is the "brain" of the system; it processes the video in real-time and converts it into a customized set of stimulation instructions. These instructions, along with power for the entire system, are then sent wirelessly from a transmitter coil mounted on the glasses.

The implanted portion includes a receiver coil that picks up the wireless data and power signals from the external transmitter. This receiver passes the instructions to an electronics case containing a stimulator. The stimulator generates precise electrical pulses based on the received instructions. These pulses are delivered to a 6x10 array of 60 tiny electrodes that is surgically implanted and secured directly onto the surface of the patient's retina.

In operation, the electrodes deliver these small electrical pulses to the retina's viable nerve cells. These cells then transmit the signals through the optic nerve to the brain. The brain perceives these signals as patterns of light, allowing the user to discern shapes, movement, and contrast, effectively restoring a form of vision.
