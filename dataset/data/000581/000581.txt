This model describes an Automated External Defibrillator (AED), a portable medical device designed to treat patients experiencing sudden cardiac arrest. Its primary function is to automatically analyze a patient's heart rhythm and, if a life-threatening arrhythmia is detected, deliver a controlled electrical shock to restore a normal heartbeat.

The system is comprised of several key integrated components. A central **Microcontroller Unit** acts as the brain, orchestrating the entire life-saving sequence. Power is supplied by a high-capacity **Battery Module**, which is robust enough to deliver numerous shocks or provide hours of continuous patient monitoring.

When in use, disposable **Defibrillation Electrodes** are attached to the patient's chest. These pads serve a dual purpose: they sense the heart's electrical signal (ECG) and deliver the therapeutic shock. The captured ECG signal is fed to an **ECG Analysis Module**, which uses a sophisticated algorithm to determine if the heart rhythm is "shockable," such as in cases of ventricular fibrillation.

If a shock is advised, the microcontroller instructs the battery to charge a **High-Voltage Capacitor**, which rapidly stores the precise amount of energy needed for the therapy. The device alerts the operator that it is charging and to stand clear. Once charged, a **Therapy Delivery Circuit** takes over. This circuit measures the patient's body impedance through the electrodes and tailors the electrical waveform of the shock for maximum effectiveness and safety.

The entire process is guided by an **Operator Interface**, which provides clear, step-by-step voice prompts and visual instructions on a display screen. This interface is designed for use by untrained bystanders, telling them when to apply the pads, when to stand clear, and when to press the button to deliver the shock. After a shock is delivered, the system immediately re-analyzes the patient's heart rhythm to assess the response and determine if further shocks are necessary, continuing to guide the user until professional help arrives.
