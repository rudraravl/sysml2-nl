This model describes an automated liquid cooling system designed for managing the temperature of a heat-generating component, such as a computer processor or an industrial electronic device. Its purpose is to actively transfer thermal energy from the component and dissipate it into the surrounding air, ensuring the component remains within its optimal operating temperature range.

The system operates as a closed loop. A **water block** makes direct contact with the heat source to efficiently absorb its thermal energy into a liquid coolant. A **pump** circulates this now-heated coolant away from the component and towards a **radiator**. The radiator, which has a large surface area, is paired with one or more **fans** that force ambient air across it, transferring the heat from the coolant into the air. The cooled liquid then flows through a **reservoir**, which holds a reserve supply of coolant, before returning to the pump to begin the cycle again.

The intelligence of the system lies in its **controller**, which acts as the brain. It uses a **temperature sensor** and a **flow sensor** to get real-time data on the system's performance. Based on a pre-defined target temperature, the controller dynamically adjusts the speed of both the pump and the fans. If the component gets hotter, the controller can increase the pump and fan speeds to provide more cooling power.

The system is designed with multiple operational states, including standby, active cooling, and a protective fault mode. It can automatically detect critical issues, such as the coolant level in the reservoir dropping too low or the temperature rising above safe limits, and enter a fault state to prevent damage to the component it is cooling.
