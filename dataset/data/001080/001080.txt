This model describes a compact, electronic Heartbeat Sensing Unit, likely designed for integration into wearable devices like fitness trackers or medical monitors. Its primary function is to accurately measure a person's heart rate and assess the quality of the signal it receives.

The unit is composed of three main components that work in sequence. First, an optical (PPG) sensor uses green light to detect the user's pulse by measuring changes in blood flow through the skin. This sensor generates a raw, continuous pulse signal.

This raw signal is then sent to an internal Signal Processor, which acts as the brain of the unit. The processor's job is to clean up the signal by filtering out noise and motion artifacts. It then analyzes the clean signal to identify individual heartbeats, calculates the rate in beats per minute (BPM), and evaluates the signal's strength to determine if the measurement is reliable and valid.

The final, validated heart rate measurement is then available at an output port for another system (like a smartwatch display) to use, and is also temporarily held in a small data buffer.

The entire unit operates through a clear lifecycle: it powers on and initializes, enters a low-power ready state, actively measures when prompted, transmits the data, and then returns to the ready state. It also includes a fault state to handle any errors. Key performance requirements for the unit demand that it measures heart rates between 40 and 200 BPM with an accuracy of ±2 BPM, and it must provide an updated reading within two seconds.
