This model represents a large-scale electric power transmission system, designed to efficiently and reliably move electricity from a power generation plant to a local distribution network over a significant distance.

The system begins at a power plant which generates 800 megawatts of electricity at a frequency of 60 Hz. This power is initially generated at a medium voltage of 22,000 volts.

To prepare the electricity for long-distance travel, it immediately flows into a step-up transformer. This component dramatically increases the voltage from 22,000 volts to a high-voltage level of 345,000 volts. This is crucial for minimizing energy loss over long distances.

The high-voltage electricity then travels across a 250-kilometer high-voltage transmission line to its destination. During this transit, a small amount of power is naturally lost due to the line's electrical resistance.

At the end of the transmission line, a step-down transformer receives the power and reduces the voltage from 345,000 volts back down to a more manageable 34,500 volts. Finally, this power arrives at a distribution substation, which then supplies it to local communities and industries through eight separate feeder lines.

Overseeing the entire operation is a protection system. It continuously monitors the electrical grid's voltage and current at a key point. If it detects a dangerous condition like a short circuit (a fault), it can react very quickly—within 80 milliseconds—to isolate the problem, prevent damage, and maintain stability in the rest of the grid. The system's operational state can shift from normal operation to a fault isolation mode when a problem is detected, and it returns to normal once the fault is cleared.
