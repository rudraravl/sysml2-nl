This model represents a comprehensive Medical Device Connectivity System designed to operate within a hospital environment. Its primary purpose is to automatically and wirelessly collect real-time patient data and alarms from various bedside medical devices and securely deliver this information to the central Hospital Information System (HIS).

The system consists of three main components: Medical Devices, a Connectivity Gateway, and the Hospital Information System.

**Medical Devices** are the patient-side equipment, such as ventilators, patient monitors, and infusion pumps. Each device is equipped with a sensor module for acquiring patient data (like heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation), a processing unit, a power management system for battery operation, and a wireless communication module to transmit its data.

The **Connectivity Gateway** acts as a central hub, managing wireless connections from multiple medical devices simultaneously. It is designed to handle at least 20 devices. The gateway's key functions are to discover and securely authenticate devices, receive and aggregate the incoming streams of vital signs and alarm messages, and then forward this consolidated data over a wired network. It includes a security module to encrypt all data, ensuring patient information is protected.

The **Hospital Information System (HIS)** is the back-end IT infrastructure. It receives the validated and aggregated data from the gateway and ingests it into its central data repository, effectively updating patient records in real-time. This system provides a user interface for clinicians to view patient data, a clinical decision support system to analyze data and flag potential issues, and an audit log for compliance.

In operation, a medical device goes through a connection lifecycle that includes discovery, authentication, and then an operational state for transmitting data. The system is built for high reliability, actively managing the connectivity of each device. If a connection weakens, it can adjust to a degraded mode; if it fails, it will attempt to recover the connection automatically. This ensures a continuous and reliable flow of critical patient data from the bedside to the clinical staff, supporting timely decision-making and patient care.
