This model describes a complete peristaltic pump system, designed to accurately transfer fluid from a source reservoir to a delivery line. This type of pump is often used in medical or laboratory settings where sterility is critical, as the fluid never touches the pump's mechanical components.

The core of the system is a flexible, elastic tube through which the fluid passes. This tube is housed within the main pump assembly. A central rotor, driven by an electric motor, is equipped with three rollers. As the rotor turns, these rollers sequentially squeeze and release the flexible tube, creating a wave-like motion (peristalsis) that pushes pockets of fluid forward from the inlet to the outlet.

The system is managed by an electronic pump controller, which acts as the brain. It regulates the flow rate by adjusting the speed of the drive motor based on user settings and real-time sensor feedback. To ensure accurate and safe operation, the system includes pressure sensors at both the inlet and outlet, as well as a flow rate sensor at the outlet. This allows the controller to precisely manage the pumping process and to detect critical issues like a downstream blockage (occlusion), which would cause a spike in pressure.

The pump operates in several modes, including an idle state, a priming sequence to fill the tube with fluid, a continuous running mode, a reversing mode to change the direction of flow, and a faulted state that stops the pump for safety if an occlusion is detected. The entire system includes the pump itself, a source reservoir to hold the fluid, and a delivery line to carry the fluid to its destination.
