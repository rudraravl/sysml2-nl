This model represents a system designed for managing the live, in-place technological upgrade of a critical data processing infrastructure, such as a telecommunications network core or a large-scale cloud computing facility. Its primary purpose is to seamlessly migrate operations from an older, less efficient "legacy" system to a new, high-performance "modern" system without any service downtime.

The system is composed of two main processing units: a **Legacy Processor**, characterized by lower capacity, higher power consumption, and greater delay, and a **Modern Processor**, which offers significantly higher throughput, better energy efficiency, lower latency, and advanced capabilities like AI.

The entire upgrade is orchestrated by a central **TransitionController**. This component acts as the brain of the operation, managing the migration through a series of distinct phases: assessment, planning, implementation, validation, and optimization. It intelligently controls both processing units to ensure a smooth handover.

During the transition, a **Data Router** directs incoming data traffic. It can send all traffic to the legacy system, split it between both systems in a hybrid mode, or eventually direct all traffic to the fully operational modern system. This dynamic routing is crucial for maintaining continuous service.

Supporting this process are two key subsystems. A **Power Management Unit** balances the electrical load, distributing power to both the legacy and modern components and ensuring the facility's total power capacity is never exceeded. A **Monitoring Subsystem** continuously gathers performance data from both processors, providing real-time feedback on efficiency, errors, and throughput, which the Transition Controller uses to validate the success of each phase and make adjustments if necessary.

The system is designed to operate in different modes, starting in a fully `legacy` state, moving through a `hybrid` state where both processors run in parallel, and culminating in a `fullModern` state. This phased approach allows the modern system to be gradually brought online and tested while the legacy system is carefully phased out, guaranteeing operational continuity throughout the entire upgrade lifecycle.
