This is a specialized medical mechanical ventilator designed to prevent atelectotrauma, a type of lung injury caused by the repetitive collapse and reopening of the lung's small air sacs (alveoli) during breathing support. The system's primary goal is to protect the patient's lungs by intelligently managing ventilation.

The system is built around a central control processor that acts as its brain. It continuously receives real-time data from pressure and volume sensors that monitor the patient's lung mechanics, measuring things like airway pressure and the volume of each breath.

Based on this sensor data, the control processor analyzes the stability of the patient's alveoli. If it detects conditions that could lead to their collapse, it automatically calculates the optimal "positive end-expiratory pressure" (PEEP) required to keep them safely open. It then sends new commands to the ventilator's gas delivery unit (the actuator), which adjusts the flow of air and oxygen to the patient. This creates a closed-loop feedback system that dynamically adapts to the patient's changing condition to maintain lung recruitment.

For safety, the system operates within defined pressure and volume limits to prevent other forms of lung injury. An integrated alarm system immediately alerts medical staff if it detects a high risk of atelectotrauma or if critical parameters go outside of safe ranges. The ventilator operates through distinct states in a breathing cycle, including inspiration (delivering a breath), expiration (allowing exhalation), and a crucial PEEP maintenance phase to ensure the lungs remain properly inflated between breaths.
