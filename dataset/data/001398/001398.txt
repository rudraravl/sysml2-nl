This model represents an automotive differential system, a mechanical component in a vehicle's drivetrain. Its primary purpose is to take rotational power from the engine via a single driveshaft and distribute it to two drive wheels, crucially allowing them to rotate at different speeds.

The system is composed of a central differential gear set that connects an input driveshaft to two output axles, one for the left wheel and one for the right. When the vehicle is driving in a straight line, the differential distributes engine torque evenly, causing both wheels to spin at the same speed.

The key function of the system becomes apparent during a turn. When cornering, the outer wheel must travel a longer distance than the inner wheel and therefore needs to spin faster. The differential's gear assembly automatically accommodates this by allowing the rotational speeds of the left and right wheels to differ while still delivering power to both. The system's behavior is managed through different operating states: an idle state when no power is applied, a straight-driving state for equal speed rotation, and a cornering state for differential speed rotation. The design must also adhere to strict requirements for structural integrity, ensuring all components can withstand the maximum rated engine torque without failing.
