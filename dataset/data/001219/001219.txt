This is a Radio Direction Finder (RDF) system designed to locate the source of a radio frequency (RF) signal by determining its direction, or bearing. The system is built around a core principle known as phase interferometry, which calculates the direction of a signal based on the minute differences in its arrival time at multiple antennas.

The physical system consists of an antenna array, typically with four elements arranged in a known circular pattern. Each of these antenna elements is connected to its own dedicated RF receiver. The receivers are tuned to a specific target frequency to listen for a signal of interest.

The signals from all four receivers are passed to a central signal processor, which is the brain of the operation. This processor continuously compares the phase of the incoming signals from each antenna. By analyzing these phase differences, along with the known spacing of the antennas, it calculates the bearing to the signal's origin and assigns a confidence level to the result.

The final bearing information is sent to a display unit, which presents the direction to an operator, likely on a polar plot. The entire process is managed by a control unit that allows an operator to set the target frequency and manage the system's operational mode.

The system operates in several states, including `powerOff`, a `standby` mode for self-testing, and a `ready` state. When active, it enters a `tracking` mode, where it continuously cycles through scanning for signals, processing them to find a bearing, and updating the display. This allows for real-time tracking of a radio source. The system is designed to be highly accurate, operate across a wide frequency spectrum (HF to UHF), and provide rapid updates on the target's bearing.
