This model describes an Artificial Urinary Sphincter (AUS), an implantable medical device designed to treat severe urinary incontinence. Its purpose is to restore urinary control by providing a mechanical means to close the urethra, preventing leakage, while also allowing the patient to urinate voluntarily.

The system is composed of several key components that work together using a sterile saline fluid circuit. The central component is an **Inflatable Cuff**, which is surgically placed around the urethra. When filled with saline, this cuff gently squeezes the urethra closed, maintaining continence.

A small **Fluid Reservoir**, typically a pressure-regulating balloon, stores the saline solution when it's not in the cuff. A **Control Pump** is responsible for actively moving the saline from the reservoir into the cuff to inflate it. Conversely, a **Release Valve** opens to allow the fluid to flow back from the cuff into the reservoir, deflating the cuff.

The entire system is managed by an electronic **Control Module**, which acts as the brain. It receives input from a **Pressure Sensor** that constantly monitors the pressure inside the cuff to ensure it's at a safe and effective level. The patient interacts with the system via an **Activation Button**, a small pump-like device implanted under the skin (e.g., in the scrotum) that they can easily press.

In its normal, resting state, the cuff is inflated, keeping the urethra closed. When the patient wishes to urinate, they press the Activation Button. This signal instructs the Control Module to open the Release Valve, causing the cuff to deflate and allowing urine to flow. After a pre-set duration sufficient for voiding, the system automatically reactivates. The Control Module commands the pump to reinflate the cuff, returning the system to its closed state to prevent any further leakage.
