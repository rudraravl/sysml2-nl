This is an energy-recovering fluid pumping system. Its core purpose is to move a fluid from a low-pressure source to a high-pressure destination while simultaneously recovering energy from a separate high-pressure fluid stream to reduce overall power consumption.

The system is built around a clever pairing of two main components: a conventional pump and a special "Pump-as-Turbine" (PAT). The PAT is essentially a pump running in reverse; instead of consuming power to build pressure, it uses a drop in fluid pressure to spin its shaft and generate mechanical power.

Here's how it works together: A high-pressure reservoir supplies fluid to the PAT. As the fluid flows through the PAT, its pressure drops, and the energy released is converted into rotational mechanical power. This lower-pressure fluid is then discharged into a low-pressure reservoir. The mechanical power generated by the PAT is transferred through a direct mechanical coupling to the main pump. This pump draws fluid from the low-pressure reservoir and, using the power supplied by the PAT, boosts it to the high pressure required at the final discharge point.

The system also includes various sensors to monitor pressure and flow rates, and a controller to manage its operation. Both the pump and the turbine operate through a series of states, including idle, starting, stopping, and a main operating state which is further divided into normal, high-flow, and low-flow modes to adapt to varying conditions.

In essence, this is a highly efficient hydraulic system designed to perform a pumping task by harnessing energy from a pressure reduction process that might otherwise be wasted, thereby significantly lowering its net energy usage.
