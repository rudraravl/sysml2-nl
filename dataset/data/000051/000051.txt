This model represents a generic system by separating its logical, or functional, architecture from its physical, hardware-based architecture.

The system's logical side defines its functions and includes a specific 'component' responsible for a particular task. Think of this as a piece of software or a defined capability, like a data processing algorithm.

The physical side represents the tangible hardware. It is structured with a main 'assembly' that, in turn, contains a smaller 'element'. This could be analogous to a computer's main circuit board (the assembly) which holds a specific processor chip (the element).

The central purpose of this model is to show how these two views are connected. The logical 'component' is explicitly assigned to be carried out by the physical 'element'. This means the specific function is officially hosted on and executed by that specific piece of hardware. On a larger scale, the entire logical system is mapped to the overall physical system, indicating that the hardware is the platform that brings all the defined functions to life.
