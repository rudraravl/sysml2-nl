This is a medical device, the Rightspot pH Indicator system, designed to verify the correct placement of nasogastric feeding tubes in a patient. Its primary function is to prevent life-threatening complications that can occur if a feeding tube is mistakenly placed in the respiratory tract instead of the stomach. The system operates by measuring the pH level of a fluid sample (aspirate) drawn from the tube to confirm it is in the acidic environment of the stomach.

The device is composed of several key components working in sequence:

*   An electrochemical **pH Sensor Probe** is the part that directly interacts with the patient's aspirate sample. It takes an initial, raw measurement of both the pH and the temperature of the fluid.
*   A **Signal Processor** receives the raw data from the sensor. It refines this information by applying calibration formulas and compensating for temperature variations to produce a more accurate pH value.
*   A **Placement Analyzer** then interprets this corrected pH reading. It uses a critical threshold (a pH of 5.5 or lower) to determine if the tube placement is **gastric** (correctly in the stomach), **non-gastric**, or **indeterminate**. The analyzer also assigns a confidence level to its conclusion.
*   The final results, including the precise pH value, the placement status, and the confidence score, are shown to the healthcare professional on a clear **Visual Display**.
*   A central **Control Unit** orchestrates the entire process. It manages the system's operational states, moving from idle to ready when a sample is introduced, then through the measuring and analysis phases, and finally to displaying the result before resetting for the next use. The system also includes an error state to handle any malfunctions safely.

In essence, a clinician introduces a fluid sample from the feeding tube into the device, and within seconds, the system provides a clear, data-backed confirmation of whether the tube is safely positioned in the stomach.
