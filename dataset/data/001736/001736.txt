This model describes a Roots-type supercharger system designed to be fitted to an internal combustion engine to increase its power output. It works by mechanically forcing more air into the engine's cylinders than would be possible under normal atmospheric pressure.

The core of the system is the supercharger unit, which is mechanically driven by the engine's crankshaft through a belt and pulley system. This direct connection means the supercharger's speed is always proportional to the engine's speed, providing immediate boost without lag.

Inside the supercharger's aluminum casing is a pair of precisely engineered rotors with interlocking lobes (typically a three-lobe "male" rotor and a two-lobe "female" rotor). As these rotors spin in opposite directions, they don't compress the air internally. Instead, they trap pockets of air at the inlet and displace them, moving them to the outlet side. The actual compression happens when this displaced air is forced into the higher-pressure intake manifold of the engine.

This compression process heats the air, so the system includes an intercooler. This component acts as a heat exchanger, cooling the hot, compressed air before it enters the engine. This makes the air denser, allowing for a more powerful combustion. To protect the engine from excessive pressure, a bypass valve is included. If the boost level exceeds a safe, predetermined limit, this valve opens to vent the excess pressure.

The system operates in several modes depending on the engine's state. It is idle when the engine is off, begins spooling up as engine RPMs increase, and enters a "boosting" state when actively forcing air into the engine. If the pressure gets too high, it will enter a "bypass" mode to regulate it before returning to its normal boosting operation.
