This model describes a classic siphon system used to transfer fluid from a high-elevation reservoir to a lower one without any mechanical pumps. The system relies entirely on gravitational potential energy and atmospheric pressure to function.

The physical setup consists of three main components: an upper reservoir that holds the initial supply of fluid, a lower reservoir to collect the transferred fluid, and a simple bent tube that connects them. The tube is positioned so that it arches up and over the edge of the upper reservoir, with its highest point (the apex) being above the fluid's surface, before descending into the lower reservoir.

The system's operation involves several distinct phases. It starts in an idle state. To begin the transfer, the siphon must be "primed," which involves completely filling the connecting tube with fluid to remove all air. Once successfully primed, the pressure difference created by the column of fluid in the tube's descending leg initiates a continuous, gravity-driven flow.

While flowing, the system continuously calculates the flow rate based on the height difference between the two reservoirs and the tube's diameter. As fluid is transferred, the level in the upper reservoir decreases while the level in the lower reservoir rises. The system will automatically stop if the conditions for siphoning are no longer met—for example, if the fluid level in the upper reservoir drops below the tube's intake or if the height difference becomes zero. The transfer can also be stopped manually.
