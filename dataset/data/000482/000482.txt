This model describes a portable Organ Care System, a sophisticated medical device designed to keep a donor organ viable outside the human body, typically during transport from a donor to a recipient. Its primary function is to extend the preservation time and improve the health of the organ compared to traditional static cold storage.

The system works by actively perfusing the organ, meaning it continuously circulates a specially formulated, nutrient-rich preservation solution through the organ's own blood vessels. This process mimics the body's natural functions to provide oxygen and maintain a stable environment.

At the heart of the system is a **Perfusion Chamber**, which securely houses the donor organ. A **Circulation Pump** draws the preservation solution from a **Fluid Reservoir** and pushes it through a closed-loop circuit. Before the solution reaches the organ, it passes through two key modules: an **Oxygenation Module**, which acts like an artificial lung to dissolve a precise amount of oxygen into the fluid, and a **Thermal Regulator**, which chills the solution to a specific hypothermic temperature. This cooling slows the organ's metabolic rate, reducing its need for oxygen and protecting it from damage.

An integrated **BioSensor Array** continuously samples the solution to monitor critical health indicators such as temperature, pressure, flow rate, oxygen levels, and lactate levels (a key marker of metabolic stress). This data is fed to a central **Control Processor**, the brain of the system. The processor analyzes the sensor readings and automatically adjusts the pump speed and the thermal regulator's output to keep all parameters within a predefined safe range.

A **User Interface** provides medical personnel with real-time status updates on the organ's condition and the system's performance. The system operates through a series of automated states, including a 'priming' phase to prepare the circuit, an 'active perfusion' phase to sustain the organ, and a 'monitoring' state for stable transport. If any parameter goes outside its target range, the system enters an 'alarm' state to alert the user and attempt to correct the issue automatically.
