This model describes a high-pressure hydraulic pumping subsystem, designed to generate and deliver a controlled flow of pressurized hydraulic fluid for use in industrial machinery or other hydraulic systems.

The core of the subsystem is a radial piston pump, which is driven by an electric motor. This specific pump contains seven pistons arranged radially around a central drive shaft. As the motor turns the shaft, an eccentric cam ring causes each piston to reciprocate within its cylinder. This motion creates a pumping action: on the intake stroke, a piston draws low-pressure hydraulic fluid from a reservoir; on the compression stroke, it pressurizes the fluid and expels it at high pressure. Each cylinder has its own set of one-way check valves to control the flow of fluid in and out.

The subsystem includes all the necessary supporting components for operation and safety. A fluid reservoir holds the supply of hydraulic oil. On the output side of the pump, an accumulator vessel is used to store pressurized fluid, which helps to dampen the pressure pulses created by the individual piston strokes and provides a smoother, more consistent flow.

For safety and monitoring, the system is equipped with a relief valve that automatically opens to divert fluid back to the reservoir if the system pressure exceeds a safe limit. A suite of sensors, including a pressure transducer, a flow meter, and a temperature sensor, continuously monitors the system's vital signs.

The entire subsystem operates through a sequence of states, including startup, priming (to ensure the pump is full of fluid), normal operation, and shutdown. It is also designed to detect fault conditions such as overpressure, high temperature, or cavitation (the formation of vapor bubbles in the fluid) and enter a safe fault state.
