This describes a Word catheter treatment system, a medical device designed for the treatment of Bartholin gland cysts and abscesses. The primary goal of the system is to drain the abscess and create a permanent drainage channel (a tract) to prevent the condition from recurring.

The core of the system is the Word catheter, a small, flexible tube made of medical-grade silicone. The catheter has two main functional parts:

1.  **An Inflatable Bulb:** Located at the tip that is inserted into the abscess cavity. After insertion, this small bulb is inflated with a saline solution using a syringe. Once inflated, it acts as an anchor, securely holding the catheter in place for the duration of the treatment, which typically lasts several weeks.
2.  **A Drainage Lumen:** This is an internal channel running the length of the catheter. It allows fluid, such as pus and exudate, from the abscess to continuously drain out of the body and into a collection reservoir.

The complete system includes the catheter itself, an inflation syringe for filling the bulb, and a collection reservoir for gathering the drained fluid.

The treatment process involves a healthcare professional making a small incision, inserting the catheter, and then inflating the bulb to secure it. The catheter is left in place to allow for continuous drainage and healing. During this time, the system's performance is monitored to ensure the drainage channel remains open (patent) and is not obstructed. After several weeks, once the body has formed a new, healed drainage tract (a process called epithelialization), the bulb is deflated, and the catheter is easily removed. The catheter itself progresses through distinct states, from being uninflated and inserted, to inflated and draining, and finally deflated and removed.
