This model represents an integrated coastal oil terminal facility, a large-scale industrial plant designed for the reception, storage, and distribution of petroleum products. Its primary purpose is to act as a hub, handling various grades of oil such as crude oil, diesel, and gasoline, which arrive via marine vessels and are then dispatched into pipeline networks.

The terminal's operations begin when a tanker vessel arrives. Large, articulated **loading arms** extend and connect to the vessel to create a secure link for transferring oil. Powerful **pumping stations** then draw the product from the ship, moving it through a network of **pipelines**. As the oil flows into the terminal, it passes through high-precision **metering systems** that accurately measure the volume for inventory and commercial purposes. A **manifold system**, acting like a complex valve and pipe junction, directs the incoming oil to the appropriate storage area.

The core of the facility is its tank farm, which consists of numerous large-scale **storage tanks**. These tanks are segregated by product type to prevent contamination, with dedicated tanks for crude oil, diesel, and gasoline. Each tank is equipped with sensors to monitor its current level, pressure, and temperature, ensuring it operates within safe limits. A tank's operational cycle is managed through states such as empty, filling, full, and discharging.

The terminal is not just a passive storage facility; it actively manages its inventory. It can perform internal transfers, using a dedicated set of pumps and pipelines to move products between tanks. It also has the capability to blend different products to meet specific customer specifications. Finally, when ready for distribution, a separate set of dispatch pumps moves the finished product from the storage tanks, through another manifold and metering system, and into the external pipeline network for delivery to refineries or other facilities.

Overseeing all these operations is a comprehensive **safety monitoring system**. This system continuously gathers data from sensors on the tanks, pumps, and pipelines to check for hazardous conditions like pressure abnormalities or leaks. If a critical issue is detected, the system can automatically trigger an emergency shutdown, stopping all transfers to ensure the safety of personnel and the environment.
