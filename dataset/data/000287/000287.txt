This model describes a conventional ground vehicle, complete with its powertrain, operational states, and the engineering requirements that govern its design.

The vehicle's core is a powertrain consisting of a six-cylinder engine connected to a transmission. The engine's primary function is to generate torque in response to fuel and ignition commands. This torque is then transferred to the transmission, which amplifies it to propel the vehicle. The system is defined with detailed physical attributes, such as its overall mass, which is calculated from the sum of its dry mass (including a 200 kg engine and 60 kg transmission), its cargo load, and its fuel.

The vehicle operates in distinct states. It can be 'off' or 'on', with the transition triggered by an ignition command. When turned on, it performs a self-test and begins to provide power, with a limit of 500W for its electrical systems. When turned off, it automatically applies a parking brake. The vehicle also monitors its own condition, existing in either a 'normal' or 'degraded' health state.

A key aspect of this system is the set of formal engineering requirements it must meet. These include a strict maximum mass limit of 2000 kg and specific fuel economy targets for both city (10 km/L) and highway (12.75 km/L) driving. To ensure these targets are met, the model includes a sophisticated fuel economy analysis. This analysis simulates the vehicle's performance over a given driving scenario, calculating fuel consumption based on principles that factor in the vehicle's mass, engine displacement, and idling fuel rate.

Finally, the system definition includes formal verification procedures. For example, there is a defined test case for confirming the vehicle's mass, which specifies the actions of weighing the vehicle on a scale and having an operator evaluate whether it passes the requirement.
