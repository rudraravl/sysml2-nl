This model describes a configurable temperature monitoring and alarm system. It is a self-contained unit designed to detect when a physical temperature exceeds a predefined limit and issue a corresponding, time-stamped alarm.

The system is built around two primary components: a **Temperature Sensor** and an **Alarm Evaluator**. The Temperature Sensor is responsible for taking the actual temperature measurements from the environment. Each sensor has a unique identifier and can be calibrated for accuracy. The Alarm Evaluator receives the temperature data from the sensor and continuously compares it against a configurable operating threshold to determine if an alarm condition exists.

Functionally, the system operates in a cycle. The sensor acquires a temperature reading and passes it to the evaluator. The evaluator then decides whether to trigger an alarm, what the severity of that alarm should be (e.g., "warning" or "critical"), and sends out an alarm status signal. To prevent the alarm from rapidly switching on and off right at the threshold, the system incorporates a hysteresis value, meaning the temperature must drop a certain amount below the threshold before the alarm deactivates.

The system's behavior is state-driven. The sensor itself can be in different operational modes, such as standby, active monitoring, and the process of taking a measurement. Similarly, the alarm has distinct states like normal, warning, and critical, escalating or de-escalating between them as the temperature changes. The entire unit can be enabled or have its temperature threshold reconfigured externally. Key performance requirements for the system include providing temperature readings accurate to within ±1.5°C and responding to a threshold breach by activating an alarm within 200 milliseconds.
