This model represents a complete pumpjack system, a type of artificial lift system commonly used in onshore oil fields to extract crude oil from underground reservoirs. Its primary function is to convert electrical energy into a powerful, reciprocating motion that drives a pump located deep within an oil well, lifting fluids to the surface.

The system's operation begins with a large electric motor that provides the initial rotational power. This high-speed, low-torque output is fed into a speed reducer, essentially a gearbox, which converts it into low-speed, high-torque rotation. This powerful rotation drives a crank assembly equipped with large counterweights to balance the load.

The crank's motion is transferred to a large, pivoting walking beam, the iconic "nodding" component of the pumpjack. The walking beam converts the rotary motion into a steady, vertical up-and-down stroke at the surface. This surface motion is transmitted deep into the well via a long, connected series of steel rods known as a polished rod string.

At the bottom of the well, this rod string is connected to a subsurface pump. As the rod string moves up, it lifts a column of fluid (a mixture of crude oil, water, and gas) from the reservoir. This fluid travels up through production tubing to the wellhead assembly at the surface, which manages the pressure and directs the output. From the wellhead, the extracted fluid is then transported through a flowline pipeline to a storage or processing facility.

A dedicated control system manages the pumpjack's operational speed, or stroke rate, to optimize production. The entire system operates through distinct states, including an idle standby mode, an active pumping state which cycles continuously between upstroke and downstroke, and an emergency shutdown state for safety.
