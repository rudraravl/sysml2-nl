This model describes a Wheatstone bridge, an electrical circuit designed for making highly precise measurements of an unknown electrical resistance.

The system is composed of several key electrical components. It includes a DC voltage source that powers the entire circuit, two known reference resistors with fixed values, and an adjustable variable resistor. The component whose resistance needs to be determined is treated as the fourth, 'unknown' resistor. At the heart of the measurement system is a galvanometer, which is a very sensitive voltage detector.

These components are arranged in a bridge or diamond-shaped configuration with two parallel branches. The voltage source is connected across the top and bottom of this diamond, supplying power to both branches. The galvanometer is connected between the midpoints of these two branches to detect any voltage difference between them.

The system operates by adjusting the variable resistor until the galvanometer shows a reading of zero volts. This state, known as a "balanced bridge," indicates that there is no voltage difference between the midpoints. When the bridge is balanced, the value of the unknown resistor can be calculated with great accuracy using a simple ratio of the three known resistance values. This method is valuable in scientific and engineering applications where precision is critical.
