This model describes a laboratory centrifuge system, a piece of equipment designed to separate components within liquid samples by spinning them at very high speeds. Its primary function is to use centrifugal force to separate particles in a mixture based on their size, shape, and density, which is a common process in clinical diagnostics, biological research, and industrial labs.

The system is composed of several key interacting parts. At its core is a high-speed **Motor** that drives a central **Rotor**. This rotor is designed with a specific number of buckets to hold sample tubes. To work correctly and safely, the samples must be carefully loaded to keep the rotor balanced.

A sophisticated **Control Unit** serves as the brain of the system, allowing a user to program a specific separation protocol, including the desired rotational speed, the duration of the run, and the operating temperature. This unit manages the centrifuge's operational states, which include idle, loading, accelerating, the main separating phase, decelerating, and unloading.

To protect sensitive biological or chemical samples from heat generated by the high-speed rotation, a **Temperature Control** system is integrated, actively cooling the chamber to maintain a precise setpoint.

Safety is a critical aspect of the design. A dedicated **Safety System** ensures the lid is securely locked before the motor will start and remains locked until the rotor has come to a complete stop. It also monitors for load imbalances and excessive vibrations, and can trigger an emergency stop if unsafe conditions are detected. The entire system is powered through a single power interface and allows for user interaction through a control interface for setting up and monitoring a run.
