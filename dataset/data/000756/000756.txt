This model describes a Scrambler Therapy System, a non-invasive medical device designed to treat chronic and neuropathic pain. The system's core function is to intercept pain signals traveling through the body's nerves and replace them with synthetic, "non-pain" information. This is achieved by delivering precisely controlled electrical signals through electrodes placed on the patient's skin, effectively "scrambling" the original pain message before it reaches the brain.

The system is composed of several key components that work together to deliver safe and effective therapy:

*   A **Signal Processing Unit** acts as the brain of the device. It generates the complex, variable electrical waveforms that mimic non-pain nerve signals. This unit continuously adjusts the signal's amplitude and frequency based on real-time feedback to optimize the treatment for the individual patient.
*   A **Surface Electrode Array** consists of the physical patches that are placed on the patient's skin. These electrodes serve as the interface to the nervous system, delivering the therapeutic electrical signals generated by the processing unit.
*   A **Control Panel** provides the user interface for the clinician. Through this panel, they can set the duration of the therapy session, monitor progress, and input patient feedback regarding pain levels and comfort.
*   A **Safety Monitoring Unit** is a critical component that continuously checks the electrical output and the quality of the electrode-to-skin connection. It ensures that the current, voltage, and skin impedance remain within safe, predefined limits, and it can halt the therapy instantly if any unsafe condition is detected.
*   A **Power Management Unit** supplies stable electrical power to all other components, likely from a rechargeable battery or a mains connection.

A typical therapy session involves an initial assessment of the patient's pain level. The system then enters an active therapy cycle where it generates and delivers the scrambler signals. Throughout the session, the clinician monitors the patient's response and provides feedback to the system via the control panel. The system uses this feedback to automatically fine-tune the stimulation parameters, creating an adaptive and personalized treatment. This cycle of delivering, monitoring, and adjusting repeats for the entire duration of the session, which typically lasts between 30 and 60 minutes. Once completed, a final pain assessment is conducted to measure the treatment's effectiveness. The entire process is managed through different operational states, including idle, configuring, active therapy, paused, and an emergency stop state.
