This model represents a standardized digital library of physical quantities, units, and definitions for the field of condensed matter physics. It is a formal, machine-readable implementation of the international standard ISO 80000-12, which governs the terminology and concepts used to describe the properties of solid materials.

The library serves as a foundational reference for scientists and engineers, defining a comprehensive set of concepts used to analyze and model solids. These concepts cover several key areas:

*   **Crystal Structure:** It defines the fundamental geometric properties of crystalline materials, such as the `lattice vector` which describes the repeating atomic arrangement, the `lattice plane spacing`, and the `Bragg angle` used in X-ray diffraction to study these structures.
*   **Vibrational and Thermal Properties:** The model includes quantities related to how atoms vibrate within a solid (phonons). This includes concepts like the `Debye temperature` and `Debye angular frequency`, which characterize the vibrational spectrum of a solid, and the `Grüneisen parameter`, which relates thermal expansion to heat capacity.
*   **Electronic Properties:** A large portion of the library is dedicated to describing the behavior of electrons in materials. This includes fundamental concepts like `electron density`, `Fermi energy` (the highest energy level occupied by electrons at absolute zero), `work function` (the energy needed to remove an electron from a solid), and `gap energy` (which distinguishes metals, semiconductors, and insulators).
*   **Transport and Thermoelectric Phenomena:** It defines quantities that describe how electrons and heat move through a material, such as `residual resistivity`, the `Hall coefficient` (related to charge carrier density), and the `Lorenz coefficient`. It also covers thermoelectric effects with the `Seebeck`, `Peltier`, and `Thomson coefficients`, which describe how temperature differences can generate voltages and vice versa.
*   **Magnetism and Superconductivity:** The library defines critical temperatures that mark phase transitions, such as the `Curie temperature` for ferromagnetic materials and the `Néel temperature` for antiferromagnetic materials. For superconductors, it defines the `superconduction transition temperature`, `critical magnetic flux densities`, and characteristic lengths like the `London penetration depth` and `coherence length`.

In essence, this is not a model of a single physical object but a comprehensive, standardized dictionary of physics concepts. Its purpose is to ensure that engineering models, simulations, and scientific data exchange use a consistent, unambiguous, and internationally recognized vocabulary for describing the physical world at the atomic and electronic scale.
