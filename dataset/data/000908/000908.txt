This model represents a manually operated fluid transfer system built around a Kearny Air Pump. Its purpose is to move a fluid, like water, from a source reservoir to a destination tank using human power.

The central component is the Kearny Air Pump, which uses compressed air to move the fluid. It is operated by a manual bellows unit. When a person compresses the bellows, it sends a pulse of compressed air into a pressure chamber. This chamber contains the fluid that has been drawn in from the source. The incoming air pressurizes the fluid, forcing it out through a one-way outlet valve towards the destination tank.

The flow of fluid into and out of the pump is controlled by two check valves, which ensure the fluid only moves in one direction. An inlet valve allows fluid to enter the pump's internal supply reservoir from the source, and the outlet valve allows the pressurized fluid to exit.

The pump's operation follows a distinct cycle:
1.  **Intake:** Fluid is drawn from the source reservoir into the pump.
2.  **Compression:** The manual bellows forces air into the pressure chamber, increasing the pressure of the fluid inside.
3.  **Discharge:** The highly pressurized fluid opens the outlet valve and is expelled towards the destination tank.

This cycle repeats with each compression of the bellows, creating a pulsed flow. The system is designed to meet specific performance targets, such as a minimum flow rate and pressure, and is required to be reliable enough to operate continuously for at least two hours.
