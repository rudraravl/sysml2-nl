This model describes a mechanical field tourniquet, a medical device designed to control life-threatening hemorrhage in a limb. Its primary function is to apply significant pressure around an arm or leg to stop arterial blood flow and prevent fatal blood loss in emergency situations.

The tourniquet is composed of several key mechanical parts that work together:

*   **Compression Strap:** A wide, durable band that is wrapped around the injured limb. It is designed to distribute pressure evenly and withstand high tension without tearing.
*   **Windlass Mechanism:** A rigid rod used to tighten the compression strap. By twisting the windlass, a user can generate a high degree of mechanical tension in the strap, far more than could be achieved by pulling by hand. This tension is what creates the pressure needed to occlude blood flow.
*   **Securing Clip:** A C-shaped or forked clip that locks the windlass rod in place after it has been tightened. This is a critical safety feature that prevents the windlass from unwinding and ensures that pressure on the limb is reliably maintained.
*   **Time Marker:** A dedicated area, typically a white tab on the strap, where the person applying the tourniquet can write down the exact time of application. This information is vital for medical professionals who later treat the patient.

The device operates through a clear sequence of states. It starts in an idle state, is then positioned on the limb, and enters a tightening phase where the user turns the windlass. Once bleeding has stopped, the windlass is locked into the clip, securing the device. It remains secured until it is safely released by a qualified professional. The entire system is designed for rapid, single-person operation in high-stress field conditions, emphasizing reliability and the ability to maintain compression over time.
