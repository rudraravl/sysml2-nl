This model describes the Medipines Respiratory System, an advanced medical ventilator designed to provide life-sustaining breathing support for patients. Its primary function is to deliver a precise, controlled mixture of air and oxygen to a patient's lungs, manage the breathing cycle, and continuously monitor respiratory functions to ensure patient safety.

The system is composed of several key components that work together to form a complete ventilation circuit. An **Oxygen Blender** takes in medical-grade air and pure oxygen, mixing them to achieve the exact oxygen concentration prescribed by a clinician. This gas mixture is then precisely controlled by an **Inspiratory Valve**, which opens to allow the gas to flow through a **Patient Circuit** (the tubing connecting the machine to the patient) and into the lungs.

Throughout this process, a suite of sensors provides critical real-time feedback. An **Inspiratory Flow Sensor** measures the volume and speed of the gas being delivered, while an **Airway Pressure Sensor** monitors the pressure within the patient's lungs to prevent injury. For the exhalation phase, an **Expiratory Valve** opens, and the patient's exhaled breath is monitored by an **Expiratory Flow Sensor** and a **CO2 Monitor** to ensure proper ventilation and gas exchange.

At the heart of the system is a central **Control Processor**, which acts as the brain. It takes the ventilation settings entered by a clinician via the **User Interface** (such as breath rate and volume) and uses feedback from the various sensors to precisely control the inspiratory and expiratory valves, ensuring the correct breath is delivered every time. A dedicated **Alarm System** constantly compares patient data against safe limits, triggering immediate audible and visual alerts on the user interface if any measurement, like airway pressure, goes outside the safe range.

The ventilator operates in several distinct states to ensure safe and predictable behavior. Upon startup, it runs an initialization and self-test sequence. If successful, it enters a **standby** mode, ready for operation. When activated, it proceeds to the **ventilating** state, where it continuously cycles between inspiration and expiration phases. If a critical issue is detected, the system will enter a **fault** state to alert clinicians and ensure patient safety.
