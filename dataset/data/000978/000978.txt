This model represents an Atomic Force Microscope (AFM), a high-resolution scientific instrument designed to image and measure surfaces at the nanoscale. Its primary function is to generate three-dimensional topographical maps of a sample's surface with near-atomic resolution. The system can also measure mechanical properties by analyzing the forces between its probe and the sample.

The heart of the system is a microscopic cantilever with an extremely sharp tip that physically interacts with the sample's surface. A high-precision piezo scanner controls the sample's position, moving it in a raster pattern beneath the cantilever tip with sub-nanometer accuracy.

To track the cantilever's movement, a laser system directs a beam onto its reflective back. The reflected beam is captured by a multi-quadrant photodetector, which can precisely measure even the slightest vertical and lateral deflections of the cantilever as the tip scans over surface features.

A critical feedback controller maintains a constant, gentle force between the tip and the sample. It does this by continuously comparing the measured cantilever deflection to a user-defined setpoint and sending control signals to the piezo scanner to adjust the sample's height accordingly. As the scanner moves the sample, a data acquisition system records these height adjustments at every coordinate. This information is then processed to construct the final high-resolution surface image and force data maps. The system is configurable for different operating modes, such as contact or tapping mode, depending on the sample and the desired measurement.
