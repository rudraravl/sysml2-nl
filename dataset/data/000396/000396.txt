This model describes a robotic work cell, the kind you might find on an automated manufacturing or assembly line. The central component is a sophisticated industrial robot designed to perform a complete pick-process-and-place task.

The robot itself consists of a multi-jointed arm with a reach of 1.2 meters, constructed with a base, shoulder, elbow, and wrist joint, allowing for complex and precise movements. At the end of the arm is a two-fingered gripper capable of exerting up to 150 Newtons of force to securely hold workpieces.

To interact with its environment, the robot is equipped with a suite of sensors. A vision sensor, functioning like a high-resolution camera, allows it to "see" and locate objects, while a highly sensitive force sensor enables it to detect contact and handle items with care.

The robot's "brain" is a high-frequency controller that plans and executes movements. The entire system is powered by a 24-volt power supply that provides electrical energy to the arm's joints and the gripper.

In operation, the robot follows a defined sequence of states. It starts in an idle state, waiting for a task. Once a command is received, it uses its sensors to perceive the work area, plans a trajectory to a target, and moves its arm into position. It then grips a workpiece, performs a processing task on it, moves to a release location, and lets go of the finished item before returning to an idle state to await the next task. The system is also designed to handle errors, such as failing to grasp an item or detecting an obstacle, by entering a safe error state.
