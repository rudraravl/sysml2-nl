This describes an active sonar system designed for underwater detection, classification, and ranging of various targets. The system is intended to be used on a vessel or underwater platform to identify objects such as obstacles, the seafloor, marine life, or other vessels.

The system's core operation is a continuous cycle. It begins with a powerful transducer, which emits a high-frequency acoustic pulse, or "ping," into the water. This same transducer then switches to a listening mode to detect any returning echoes from objects that the sound pulse has bounced off.

Once an echo is received, it is sent to a dedicated signal processor. This component's job is to clean up the raw signal, filtering out ambient ocean noise to isolate the true echo. The processed signal is then passed to a target analyzer, which acts as the brain of the system. The analyzer calculates the target's distance by measuring the precise time it took for the echo to travel back, using a standard value for the speed of sound in water (1500 m/s). It also examines the characteristics of the echo to classify the object it detected.

Finally, the compiled target data—including its distance, classification, and echo strength—is sent to a display unit, providing a clear visual representation for an operator. The entire system is engineered for reliable, continuous operation, with a specified detection range from 1 meter up to 5 kilometers and rapid signal processing to ensure timely and accurate information.
