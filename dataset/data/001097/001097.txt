This model describes a self-contained Impact Sensing Unit, a compact electronic device designed to detect, analyze, and record physical impact events like collisions, drops, or sudden jolts.

The system's core component is an Impact Sensor, which is a modular assembly of several specialized parts. It begins with a three-axis Accelerometer, the primary sensing element that continuously measures acceleration. The raw, often noisy, signal from this accelerometer is immediately sent to a Signal Processor. This processor cleans the signal by filtering out unwanted noise, amplifying it for clarity, and applying calibration adjustments to ensure accuracy.

The refined acceleration data is then passed to an Impact Detector. This component acts as the decision-making brain of the sensor. It constantly calculates the magnitude of the incoming acceleration and compares it against a pre-set sensitivity threshold. If the acceleration exceeds this threshold, the detector registers an official "Impact Event," creating a detailed data packet that includes the event's magnitude, direction, and a precise timestamp.

The entire unit is powered by an integrated Power Supply. Once the Impact Sensor generates an impact event, this data, along with continuous acceleration readings, is sent to an onboard Data Logger for storage. The unit is also designed with an external data port, allowing it to stream this information to another system for real-time monitoring or later retrieval. The system is engineered to meet specific performance requirements, including a high sampling rate for precision, a defined threshold for what constitutes an impact, and a rapid response time to ensure events are captured almost instantaneously.
