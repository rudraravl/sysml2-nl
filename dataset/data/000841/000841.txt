This model describes an automated, air-driven pumping system designed to transfer a fluid from a storage reservoir to a receiving tank and maintain a specific target pressure within that receiver. The system uses compressed air as its power source to operate the main pump.

The system's operation is a coordinated process involving several key components:

*   **Air Supply:** An **Air Compressor** generates high-pressure air (700 kPa), which is then passed through a **Pressure Regulator** to step it down to a stable, controlled operating pressure (600 kPa). This regulated air acts as the energy source for the system.
*   **Fluid Source and Destination:** The fluid to be moved is initially stored in a 100-liter **Fluid Reservoir**. The system's goal is to transfer this fluid into an 80-liter **Fluid Receiver** tank.
*   **Pumping Action:** The central component is the **Air-Driven Pump**. It uses the regulated compressed air to draw fluid from the reservoir and discharge it at a higher pressure. This pump is designed to deliver a flow rate of up to 80 liters per minute and has a built-in safety limit to prevent exceeding a maximum discharge pressure. After use, the air is vented through an exhaust port.
*   **Fluid Delivery and Safety:** As the fluid leaves the pump, it passes through a **Check Valve** which ensures the fluid can only flow in one direction, preventing backflow into the pump.
*   **Automated Control Loop:** The entire system is managed by a **System Controller**. A **Pressure Sensor** constantly monitors the fluid pressure inside the receiving tank and feeds this information back to the controller. The controller's logic is designed to maintain a target pressure of 400 kPa. If the pressure drops too low (below 350 kPa), the controller automatically sends a 'start' command to the pump. Once the pressure rises above a set threshold (450 kPa), it sends a 'stop' command. This creates a self-regulating cycle that keeps the receiver's pressure within the desired range.

The pump itself has its own internal logic for managing its operational state, moving between idle, priming, active pumping, and stopping. It also includes a fault state to shut down safely if it detects an over-pressure condition.
