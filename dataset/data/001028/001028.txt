This model describes a modular electronic system called a Displacement Sensing Unit, designed to precisely measure the physical movement or change in position of an object and convert it into a digital data reading.

The system is composed of three main parts that work together in a sequence: a Displacement Sensor, a Signal Conditioner, and a Measurement Processor.

First, the **Displacement Sensor** is the component that physically interacts with the object being measured. It acts as a transducer, converting the mechanical movement into a raw, low-level analog electrical signal (a voltage). This particular sensor is specified to measure a range of up to 100 millimeters with a very high accuracy of 0.01 millimeters.

Next, the raw electrical signal from the sensor is fed into the **Signal Conditioner**. Since the initial signal can be weak and affected by electrical noise, this component's job is to clean and strengthen it. It first amplifies the signal to make it more robust (with a gain of 10) and then filters out any unwanted noise to produce a clean, stable signal.

Finally, the clean, amplified signal is passed to the **Measurement Processor**. This is the digital "brains" of the unit. It takes the conditioned analog signal, samples it at a high rate (1000 times per second), and converts the voltage level back into a precise, calculated displacement value. It then packages this value with a timestamp to create a complete, final reading.

In operation, the entire unit takes a physical movement as its input and outputs a stream of accurate, timestamped digital measurements, making it suitable for applications in industrial automation, robotics, or structural health monitoring where precise position tracking is required.
