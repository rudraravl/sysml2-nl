This model describes the "Watson Capsule," an ingestible, autonomous diagnostic device, often referred to as a "smart pill." It is designed to be swallowed by a patient to travel through their gastrointestinal tract, acting as a miniature, self-contained laboratory to perform real-time medical analysis.

The capsule itself is small and built from a biocompatible medical-grade polymer, ensuring it can pass safely through the human body. Its core function is to collect and analyze biological samples as it moves through the digestive system.

The system is composed of several key components working in sequence:

*   A **Sensor Array** is responsible for collecting the biological sample. It measures key indicators like biomarker concentrations, pH, and temperature from its immediate surroundings.
*   The raw data from the sensors is then fed into an **Analytics Engine**. This is the "brain" of the capsule, running a sophisticated AI model (specifically "Watson-AI-v3.2") to interpret the sensor data. It generates a detailed diagnosis, including a confidence level and potential recommendations.
*   Once an analysis is complete, a **Communication Module** wirelessly transmits the final result using a low-energy protocol (like Bluetooth) to an external monitoring system, such as a device worn by the patient or a nearby receiver in a clinical setting.
*   A **Power Management** unit with an internal battery provides the necessary energy for the capsule to operate for the entire duration of its journey through the digestive tract, which can last from 24 to 72 hours.

In essence, the Watson Capsule is designed to autonomously perform a complete diagnostic workflow: it samples its environment, analyzes the findings using onboard AI, and reports a medical diagnosis to the outside world, all from within the patient's body.
