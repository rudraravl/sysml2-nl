This is a medical system for gastric decompression, centered around a specialized double-lumen nasogastric tube known as a Replogle tube. Its primary function is to safely and continuously remove fluids and air from a patient's stomach, which is a critical procedure often used for newborns with certain esophageal conditions.

The system consists of three main components that work together: the Replogle tube itself, an external suction apparatus, and an irrigation system.

The **Replogle tube** is a flexible tube inserted into the patient's stomach. Its key feature is its dual-channel design. A larger, primary channel, the suction lumen, is used to actively withdraw gastric fluids and swallowed air. A second, smaller channel, the vent lumen, is not connected to suction but is left open to the atmosphere. This vent acts as a crucial safety feature, allowing ambient air to enter the stomach if the suction pressure becomes too high, which prevents the tube's tip from adhering to and damaging the delicate stomach lining. The end of the tube that sits in the stomach has several openings to facilitate effective drainage.

The tube is connected to a **suction apparatus**, which is an external medical device that generates a continuous, low-pressure vacuum. This apparatus includes a pressure regulator to ensure the suction is maintained within a safe and effective range, typically between 40 and 80 mmHg. It also has a collection container to safely gather all the fluids removed from the stomach.

An **irrigation system** is also part of the setup. It is used to periodically flush the tube, typically with a small amount of saline solution. This flushing is essential to clear any potential blockages in the lumens, ensuring the suction path remains open and, most importantly, that the air vent remains clear and functional.

In operation, the system performs gastric decompression by applying continuous, gentle suction through the main lumen while the vent lumen simultaneously provides a constant air inlet to relieve any excess negative pressure. The system is designed to monitor the patency of the vent; if it becomes blocked, an irrigation cycle is initiated to clear it, thereby ensuring the continuous and safe operation of the entire system.
