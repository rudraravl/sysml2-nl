This is an atmospheric ozone monitoring system designed to continuously measure ozone concentration in the air, calculate the corresponding Air Quality Index (AQI), and alert users to potentially hazardous conditions. Its primary purpose is to protect public health by providing real-time air quality information.

The system is built from several interconnected components. At its core is an electrochemical **Ozone Sensor** that periodically samples the surrounding air to detect ozone molecules. This raw sensor data, which includes ozone level, temperature, and humidity, is sent to a central **Data Processor**.

The Data Processor acts as the brain of the system. It analyzes the sample to determine the precise ozone concentration, calculates the AQI value, and classifies the current air quality into one of four levels: good, moderate, unhealthy, or hazardous, based on predefined thresholds.

This processed information is then sent to a **Display Unit**, which provides a real-time visual readout of the current ozone concentration, AQI, and overall alert status. Simultaneously, the measurement data is recorded by a **Data Logger** to store a historical log for trend analysis.

If the Data Processor determines that ozone levels have become hazardous, it triggers an **Alarm Unit**, which activates an audible or visual warning to alert people in the vicinity. The entire system is powered by a central **Power Module** that provides regulated electricity to all the components.

In operation, the system cycles through different states, moving from idle to actively measuring, then processing the data, and finally displaying the results. If a dangerous condition is detected, it enters an alarming state before returning to its regular monitoring cycle.
