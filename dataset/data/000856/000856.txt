This model describes an automated centrifugal pumping subsystem designed to transfer a fluid from a source reservoir to a discharge point while precisely controlling its flow rate and pressure.

The system is composed of several physical components working together. At its heart is a centrifugal pump, which is powered by a connected electric motor. Fluid is drawn from a source reservoir through an initial set of pipes (suction piping) into the pump. The motor provides rotational energy to the pump's impeller, which in turn increases the pressure and velocity of the fluid, pushing it out into a second set of pipes (discharge piping).

What makes this a "subsystem" rather than just a simple pump is its sophisticated control loop. To ensure the system operates correctly, a pressure sensor and a flow meter are installed on the discharge piping. These sensors constantly monitor the output of the pump and send their readings as signals to a central controller. This controller, the "brain" of the system, compares the actual pressure and flow rate to desired target values. If the pressure is too low, for example, the controller commands the motor to increase its speed, thereby boosting the pump's output. Conversely, if the pressure is too high, it slows the motor down.

This closed-loop control allows the system to automatically maintain a stable and consistent output, adapting to changes in conditions. The entire subsystem is engineered to meet specific performance requirements, including delivering a minimum flow rate, maintaining discharge pressure within a defined range (e.g., 2 to 5 bar), and operating with high energy efficiency and long-term reliability.
