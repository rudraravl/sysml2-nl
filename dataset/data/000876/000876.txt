This model describes a complete flexible impeller pump system designed to transfer various types of fluids, including those that are viscous or contain entrained air.

The core of the system is the pump unit, which consists of a flexible, rubber-like impeller with multiple blades housed within a specially shaped bronze casing. This pump unit is driven by an electric motor, with power transmitted through a stainless steel drive shaft. To ensure the fluid moves in the correct direction and to prevent backflow, the system includes check valves on both the suction (inlet) and discharge (outlet) lines.

The pumping action is generated as the motor rotates the impeller. Due to the eccentric shape of the casing, the flexible blades of the impeller bend as they pass a narrow point and then straighten out as they approach the inlet port. This straightening action creates a partial vacuum, which draws fluid into the pump. The fluid is then trapped in the spaces between the blades and carried towards the outlet. As the blades approach the outlet port, the casing narrows again, squeezing the blades and pressurizing the fluid, which forces it out of the discharge port. This gentle, positive displacement mechanism makes the pump self-priming and capable of handling delicate fluids.

The system operates through several distinct states, managed by external start, stop, and emergency stop commands. It has an 'off' state, a 'starting' phase where the motor accelerates to its operational speed, a continuous 'running' state for normal pumping, and a 'stopping' phase for shutdown. The entire assembly is designed to meet specific performance requirements for flow rate and pressure, as well as durability requirements for its key components, especially the flexible impeller, which must withstand wear, temperature, and brief periods of running dry.
