This model describes the functional architecture of a spacecraft, which is the "space segment" of a complete space mission. The system is designed for autonomous operation in orbit, responsible for carrying out a specific mission by managing both its own core systems (the platform) and a dedicated mission payload.

The spacecraft's core functions are managed by a central processing unit. This "brain" oversees attitude and orbit control, manages the mission payload, and maintains the overall health of the platform through power, thermal, and security management. It is designed to communicate with a ground station by receiving commands (telecommands) and sending back health and status information (telemetry).

The system executes complex sequences of actions, particularly during critical mission phases. For example, after launch, it performs an initial deployment sequence which includes stabilizing its rotation (detumbling), deploying its solar arrays to generate power, activating its thrusters, and achieving a stable orientation in space.

To handle different situations, the spacecraft operates in several distinct modes. These include a "Nominal mode" for normal mission operations, an "Eclipse mode" for conserving power when it passes through the Earth's shadow, an "Orbit maintenance mode" for firing thrusters to adjust its orbital path, and a "Safe mode" which is a minimal-functionality state designed to ensure the spacecraft's survival in the event of an anomaly. The system is designed to transition between these modes as required by the mission phase or its own operational status.
