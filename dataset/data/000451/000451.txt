This is a Gastric Balloon System, a minimally invasive medical device designed for weight loss by temporarily occupying space in a patient's stomach. The primary goal is to promote a feeling of fullness (satiety), which helps reduce food intake and supports a target weight loss of 10-15% over a six-month treatment period.

The core of the system is the Intragastric Balloon, a soft, durable device made of biocompatible materials. It is inserted into the stomach endoscopically while in a deflated state. Once positioned correctly, it is filled with a sterile saline solution using a flexible Catheter System. An external Inflation Pump pushes the saline through the catheter to fill the balloon to a precise volume, typically between 400 and 700 mL.

To ensure patient safety, the system includes a comprehensive control and monitoring architecture. The balloon is equipped with sensors that continuously track its internal pressure, volume, and temperature. This data is fed to a dedicated Monitoring Unit, which will trigger safety alerts if any readings exceed predefined safe thresholds, preventing issues like over-inflation or rupture. A Control Unit manages the inflation and deflation commands sent to the pumps and keeps track of the overall treatment timeline.

The system's operational lifecycle is carefully managed. It begins with the deployment and inflation process. Once successfully deployed, the balloon remains in the stomach for the treatment duration, undergoing continuous monitoring. At the end of the treatment, or if a safety issue arises, a Deflation Pump is used to extract the saline from the balloon. Once completely empty, the deflated balloon is safely removed from the patient via endoscopy.
