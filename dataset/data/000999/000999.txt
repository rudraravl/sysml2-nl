This model describes a scientific instrument known as a calorimeter, designed for precisely measuring the heat capacity and other thermal properties of material samples.

The core of the system is a thermally insulated **Sample Chamber** where a material sample is placed for testing. A high-precision **Temperature Sensor** is directly connected to this chamber to continuously monitor its temperature with a very fast response time. To manipulate the sample's temperature, a controllable **Heating Element** applies a precise amount of heat to the chamber. The entire assembly is enclosed in **Thermal Insulation** to minimize heat loss to the environment, ensuring the measurement's accuracy.

The operation is managed by a **Control Unit** and a **Data Acquisition System**. An operator can start or stop a measurement and select an operating mode, such as isothermal (maintaining a constant temperature) or adiabatic (preventing heat exchange). During an experiment, the Control Unit reads the current temperature from the sensor and directs the Heating Element to supply the necessary power to reach or maintain a target temperature.

Simultaneously, the Data Acquisition System logs a continuous stream of data, creating a detailed record that includes timestamps, temperature readings, and the amount of heat being supplied by the heater at any given moment. Once the experiment is complete, the system uses this collected data—along with the known mass of the sample—to automatically calculate the material's specific heat capacity. The system is built to meet strict performance standards, including high temperature accuracy, effective thermal insulation, and a rapid data sampling rate.
