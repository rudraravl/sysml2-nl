This model describes a standardized, modular system for outfitting a vehicle or equipment chassis, such as a configurable spacecraft, a customizable robot, or industrial machinery. The system is designed around a main body, or "hull," which features different types of standardized "slots" for installing various functional modules. This design allows for easy customization and mission-specific configuration by simply plugging in the required components.

There are three distinct classes of slots, each offering different levels of integration with the main hull:

*   **High Slots:** These are high-capability connection points. Modules installed here receive both electrical power and a basic 'on/off' activation command directly from the hull. They are intended for core components that require significant energy, such as primary weapon systems, shield emitters, or main thrusters.

*   **Medium Slots:** Similar to high slots, these provide both power and an activation signal from the hull. They are likely designed for auxiliary systems or components with moderate power needs, such as sensor arrays, electronic warfare suites, or secondary utility tools.

*   **Low Slots:** These are the simplest interface points. They only provide an activation command from the hull and do not supply any power. Any module fitted into a low slot must have its own internal power source. This makes them suitable for passive equipment, self-powered subsystems, or simple control modules like armor plating reinforcements or internal system toggles.

In essence, this system defines the standardized "rules of connection" between a central structure and its interchangeable parts, ensuring that modules receive the correct power and control they need based on the type of slot they are designed for.
