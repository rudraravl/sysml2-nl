This model represents an automated liquid level control system, commonly used for applications like sump pumps or drainage management. Its primary function is to prevent a tank from overflowing by automatically pumping out excess liquid when the level gets too high.

The system is comprised of four main components: a liquid tank, a float switch, a pump, and a liquid source. The tank serves as a collection basin for liquid coming from the source.

At the heart of the system is the float switch, which continuously monitors the liquid level. This switch consists of a buoyant float mechanism that physically rises and falls with the water level, and an electrical switch contact that it operates.

The system's operation is straightforward: as liquid fills the tank, the float rises. When the liquid reaches a pre-set high point, called the "activation level," the float mechanism triggers the electrical switch to a "closed" state. This closed switch completes a circuit, sending a signal that turns the pump on. The pump then begins to remove liquid from the tank.

As the pump drains the tank, the liquid level and the float fall. When the level drops to a pre-set low point, the "deactivation level," the float opens the switch. This breaks the circuit, turning the pump off. The system then remains idle until the liquid level rises again. A key feature is the gap between the activation and deactivation levels (hysteresis), which prevents the pump from rapidly cycling on and off, thereby protecting the motor from wear.
