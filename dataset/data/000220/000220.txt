This model describes a system for the virtual analysis of a vehicle's fuel economy. The system is designed to determine if a specific vehicle configuration can meet predefined performance targets for both city and highway driving.

The central object of the analysis is a vehicle, which is composed of an engine and a four-speed transmission. The system's purpose is to simulate this vehicle's performance under different conditions to calculate its fuel consumption.

The analysis is driven by two specific requirements: the vehicle must achieve a fuel economy of at least 25 miles per gallon in the city and 30 miles per gallon on the highway. To test this, the vehicle design is subjected to two distinct virtual driving scenarios, one representing typical city driving and the other for highway driving. These scenarios dictate factors like the vehicle's speed, acceleration, and the incline of the road over time.

During the simulation, the system calculates how much engine power is needed to follow the driving scenario, considering a constant cargo weight of 1000 pounds as well as other assumptions like wheel size and drivetrain efficiency. Based on the required power, vehicle speed, and the current gear of the transmission, the analysis then calculates the total fuel consumed. The final result is a calculated fuel economy (in miles per gallon) for each scenario, which is then compared against the requirements to verify the design.
