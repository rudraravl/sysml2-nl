This model describes the powertrain of a conventional, fuel-powered vehicle. Its primary function is to convert the chemical energy in fuel into the mechanical force needed to propel the vehicle by turning its wheels.

The system operates as a sequential flow of power, beginning with an engine. Based on a command for fuel, the engine generates an initial rotational force (torque). This torque is then sent to a transmission, which modifies and amplifies it. From the transmission, the power is transferred along a driveshaft to the final drive assembly. This final component, likely a differential, distributes the torque to two individual wheels.

The entire power-delivery process is managed by start and stop commands. The sequence is activated by an "engine start" signal and runs continuously until it receives an "engine off" signal, at which point the cycle terminates.
