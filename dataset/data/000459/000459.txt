This model represents an Impella Cardiac Support System, a temporary mechanical circulatory support device designed to assist patients with acute heart failure or cardiogenic shock.

The core of the system is a catheter-based microaxial heart pump that is minimally invasively inserted into the patient's heart. The catheter is positioned across the aortic valve, with its inlet in the left ventricle and its outlet in the ascending aorta. It actively pumps blood from the ventricle to the aorta, reducing the heart's workload and improving blood circulation throughout the body.

The pump itself consists of an impeller assembly driven by a high-speed microaxial motor, capable of spinning at tens of thousands of RPMs to generate a continuous flow of blood. To prevent blood from entering and clotting within the motor housing, a dedicated purge system continuously delivers a special fluid containing dextrose and heparin through a channel in the catheter.

The entire device is managed by an external console controller. This console powers the pump, runs sophisticated control algorithms to adjust the motor speed and achieve the target blood flow, and displays vital information like flow rate and pressure on a user interface for clinicians.

The system is equipped with a suite of sensors to ensure safe and effective operation. These include pressure and flow sensors to monitor hemodynamic performance, as well as a position sensor to confirm the catheter is correctly placed within the heart. The console continuously evaluates data from these sensors and the motor's status (temperature, current) to trigger alarms for conditions like incorrect positioning, flow obstruction, or potential motor failure.

The system operates through various clinical states, including a `priming` phase to prepare the pump, `ramping` to gradually increase support, a `fullSupport` mode for sustained operation, and a `weaning` phase to slowly reduce assistance as the patient's heart recovers.
