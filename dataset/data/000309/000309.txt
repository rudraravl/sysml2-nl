This model represents a standardized framework of quantities and units used in information science and technology, based on the international standard IEC 80000-13. It is not a physical object but a foundational library that provides a precise, common vocabulary for engineers and scientists to measure, describe, and analyze systems that handle information.

The framework is organized into several key areas:

*   **Telecommunications Traffic Analysis:** It defines metrics for understanding the performance of networks, such as telephone systems or data networks. This includes concepts like **Traffic Intensity** (measured in erlangs), which describes how busy a set of resources is, as well as the **Calling Rate** and the probabilities of calls being lost or having to wait in a queue.

*   **Data Storage and Transfer:** It provides standard definitions for fundamental digital concepts. This includes **Storage Capacity** (how much data a device can hold, in bits or bytes) and various data transfer speeds, such as the general **Transfer Rate** and the more specific **Bit Rate** (bits per second). It also defines the inverse relationship, such as the **Bit Period**, which is the duration of a single bit.

*   **Signal Characteristics and Transmission:** The framework includes quantities for describing the physical signals used in data transmission. This covers the **Modulation Rate** (measured in baud), which relates to how often a signal changes state, and the **Clock Frequency** that governs the timing of digital circuits. It also defines power-related metrics like **Carrier Power** and the **Signal Energy per Bit**.

*   **Information Theory and Data Integrity:** It delves into the theoretical foundations of information itself, defining concepts like **Entropy**, which measures the amount of uncertainty or information in a message, and **Redundancy**. It also formalizes measures of data transmission quality, such as **Error Probability** (the likelihood of a bit being received incorrectly) and **Channel Capacity**, which represents the theoretical maximum rate at which information can be transmitted over a communication channel without error.

Essentially, this is a comprehensive dictionary of an engineering language, ensuring that when a system's "bit rate" or "channel capacity" is specified, it has a single, unambiguous meaning grounded in international standards.
