This model describes a portable oxygen concentrator, a medical device designed to provide supplemental, high-purity oxygen to patients requiring respiratory therapy. The system is compact, battery-powered, and intended for ambulatory use.

The device operates by drawing in ambient air through a filtered intake. An internal air compressor then pressurizes this air and forces it into a molecular sieve assembly. This core component uses a material like Zeolite to selectively absorb nitrogen from the air, allowing the concentrated oxygen to pass through. The separated nitrogen is then safely vented out as waste.

The resulting high-purity oxygen is managed by a flow regulator, which allows the user to set a specific delivery rate. The system is equipped with a suite of sensors to ensure safe and effective operation. An oxygen sensor continuously monitors the purity of the output gas, while pressure and flow sensors monitor the internal state of the system.

A central control module acts as the system's brain. It processes information from the sensors, the user's settings from the interface, and the battery's charge level. Based on this data, it controls the compressor's operation and adjusts the flow regulator to meet the patient's needs. The controller also manages the system's various operating states, such as standby, continuous flow, and pulse-dose modes.

For portability, the concentrator is powered by a rechargeable battery pack. It also includes an AC power adapter for use at home, which can power the device directly and recharge the battery. A user interface, featuring a display screen and control buttons, allows the patient to turn the device on and off, adjust the oxygen flow rate, and monitor critical information like battery life, current flow setting, and any system alarms. If the system detects an issue, such as low oxygen purity, incorrect pressure, or a low battery, it will trigger an alarm to alert the user.
