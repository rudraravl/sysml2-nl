This model describes an automated aspirator system, a device designed to create and maintain a specific level of vacuum. It functions like a vacuum pump but operates on the Venturi principle, using a high-pressure "motive" fluid (like compressed air or water) to suck in another fluid (gas or liquid) from a different source.

The system is built around a core component called a **Venturi Tube**. A high-pressure motive fluid from a dedicated **Motive Fluid Source** is passed through a smart **Flow Regulation Valve** and then accelerated through a narrow constriction in the Venturi tube. This high-speed flow creates a low-pressure area, generating suction.

This suction draws fluid in through a suction inlet and into a **Suction Chamber**. The sucked-in fluid then merges with the motive fluid inside the Venturi tube. The combined fluids are then expelled from the system through a **Discharge Chamber**.

The entire process is managed by a closed-loop control system. A **Vacuum Sensor** continuously measures the pressure level within the suction chamber and sends this data to an **Aspirator Controller**. The controller compares the actual vacuum level to a desired target setpoint. If the vacuum isn't strong enough, the controller signals the flow regulation valve to allow more motive fluid through; if it's too strong, it reduces the flow. This feedback loop ensures a stable and precise vacuum is maintained.

The system operates through several distinct states, including an idle state, a startup sequence, a normal operating mode, a controlled shutdown, and an emergency stop capability that can be triggered at any time for safety.
