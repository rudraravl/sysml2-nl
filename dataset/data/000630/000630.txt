This is a model of a classic hot water bottle system, designed to provide sustained therapeutic warmth for comfort and pain relief. The system is composed of three main physical components that work together to safely contain and gradually release heat from hot water.

The core of the system is a flexible **Rubber Container**, which has a specific volume capacity (e.g., 2 liters) and is made of a material with defined thickness and thermal properties. This is the main pouch that holds the hot water. It is designed to withstand a specific range of operating temperatures without degrading.

To prevent leaks, a **Screw Cap** securely fastens to the container's filling opening. The effectiveness of this cap is critical, and its ability to form a tight seal is a key attribute of the system's safety.

Finally, a soft **Fabric Cover**, typically made of a material like cotton fleece, encases the rubber container. This cover serves a dual purpose: it provides thermal insulation to slow down the rate of heat loss, and it ensures the external surface remains at a comfortable temperature for direct contact with the skin.

The hot water bottle operates through a simple lifecycle. It starts in an **empty** state at room temperature. A user then **fills** it with hot water, taking care not to exceed its capacity. After filling, the bottle is **sealed** with the screw cap. Once sealed and applied to the body, it enters an **active** state where it radiates heat, providing warmth. Over time, as it transfers thermal energy to its surroundings, it gradually cools down until it is eventually emptied and returned to its initial state. The system is designed to meet specific performance requirements, such as retaining a therapeutic level of warmth for at least two hours.
