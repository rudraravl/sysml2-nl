This model represents a high-performance surveillance radar system, specifically the AN/APG-77, which is designed to detect, locate, and track airborne and surface targets. The system operates by transmitting focused radio-frequency (RF) pulses and then capturing and analyzing the weak echoes that reflect off objects in its environment.

The radar is composed of several key components that work together in a coordinated cycle. A **Transmitter** generates the powerful, high-frequency pulses needed for detection. These pulses are sent to an **Antenna**, which focuses them into a directional beam and radiates them into the designated search area. The antenna can electronically steer this beam to scan a large volume of space or to focus on a single object.

When a pulse hits a target, a faint echo bounces back and is collected by the same antenna. This weak signal is passed to a sensitive **Receiver**, which amplifies it and filters out background noise. The amplified echo is then sent to a **Signal Processor**, the system's "brain." The processor analyzes the echo's properties to extract critical information: it calculates the target's distance (range) based on the time delay of the echo's return, its speed (velocity) based on the Doppler frequency shift, and its precise direction (azimuth and elevation).

This entire process is managed by a **Mode Controller**, which dictates the radar's operational state based on external commands or situational changes. The system can operate in a passive **Standby** mode, an active **Search** mode where it scans a wide area for new targets, or a dedicated **Track** mode where it continuously locks onto and monitors a specific target after detection. The final, processed target data is then output to an external system, such as a vehicle's mission computer or a pilot's display.
