This SysML v2 model describes a system-of-systems architecture.  The model focuses on the relationships between different enterprises and the systems they own.

The core components are:

* **Enterprises:**  Abstract entities representing organizations (defined as `Enterprise`).  The model specifically shows `Enterprise_alpha` and `Enterprise_beta`.

* **Systems:**  Three types of systems are defined: `System_a`, `System_b`, and `System_c`.  These are abstract system types.

* **Individual Systems:** The model instantiates specific instances of these systems: `system_a_alpha`, `system_a_beta`, `system_b_beta`, `System_a1`, `System_a2`, `System_b1`, and `System_c1`.  These represent concrete systems within the system-of-systems.

**Relationships and Ownership:**

* `Enterprise_alpha` owns `system_a_alpha`, which is further specified as `system_a1`.
* `Enterprise_beta` owns `system_a_beta` (specified as `system_a2`) and `system_b_beta` (specified as `system_b1`).

The model highlights the key characteristic of a system-of-systems:  **the distribution of systems across different enterprises.**

* **What constitutes a system-of-systems:** The model uses two examples of `System_c1` to illustrate this.  One example (`'not system-of-systems'`) shows `System_a2` and `System_b1` together, but within the *same* enterprise; this is *not* considered a system-of-systems.  The other example (`'system-of-systems'`) shows `System_a1` and `System_a2` together, but owned by *different* enterprises (`Enterprise_alpha` and `Enterprise_beta` respectively); this *is* considered a system-of-systems.

In summary, the model uses SysML's capabilities to define types and instances of enterprises and systems, establishing ownership relationships and illustrating the crucial difference between a collection of systems and a true system-of-systems based on the distribution of ownership across different organizational entities.
