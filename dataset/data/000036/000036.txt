This model describes a conceptual framework for defining and calculating the mass of various physical objects, from simple parts to complex assemblies. It establishes a set of rules for how mass "rolls up" from individual components into a final, total value.

At its core, any object in this system is understood to have two types of mass: its own intrinsic mass (the weight of the object by itself) and a total mass (which includes the weight of any parts it contains).

The system defines three distinct types of objects:

1.  A **simple object**, which is a fundamental, indivisible part like a single bolt or a solid block of metal. For this type of object, its total mass is exactly the same as its own intrinsic mass.

2.  A **composite object**, which is an assembly made of other subcomponents. Its total mass is calculated by taking its own intrinsic mass (for example, the weight of an empty car chassis or an electronics enclosure) and adding the total masses of all the subcomponents it contains.

3.  A special **filtered-mass composite object**, which is an assembly where the mass calculation is selective. Like a standard composite object, it adds its own mass to that of its subcomponents. However, it *only includes subcomponents that weigh more than a specific minimum threshold*. Any part lighter than this threshold is ignored in the final mass calculation. This could be used, for example, to calculate the structural weight of an assembly by including major components but intentionally excluding minor items like wiring, labels, or small fasteners.
