This model describes a complete digital Resistance Thermometer system, a device designed for precise temperature measurement. It functions by measuring the change in electrical resistance of a sensor, which varies predictably with the surrounding temperature.

The system is composed of several interconnected components that work together to produce a temperature reading. At its core is an RTD (Resistance Temperature Detector) sensor, which is the actual sensing element. This sensor requires a stable, low-level electrical current to operate, which is provided by a dedicated Excitation Source.

The measurement process begins when the excitation source sends a constant current through the RTD sensor. The sensor's electrical resistance changes based on the ambient temperature, which in turn alters the voltage of the signal. This raw, low-level voltage signal is then sent to a Signal Conditioner. This component's job is to amplify the weak signal and filter out any electrical noise, ensuring a clean and reliable input for the next stage.

Once conditioned, the signal is passed to a Temperature Converter. This unit uses a known calibration formula to translate the measured electrical resistance value into an actual temperature reading. The final reading, which includes the temperature, a timestamp, and an accuracy value, is then sent to a Display for the user to see.

The entire system is designed to meet specific performance goals, including high accuracy, a fast response time of under two seconds, and the ability to operate across a very wide temperature range from -200°C to 850°C. The thermometer operates in a cycle, moving from an idle state, through the distinct phases of sensing, converting, and displaying, before returning to idle to await the next measurement.
