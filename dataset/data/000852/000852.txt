This model describes an automated bilge pumping system, typically found on marine vessels like boats and ships. Its primary purpose is to automatically detect and remove unwanted water that accumulates in the bilge, the lowest compartment of a hull, to prevent flooding and ensure the vessel's stability and safety.

The system is composed of several key components working together. A **Bilge Compartment** acts as the collection point for water. The water level within this compartment is continuously monitored by a **Float Switch**. This switch is the system's main sensor and controller; it's designed to automatically activate when the water rises to a pre-set high-water mark and deactivate once the water has been pumped down to a low-water mark.

When the float switch activates, it sends a start command to the electric **Bilge Pump**. This pump, powered by the vessel's **Power Supply** (typically a 12-volt system), draws water from the bilge compartment and expels it overboard through a **Discharge Outlet**.

The pump's operation is managed by an internal controller. When it receives a start command, it first enters a brief priming state before running continuously to pump the water out. The system is designed with safety in mind; it can detect fault conditions such as running dry (no water at the inlet) or excessive back-pressure from a blockage. If a fault is detected, the pump automatically shuts down to prevent damage and signals an error. The pump's operation ceases when the float switch detects the water has reached the low-water mark, sending a stop command. The system then remains in a standby state, ready for the next cycle.
