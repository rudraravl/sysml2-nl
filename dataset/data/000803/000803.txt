This model describes a surgical trocar system, a medical device used to create an access port into a patient's body cavity during minimally invasive (laparoscopic) surgery. Its primary function is to puncture the abdominal wall and establish a sealed channel through which surgical instruments and a camera can be inserted, while also allowing the cavity to be inflated with gas.

The system is composed of four main parts that work together:

1.  **Obturator:** This is a removable, sharp-tipped rod that sits inside the cannula during insertion. Its purpose is to pierce the abdominal tissue. The system can be configured with different types of tips—such as bladed, non-bladed, or optical—to minimize tissue trauma during entry. Once the device is in place, the obturator is removed.

2.  **Cannula:** This is the main hollow tube that remains in the body wall after the obturator is withdrawn. It acts as the working channel, or port, allowing surgeons to pass instruments of various sizes through it into the surgical site.

3.  **Seal Assembly:** Located at the top of the cannula, this is a crucial component that prevents the insufflation gas from leaking out of the abdominal cavity. It is designed to maintain a tight seal around surgical instruments as they are inserted, manipulated, and removed. The seal has several operational states, including an initial open state, a sealed state for surgery, and a compromised state if a leak is detected.

4.  **Valve Assembly:** This is a port on the side of the cannula that connects to a gas supply. It regulates the flow of insufflation gas (like CO2) into the abdomen to create and maintain a stable pressure, a condition known as pneumoperitoneum. This inflation lifts the abdominal wall away from the internal organs, creating a clear and safe space for the surgeon to work.

In a typical surgical procedure, the surgeon first uses the complete trocar assembly to penetrate the abdominal wall. The obturator is then removed, the seal is activated, and the abdomen is inflated with gas through the valve. The cannula then serves as a stable, sealed port for the duration of the operation, enabling the safe and effective use of surgical instruments. The entire system is designed with key safety requirements in mind, including limiting the insertion force, ensuring instrument compatibility, and maintaining the integrity of the gas seal.
