This model describes a complete nasal cannula oxygen delivery system, a medical device designed to provide supplemental oxygen therapy to a patient with breathing difficulties. The primary goal is to increase the patient's blood oxygen saturation by delivering a controlled flow of high-purity oxygen directly into their nostrils.

The system is composed of several key physical components that work in sequence. It begins with an **Oxygen Source**, such as a compressed oxygen tank or a hospital wall outlet, which supplies medical-grade, high-concentration oxygen under pressure. This oxygen then flows into a **Flow Meter**, a precision device that allows a clinician to set and regulate the exact rate of oxygen delivery, typically measured in liters per minute.

From the flow meter, the oxygen is transported through flexible, medical-grade **Oxygen Tubing** to the patient. At the end of the tubing is the **Nasal Cannula**, which consists of two soft prongs that are comfortably inserted into the patient's nostrils. These prongs deliver the oxygen, which then mixes with the air the patient inhales, enriching it with a higher oxygen concentration.

The system operates in several states. It can be in an **idle** state, where it is powered on and ready but not delivering oxygen. Upon receiving a start command, it enters an **active** state, where it continuously regulates the oxygen flow and monitors the patient's response. The therapy can be adjusted as needed and stopped with a specific command, returning the system to idle. An **alarm** state exists to indicate a system fault or problem. Overall, the system is designed with patient safety and comfort in mind, ensuring a stable, controlled flow of oxygen at safe pressures.
