This is a hydraulic piston actuation system designed to convert pressurized fluid energy into powerful, controlled linear mechanical force and motion. It is the type of system commonly found in industrial machinery, construction equipment, or manufacturing presses.

The system is composed of three main functional units: a power unit, a control valve, and an actuator.

The **Hydraulic Power Unit** acts as the heart of the system, containing a pump that generates high-pressure hydraulic fluid. This unit supplies the pressurized fluid needed to drive the entire operation.

A **Control Valve** directs the flow of this high-pressure fluid from the power unit. Based on external commands, the valve can be set to different positions to make the piston extend, retract, or hold its current position by trapping fluid in the cylinder.

The **Piston Actuator** is the component that performs the mechanical work. It consists of a hollow **Cylinder Body** that houses a **Piston Head**. This piston head is attached to a solid **Piston Rod** that extends outside the cylinder to connect to an external load. Specialized **Seals** are used to prevent fluid leakage and ensure pressure is maintained.

In operation, the control valve directs pressurized fluid into the cylinder, pushing against the piston head. This creates a strong force that drives the piston and rod outwards, performing an extension stroke. To retract, the valve redirects the fluid flow, pushing the piston back to its starting position. The system operates through distinct states, including idle (waiting for commands), extending, retracting, and holding a fixed position, allowing for precise control over its movement and force application.
