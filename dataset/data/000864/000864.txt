This model describes a fluid pumping system designed to transfer a liquid from a source reservoir to a piece of process equipment.

The core of the system is a diaphragm pump, which is composed of several key components working in unison. A flexible diaphragm, in this case made of PTFE, is moved back and forth by an actuator (specified as a pneumatic type). This movement alters the volume of an internal pump chamber.

The pump's operation is a two-stroke cycle. During the intake stroke, the actuator retracts the diaphragm, increasing the chamber's volume and creating a low-pressure area. This pressure drop opens a one-way inlet check valve, drawing fluid into the chamber from the reservoir.

Following this, the discharge stroke begins. The actuator pushes the diaphragm forward, compressing the fluid inside the chamber. This action forces the inlet valve shut and opens a separate outlet check valve, expelling the pressurized fluid downstream to the process equipment. The system repeats this cycle at a specific frequency to create a continuous flow.

The overall system includes the source reservoir, the diaphragm pump assembly, and the destination equipment. It is also instrumented with sensors to monitor performance, including pressure sensors at the pump's inlet and outlet, and a flow sensor to measure the final output. The design has specific performance and reliability requirements, such as delivering a minimum flow rate and pressure, operating efficiently, and ensuring a long cycle life for the diaphragm.
