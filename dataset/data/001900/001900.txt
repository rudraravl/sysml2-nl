This model describes a complete aircraft wing system, designed to generate the necessary aerodynamic lift to keep an aircraft airborne and to provide control for maneuvering. The system is defined by its physical structure, its control surfaces, and its aerodynamic behavior under various flight conditions.

The wing itself has a 12-meter span and is swept back with a slight upward (dihedral) angle, a common design for stability and performance. Its physical construction is based on a conventional aluminum structure. The core strength comes from two main load-bearing beams, a front spar and a rear spar, that run the length of the wing. A series of 24 ribs are spaced along these spars to define and maintain the wing's precise airfoil shape, which is the curved cross-section responsible for generating lift. The entire framework is covered by a thin aluminum skin on both the upper and lower surfaces, creating a smooth, aerodynamic profile and contributing to the wing's overall strength.

For flight control, the wing is equipped with movable control surfaces. These include ailerons, which are used to roll the aircraft left or right, and flaps, which can be extended to increase lift at lower speeds, such as during takeoff and landing.

Functionally, the system is designed to interact with the surrounding airflow to produce lift and drag. It models how the wing senses the properties of the air it moves through—specifically its velocity, pressure, density, and its angle relative to the wing (angle of attack). Based on these real-time conditions, the system calculates the resulting aerodynamic forces.

The wing's operational life is characterized by several distinct states. It is considered `idle` when on the ground with no significant airflow. As the aircraft gains speed, the wing transitions to a `generatingLift` state, which is its normal condition during flight. When the pilot uses the controls, it enters a `maneuvering` state as the ailerons or flaps are deflected. The model also accounts for a critical flight condition: if the wing's angle of attack becomes too high, it will enter a `stalled` state, losing lift. It can only recover to normal flight once the angle is corrected and stable airflow is restored.
