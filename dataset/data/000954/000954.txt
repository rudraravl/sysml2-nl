This model describes a volute centrifugal pump system, a type of mechanical device designed to move a fluid by converting rotational kinetic energy into hydrodynamic energy.

The system is composed of three primary components that work in sequence: an impeller, a volute casing, and a discharge pipe. The process begins when an external drive shaft provides rotational power to the impeller. This impeller, a spinning component with blades, draws fluid in through a central suction port. As the impeller rotates, it flings the fluid outward at high velocity using centrifugal force.

This high-velocity fluid then exits the impeller and enters the volute casing, a specially designed spiral-shaped chamber that progressively widens. This expanding shape forces the fluid to slow down, and in doing so, efficiently converts its kinetic energy (velocity) into static pressure. Finally, the now-pressurized fluid is channeled through a discharge pipe and out of the pump's discharge port.

The pump's operation is managed through several states. It starts in a "stopped" state and transitions to a "starting" phase to ramp up to its nominal speed. Once running, it continuously pumps fluid while monitoring for potentially damaging conditions like cavitation (the formation of vapor bubbles due to low inlet pressure). On command, the system enters a "stopping" phase, safely coasting down until it is fully stopped. The entire system is designed to meet specific performance requirements for flow rate, pressure increase, and hydraulic efficiency.
