This model describes an Odon Device, a medical system designed to assist in vaginal childbirth during the second stage of labor. Its primary function is to apply gentle, controlled traction to the baby's head to facilitate a safe delivery.

The core of the device is a rigid, cup-shaped applicator made of medical-grade plastic. Housed inside this cup is a soft, inflatable sleeve. In operation, this applicator is carefully placed over the baby's head. An external air pump then supplies air, managed by a pressure regulator, to inflate the sleeve. This inflation creates a soft and secure grip around the baby's head without causing trauma. A critical safety valve is integrated into the air system to automatically vent excess pressure, preventing over-inflation.

Once a secure grip is established, a medical professional uses an attached traction handle to apply a gentle pulling force at a controlled angle. The system is equipped with sensors that continuously monitor both the pressure within the inflatable sleeve and the amount of traction force being applied. This data is fed to a control unit, which ensures that all actions remain within strict, predefined safety limits for pressure and force, protecting both the mother and the baby.

The device operates through a clear sequence of states, starting from an idle state, then moving through positioning, inflating the sleeve, gripping the head, applying traction, and finally deflating and removing the device once the delivery is complete. This entire procedure is designed to be completed quickly to minimize stress.
