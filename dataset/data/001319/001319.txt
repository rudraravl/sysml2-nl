This model describes a self-contained, wearable "Wired Glove" system designed for real-time hand and finger motion capture. Its primary purpose is to translate complex hand movements into digital data, making it ideal for applications like virtual reality interaction, robotics control, or sign language translation.

The system is built around a physical glove made of conductive textile, which houses a suite of integrated electronic components. To capture detailed finger movements, it features five flex sensors, one for each finger, that measure the precise bend angle. Additionally, force sensors are embedded in the thumb and index finger to detect pressure and grip strength. An Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) is also included to track the overall position and orientation (roll, pitch, and yaw) of the hand in three-dimensional space.

All the raw data from these sensors is channeled to an onboard data processor. This central unit acts as the brain of the glove, first filtering the sensor data to remove noise and then applying calibration data to ensure accuracy. It then uses this refined information to recognize specific gestures, assigning a confidence score to each recognition.

Once a gesture is identified, the processed data—including the gesture type, hand position, and orientation—is sent to a wireless transmitter. This transmitter then relays the information to an external computer or device. The entire glove is powered by a rechargeable battery, designed to provide at least eight hours of continuous operation on a single charge.

The glove operates through a clear lifecycle: upon powering on, it enters a calibration mode. Once calibration is complete, it becomes active and begins capturing and transmitting gesture data in a continuous loop. The system is engineered for high performance, with requirements for low-latency (under 50 milliseconds) and high-accuracy (over 90%) gesture recognition.
