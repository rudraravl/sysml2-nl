This model represents the LUCAS (Lund University Cardiopulmonary Assist System), an automated mechanical device designed to perform high-quality chest compressions on patients in cardiac arrest. Its primary function is to provide continuous and consistent CPR, adhering to medical guidelines, which frees up medical personnel to focus on other critical tasks like airway management or administering medication.

The system is a portable, battery-powered unit designed for rapid deployment in emergency situations. A rechargeable **Battery Pack** provides power for at least 45 minutes of continuous operation. This battery powers the two main electronic components: an **Electric Motor** and a **Control Processor**.

At its core is a mechanical assembly driven by the electric motor. The motor powers a **Compression Piston** that moves up and down to perform the compressions. Attached to the end of this piston is a **Suction Cup Assembly** which is placed on the patient's chest. This suction cup is critical as it not only delivers the downward compression force but also actively lifts the chest during the decompression phase, ensuring full chest recoil.

A sophisticated Control Processor acts as the brain of the device. It uses feedback from an integrated **Depth Sensor** and **Force Sensor** to continuously monitor and adjust the compressions in real-time. This closed-loop control system ensures that each compression reaches the precise target depth (typically 50-55 mm) and rate (around 102 per minute) without applying excessive force.

An operator interacts with the LUCAS device through a simple **User Interface**. From here, they can power the device on, start, pause, or resume the CPR protocol. The device operates in several distinct modes: it can be powered **off**, placed in a **standby** mode ready for deployment, or become **active** to perform CPR. During active operation, it can be temporarily **paused** to allow for other medical procedures and then resumed. For safety, it includes an **emergencyStop** function that immediately halts the mechanism. The system also monitors its own health, entering a **lowBattery** state to warn operators when power is running low.
