This model represents a Michaab, a traditional, gravity-powered water management system designed for the dual purpose of milling grain and irrigating agricultural land. It is an integrated system that uses the natural flow of water to perform mechanical work and distribute resources.

The system begins with an Upper Reservoir, which serves as a large basin to collect and store water. From this reservoir, water is released into a primary Distribution Channel. The flow of water from this channel is precisely managed by a main Control Gate. This gate can be adjusted to various positions—from fully closed to fully open—to regulate the volume and speed of the water moving through the system.

After passing through the main gate, the water's path is split. A portion of the flow is directed through a secondary channel to a large Water Wheel. The force of the moving water turns the wheel, generating rotational mechanical power. This power is transferred directly to a Milling Mechanism, which consists of heavy upper and lower millstones. The rotation from the water wheel drives the millstones to grind grain. After powering the wheel, the water is collected in a Lower Reservoir.

Simultaneously, the main gate can divert a separate stream of water to an Irrigation Outlet. This secondary flow is controlled by its own dedicated gate, ensuring that a measured amount of water is delivered to service surrounding agricultural areas.

The entire system operates in several distinct modes, including Filling the reservoir, Milling grain, providing Irrigation, or being in an Idle or Maintenance state. The system includes automated control logic; for example, it continuously monitors the water level in the Upper Reservoir and adjusts the main gate's position accordingly to maintain optimal flow. It also assesses water quality and can automatically reduce the flow to the irrigation outlets if the quality is deemed unsuitable for crops.
