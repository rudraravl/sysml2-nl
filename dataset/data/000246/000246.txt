This model describes a system for defining and classifying engineering materials based on their composition and physical properties. It establishes a classification hierarchy that distinguishes between fundamental substances, general materials, and more specific types like pure metals and alloys.

Pure metals, such as iron, carbon, and manganese, are treated as basic building blocks, each defined by its unique atomic mass. Alloys are represented as more complex materials, created by combining multiple base materials. The exact composition of an alloy is detailed by specifying each constituent material and its corresponding mass fraction—the percentage of its contribution to the total weight.

The system provides a concrete example of a high-tensile-strength steel, "Steel_980". This specific steel is defined as an alloy composed of iron, carbon, and manganese. A key performance property, its tensile strength, is explicitly quantified as 980 N/mm², indicating its high resistance to being pulled apart under tension.
