This model describes a Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) for a vehicle. The system is designed to continuously track the air pressure and temperature in all four tires and alert the driver if the pressure becomes dangerously low or high.

The system is composed of four main electronic components working together. First, there is a battery-powered sensor installed in each of the vehicle's four tires (front-left, front-right, rear-left, and rear-right). These sensors periodically measure the specific pressure and temperature inside their respective tire.

Once a measurement is taken, each sensor wirelessly transmits its data, which includes its location, the pressure and temperature readings, its battery level, and a timestamp. A central Radio Frequency (RF) Receiver, mounted in the vehicle's body, picks up these signals from all four sensors.

The receiver then passes this information to the system's central "brain," an Electronic Control Unit (ECU). The ECU analyzes the data from each tire, comparing the measured pressure against pre-set safe operating thresholds for nominal, low, and critically low pressure.

Based on this evaluation, the ECU determines the status of each tire. If all pressures are normal, the system remains in a quiet monitoring state. However, if a tire's pressure drops to a warning level (e.g., below 28 psi) or a critical level (e.g., below 22 psi), the ECU generates a specific alert message. This message identifies the affected tire and the severity of the situation.

Finally, this alert message is sent to a Driver Display unit on the vehicle's dashboard. The display, which is normally idle, activates to show a visual warning and may also play an audible alert to immediately notify the driver about the specific tire that requires attention. The system will continue to monitor the situation and will return to a normal state once the tire pressure is corrected.
