This is an Optoelectronic Plethysmography (OEP) system, a non-invasive medical device designed for continuous, real-time monitoring of a patient's breathing. The system works by precisely tracking the three-dimensional movement of the chest and abdomen during respiration to calculate key metrics like breathing rate and the volume of air inhaled and exhaled.

The physical setup involves placing a set of 89 small, reflective markers on the patient's torso, covering the rib cage and abdominal areas. These markers are illuminated by eight infrared light sources positioned around the patient. A corresponding set of eight high-speed infrared cameras captures the light reflected from the moving markers as the patient breathes.

The data processing pipeline begins with an Image Processing Unit, which receives the video feeds from all cameras. It identifies the markers in each frame and uses triangulation to calculate the precise 3D coordinates of every marker. This positional data is then passed to a Volume Computation Unit, which mathematically reconstructs the chest wall surface and calculates the changing volumes of the rib cage and abdominal compartments.

This stream of volume data is fed into a Respiratory Analysis Unit. This component analyzes the volume changes over time to derive clinically relevant metrics, such as tidal volume (the volume of a single breath), respiratory rate (breaths per minute), and minute ventilation. This unit is also responsible for patient safety; it compares the measured values against normal physiological ranges and can trigger an alert if it detects abnormal breathing patterns.

A central controller manages the system's overall operation, transitioning it through different states such as offline, calibrating, monitoring, and alerting. All computed respiratory information is sent to a display monitor, allowing clinical staff to observe the patient's breathing status continuously. The entire system is powered by a central power supply.
