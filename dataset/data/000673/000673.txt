This model represents a Mammotome system, a medical device used for performing minimally invasive, vacuum-assisted breast biopsies. The system is designed to accurately acquire multiple tissue samples from a suspicious area in the breast through a single, small incision, guided by real-time imaging.

The system is composed of several integrated subsystems that work in sequence. An **Imaging Subsystem**, likely an ultrasound probe, is used first to locate the target tissue and determine the precise depth for insertion. A central **Control Console** serves as the user interface, allowing a clinician to manage the entire procedure.

Once the target is identified, a sterile, disposable **Probe Assembly** is inserted into the breast. This probe is the primary tool for the biopsy, containing a hollow chamber with an opening (or aperture) and an internal rotating cutter. A powerful **Vacuum Subsystem** is then activated, which creates negative pressure through the probe to gently pull the targeted tissue into the aperture.

With the tissue secured by the vacuum, the **Cutter Subsystem** activates a high-speed rotating blade inside the probe, which precisely excises a tissue sample. This sample is then transported by the vacuum through the probe and deposited into a **Sample Collector** for later pathological examination.

A key feature of the system is its ability to collect multiple samples without removing the probe. After each sample is taken, the probe's aperture can be rotated to a new position, allowing the clinician to sample different areas around the initial site. The system operates through distinct modes, including standby, imaging, positioning the probe, sampling, and retrieving the sample, ensuring a controlled and safe procedure.
