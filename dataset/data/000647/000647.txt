This model describes an integrated Medical Examination Station, a comprehensive system designed to acquire, analyze, and record a patient's key vital signs in a clinical setting. It acts as a modern, all-in-one vital signs cart or bedside monitoring unit.

The station is composed of several distinct medical instruments that work together:

*   **A Digital Thermometer:** An infrared device for taking body temperature readings.
*   **A Sphygmomanometer:** An automated, cuff-based monitor for measuring systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
*   **A Pulse Oximeter:** A sensor that measures both the oxygen saturation level in the blood and the patient's heart rate.
*   **A Stethoscope:** A dual-head acoustic device used by a clinician to listen to heart and lung sounds, from which an estimated respiratory rate is also derived.

These individual instruments are all connected to a **Central Monitoring Unit**, which serves as the brain of the system. This unit collects the data from each sensor, compiles it into a single timestamped record for a specific patient, and then analyzes the information. It compares the readings against established normal ranges for vital signs like temperature, blood pressure, and heart rate. Based on this analysis, it generates a diagnostic assessment, flagging any abnormal results and classifying their severity from mild to critical.

All the electronic components receive power from a central **Power Distribution Unit**, which ensures they get a stable and appropriate voltage.

The system's operational workflow is designed for clinical efficiency. It operates in several states:
1.  **Idle:** The station is on and ready for use.
2.  **Measuring:** Upon starting an examination, the various instruments begin actively acquiring patient data.
3.  **Analyzing:** Once measurements are complete, the central unit processes the data to look for abnormalities.
4.  **Alerting:** If a critical issue is detected (e.g., dangerously low oxygen), the system immediately enters an alerting state, activating alarms to notify medical staff.
5.  **Recording:** All collected data is sent to a dedicated **Data Recorder**, which stores the patient's vital signs for their medical record and allows for later retrieval of historical data.

After an alert is acknowledged or data is successfully recorded, the system returns to its idle state, ready for the next patient. The entire system is designed for accuracy, speed (completing a full assessment in under 5 minutes), and safety, adhering to medical standards for electrical safety and patient data privacy.
