This model describes a foundational system of standardized connectors and interfaces, defining the fundamental rules for how different components within a larger structure are linked together. It's not a single physical object, but rather the blueprint for a universal connection framework, much like the standards for plumbing fittings or electrical wiring.

The core of this system is the general concept of a "Connection." This represents any type of link or interface that joins components. It is treated as both a distinct part in itself (like a physical cable, pipe, or bracket) and as the functional link that creates a relationship between other parts.

A more specific and common type of connection defined here is the "Binary Connection." This is a simple point-to-point link that joins exactly two components, having a clear "source" and "target" end. Real-world examples would be a single ethernet cable running between a computer and a router, a pipe connecting a pump to a tank, or a structural beam joining two support columns.

Finally, the system recognizes that any complex assembly is made up of numerous connections. It describes the ability for a system to contain a whole network or collection of these links, representing the complete set of all wiring, piping, or structural joints that hold the entire structure together and allow its parts to interact.
