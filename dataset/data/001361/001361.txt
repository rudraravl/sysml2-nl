This is a sophisticated decision-support system designed to manage and mitigate "automation bias" in environments where a human operator works alongside an automated assistant. The system's primary goal is to ensure that operators remain critically engaged and don't blindly trust or over-rely on automated recommendations, especially in complex or high-stakes situations.

The system operates in a continuous cycle. First, a **Situation Analyzer** ingests and processes raw data about a current event or problem. This processed information is then passed to an **Automated Advisor**, which acts as an AI or expert system. The Advisor generates a formal recommendation, which includes not only a suggested course of action but also a confidence level (from very low to very high) and the rationale behind its conclusion.

This automated assessment is then presented to a human at an **Operator Workstation**. The system carefully captures the operator's response, including their final decision, whether they accepted the automation's advice, and—critically—how long they took to deliberate.

Simultaneously, a dedicated **Bias Monitor** analyzes the operator's pattern of behavior. It calculates a bias metric by considering factors like the automation's confidence, the operator's acceptance rate over time, and their deliberation speed. If the monitor detects that the operator is accepting recommendations too quickly or too frequently, it determines that automation bias is emerging. Depending on the severity of the bias (from minimal to critical), the system can issue alerts to the operator's workstation, prompting them to be more diligent or providing guidance on how to mitigate their over-reliance.

Finally, a **Decision Integrator** combines the machine's recommendation with the operator's input to produce the final, official decision. This component is intelligent; if it knows that a high level of bias has been detected, it can be configured to apply a "compensation factor," giving more weight to the operator's judgment to ensure a balanced outcome.

The entire event—from the initial data to the final decision, including the operator's response and the calculated bias level—is logged by an **Event Recorder**. This creates a comprehensive history that can be used for later analysis, training, and improving the system's bias detection capabilities.
