This model describes a medical Bubble Oxygenator System, a life-support device that temporarily functions as an artificial lung for a patient, typically during open-heart surgery. Its primary purpose is to add oxygen to and remove carbon dioxide from the patient's blood outside of their body (extracorporeally).

The system is composed of several key modules that work in sequence. Deoxygenated venous blood from the patient enters the system and flows into an **Oxygenation Column**. Simultaneously, medical-grade oxygen is supplied to a **Bubble Generation Module**, which creates a stream of very fine oxygen bubbles.

These bubbles are introduced directly into the blood within the Oxygenation Column. The large surface area of the countless bubbles facilitates a rapid and efficient gas exchange: oxygen dissolves into the blood, binding with hemoglobin, while excess carbon dioxide diffuses out of the blood and into the bubbles.

After the gas exchange is complete, the now-oxygenated blood is full of residual gas bubbles. To prevent a fatal gas embolism, the blood is passed through a critical **Defoaming Unit**. This component uses specialized surfaces to coalesce and remove all gas bubbles, separating the bubble-free blood from the waste gas (mostly carbon dioxide and unused oxygen), which is then safely vented through an exhaust port.

Throughout this process, a **Temperature Control Unit** actively manages the blood's temperature, either heating or cooling it as necessary to keep it within a safe, physiological range. The overall blood flow through the device is managed by a **Flow Regulator**, ensuring the rate is appropriate for the patient's needs. Finally, an integrated **Oxygenation Monitor** continuously samples the outgoing arterial blood to measure its oxygen and carbon dioxide levels, providing real-time feedback on the system's performance and ensuring the blood is safe to return to the patient.
