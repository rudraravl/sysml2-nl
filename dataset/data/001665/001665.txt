This describes an advanced, autonomous software-defined radio (SDR) receiver system named Lagotek. Its primary purpose is to continuously monitor a wide range of the electromagnetic spectrum, from 100 MHz to 6 GHz, in order to capture specific radio frequency (RF) signals, process them in real-time, and extract the digital data they carry. The system is designed for high-sensitivity, reliable, and continuous operation, capable of running for days at a time without manual intervention.

The system's operation is a coordinated effort among its specialized components:

1.  An **Antenna Array** first captures the ambient RF signals from the environment.
2.  The signal is then passed to a **Front-End Receiver**, which acts as a sensitive amplifier and tuner. It boosts the faint signal and converts it to a frequency suitable for digital processing.
3.  The core of the system is the **Digital Signal Processor (DSP)**. This high-speed processing engine takes the prepared signal, converts it from analog to digital, and performs the complex tasks of demodulating it (separating the information from the carrier wave), decoding the raw data, and correcting transmission errors.
4.  Overseeing the entire process is a **Control Processor**. This acts as the system's brain, autonomously managing the other components. It monitors signal quality and intelligently adjusts the receiver's parameters, such as frequency and gain, to find and maintain a stable lock on a target signal.
5.  Finally, a **Data Output Interface** takes the clean, error-corrected data from the DSP, buffers it, and streams it to external systems over a high-speed Gigabit Ethernet connection.

The Lagotek system operates through several intelligent states. After powering on, it enters an idle mode. When activated, it begins searching for a signal of interest. Once a signal is detected, the system locks onto it, tracking it even if it fluctuates. It then continuously processes the signal to extract and output a steady stream of data. If the signal lock is ever lost, the system automatically returns to its searching state to reacquire it, ensuring resilient and persistent operation.
