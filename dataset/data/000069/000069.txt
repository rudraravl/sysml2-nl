This model describes a generic system for tracking and analyzing complex, hierarchical processes or events over time. It's a conceptual framework rather than a single physical object, applicable to things like project management, manufacturing workflows, or transaction logging.

The fundamental element of this system is a "process" or "occurrence." Each process has its own distinct properties and can be broken down into smaller, nested sub-processes. This allows for a detailed, hierarchical view of a complex operation. Furthermore, any process can be formally linked to another, separate process, enabling the system to model dependencies or relationships between them.

A process involves various physical items or data objects and is composed of different functional parts. The system is designed to capture the state of any process at a specific moment in time, creating a "snapshot" for later analysis. It can also isolate and examine the activity of a process over a specific period, referred to as a "timeslice."

In addition to these general processes, the system also tracks unique, one-time "individual events." These are singular, non-repeatable occurrences that can be analyzed with the same level of detail, having their own specific snapshots and time intervals, representing significant milestones or singular incidents within the overall system.
