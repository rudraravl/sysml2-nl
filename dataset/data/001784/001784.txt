This model represents a comprehensive natural gas distribution system, designed to take natural gas from a high-pressure transmission pipeline, reduce its pressure safely, and deliver it to various end-users.

The system begins at a high-pressure supply point, which serves as the entry for gas from a larger transmission network. This high-pressure gas is then fed into a central pressure regulation station, the core of the system. This station's primary job is to step down the gas pressure to a lower, safer level suitable for the local distribution network.

From the regulation station, the gas flows into an extensive distribution pipeline network that transports it across a wide area. Integrated into this network are critical safety and monitoring components. A dedicated pressure monitor constantly tracks the system's pressure, ready to issue warnings or alarms if it deviates from safe operational limits. For overpressure protection, a safety valve is installed, which will automatically open and vent excess gas if the pressure exceeds a critical set point.

The pipeline network branches out to deliver gas to different types of customers, each served by a dedicated metering station. These stations are tailored for residential, commercial, and industrial users, where they measure the volume of gas consumed and perform a final pressure regulation for delivery to the end-user's equipment.

The system's overall operation is managed through several distinct states. It undergoes a 'startup' sequence with system checks before entering 'normal operation,' where it continuously monitors and regulates pressure. To handle periods of high consumption, it can enter a 'peak demand' state to increase flow capacity. The system also accommodates planned 'maintenance' and has an 'emergency' state that activates safety protocols in response to critical overpressure events.
