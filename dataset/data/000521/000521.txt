This model describes a visual prosthesis system, commonly known as a bionic eye, designed to restore partial vision to individuals with certain types of blindness, such as retinal degenerative diseases.

The system is composed of several key components that work together to capture the visual world and translate it into signals the brain can understand. An external camera, likely mounted on a pair of glasses, continuously captures the user's surroundings at 30 frames per second. This live video feed is sent to a portable Image Processing Unit. This processor enhances the raw image by performing operations like edge detection and contrast enhancement, simplifying the visual information to make it more suitable for the implant.

The processed, high-contrast image is then passed to a Stimulus Generator. This component acts as a translator, converting the visual pattern of the image into a corresponding electrical stimulation pattern. It maps different parts of the image to a 60-electrode grid and calculates the precise current levels needed for each electrode.

This electrical pattern is sent to the core of the system: a Retinal Electrode Array. This is a small implant surgically placed on the surface of the user's retina. When activated, its 60 microelectrodes deliver tiny, controlled electrical pulses to the remaining healthy retinal nerve cells. These cells then transmit signals to the brain, which interprets them as a pattern of light, allowing the user to perceive shapes, outlines, and movement. For safety, the implant also monitors the retinal tissue's response to the stimulation.

The entire system is powered by a central, rechargeable battery unit. This unit manages power distribution to all components and can operate in a low-power mode to conserve energy when the battery level is low. Additionally, a wireless transceiver is included, enabling communication for functions like receiving control commands or transmitting system data for calibration and monitoring.
