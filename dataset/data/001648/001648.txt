This model describes an autonomous robotic vacuum cleaner system, named "Dustbot," designed for automated cleaning of indoor floor surfaces. The system consists of the mobile robot itself and a stationary docking station used for charging.

The Dustbot is equipped with a comprehensive sensor suite for navigating and interacting with its environment. It uses six proximity sensors to detect and avoid obstacles from up to 0.8 meters away, preventing collisions with furniture and walls. It also has three dedicated floor sensors to assess the level of dirt on the surface, allowing it to focus cleaning efforts where they are most needed.

Its cleaning mechanism is composed of a main vacuum unit and a rotating brush assembly, which work together to lift and collect dust and debris from the floor. This collected waste is stored in an internal dust bin with a capacity of 0.6 kg. The robot's movement is managed by a navigation module that processes sensor data to plan paths and a motor controller that drives the wheels.

The Dustbot operates with a high degree of autonomy. Its core behavior involves continuously scanning the environment, navigating the cleaning area, and activating its vacuum and brushes when dirt is detected. The system is designed for self-sufficiency, featuring automated power and waste management. It constantly monitors its internal battery level; when the charge runs low, it will automatically cease cleaning and return to its docking station to recharge. Likewise, if its dust bin becomes full, it will navigate back to the dock to be emptied before resuming its cleaning tasks.

The robot operates through several distinct states, such as 'standby' while idle, 'active' for cleaning and navigating, and 'charging' when connected to its dock. This allows it to manage its mission, respond to environmental cues like obstacles or dirt, and maintain its own operational readiness without human intervention.
