This model represents a comprehensive, standardized digital library for defining physical quantities related to light, radiation, and color, based on the international standard ISO 80000-7. Instead of describing a single physical device, this model provides a formal and precise vocabulary for scientists and engineers to describe and analyze any system involving optics, lighting, or electromagnetic radiation. It functions as a foundational knowledge base.

The library categorizes concepts into several related domains:

*   **General Properties of Light:** It defines fundamental concepts like the **speed of light in a medium** and the **refractive index**, which describes how much light bends when passing through a material.

*   **Radiometry (Measurement of Radiation):** This section covers the measurement of electromagnetic radiation in terms of absolute power. It includes definitions for:
    *   **Radiant Energy:** The total energy of electromagnetic waves.
    *   **Radiant Flux (or Power):** The rate at which this energy is transferred, measured in Watts.
    *   **Irradiance:** The power of radiation received per unit area on a surface (e.g., sunlight hitting the ground).
    *   **Radiance:** The intensity of radiation from a source or surface in a specific direction.
    *   Many of these have "spectral" versions (e.g., **Spectral Radiance**) which describe how the quantity is distributed across different wavelengths or colors.

*   **Photometry (Measurement of Visible Light):** This section defines quantities based on how they are perceived by the human eye. These are analogous to the radiometric quantities but are weighted by the eye's sensitivity to different colors. Key concepts include:
    *   **Luminous Flux:** The perceived power of light, measured in lumens.
    *   **Illuminance:** The amount of luminous flux falling on a surface, measured in lux. This is what a light meter in photography measures.
    *   **Luminance:** The perceived brightness of a surface from a particular angle, measured in candelas per square meter.
    *   **Luminous Efficacy:** A measure of how efficiently a light source converts power (Watts) into visible light (lumens).

*   **Photon Quantities:** It also defines light from a quantum perspective, describing quantities related to photons, the fundamental particles of light. This includes **photon number**, **photon flux** (the rate of photons flowing), and **photon energy**.

*   **Material Interaction:** The model defines how light interacts with materials, including:
    *   **Reflectance, Absorptance, and Transmittance:** Ratios describing how much incident light is reflected, absorbed, or transmitted through a material.
    *   **Emissivity:** A measure of a material's ability to emit thermal radiation.

*   **Colorimetry (Science of Color):** It provides a framework for measuring and specifying color based on human perception, using standard observer models from the CIE (International Commission on Illumination). This includes definitions for **tristimulus values (XYZ)**, **chromaticity coordinates**, and **color temperature**.

In essence, this is a foundational system of interconnected definitions that allows for the unambiguous modeling and simulation of anything from a simple light bulb and the room it illuminates, to complex camera sensors, solar panels, or the color science behind modern display screens.
