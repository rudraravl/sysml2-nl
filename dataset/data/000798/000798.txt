This model describes a portable medical traction splint, a device used by emergency responders to treat a patient with a fractured femur (thigh bone) in the field. Its primary purpose is to immobilize the leg and apply a controlled, steady pulling force (traction). This action helps to realign the broken bone, reduce pain and muscle spasms, and prevent further injury to blood vessels and tissues during transport to a hospital.

The splint is built around an adjustable, telescopic metal frame that can be extended or shortened to fit the length of a patient's leg. At the top of the frame is a proximal anchor pad, which is secured high up against the patient's groin and hip area. This pad acts as a stable brace, providing the counter-force needed for traction.

At the bottom of the frame, a ratchet windlass mechanism is used to apply the pulling force. This mechanism is connected to a distal ankle hitch, which is a specialized strap securely fastened around the patient's ankle above the boot. By turning the windlass, the medic can precisely increase the tension, gently stretching the leg. A built-in tension indicator provides a clear reading of the applied force, ensuring it stays within a safe and effective therapeutic range.

To fully stabilize the injured limb, a set of four support straps are used to secure the leg to the splint's frame along its length. The entire system is designed to be lightweight and portable for easy deployment in emergency situations.

Operationally, the splint is first taken from its stowed state and adjusted to the correct length. It is then positioned on the patient, with the anchor pad and ankle hitch being secured. The medic then uses the windlass to apply the required traction, constantly monitoring the force on the indicator. Once the correct tension is achieved, the leg is fully secured with the support straps. The tension is monitored and adjusted as needed throughout patient transport until it is professionally released at a medical facility.
