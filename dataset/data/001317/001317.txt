This is a wheel speed sensing system, a common component in vehicles used for anti-lock braking (ABS), traction control, and speedometer systems. It is designed to accurately measure the rotational speed of a vehicle's wheel and provide that data to the car's main computer.

The system is composed of two main physical parts: a toothed, ferromagnetic `Tone Ring` that rotates with the wheel, and a stationary `Wheel Speed Sensor` mounted close to it.

The sensor itself is a self-contained unit housed in a durable, IP67-rated stainless steel casing to protect it from harsh road conditions. Inside this housing, several components work together:
*   A `Sensing Element` detects the magnetic field variations created by the passing teeth of the rotating tone ring, generating a raw electrical pulse signal. The frequency of these pulses is directly proportional to the wheel's speed.
*   A `Signal Conditioner` takes this raw, often noisy signal, amplifies it, and filters it to produce a clean, reliable pulse train.
*   A `Speed Calculator` processor then uses the frequency of this clean signal, along with pre-configured information about the wheel's radius and the number of teeth on the tone ring, to calculate the wheel's precise angular velocity and the vehicle's corresponding linear speed.

The sensor continuously performs self-diagnostics, checking its power supply, signal quality, and internal temperature to ensure it is functioning correctly. It operates through various states: upon receiving power, it initializes and runs a self-check. If everything is normal, it becomes operational, ready to measure speed. If a fault is detected, it enters a fault state and can attempt to recover.

The final output is a structured data package containing the calculated speed, a timestamp, and a quality score, which is sent to other vehicle systems. The sensor also reports its operational status and any fault codes through a separate diagnostic port.
