This model describes an autonomous, two-wheeled mobile robot designed for reactive navigation in unknown or changing environments. The robot's core principle is to navigate by continuously and rapidly reacting to its immediate surroundings, rather than by planning a detailed path using an internal map.

The robot is equipped with a suite of sensors for 360-degree obstacle detection. This includes a forward-facing long-range ultrasonic sensor, a rear-facing ultrasonic sensor, and infrared proximity sensors on its left and right sides. These sensors constantly provide data about the distance and direction of nearby objects.

Its movement is controlled by two independent wheel motors, allowing it to move forward, backward, and turn on the spot.

The robot's "brain" is composed of two main subsystems: a perception system and a reactive controller.
1.  The **Perception Subsystem** gathers all the raw data from the ultrasonic and infrared sensors. It fuses this information to create a simple, immediate model of the environment, primarily focused on identifying the closest obstacle and the most open direction for travel.
2.  The **Reactive Behavior Controller** takes this simple environmental model and, based on a set of predefined rules (such as maintaining a minimum safe distance), makes an instantaneous decision. It then sends direct speed commands to the left and right wheel motors to execute the appropriate action, like turning away from an obstacle or moving forward into an open space.

The entire process—from sensing the environment to moving the wheels—operates in a very fast, continuous loop. This allows the robot's seemingly intelligent navigation to emerge from simple, rapid-fire reactions, enabling it to effectively avoid obstacles and explore spaces without prior knowledge or complex planning.
