This model describes an Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO) system, a sophisticated medical life-support machine that serves as an artificial heart and lung for patients in critical condition. Its primary function is to circulate a patient's blood outside their body, artificially oxygenate it, and return it to their circulatory system, bypassing the patient's failing organs.

The core of the system is a closed-loop circuit through which blood flows. The process begins with a **Venous Cannula**, a specialized tube that drains deoxygenated blood from the patient. This blood is then propelled through the circuit by a **Centrifugal Pump**, which acts as the system's heart, controlling the precise rate of blood flow.

From the pump, the blood enters a **Membrane Oxygenator**, the system's artificial lung. Here, the blood passes over a specialized membrane while oxygen is supplied on the other side. This process allows oxygen to diffuse into the blood and carbon dioxide to be removed, mimicking the function of healthy lungs.

Next, the newly oxygenated blood flows through a **Heat Exchanger**. This component carefully regulates the blood's temperature, warming it back to a normal body temperature (typically 37°C) before it is returned to the patient. Finally, the treated blood is sent back into the patient's body through an **Arterial Cannula**.

The entire operation is managed and monitored by a central **Control Unit**, which acts as the brain of the system. This unit receives continuous data from a suite of sensors placed throughout the circuit, including **Flow, Oxygen, Pressure, and Temperature Sensors**. Based on this real-time information and the settings programmed by a medical professional, the control unit constantly adjusts the pump's speed to maintain the target blood flow and oxygen levels.

For safety, a dedicated **Alarm System** is integrated with the control unit. If any parameter, such as pressure or flow rate, deviates from safe limits, the system triggers an immediate alarm to alert the clinical staff. The ECMO system operates through several distinct states, including an **off** state, a **priming** state to prepare the circuit by removing air, a **standby** mode ready for use, and an **active** state where it provides life-saving therapy.
