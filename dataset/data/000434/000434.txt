This model describes a Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) system, a medical device designed to track a person's glucose levels in real-time throughout the day and night.

The system consists of three main physical components. The first is a small, disposable sensor with a tiny filament that is inserted just under the skin. This electrochemical sensor continuously measures glucose concentration in the body's interstitial fluid, taking a new reading every five minutes. It is designed to function accurately for about 10 days before needing replacement.

Attached to the sensor is a compact wireless transmitter. This component takes the raw data from the sensor, processes it, and transmits it wirelessly over a range of up to 6 meters. It is powered by a small battery and is responsible for sending the glucose readings to a receiver.

The third component is a handheld receiver and display unit. This device receives the wireless data and presents it to the user. It shows the current glucose reading, a timestamp, and a trend arrow indicating if the user's glucose is rising, falling, or steady. The receiver can store up to 90 days of historical glucose data for review and analysis.

Internally, the system uses a data analysis processor to refine the raw sensor readings. This processor filters out signal noise to improve accuracy, calculates the rate and direction of glucose changes, and can even predict glucose levels up to 30 minutes into the future.

A critical part of the system is its alert management functionality. It constantly monitors glucose levels and will automatically generate alerts if they fall below a low threshold (70 mg/dL) or rise above a high threshold (180 mg/dL). It also has separate, more urgent alerts for critically low or high levels. These alerts can be delivered through audio or vibration on the receiver, warning the user of potentially dangerous situations.

To maintain its accuracy, the system includes a calibration manager. This requires the user to perform a traditional fingerstick blood glucose test and enter the value into the receiver at least once every 12 hours. This reference measurement is used to calibrate the sensor and ensure its readings remain reliable over its lifetime.
