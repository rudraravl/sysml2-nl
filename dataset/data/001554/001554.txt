This is a biomimetic robotic fish system designed for autonomous underwater exploration and environmental monitoring. The robot, which is half a meter long and weighs 3.5 kilograms, mimics the efficient swimming motion of a real fish. It uses an oscillating tail for primary propulsion, allowing it to reach speeds of up to 1.5 meters per second, and two independently controlled pectoral fins for steering, maneuvering, and maintaining stability in the water.

The system is engineered to operate autonomously for missions lasting up to four hours on a single battery charge and can dive to depths of 50 meters. A central navigation controller acts as the brain of the fish, processing data from a comprehensive suite of sensors. These include a proximity sensor to detect and avoid obstacles, a depth sensor to maintain its operational altitude in the water, and an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) to track its orientation and heading. It is also equipped with an environmental sensor package to collect scientific data like water temperature and salinity.

The robotic fish operates in several distinct modes depending on its mission and immediate surroundings. It uses a "Cruise" mode for efficient, straight-line travel, switches to a "Maneuver" mode to navigate around detected obstacles, and can enter a "Station Keeping" mode to hold its position for detailed observation. In case of a critical issue or low battery, it can initiate a "Surface" mode to safely return to the surface.

A dedicated battery pack powers all onboard electronics, including the sensors, actuators, and the navigation controller. The system features a critical battery management function that constantly monitors its power level and will automatically trigger an emergency return or surfacing procedure if the charge drops below a safe threshold. Finally, a communication module allows the robotic fish to transmit its collected environmental data and system status back to a base station and receive new mission commands.
