This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of quantities and units for the field of acoustics, based on the international standard ISO 80000-8. It is not a physical device, but rather a formal conceptual framework that provides a complete and unambiguous language for scientists and engineers to measure, analyze, and describe sound and acoustic phenomena.

The system defines the fundamental properties of sound waves. It begins with pressure, distinguishing between the baseline **static pressure** of a medium (like air) and the **sound pressure**, which is the rapid fluctuation above and below that static pressure caused by a sound wave. It also describes the physical motion of particles in the medium as the wave passes, defining their **displacement**, **velocity**, and **acceleration**.

Building on these basics, the framework covers the energy and power aspects of sound. It defines concepts like **sound energy density** (the amount of sound energy contained in a given volume), total **sound energy**, and **sound power** (the rate at which a source, like a speaker, radiates acoustic energy). It also defines **sound intensity**, which describes the flow of this power through a specific area and includes its direction. Another key concept is **sound exposure**, which measures the total acoustic energy received over a period of time.

Crucially for the field of acoustics, the system formalizes the common logarithmic scales used for measurement. This includes **Sound Pressure Level (SPL)**, **Sound Power Level**, and **Sound Exposure Level**, all of which are measured in decibels (dB). The definitions include the specific reference values required for accurate measurements in different media, such as in air versus in water.

Finally, the library specifies properties of the medium itself that affect sound, such as **acoustic impedance**, which characterizes how much a medium resists sound transmission. It also includes concepts for analyzing acoustic environments, like **reverberation time**, which is used to measure how long it takes for a sound to fade away in an enclosed space like a concert hall.
