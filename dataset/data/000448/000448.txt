This is a model of an esophageal stent system, a medical device designed to treat blockages or narrowing (strictures) in a patient's esophagus. Its primary purpose is to hold the esophagus open, restoring the patient's ability to swallow food and liquids.

The core of the system is the **Esophageal Stent**, a self-expanding, flexible tube made of a metallic mesh, typically nitinol. This mesh is designed to exert a continuous outward radial force to counteract the pressure from the diseased esophageal tissue, thereby keeping the passage open. The stent is often covered with a material like silicone to prevent tissue from growing into it and to facilitate smoother passage of food.

The stent has several key components that work together:
*   **Stent Mesh:** The main structural body that provides the strength and outward force.
*   **Stent Covering:** A partial or full layer over the mesh that ensures biocompatibility and prevents blockages from tissue ingrowth.
*   **Proximal and Distal Flanges:** These are flared ends on either side of the stent. They are wider than the main body and serve as anchors to press against the esophageal wall, preventing the stent from moving or migrating out of place.
*   **Lumen Channel:** This is the hollow, open passage through the center of the stent that allows a food bolus to travel from the throat to the stomach.

The system also includes a **Delivery System**, which is essentially a long, thin catheter used by a physician to insert and position the stent. The stent is loaded into this catheter in a compressed state. Once the catheter is guided to the location of the stricture, a control is activated to release the stent, which then automatically expands to its intended diameter.

The stent's operational life involves several states. It begins as **compressed** inside the delivery system, transitions to **deploying** as it expands, and becomes **fully expanded** once in place. In its ideal functional state, it is **patent**, meaning the lumen is open and clear. Over time, potential failure states include **migrated**, where it has moved from its original position, or **occluded**, where it has become blocked.
