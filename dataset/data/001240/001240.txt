This model describes a modular environmental sensing unit designed to measure physical conditions, process the raw data, and log the results for analysis.

The system is composed of three main parts: one or more sensors, a signal processor, and a data logger. The sensors are the primary interface with the physical world, responsible for taking measurements. The unit can be configured with different types of sensors; this specific example includes a temperature sensor and a pressure sensor, each with a defined operating range and a unique calibration factor to ensure accuracy.

When a measurement is taken, the raw data from a sensor is first sent to the signal processor. This component's job is to filter or otherwise clean the initial reading to produce a more accurate and reliable result. This processed measurement, which is a structured data packet containing the numeric value, its unit (e.g., Kelvin or Pascals), a timestamp, and a data quality score, is then sent to the data logger. The logger stores these readings in an internal buffer for later retrieval and analysis, ensuring a traceable history of measurements.

The entire unit operates according to a defined lifecycle. It begins in an "idle" state, waiting for a command. Upon activation, it enters a "measuring" state to capture a physical phenomenon. After data is acquired, it transitions to "processing," then "logging." Once the data is securely logged, the unit returns to its idle state. The system also includes a dedicated error state to manage and report any faults that might occur during the measurement phase.
