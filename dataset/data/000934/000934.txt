This model represents a Rushton Turbine Mixing System, a type of industrial equipment designed to effectively blend and disperse different fluid components within a large tank. Its primary purpose is to create a uniform, homogeneous mixture by generating high-intensity turbulence and large-scale fluid circulation.

The system consists of several key physical components working together. At its heart is a large, cylindrical **Mixing Vessel** which contains the fluid to be processed. Mounted on the walls inside this vessel are four stationary plates called **baffles**, which are crucial for preventing the entire liquid mass from simply spinning in a vortex, thereby promoting more effective top-to-bottom mixing.

An **Electric Motor** provides the power for the entire operation. This motor is connected to a long, vertical **Drive Shaft** that extends down into the vessel. At the end of this shaft, submerged in the fluid, is a specialized **Rushton Impeller**. This impeller is a disk with six vertical, flat blades, specifically designed to draw fluid in from above and below and expel it outwards in a strong, high-velocity radial flow towards the vessel walls.

The mixing process unfolds as a sequence of fluid dynamics. When the motor is started, it spins the shaft and the impeller. The impeller's rotation creates a powerful outward jet of fluid. This radial flow travels until it hits the vessel wall, where it is deflected by both the wall and the baffles, splitting into two large vertical circulation loops—one rising toward the surface and one descending toward the bottom. This continuous, large-scale circulation ensures the entire volume of fluid is constantly moving and turned over, while the high-speed flow from the impeller blades creates the intense turbulence needed to break down and blend components on a smaller scale.

The system's operation is managed through distinct states. It begins in an **Idle** state and transitions to a **Running** state upon receiving a start command. During operation, it maintains a target rotational speed to achieve the desired mixing efficiency. A stop command returns it to the idle state after spinning down.
