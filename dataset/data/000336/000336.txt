This model describes a foundational framework for representing any physical object or substance within a system. It's not a specific device, but rather a set of universal rules and concepts for defining the physical characteristics and interactions of components.

The core of this framework is the concept of an "Item," which represents any tangible object. Every item is defined by its physical properties: it has a distinct shape, occupies space, and exists for a specific duration of time.

Items can be simple and solid, or they can be complex, containing internal empty spaces or "voids." The framework also describes how items can be composed of smaller sub-items, allowing for the creation of complex assemblies from basic parts.

Furthermore, it defines the spatial relationships between different items. An item can be completely enclosed or enveloped by another. A specific interaction, "Touches," is defined for when two separate items are in direct physical contact with each other at the same moment in time. This provides a fundamental basis for modeling assembly, collision, and physical interaction in any real-world system.
