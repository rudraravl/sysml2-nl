This model describes a complete, self-contained steam-powered water pump system. Its primary purpose is to convert the thermal energy of high-pressure steam into mechanical work to move water from a low-pressure source to a high-pressure destination. It is a closed-loop system designed for continuous and reliable operation.

The system's core components work together in a clear sequence. A **steam boiler** acts as the power source, generating high-pressure steam. This steam is piped to a **steam cylinder**, which functions as the engine. Inside the cylinder, the expanding steam pushes a piston, converting thermal energy into mechanical power.

This mechanical power is used to drive a **reciprocating pump**, which is the component responsible for moving the water. The pump draws in water through a **suction check valve** and pushes it out at a higher pressure through a **discharge check valve**, ensuring water flows in only one direction. After the steam has done its work in the cylinder, the lower-pressure exhaust is sent to a **condenser** to be cooled and turned back into liquid water for discharge.

For safety and monitoring, the system is equipped with a **safety valve** that automatically releases pressure if it exceeds a critical threshold, preventing catastrophic failure. **Pressure gauges** are installed to monitor both the steam and water discharge pressures, while a **flow meter** measures the output of pumped water.

The pump operates through several distinct states. It remains in a **stopped** state until it receives a start command. It then enters a **starting** phase to warm up the cylinder and prime the pump. During its main **operating** state, the system can adjust its performance to handle high, normal, or low demand by regulating the steam flow. It can be shut down gracefully in a **stopping** sequence or enter an **emergency** state that immediately cuts off steam in response to a critical failure.
