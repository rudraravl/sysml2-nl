This model represents the International System of Quantities (ISQ), the internationally recognized conceptual framework for all physical measurements. It is not a physical object but rather a formal system that defines the fundamental quantities used in science and technology, based on the ISO/IEC 80000 standard.

The core of this system is a set of seven base quantities, from which all other physical quantities (such as velocity, force, or energy) can be derived. These base quantities are:

*   **Length:** The measure of linear distance or extent in space, with its standard unit being the meter.
*   **Mass:** A body's property related to its inertia and gravitational attraction, measured in kilograms.
*   **Duration (Time):** The measurement of the interval between two events, with its standard unit being the second.
*   **Electric Current:** The flow rate of electric charge, measured in amperes.
*   **Thermodynamic Temperature:** An absolute measure of thermal energy in a system, with its standard unit being the kelvin.
*   **Luminous Intensity:** A measure of the power emitted by a light source in a specific direction, with its standard unit being the candela.
*   **Amount of Substance:** A count of a specific type of elementary entity (like atoms or molecules), with its standard unit being the mole.

This system acts as a universal language for science and engineering, ensuring that measurements are consistent, comparable, and understood globally. Each quantity is formally defined along with its dimension and standard unit, forming a coherent and logical foundation for quantitative analysis.
