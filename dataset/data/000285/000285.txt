This is a model of a socio-legal system that defines a family structure and the formal process of child adoption. The system is composed of individuals who hold various roles such as mother, father, child, judge, and adoptive parent.

The core of the system models two types of relationships: biological and legal. A child is inherently defined as a connection between their biological mother and father. The system then introduces a formal legal process to create new parental relationships through an "Adoption Certificate." This certificate is an administrative document that legally binds a child to one or two adoptive parents, formalizing the new family structure.

The central function is the adoption certification process. This is a structured interaction between a judge and the prospective adoptive parents. The judge first communicates the relevant laws, the parents must formally agree, and finally, the judge asserts and issues the certificate, which is also registered with social services. The system enforces legal rules, such as a requirement that all adoptive parents must be over the age of 18.

Communication between the individuals is modeled as a verbal exchange, representing spoken interactions. People in this system have simple states, such as being awake or asleep, and can only participate in these formal communications when they are in an "awake" state.

The model is designed to handle several common adoption scenarios. It includes variations for step-parent adoptions (where one biological parent and one new parent are on the certificate) as well as adoptions by two new parents who are not biologically related to the child.
