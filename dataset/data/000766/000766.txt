This model represents a Somanetics INVOS Cerebral Oximeter system, a medical device designed for non-invasive, continuous monitoring of oxygen saturation levels specifically in the brain tissue of a patient. It is used in clinical settings like operating rooms and intensive care units to help clinicians detect and prevent brain oxygen deprivation during critical procedures.

The core of the system is its use of near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) technology. This works by using adhesive optical sensor heads that are placed on the patient's forehead, typically one on each side for bilateral monitoring of the left and right frontal cortex. Each sensor head contains a light emitter and a photodetector. The emitter shines near-infrared light at two specific wavelengths (730nm and 810nm) through the scalp and skull into the brain tissue. A nearby photodetector then measures the amount of light that is reflected back.

A central Signal Processing Unit analyzes the difference in the absorption of these two light wavelengths. Because oxygenated and deoxygenated blood absorb light differently, the processor can calculate the regional oxygen saturation (rSO2), which represents the balance of oxygen supply and demand in that specific area of the brain.

The system's components work together to provide real-time clinical data. The results, including the current oxygen saturation values and historical trends over several hours, are shown on a dedicated Display Module. An integrated Alarm Module continuously checks the measured oxygen levels against preset low and critical thresholds. If a patient's brain oxygen saturation drops to a concerning level, the system triggers both audible and visual alarms to alert medical staff immediately.

The entire system is powered by a central Power Supply Unit, which includes a battery backup to ensure it can continue operating for at least an hour during a power outage, a critical safety feature. All patient monitoring data is saved by a Data Storage Unit, allowing for later review and trend analysis by clinicians. The system operates through several states, including an initialization phase where it performs a self-test, a standby mode, an active monitoring state, and an alarm state if oxygen levels become critical.
