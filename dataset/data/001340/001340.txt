This model represents an amorphous metal transformer, a high-efficiency system designed to change electrical voltage levels for power distribution. Its defining feature is a core made from amorphous metal, which has a random, non-crystalline atomic structure. This special core provides superior magnetic properties, resulting in significantly lower energy losses and higher efficiency compared to traditional transformers.

The system is composed of several key components that work together. A **primary winding** receives incoming high-voltage electrical power, and a **secondary winding** delivers the outgoing, transformed low-voltage power. These two windings are magnetically linked by the central **amorphous metal core**, which efficiently channels the magnetic field between them.

During operation, energy losses in the core generate heat. A dedicated **cooling system** is connected to the core to dissipate this thermal energy, ensuring the transformer remains within its safe operating temperature and preventing its magnetic properties from degrading. For safety, a **protection circuit** continuously monitors the output power, safeguarding the system against electrical overloads and overvoltage conditions.

The transformer operates through a series of states. It begins in an `offline` state, then moves to `startup` when energized to build its magnetic field. Once stable, it enters `normal operation`, efficiently transforming power. If the demand becomes too high, it enters an `overload` state, and if it overheats or experiences an electrical fault, it will trip the protection circuit and shut down safely until the issue is resolved.
