This model describes an electromagnetic relay, which is an electrically operated switch. Its primary function is to use a small electrical signal to control a separate, often much higher-power, electrical circuit, while keeping the two circuits electrically isolated from each other.

The relay is composed of four main mechanical and electrical parts that work together:

1.  **Electromagnetic Coil:** This is the heart of the relay. When a low-power control voltage is applied to it, the coil generates a magnetic field.
2.  **Armature:** A movable metallic lever that is attracted by the magnetic field produced by the coil.
3.  **Return Spring:** This spring holds the armature in a default, or "normal," position when the coil is not powered.
4.  **Contact Set:** These are the actual electrical switch terminals, typically made of a silver alloy. The movement of the armature physically opens or closes these contacts.

The operation is a clear sequence of events. When the control voltage is applied to the coil, the resulting magnetic field pulls the armature towards it, overcoming the force of the return spring. This movement flips the state of the contacts, either connecting a circuit (closing normally open contacts) or breaking a circuit (opening normally closed contacts). When the control voltage is removed, the magnetic field disappears, and the return spring pushes the armature back to its original position, returning the contacts to their normal state.

The system's behavior is defined by distinct operational states: it is `de-energized` at rest, transitions through `energizing` as the armature moves, remains `energized` while active, and goes through a `releasing` phase as the spring returns the armature. Key performance characteristics like the activation voltage, switching speed, and the maximum power the contacts can handle are critical to its design and function.
