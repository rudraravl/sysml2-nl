This is a consumer infrared (IR) remote control system designed to wirelessly operate electronic appliances, specifically a television. The system enables a user to control the television's functions from a distance using a handheld remote.

The system consists of two main components: a handheld remote control unit and a receiver module integrated into the television.

The remote control is a battery-powered device with 25 buttons. When a user presses a button, an internal microcontroller generates a specific digital command. This command, which includes a device address and a function code (like 'power on' or 'volume up'), is sent to an infrared (IR) emitter. The emitter, an LED, converts this digital command into a modulated beam of infrared light, transmitting it at a specific carrier frequency of 38 kHz.

The television is equipped with a receiver module that must be in the line of sight to capture these IR signals. This module includes an IR photodetector sensitive enough to detect the signal from up to 12 meters away. Once the light is detected, a demodulator filters and extracts the original digital command. After validating the command, the television's internal systems execute the requested action.

The television itself operates in several states, including Power Off, Standby, and Active. The received IR commands trigger transitions between these states. For example, a power button press can switch the TV from Standby to Active, while other commands, like changing the channel or adjusting the volume, are processed when the TV is in its Active state. The entire process, from pressing a button on the remote to the television responding, is designed to be very fast, with a latency of less than 100 milliseconds.
