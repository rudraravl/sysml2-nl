This SysML model represents a comprehensive, standardized digital library of physical quantities, units, and concepts for the field of thermodynamics. It is not a model of a single physical object but rather a foundational toolkit, or a formal dictionary, for describing and analyzing any system where heat, energy, and temperature are important.

The library is based on the international standard ISO 80000-5, ensuring that the definitions are scientifically rigorous and universally understood. It defines a wide range of thermodynamic properties and behaviors that are essential for engineering and physics.

Key concepts defined in this library include:

*   **Fundamental Quantities:** Core concepts like Temperature (in both Kelvin and Celsius), Heat, various forms of Energy (such as internal energy, enthalpy, and Gibbs energy), and Entropy are formally defined.
*   **Material Properties:** It specifies how materials behave with changes in temperature. This includes coefficients for linear and cubic expansion (how much a material grows or shrinks), thermal conductivity (how well it conducts heat), and specific heat capacity (the amount of energy needed to change its temperature).
*   **Heat Transfer:** The library defines quantities for analyzing the movement of heat, such as heat flow rate, thermal resistance (a material's ability to insulate), thermal conductance (the inverse of resistance), and coefficients of heat transfer.
*   **Fluid and Gas Dynamics:** It includes properties like compressibility (how much a substance's volume changes under pressure), pressure coefficients, and the isentropic exponent, which are crucial for analyzing gases and fluids in systems like engines or turbines.
*   **Moisture and Humidity:** The model provides a detailed set of definitions for dealing with mixtures of gases and vapors, particularly air and water. This includes concepts like relative humidity, dew-point temperature, and various mass concentrations of water vapor.

In essence, this system is a digital representation of a scientific standard. It provides the precise, unambiguous building blocks that engineers and scientists would use to create models of real-world systems like power plants, HVAC units, chemical reactors, or insulation systems, ensuring that all calculations and simulations are based on a consistent and correct understanding of thermodynamics.
