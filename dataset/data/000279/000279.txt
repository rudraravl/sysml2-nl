This is an engineering model for a modular naval frigate, used for design analysis and trade-off studies. The system defines a frigate as a hierarchical collection of components, allowing engineers to calculate the total mass and power consumption of any given configuration.

The frigate's structure is broken down into two types of components:

*   **Simple Components:** These are the fundamental, individual parts of the ship that have their own intrinsic mass and power usage. Examples would include a single radar antenna, a weapon turret, a crew bunk, or a piece of armor plating.

*   **Composite Components:** These are larger, more complex subsystems that are themselves made up of other components (either simple or other composites). For instance, the entire propulsion system would be a composite component, containing engines, fuel pumps, and cooling systems.

The system works by "rolling up" the values from the bottom up. The total mass of a composite system (like the propulsion system) is its own structural mass plus the combined total mass of all the parts it contains. This calculation method allows an engineer to easily swap out a single part—like choosing a different type of sensor—and immediately see the impact on the total mass and power requirements for the entire frigate, ensuring the overall design remains balanced and within its operational limits.
