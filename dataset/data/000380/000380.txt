This model describes the "Eagle Eye Mission," a space-based Earth observation system designed for comprehensive environmental monitoring. Its primary purpose is to gather long-term, high-accuracy data on coastal and maritime areas to monitor their health, support weather forecasting, and contribute to climate change research. The data is intended for a wide range of users, including environmental scientists, coastal and maritime biologists, weather forecasters, and policy-makers.

The overall system is composed of three main segments:
1.  **The Space Segment:** The satellite itself, which is the main focus of this model.
2.  **The Launcher Segment:** The rocket used to place the satellite into orbit.
3.  **The Ground Segment:** The ESA/ESOC control center on Earth, responsible for command, control, and data reception.

The mission unfolds in several distinct phases. It begins with the **Launch and Early Orbit Phase**, where the satellite is placed into its initial orbit. This is followed by a **Commissioning Phase**, during which the satellite's main platform and its scientific instruments (the payload) are tested and calibrated.

Once commissioned, the satellite enters its main **Operations Phase**. In this phase, it continuously orbits the Earth, cycling between the day and night sides, to carry out its primary function: acquiring observation data. A typical operational loop involves collecting orbital data to verify its position, performing maneuvers to correct its orbit if necessary, and then achieving fine, precise pointing to aim its instruments at the Earth.

The mission includes a **Maintenance Phase** for upkeep and concludes with a **De-Orbiting Phase**. When the satellite reaches its end of life, the ground station sends a disposal command. The satellite then performs a final maneuver to safely de-orbit and is passivated (shut down) to complete its mission.
