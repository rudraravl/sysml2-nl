This model represents an Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator (ICD) system, a life-saving medical device designed to continuously monitor a patient's heart and automatically treat life-threatening heart rhythm disorders. The system is implanted in a patient and connected directly to the heart.

The core of the system is the **ICD device**, which houses all the electronics and a long-life **Battery Module** for power. This device is connected to the patient's heart via a **Lead System**, which consists of specialized wires. These leads perform two main functions: sensing the heart's natural electrical activity (ECG signals) from both the atria and ventricles, and delivering electrical therapy directly to the heart muscle.

Internally, the ICD operates as an autonomous diagnostic and treatment system. A **Sensing Circuit** receives the raw electrical signals from the heart, filtering and amplifying them for analysis. This processed signal is fed to an **Arrhythmia Detector**, which acts as the system's diagnostic brain. It continuously analyzes the heart's rhythm to identify specific abnormalities, such as ventricular fibrillation (a chaotic, life-threatening rhythm), ventricular tachycardia (a dangerously fast rhythm), or bradycardia (a heart rate that is too slow).

Based on the diagnosis, a **Therapy Controller** decides on the appropriate course of action. If a lethal arrhythmia is detected, it commands the **High-Voltage Therapy Unit** to prepare and deliver a corrective electrical shock. This unit charges an internal capacitor and releases a high-energy pulse to reset the heart to a normal rhythm. If the heart is beating too slowly, the same unit can deliver a series of low-energy pacing pulses to maintain an adequate heart rate.

The entire process is automated and operates in a cycle. The device is typically in a **monitoring** state. Upon detecting a problem, it moves to an **analysis** state, followed by **charging** for a shock or initiating **pacing**. After delivering therapy, it assesses its effectiveness before returning to continuous monitoring.

Finally, a **Telemetry Unit** allows for wireless communication between the implanted device and an external programmer used by a physician. This enables doctors to review the device's history, check the battery status, and fine-tune its therapeutic settings without surgery. The system logs all detected events and delivered therapies, providing crucial diagnostic data for patient care.
