This is a comprehensive library of standardized definitions for physical quantities used in the field of atomic and nuclear physics. It essentially functions as a foundational vocabulary or knowledge base, based on the international standard ISO 80000-10, for describing the properties and behaviors of atoms, nuclei, subatomic particles, and their interactions with matter and radiation.

The system is organized into several key conceptual areas:

*   **Fundamental Atomic and Nuclear Structure:** It defines the basic building blocks of atoms, such as the number of protons (`atomicNumber`), neutrons (`neutronNumber`), and total nucleons (`nucleonNumber`). It also includes fundamental properties like the mass of particles at rest (`restMass`), the mass of specific atoms (`atomicMass`), and the energy equivalent of rest mass (`restEnergy`).

*   **Quantum Mechanics and Atomic Properties:** The library defines the quantum numbers (principal, orbital, magnetic, spin) that describe the state of an electron in an atom. It also includes concepts like the intrinsic angular momentum of a particle (`spin`), its `magneticDipoleMoment`, and key physical constants that govern atomic scale, such as the `BohrRadius` and the `RydbergConstant`.

*   **Radioactive Decay and Activity:** It formalizes the concepts related to radioactivity. This includes the rate at which unstable nuclei decay (`decayConstant`), the time it takes for half of a sample to decay (`halfLife`), and the overall rate of disintegrations in a sample (`nuclearActivity`, measured in Becquerels). It also defines the energy released during different types of decay, such as `alphaDisintegrationEnergy` and `betaDisintegrationEnergy`.

*   **Particle Interactions and Radiation:** This section describes how particles interact with matter. A core concept is the `crossSection`, which represents the effective target area a particle presents for a specific interaction. It also defines how radiation passes through a material, including `particleFluence` (the number of particles passing through an area), `linearAttenuationCoefficient` (how a beam's intensity is reduced), and `stoppingPower` (the rate at which a charged particle loses energy in a medium).

*   **Radiation Dosimetry and Protection:** For applications in health and safety, the library defines quantities for measuring the effects of ionizing radiation. This includes `absorbedDose` (the energy deposited per unit mass, measured in Grays), `kerma` (kinetic energy released in matter), and `doseEquivalent` (which weights the absorbed dose by a `qualityFactor` to account for the biological effectiveness of different radiation types, measured in Sieverts).

*   **Nuclear Reactor Physics:** It includes specialized terms for describing processes within a nuclear reactor, such as the `multiplicationFactor` (which determines if a chain reaction is sustained), `neutronYieldPerFission`, `slowingDownArea`, and the `reactorTimeConstant`.

In essence, this system is not a physical object but a structured, digital encyclopedia of the precise scientific language needed to perform calculations, conduct experiments, and ensure safety in the domain of atomic and nuclear physics.
