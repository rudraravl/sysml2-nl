This model represents a comprehensive digital library of standardized physical quantities and units for the field of mechanics. It is a formal, machine-readable implementation of the international standard ISO 80000-4, designed to provide a foundational and unambiguous vocabulary for engineering and physics-based simulations.

This is not a physical system, but rather a structured information system that defines the concepts used to describe physical systems. Its "components" are the definitions of various mechanical properties and phenomena. These include:

*   **Properties of Matter:** Core concepts like mass, mass density (per volume, area, or length), and moment of inertia (a body's resistance to rotational motion).
*   **Motion and Forces:** Definitions for momentum, force (including specific types like weight, friction, and drag), impulse (the effect of a force over time), pressure, and stress (internal forces within a material).
*   **Rotational Dynamics:** Concepts related to rotation, such as angular momentum, moment of force, and torque (the twisting effect of a force).
*   **Material Behavior:** Properties that describe how materials deform, such as strain (deformation), various moduli of elasticity (like Young's modulus, which relates stress and strain), and viscosity (a fluid's resistance to flow).
*   **Energy and Power:** Definitions for mechanical work, potential and kinetic energy, power (the rate of doing work), and mechanical efficiency.
*   **Fluid Dynamics:** Quantities for describing fluid movement, including mass flow rate, volume flow rate, and surface tension.

For each physical quantity, the library provides a precise definition, its standard mathematical symbol(s), its fundamental dimensions (in terms of mass, length, and time), and its corresponding measurement units (e.g., Newtons for force, Pascals for pressure). The system also formally distinguishes between scalar quantities (which have only a magnitude, like pressure), vector quantities (which have magnitude and direction, like force or velocity), and more complex tensor quantities (like stress), providing a complete framework for sophisticated engineering analysis.

Ultimately, this system serves as a foundational data model to ensure that different engineering tools, simulations, and system models can communicate and operate with a common, precise, and standardized understanding of mechanics.
