This model represents a formal, standardized library of physical quantities relevant to the field of condensed matter physics. Derived from the international standard ISO 80000-12, this system serves as a foundational vocabulary for describing the properties and behaviors of solid and liquid materials at the atomic and electronic levels. It is not a physical object, but rather a structured collection of scientific definitions.

The library comprehensively covers several key areas of materials science and solid-state physics:

*   **Crystal Structure:** It defines fundamental concepts for describing the regular, repeating arrangement of atoms in crystals, including lattice vectors, the spacing between atomic planes (lattice plane spacing), and the Bragg angle used in X-ray diffraction analysis.
*   **Electronic Properties:** A major focus is on the behavior of electrons within materials. This includes defining energy levels like the Fermi energy (the highest energy an electron can have at absolute zero), the work function (energy needed to remove an electron), and the gap energy in semiconductors. It also defines charge carrier densities for electrons and holes.
*   **Transport Phenomena:** The system details quantities that describe how materials conduct heat and electricity, and how they respond to magnetic fields. This includes definitions for the Hall coefficient (related to charge carrier density), the Lorenz coefficient (linking thermal and electrical conductivity), and various thermoelectric effects (Seebeck, Peltier, and Thomson coefficients).
*   **Thermal Properties and Vibrations:** It includes concepts related to lattice vibrations (phonons), such as the Debye temperature and Debye angular frequency, which characterize the thermal properties of solids.
*   **Magnetism and Superconductivity:** The library defines critical temperatures that mark phase transitions, such as the Curie temperature for ferromagnets and the superconduction transition temperature for superconductors. It also specifies properties of superconductors like the critical magnetic fields and the London penetration depth, which describes how far a magnetic field can enter a superconductor.

For each physical quantity, the model formally specifies its scientific definition, the standard symbol(s) used to represent it (e.g., `T_C` for Curie Temperature), its fundamental physical dimension (in terms of mass, length, time, etc.), and its standard measurement units (e.g., Kelvin, meters, Volts). This creates an unambiguous reference for scientists and engineers to ensure consistency in research, analysis, and communication within the domain of condensed matter physics.
