This model represents an automotive cruise control system designed to automatically maintain a vehicle's speed as set by the driver. The system is composed of several interconnected components within the vehicle that work together in a continuous feedback loop.

The primary components are a central cruise controller, a speedometer, and the engine. The process begins when the driver sets a desired cruising speed. This command is received by the vehicle's cruise controller, which acts as the brain of the system.

To maintain the target speed, the speedometer constantly senses the vehicle's actual speed and sends this information back to the cruise controller. The controller continuously compares the actual speed from the speedometer with the desired speed set by the driver. Based on any difference, it calculates the necessary adjustment and sends a specific fuel command to the engine. The engine then responds to this command by adjusting its power output, which in turn alters the vehicle's speed. This cycle of sensing, comparing, and adjusting ensures the vehicle holds a steady speed without requiring the driver to keep their foot on the accelerator.
