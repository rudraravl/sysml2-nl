This model describes a system and one of its key internal components, illustrating how requirements are allocated and verified. The system consists of a main assembly ('q') which relies on a critical sub-component ('p') to function correctly.

The main assembly is subject to a primary operational requirement. This requirement has a specific precondition that must be true for the test to be valid (e.g., "assuming the system is running on standard power"), and a core rule that must be met (e.g., "the system must operate below a certain temperature"). The design explicitly designates the internal component ('p') as being responsible for meeting this requirement. A successful verification check on the main assembly ('q') confirms that it meets the requirement, demonstrating that the internal component is performing its job as intended.

A second, conditional requirement is also defined for the system. This rule acts like an "if-then" statement (e.g., "if the system is in high-performance mode, then it must have auxiliary cooling"). In this particular configuration, the internal component ('p') does not satisfy this conditional requirement. As a direct result, a check on the main assembly ('q') confirms that the system as a whole also fails to meet this requirement.

In essence, the model represents a clear cause-and-effect relationship in system validation: the capabilities of the sub-component ('p') directly determine whether the entire assembly ('q') can pass or fail its designated requirements.
