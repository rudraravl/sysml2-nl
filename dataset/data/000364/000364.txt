This model represents a comprehensive, standardized framework of quantities and units for the field of acoustics, based on the international standard ISO 80000-8. It is not a physical object, but rather a foundational knowledge base or a "dictionary" that provides precise, unambiguous definitions for the scientific and engineering measurement of sound.

The system systematically defines all the fundamental properties used to describe sound waves and their effects. It starts with the basics, such as **sound pressure** (the deviation from ambient atmospheric pressure caused by a sound wave) and the physical motion of particles in the air or another medium, including their **displacement**, **velocity**, and **acceleration**.

Building on these fundamentals, the framework defines quantities related to the energy of sound. This includes concepts like **sound energy density** (how much sound energy is contained in a given volume), **sound power** (the total acoustic energy radiated by a source per unit of time), and **sound intensity** (the sound power passing through a specific area).

For practical applications and measurements, especially those related to human hearing, the system defines logarithmic scales. The most common of these are **Sound Pressure Level** and **Sound Power Level**, both measured in decibels (dB). These provide a way to express the vast range of sound magnitudes in a more manageable scale by comparing a measured value to a standard reference value.

Finally, the framework includes more specialized concepts such as **acoustic impedance** (a material's resistance to the propagation of sound), **sound exposure** (the cumulative effect of sound over a period of time), and **reverberation time** (a key characteristic of a room's acoustics, defining how long it takes for a sound to fade away).

In essence, this system provides a complete and self-consistent language for anyone working with acoustics, ensuring that measurements and calculations are standardized and universally understood.
