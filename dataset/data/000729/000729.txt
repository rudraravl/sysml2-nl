This model describes a digital Peak Expiratory Flow Meter, a handheld medical device used to measure the maximum speed at which a person can exhale. It is commonly used by patients with respiratory conditions like asthma to monitor their lung function.

The system is composed of four main physical components that work in sequence. A patient blows into a detachable **Mouthpiece**, which channels the exhaled air into the device. This stream of air passes through a **Flow Sensor**, which measures the rate and pressure of the airflow and converts it into electronic data.

This sensor data is then sent to an internal **Signal Processor**, the device's central computing unit. The processor analyzes the continuous stream of data from the sensor in real-time to identify the single highest flow rate achieved during the exhalation. Once the peak value is determined and the measurement is validated, the processor sends the final reading to a **Display Unit**. This screen then shows the numerical result to the patient or a healthcare provider.

The device operates through a clear sequence of states. It begins in an idle or ready state, waiting for a user. When it detects a patient's breath, it enters a "Measuring" state, actively capturing data. After the exhalation is complete, it analyzes the data to find the peak, and finally enters a "Displaying" state to show the result. The device can then be reset to take another measurement.
