This model describes the fundamental concept of a **"Part"**—a universal building block for constructing any system. It isn't a specific machine or device, but rather the essential blueprint that defines what any component, from a simple screw to an entire aircraft, is and how it behaves.

At its core, a "Part" is a distinct object that can be a component within a larger assembly or a complete system in itself. Every Part is defined by three key characteristics:

1.  **Structure and Interaction:** A Part can be assembled from other, smaller Parts. It connects and interacts with the outside world or other components through defined interfaces or **"ports."** These ports act as standardized connection points, like an electrical socket, a USB port, or a mechanical fuel line, governing how it exchanges energy, information, or materials.

2.  **Behavior and Actions:** A Part is not static; it can perform **actions**. This represents its function or what it does. For example, an engine's action is to rotate a shaft, a pump's action is to move fluid, and a computer's action is to process data.

3.  **States of Being:** A Part can exist in various conditions or **"states."** These states describe its operational mode at any given time, such as an engine being 'off', 'idling', or 'running at full power', or a valve being 'open' or 'closed'. A Part transitions between these states as it operates.

In essence, this model provides the foundational rules for defining any physical or logical component, ensuring that no matter how complex a system is, its constituent parts all share these fundamental properties of structure, behavior, and state.
