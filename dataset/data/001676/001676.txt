This model describes an intelligent central heating system that uses the OpenTherm communication protocol to efficiently manage indoor temperature. The system is composed of two main physical components: a smart Room Thermostat and a Modulating Boiler, which communicate digitally to optimize energy use.

The Room Thermostat acts as the system's brain or master controller. It continuously measures the ambient temperature in the room and compares it to the desired temperature set by the user. When it detects the room is too cold, it doesn't just send a simple "on" signal. Instead, it calculates a specific target water temperature needed for the heating circuit (e.g., radiators) and transmits this command to the boiler.

The Modulating Boiler is the heat source and acts as the slave unit. Unlike a traditional boiler that can only be fully on or off, this boiler can adjust, or "modulate," its flame and power output. Upon receiving a target water temperature from the thermostat, it adjusts its burner intensity to heat the water to that precise level. If a large temperature increase is needed, it will run at a higher power, and if it only needs to maintain the temperature, it will run at a very low, energy-saving level.

The communication between the two is a continuous, two-way conversation. The thermostat sends setpoint commands, and the boiler responds with detailed status updates, including the current water temperature, its power modulation level, whether the flame is active, and any fault codes. This closed-loop control allows the system to operate much more efficiently than simple on/off systems, resulting in more stable room temperatures and significant energy savings.
