This model describes a high-pressure, electronically controlled injection pumping subsystem. It is designed to draw fluid from a reservoir, pressurize it to a precise level, and deliver it at a consistent, specified flow rate.

The system's core component is a motor-driven, plunger-style injection pump. This pump physically draws fluid from a supply reservoir and uses a mechanical process involving plungers, cams, and valves to generate high pressure. The pressurized fluid is then directed into a discharge manifold, which can distribute the flow to one or more outlets.

A dedicated electronic pump controller serves as the brain of the system. It receives desired settings for flow rate and pressure and uses a PID control algorithm to manage the pump's operation. To do this, it constantly monitors the real-world performance using a suite of sensors, including pressure transducers at both the pump's suction and discharge points, and a flow meter on the output line. The controller compares this feedback to the target settings and adjusts the pump's motor speed to ensure the fluid delivery is stable and accurate.

For safety, the system includes a mechanical relief valve that automatically opens to vent excess pressure if it exceeds a critical safety threshold, preventing damage to the components. The system operates through several distinct states: it starts in an idle mode, moves to a priming state to purge air from the lines, then enters its main pumping operation. It also includes modes for flushing the system for maintenance and a fault state that safely shuts down the pump if an issue like over-pressurization is detected.
