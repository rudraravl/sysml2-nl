This is an automated attendant system for a business telephone network, often known as an IVR (Interactive Voice Response) or auto-attendant. Its purpose is to automatically answer and direct incoming calls to the correct department or person without needing a human operator. The system is designed to be highly available (99.9% uptime) and can handle at least 100 calls at the same time.

The system is composed of several key functional components that work together to manage a call:

*   A **Call Receiver** is the entry point, responsible for detecting and answering incoming phone calls from the main telephony line.
*   A **Prompt Generator** plays pre-recorded audio messages to the caller. This includes a welcome greeting, a menu of options ("Press 1 for Sales, 2 for Support..."), and messages for invalid input or call transfers.
*   An **Input Processor** listens for the caller's keypad selections (DTMF tones). It has a built-in timeout and allows a caller up to three attempts to enter a valid option before automatically routing them to a human operator.
*   A **Routing Engine** is the core logic unit. It takes the caller's validated menu choice and maps it to a specific internal phone extension, such as "1001" for Sales or "1002" for Support. It then executes the call transfer.
*   A **Call Logger** records the details of every interaction, creating a log that includes the caller's number, their menu selection, the final destination of the call, and the total time it took to handle the call.

The system operates through a clear, step-by-step process. When a call arrives, it's answered, and the caller is greeted and presented with a menu. After the caller makes a selection with their keypad, the system validates the input. If it's valid, the call is transferred to the corresponding department. If it's invalid, the caller is prompted to try again. After the call is successfully transferred or the interaction ends, a complete record of the event is logged for administrative purposes.
