This model describes an integrated, automated Air Pollution Monitoring and Control System. It's designed to be deployed in a specific area, like a city district or an industrial complex, to continuously track air quality, alert the public to hazardous conditions, and actively clean the air when necessary.

The system's operation begins with a **Multi-Pollutant Sensor**, which constantly measures the concentration of key pollutants in the air, including particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10), ozone, nitrogen dioxide, carbon monoxide, and sulfur dioxide. Each measurement is captured as a detailed reading with a timestamp and location identifier.

This raw data is sent to two places simultaneously. First, it goes to a **Data Archive System** for long-term storage, which is useful for historical analysis and regulatory compliance. Second, the live data is fed into an **Air Quality Analyzer**. This central component acts as the brain of the system, processing the complex sensor readings in real-time to calculate a single, easy-to-understand Air Quality Index (AQI) value. This AQI score categorizes the air quality into levels like "Good," "Moderate," or "Hazardous" and identifies the dominant pollutant.

Based on the calculated AQI, the system initiates several actions:

1.  **Public Information:** The current AQI is sent to a **Public Information Display**—like a digital billboard or website—to keep the public informed of the current air quality.

2.  **Alerting:** The AQI is also monitored by an **Alert Management System**. If the air quality degrades past a certain safety threshold (e.g., "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups"), this system automatically generates and issues an alert. The notification includes the severity level, the type of pollutant, and recommended health actions for the public. These alerts are also shown on the public display.

3.  **Active Mitigation:** If the AQI reaches a more dangerous level (e.g., "Unhealthy"), an **Air Filtration Controller** is triggered. This controller sends a command to a physical **Air Filtration Unit**, activating it to begin cleaning the air. The command can specify the required airflow rate and efficiency level. The filtration unit itself can operate in different states, such as standby, active, or a high-efficiency mode for severe pollution events, to physically reduce the concentration of pollutants in the atmosphere.

In essence, this is a closed-loop system that not only passively monitors air quality but also actively responds to dangerous pollution levels by warning the public and automatically deploying air-cleaning technology to protect public health.
