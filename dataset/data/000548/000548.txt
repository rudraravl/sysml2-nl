This is a bioartificial liver system, a complex medical device designed to provide temporary life support for patients experiencing acute or chronic liver failure. It functions as an external, temporary liver, bridging the patient until their own liver can recover or a transplant becomes available.

The system works by circulating a patient's blood through an external circuit. A precision **blood pump** draws blood from the patient and ensures a consistent flow rate through the device. The blood first enters a **plasma filter**, which separates the liquid plasma from the blood cells. This is a critical step, as only the plasma, which carries toxins, is sent for treatment.

The separated plasma is then passed through an **oxygenator**, a component that enriches it with oxygen. This is vital for the health of the living cells in the next stage. The core of the system is the **bioreactor module**, which contains a culture of living liver cells (hepatocytes). The oxygenated, toxin-rich plasma flows through this bioreactor, where the hepatocytes perform their natural metabolic functions: detoxifying the plasma by breaking down harmful substances like ammonia. To keep these cells alive and active, a **nutrient reservoir** continuously supplies them with a nutrient medium.

Throughout the entire process, a **temperature controller** precisely maintains the system at human body temperature (around 37°C) to ensure the liver cells remain healthy and functional. After being cleansed in the bioreactor, the detoxified plasma is recombined with the patient's blood cells and returned to their body.

A comprehensive **monitoring system** is integrated throughout the device. It constantly measures critical parameters such as blood pressure, temperature, oxygen saturation, and, most importantly, the level of toxins and the viability of the liver cells. If any parameter goes outside a safe range, the system can trigger an alarm to alert medical staff.

The device operates through several distinct states, including an **idle** state when on standby, a **priming** state to prepare the circuits for use, and an **active treatment** state. It includes clear protocols for starting, stopping, and performing an emergency shutdown of the treatment, ensuring patient safety at all times.
