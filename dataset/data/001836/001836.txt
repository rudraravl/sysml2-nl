This is an automated pressure regulation system designed to maintain a stable fluid pressure within a vessel. It is a classic closed-loop control system, likely for industrial processes where precise pressure management of a fluid is critical.

The system's core component is a 500-liter pressure vessel, built to withstand a maximum operating pressure of 1000 kPa. The primary goal is to keep the fluid inside this vessel at a target pressure of 500 kPa, with an acceptable tolerance of 5 kPa.

To achieve this, the system works through a continuous cycle of monitoring and adjustment. A pressure sensor is attached to the vessel, providing real-time pressure readings to a central pressure controller. This controller acts as the brain of the system, constantly comparing the measured pressure against the target.

Based on this comparison, the controller sends commands to an inlet control valve. This valve is the system's actuator; it regulates the amount of fluid flowing into the vessel. If the vessel's pressure drops, the controller commands the valve to open further, increasing the inflow. If the pressure rises, it instructs the valve to close, restricting the flow.

The entire system operates in different states, including an initial setup phase, a passive monitoring state when the pressure is stable, and an active regulating state when it's making adjustments. For safety, the system also features an independent relief valve. This is a failsafe device set to automatically open if the pressure ever exceeds a critical threshold of 950 kPa, venting excess pressure to prevent the vessel from rupturing. If this critical pressure is reached, the main controller will also enter an emergency state, commanding the inlet valve to shut completely and initiating a system shutdown.
