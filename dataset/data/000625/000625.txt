The core of the system is a freely rotating, magnetized needle. This needle naturally aligns itself with the Earth's magnetic field to point towards magnetic north. It is mounted on a very low-friction pivot assembly, likely using a sapphire bearing, to ensure it can move with maximum sensitivity.

To provide a usable reading, the needle's position is referenced against a graduated compass card marked with both precise angular degrees and the primary cardinal directions (N, S, E, W) and intercardinal points (NE, SW, etc.). A fixed reference marker, known as the lubber line, indicates the user's or vehicle's forward direction, allowing for an immediate heading reading.

A key feature of this compass is its liquid-filled damping chamber. The internal components are submerged in a fluid, such as mineral oil, which prevents the needle from oscillating excessively due to movement or vibration. This allows the needle to settle quickly and provide a stable, readable heading, a critical feature for use on a moving platform.

All of these internal mechanisms are protected by a durable and sealed protective housing, likely made of brass with a clear glass window for viewing the compass card. The housing is hermetically sealed to prevent moisture or contaminants from entering and affecting the delicate internal components.

In operation, the compass transitions through several states. When first oriented, it enters a "seeking" state where the needle rotates to find north, followed by a "settling" state where the damping fluid minimizes oscillations. It then reaches a "stable" state, providing an accurate reading. If it's exposed to strong magnetic interference, it becomes "disturbed" and must re-seek and settle once the interference is removed.
