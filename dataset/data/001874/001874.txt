This model describes a high-performance, bagless suction system, similar to an industrial or workshop vacuum cleaner. It is designed to create a powerful vacuum to draw in air and debris, separate the solid waste using a two-stage process, and exhaust clean, filtered air.

The heart of the system is an electric motor that drives a centrifugal impeller. When spinning, the impeller generates a strong negative pressure and high-volume airflow, which is the source of the system's suction. This suction pulls contaminated air and debris in through an intake port.

The incoming air first enters a cyclone separator. This component uses centrifugal force to spin the air, causing the heavier debris particles to be thrown outwards and fall into a removable debris collection bin. This process removes the majority of the waste before it can reach the finer filter.

After the cyclone, the partially cleaned air passes through a high-efficiency HEPA filter. This filter traps the remaining fine dust and microscopic particulates, ensuring that the air expelled from the system's exhaust port is clean. The system monitors the state of this filter, tracking whether it is clean, partially loaded, or fully clogged.

A dedicated control unit acts as the brain of the system. It uses a pressure sensor to monitor the vacuum level and adjusts the motor's power to maintain optimal performance. The controller also keeps track of the debris bin's fill level and the filter's condition, and can halt operation or signal an alert when the bin is full or the filter is clogged. The entire system is designed to be robust, with specific requirements for vacuum strength, airflow rate, and high filtration efficiency. The design is also configurable, allowing for more powerful industrial versions with larger motors and higher-capacity collection bins.
