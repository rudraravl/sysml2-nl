This model describes an **Air Quality Egg**, a community-driven environmental monitoring device designed to measure and report local air quality conditions in real-time. Its primary purpose is to allow citizens to participate in building distributed networks of air quality sensors, contributing to environmental awareness.

The system is a self-contained unit composed of several key components. It features a comprehensive sensor suite that continuously measures various atmospheric pollutants, including **Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2)**, **Carbon Monoxide (CO)**, **Ozone (O3)**, and fine **Particulate Matter (PM2.5)**. In addition to pollutants, an environmental sensor measures the ambient **temperature and humidity**.

At the heart of the device is a **central processing unit**. This unit collects the raw data from all the sensors, calibrates it for accuracy, and then computes a standardized **Air Quality Index (AQI)**. This single value provides an easy-to-understand summary of the air quality at that moment.

For communication, the Air Quality Egg is equipped with a **WiFi transceiver**. After processing a new set of readings, it connects to a local WiFi network and transmits the complete measurement data—including individual pollutant levels, environmental conditions, and the calculated AQI—to a central cloud server. This allows the data to be aggregated, analyzed, and made publicly accessible.

The entire device is powered by a **power module** that takes a standard input voltage and regulates it for the various internal components. The system operates in a continuous cycle: it samples the sensors at a configurable interval, processes the data, transmits the results, and then waits for the next cycle to begin.
