This is an industrial filter press system designed for separating solid particles from liquid slurries in a batch process. Its primary function is to take a mixed fluid (slurry), force the liquid out through a filter, and retain the solids as a compressed, dewatered block known as a filter cake.

The core of the system is a filter chamber containing a series of filter plates, which can number from 20 to 100 depending on the required capacity. Each plate is covered with a filter media, like a cloth, that allows liquid to pass through but traps solid particles. A powerful hydraulic system clamps these plates together with immense force, creating a sealed, high-pressure environment inside the chamber.

The process operates in a distinct cycle managed by a central control system. First, a feed pump moves the slurry through a feed valve and into the sealed filter chamber, filling the empty spaces between the plates. Once full, the system enters a pressurizing and filtering phase, where the pump continues to apply pressure, forcing the liquid component of the slurry through the filter media. This clarified liquid, called filtrate, is drained out through a separate valve.

As the liquid is removed, the solid particles accumulate on the filter media, forming a dense cake. The system may hold the pressure for a set time to consolidate the cake and squeeze out as much remaining moisture as possible. After filtration is complete, the hydraulic system depressurizes and releases its clamping force, allowing the filter plates to be separated. This action causes the solid filter cakes to drop out for collection. The cycle may conclude with a washing phase to clean the filter media before starting over.

This system is designed to meet specific performance targets, including producing a highly clarified filtrate (at least 98% clear) and a solid cake with low moisture content (not exceeding 30%). It operates at high pressures, up to 1500 kPa, to ensure efficient dewatering and can complete a full cycle within 90 minutes.
