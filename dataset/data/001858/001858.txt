This model describes an automated Seed Counting Machine, a system designed for accurately counting and batching seeds for agricultural or research purposes. It is a complete, self-contained unit that handles the entire process from bulk storage to final collection.

The machine consists of several integrated physical components. A **Hopper Feeder** acts as the main storage bin for the seeds, dispensing them at a controlled rate onto a **Conveyor Belt**. The conveyor's primary function is to transport the seeds in a single file line, ensuring they don't clump together, which is critical for accurate detection.

As the seeds travel along the belt, they pass through an **Optical Sensor**. This sensor works by projecting a beam of light; each time a seed passes through and interrupts the beam, it generates an electrical pulse.

This detection pulse is sent to the central **Counting Controller**, which is the brain of the system. The controller processes each signal, increments a running total, and compares it against a pre-set target count for the batch. The controller also manages the machine's overall operational state. An operator can interact with the system through this controller to start, stop, or reset a counting job.

A **Display Unit** is connected to the controller, providing the operator with real-time feedback, such as the current seed count and the system's status (e.g., "Counting," "Complete," or "Error"). Once the seeds are counted by the sensor, they fall off the end of the conveyor belt and are gathered in a **Collection Bin** for packaging or further processing.

The machine operates through a defined sequence of states. It starts in an **idle** mode, and upon receiving a start command, it becomes **ready**. When seeds begin to flow, it enters a **feeding** and then **counting** state. Once the target number of seeds is reached, it stops the feeder but allows the conveyor to clear any remaining seeds before signaling that the batch is **complete**. The system is also designed to detect faults and enter an **error** state, alerting the operator to a problem.
