This model represents a Subcutaneous Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (S-ICD), a medical device designed to protect patients at risk of sudden cardiac death. The entire system is implanted under the skin and operates automatically to continuously monitor a patient's heart and deliver a life-saving electrical shock if a dangerous heart rhythm is detected.

The system is composed of several key components working in unison. A **Sensing Electrode** continuously listens to the heart's electrical activity, picking up the cardiac signal. This raw signal is then sent to a **Signal Processing Unit**, which filters out noise and amplifies the signal to ensure it can be analyzed accurately.

The cleaned signal is passed to an **Arrhythmia Detection Unit**, the core diagnostic part of the device. This unit analyzes the heart's rate and rhythm to determine if it is normal or if a life-threatening arrhythmia, such as ventricular fibrillation, is occurring. If a dangerous rhythm is identified, a **Therapy Decision Unit** confirms the diagnosis and, based on pre-set configurations, determines that a therapeutic shock is necessary.

Upon this decision, a central **Control Processor** coordinates the therapy sequence. A long-lasting **Battery Pack** provides power to a **High-Voltage Capacitor**, which rapidly stores a powerful electrical charge. Once fully charged, this energy is delivered as a controlled pulse through a **Defibrillation Electrode** to the patient's heart, with the goal of stopping the dangerous rhythm and allowing a normal one to resume.

The device operates through a cycle of states. It remains in a constant **Monitoring** state until a potential arrhythmia is detected. It then moves into **Detection** and, if confirmed, a **Charging** state. Once the capacitor is ready, it enters the **Therapy Delivery** state to deliver the shock, followed by a **Post-Shock Assessment** to verify if the heart rhythm has been successfully restored before returning to its constant monitoring mode. The entire process is designed to be rapid, reliable, and safe, delivering therapy within seconds of detecting a critical event.
