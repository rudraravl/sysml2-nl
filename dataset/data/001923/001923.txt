This model describes a Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (RTG), a self-contained power system designed for long-duration space missions where other power sources like solar panels are not practical. Essentially, it's a type of nuclear battery that converts heat from radioactive decay directly into usable electricity.

The system's core component is the **Radioisotope Heat Source**, which contains a mass of radioactive fuel (4.8 kg). As this fuel naturally decays over many years, it consistently generates a large amount of thermal energy, causing its surface to become extremely hot.

This intense heat is channeled into a **Thermoelectric Converter Module**. This module is composed of numerous (144) thermoelectric couples that generate a voltage when there is a temperature difference across them. One side of the converter is heated by the radioactive source, while the other side is kept cool. This temperature differential allows the module to convert thermal energy directly into raw electrical power.

To maintain this crucial temperature difference, the waste heat from the "cold" side of the converter must be continuously removed. This is the job of the **Heat Rejection System**, which consists of a large radiator (1.2 m²). This radiator radiates the excess heat away from the generator into the cold vacuum of space.

The raw electricity produced by the converter is not yet suitable for powering sensitive spacecraft electronics. It is first sent to a **Power Conditioning Unit**. This component regulates the electricity, converting it into a stable, conditioned voltage (28V) that the spacecraft systems can safely use.

The RTG is designed for a very long operational life, specified at 14 years. Due to the gradual decay of its nuclear fuel, its power output slowly decreases over time. It starts its mission producing about 131 watts and is required to still provide at least 100 watts by the end of its life. The system's operational status is monitored, with states ranging from "Nominal Operation" to "Degraded Output" and "Critical Low Power" as its power output wanes, until it is finally shut down at the end of the mission.
