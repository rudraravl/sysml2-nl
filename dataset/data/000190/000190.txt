This describes a system for performing risk and failure analysis. It's a conceptual framework used by engineers or safety analysts to understand and document how things can go wrong in a complex product or system.

The framework is built around the idea of a "Scenario." Each scenario tells a story of a potential problem, from its origin to its ultimate consequence.

The basic building blocks are "Situations," which are simply any events or states that can occur. These are categorized into two main types:

1.  **Causes:** These are the root events or triggers that start a problem. Each cause is assigned a "probability" to represent how likely it is to happen. For example, a "tire puncture" could be a cause with a specific statistical probability.
2.  **Failures:** These are the final, undesirable outcomes of a scenario. Each failure is assigned a "severity" level (e.g., minor, critical, catastrophic) to describe how bad the outcome is. For instance, "loss of vehicle control" could be a failure with a high severity.

The system's key function is to connect these events together using a "Causation" link. This allows an analyst to build a logical chain showing how a specific cause can lead through one or more intermediate situations to an eventual failure. A complete chain of these linked events forms a single, understandable "Scenario," providing a clear picture of a potential risk pathway. By defining many such scenarios, a team can systematically analyze a system's weaknesses and prioritize improvements.
