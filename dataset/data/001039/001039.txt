This model describes an Electronic Nose System, a sophisticated device designed to mimic the biological sense of smell to detect and identify various chemical compounds and odors in the air.

The system's operation begins with an Air Sampling Unit, which uses an internal pump to draw in ambient air. This air is first passed through a particle filter to remove dust and other contaminants, ensuring a clean sample is delivered for analysis.

The heart of the system is a Sensor Array composed of 16 individual chemical sensors of two different types: eight metal-oxide sensors and eight conductive polymer sensors. This diverse array is designed to react to a wide range of volatile organic compounds. When the air sample flows over these sensors, the chemical compounds cause a change in each sensor's electrical resistance. The combined response from all 16 sensors creates a unique digital "fingerprint" or signature for the specific odor being analyzed.

The raw electrical signals from the sensor array are then sent to a Signal Conditioner. This component cleans up the signals by amplifying them, filtering out noise, and normalizing the data to produce a clear and consistent pattern.

This refined data pattern is then fed into the Pattern Recognition Module, which acts as the system's "brain." Using a neural network algorithm, this module analyzes the pattern, extracts its key features, and compares it against a pre-existing library of known odor signatures. Based on this comparison, it classifies the odor, identifies the specific chemical compounds present, and calculates a confidence level for its conclusion.

Finally, the results of the analysis are sent to a User Interface, which formats the data and presents a clear output to the user, displaying the detected odor category and the specific compounds identified. The entire process, from sampling the air to displaying the final classification, is designed to be completed in under 30 seconds.
