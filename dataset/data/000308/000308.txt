This model represents a comprehensive, standardized digital library of physical quantities and units for the domain of electromagnetism. It is not a physical object, but rather a formal data model based on the International System of Quantities (ISQ) and specifically the IEC 80000-6 standard. Its purpose is to provide precise, unambiguous, and machine-readable definitions for concepts used in physics and engineering simulations and models.

The library systematically defines a wide range of electromagnetic concepts, starting from fundamental quantities like electric charge and current. It builds upon these to define more complex ideas such as:

*   **Electric Field Concepts:** Electric potential (voltage), electric field strength, capacitance, and properties of materials like permittivity and electric susceptibility.
*   **Magnetic Field Concepts:** Magnetic flux, magnetic field strength, inductance, reluctance, and material properties like permeability and magnetic susceptibility.
*   **Current and Charge Distribution:** It defines not just total charge and current, but also their distribution over a line, surface, or volume (charge density and current density).
*   **Electrodynamics and Waves:** The model includes quantities related to the flow of electromagnetic energy, such as the Poynting vector, and fundamental constants like the speed of light.
*   **AC Circuit Theory:** It provides formal definitions for concepts essential to alternating current circuits, including impedance, reactance, admittance, susceptance, as well as active, reactive, and apparent power.

For each quantity, the model formally specifies its definition, standard symbols, relationship to other quantities, and its fundamental dimensions in terms of base units like length, mass, time, and electric current. It also distinguishes between scalar values (magnitudes) and their corresponding vector representations in 3D space, which is critical for spatial analysis of electromagnetic fields. Essentially, this system acts as a foundational dictionary that other engineering systems can use to ensure consistency and correctness in electromagnetic calculations.
