This model describes a verification test for a satellite's power generation system. The system being tested is a satellite, or a "space segment," which is equipped with movable solar panels.

In its real-world application, this satellite relies on these solar panels to generate the electrical power needed to run all its onboard systems while in orbit. To ensure maximum efficiency, the solar panels are designed to be articulated, meaning they can be pointed directly at the sun, regardless of the satellite's orientation.

The procedure detailed in the model is a specific ground-based test performed before launch. In a specialized facility, the satellite's solar panels are exposed to a "sun light simulator," a powerful lamp that mimics the sun's radiation. The test involves commanding the satellite to orient its panels towards this light source, then measuring the resulting electrical power output. This measured value is then compared to the expected maximum power specified in the design requirements to verify that the power system is functioning correctly and will be able to support the mission in space.
