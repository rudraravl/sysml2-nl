This model describes an intelligent, energy-efficient water delivery system. Its primary purpose is to pump and distribute water while actively optimizing its own energy consumption. The system's main goal is to maximize the volume of water delivered (in gallons) for every unit of electrical energy it uses (in watt-hours).

The system is composed of several interconnected components that work together in a closed-loop control cycle. It starts with a **Water Reservoir** that holds the main water supply. A variable-speed **Pump Unit** draws water from this reservoir and provides the pressure to move it through the system.

To achieve its efficiency goals, the system continuously monitors its own performance using dedicated sensors. An **Energy Monitor** measures the pump's electrical power consumption, while a **Flow Monitor** tracks the volume and rate of water being delivered.

The data from these two monitors is fed to an **Efficiency Analyzer**, which calculates the system's real-time performance in gallons per watt-hour. This efficiency value is then sent to the core of the system, the **System Controller**. The controller compares the current efficiency against a target value and, if performance is suboptimal, it sends a command to adjust the pump's speed. This feedback loop allows the system to automatically fine-tune its operation to save energy.

Finally, the pressurized water from the pump flows into a **Distribution Network**, which represents the pipes and delivery points where the water is ultimately used.
