This model represents an Open Flow Microperfusion (OFM) system, a sophisticated piece of biomedical equipment used for continuous, real-time monitoring of biochemical substances directly within living tissue. Its primary purpose is to sample the fluid in the spaces between cells (interstitial fluid) to measure concentrations of metabolites, drugs, or biomarkers without needing to remove a tissue sample.

The system's core component is a specialized **Microperfusion Probe** that is inserted into the tissue being studied. This probe contains a semi-permeable membrane which facilitates a passive exchange process. A sterile **Perfusion Fluid**, held in a **Fluid Reservoir**, is pushed by a high-precision **Microfluidic Pump** at a very slow and constant rate through the probe. A **Temperature Regulator** ensures this fluid is maintained at a safe, physiological temperature.

As the fluid flows along the probe's membrane, molecules from the surrounding tissue diffuse across it and into the fluid stream. This fluid, now enriched with the target biochemicals, exits the probe and is routed to an automated **Sample Collector**. This collector gathers the fluid into a series of distinct samples at predefined time intervals. These collected samples can then be analyzed to create a time-resolved profile of the tissue's biochemical activity.

The entire operation is managed and monitored by integrated control units. A **Flow Controller** works with the pump to maintain the exact desired flow rate, while a **Pressure Monitor** serves as a critical safety feature, ensuring that fluid pressure never exceeds a safe threshold that could cause damage to the tissue. The system is designed to perform long, continuous perfusion sessions, repeatedly delivering fluid, facilitating the exchange, and collecting samples, all while monitoring its status to ensure safe and reliable operation.
