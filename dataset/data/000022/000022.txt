This model describes the complete geometric layout of a standard four-wheeled passenger vehicle. The system is defined by its primary physical components and their precise spatial relationships.

The foundation of the vehicle is a single chassis, represented as a rectangular block measuring 4.8 meters in length, 1.84 meters in width, and 1.35 meters in height.

Attached to this chassis are four wheels, positioned as you'd expect: one on the front-left, front-right, rear-left, and rear-right. Each wheel is a detailed assembly, not just a simple part. Its dimensions are based on a 22-inch diameter hub with a 110 mm high tire, and it has a width of 220 mm.

A key detail is that each wheel includes five cylindrical lug bolts. These bolts are not arbitrarily placed; they are arranged in a perfect, evenly-spaced circular pattern around the center of the wheel, simulating how a real wheel is mounted to a vehicle's hub.

The entire assembly is organized around a central reference point located at the bottom-center of the chassis, with a coordinate system where 'forward' is the positive X-direction and 'up' is the positive Z-direction. The exact position and orientation of the chassis and all four wheels are defined relative to this point, establishing the vehicle's final wheelbase, track width, and overall physical structure.
