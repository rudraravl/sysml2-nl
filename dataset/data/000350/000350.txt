This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of physical quantities for the domain of atomic and nuclear physics. It is not a physical machine, but rather a formal knowledge base that provides a precise, digital vocabulary for describing the properties and behaviors of atoms, nuclei, subatomic particles, and their interactions with radiation. This system is based on the international standard ISO 80000-10.

The library is structured around several key areas of physics:

*   **Fundamental Atomic and Nuclear Properties:** It defines the core characteristics of atoms, such as the `atomicNumber` (number of protons), `neutronNumber`, and `nucleonNumber` (total protons and neutrons). It also includes foundational concepts like `restMass`, `atomicMass`, and the `elementaryCharge`.

*   **Quantum Mechanics:** The system defines essential quantum properties that govern particle behavior at the atomic scale. This includes the intrinsic `spin` of a particle, its `totalAngularMomentum`, and a variety of quantum numbers (principal, orbital, magnetic, etc.) which describe the discrete energy states and properties of electrons and nuclei.

*   **Radioactivity and Decay:** It provides a complete set of quantities to describe radioactive decay processes. These include the `decayConstant`, `halfLife` (the time for half of a sample to decay), `meanLifeTime`, and `nuclearActivity` (the rate of decay).

*   **Radiation Interaction with Matter:** A significant portion of the system is dedicated to defining how radiation interacts with materials. This covers concepts crucial for fields like nuclear engineering, medical physics, and radiation protection. Key quantities include `crossSection` (the probability of an interaction), `linearEnergyTransfer` (energy deposited by a particle along its path), `absorbedDose` (energy absorbed per unit mass, measured in Grays), `doseEquivalent` (a measure of biological harm, measured in Sieverts), and `kerma` (kinetic energy released in matter).

In essence, this system serves as a foundational dictionary for scientists and engineers, ensuring that when they model or discuss concepts like "nuclear radius," "mass defect," or "cyclotron frequency," they are using a common, unambiguous, and standardized set of definitions and units.
