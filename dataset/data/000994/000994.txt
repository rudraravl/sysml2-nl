This model describes an electronic boost gauge system for a vehicle, typically one with a turbocharged or supercharged engine. Its purpose is to measure the air pressure in the engine's intake manifold, process that data, and provide the driver with a real-time visual display of the boost level, along with a crucial safety warning for over-pressure conditions.

The system is composed of several key physical components that work in sequence. The process starts at the engine's **Intake Manifold**, which is the source of the pressurized air. A **Boost Pressure Sensor** is attached to the manifold to physically measure this pressure. This sensor acts as a transducer, converting the measured air pressure into a proportional electrical voltage signal.

This voltage signal is then transmitted to a **Signal Processor**, which serves as the system's electronic brain. The processor takes the raw voltage, converts it back into a precise pressure value (e.g., in kPa), and applies a filtering algorithm to smooth out the reading for a more stable and readable display. Crucially, this processor continuously compares the measured pressure against a pre-set safety threshold.

Based on its analysis, the processor sends final data to the **Gauge Display** located in the vehicle's cockpit. This display has two primary states of operation. In its normal state, it shows the current boost pressure, likely using a moving needle or a digital readout. However, if the processor signals that the pressure has exceeded the safety threshold, the display switches to a warning state, activating a distinct warning light to immediately alert the driver of the potentially dangerous "overboost" condition. The display reverts to its normal state once the pressure drops back to a safe level.

Overall, this is a complete monitoring and warning system designed for performance vehicles, ensuring the driver can monitor engine performance while being alerted to conditions that could cause engine damage. The system is engineered to be accurate, responsive (updating the display within 50 milliseconds of a pressure change), and reliable in its warning function.
