This model represents an emergency medical tourniquet designed to rapidly control severe bleeding from a limb in trauma situations. Its primary purpose is to save lives by stopping catastrophic hemorrhage until a patient can receive professional medical care.

The device is composed of several key mechanical components that work together to apply circumferential pressure. It features a high-strength nylon strap that is wrapped around the injured limb. A rigid windlass rod is used as a lever to twist and tighten the strap, generating the high tension needed to stop blood flow. Once the necessary pressure is achieved, the windlass is locked into place by a securing clip, which is designed to hold the tension firmly without slipping for an extended period. A pressure pad is integrated into the strap to distribute the applied force effectively over the artery.

Operationally, the tourniquet is designed for fast, intuitive, and often one-handed application, accommodating scenarios where a person must apply it to themselves. The process begins by positioning the device on the limb and securing the strap. The user then rotates the windlass repeatedly, progressively increasing pressure. The system's goal is to tighten until arterial blood flow is completely occluded. After each turn, the user would check for the cessation of bleeding. Once the bleeding stops, the windlass is locked in the clip, and the time of application is noted.

The tourniquet operates through distinct states, starting as stowed and ready for deployment. Upon use, it moves through positioning, tightening, and monitoring until it is fully secured on the limb. It is engineered to apply a pressure strong enough to stop arterial flow (typically 250-450 mmHg) but within a range that minimizes unnecessary tissue damage. The entire system is built to be a reliable, single-use, life-saving tool for first responders and individuals in emergency settings.
