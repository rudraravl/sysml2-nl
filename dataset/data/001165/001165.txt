This model represents a high-speed optical fiber communication system, the kind that forms the backbone of the internet and modern telecommunications networks. Its primary purpose is to transmit digital data over long distances by converting electrical signals into light, sending that light through a glass fiber cable, and then converting it back into an electrical signal at the receiving end.

The system is composed of three main parts: a transmitter, an optical fiber, and a receiver.

The **Optical Transmitter** takes an incoming electrical data signal and prepares it for transmission. It uses a laser diode to generate a steady beam of light at a specific wavelength (1550 nm). An optical modulator then imprints the data from the electrical signal onto this light beam, effectively turning the data into a series of modulated light pulses.

This light signal is then sent into the **Optical Fiber**, a 20-kilometer-long cable. The fiber itself consists of a central glass core surrounded by a layer of cladding with a different refractive index. This structure traps the light signal within the core through total internal reflection, allowing it to travel long distances with minimal loss. However, over the distance, the signal naturally weakens (attenuates) and can spread out (disperse), which the system is designed to account for.

Finally, the weakened light signal arrives at the **Optical Receiver**. Here, a photodetector converts the incoming light pulses back into a weak electrical current. This current is then boosted by a signal amplifier to restore it to a usable level, recreating the original electrical data signal.

The system operates in different states, including an `idle` state when not in use and an `active` state during data transmission. While active, it continuously monitors its own performance. If the signal quality drops, it can enter a `degraded` operational state, indicating a potential problem, and can return to `normal` operation if the quality is restored. This entire process is governed by strict performance, signal integrity, and reliability requirements, ensuring it can transmit data at very high speeds (at least 10 GHz) with an extremely low error rate (less than one error per trillion bits) and operate reliably under harsh environmental conditions.
