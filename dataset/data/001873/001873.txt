This model describes a complete Stirling engine system, designed to convert thermal energy into continuous rotational mechanical power. The system operates by exploiting a temperature difference between a dedicated heat source and a heat sink.

At its core is the Stirling engine itself, which contains a sealed amount of working gas. The engine's main components work together to execute a four-stage thermodynamic cycle. It features a hot-side **Expansion Chamber**, where the gas is heated, and a cold-side **Compression Chamber**, where it is cooled. A **Displacer Piston** is responsible for shuttling the working gas between these two chambers. As the gas expands and contracts due to the temperature changes, it drives a **Power Piston**, which generates linear force.

A key component for efficiency is the **Regenerator**, which sits between the hot and cold chambers. It acts like a thermal sponge, absorbing heat from the gas as it moves to the cold side and releasing that stored heat back to the gas as it returns to the hot side, significantly reducing the energy needed from the external heat source.

The linear motion of the Power Piston is converted into rotation by a **Crankshaft**. To ensure a smooth and continuous power output, a **Flywheel** is connected to the crankshaft. The flywheel's inertia smooths out the distinct power pulses from each cycle, delivering stable mechanical power.

The entire system is powered by an external **Heat Source** (e.g., burning fuel, solar concentration) and rejects waste heat into a **Heat Sink** (e.g., ambient air, a cooling system). The engine will operate continuously as long as this temperature difference is maintained, making it a versatile external combustion engine.
