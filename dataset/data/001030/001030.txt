This model describes a Doppler Radar System designed to detect and track moving objects by measuring their distance (range) and their speed relative to the system (radial velocity).

The system operates by transmitting a radio frequency signal and analyzing the characteristics of the echo that reflects off a target. The time it takes for the signal to travel to the target and return determines the target's range, while the change in the signal's frequency—known as the Doppler effect—reveals how fast the target is moving towards or away from the radar.

The system is composed of several key components that work together:

*   A **System Controller** manages the overall operation, allowing an operator to select different modes like continuous scanning, sending out pulses, or tracking a specific target.
*   The **RF Transmitter**, following commands from the controller, generates a high-frequency radio signal at a specific power level.
*   An **Antenna Assembly** directs this signal into the environment and also captures the faint returning echoes that have bounced off objects.
*   The **RF Receiver** is a highly sensitive unit that takes the weak echo from the antenna, amplifies it, and filters out background noise to create a clean signal for analysis.
*   The heart of the system is the **Doppler Processor**. It simultaneously receives a reference of the original signal from the transmitter and the processed echo from the receiver. By comparing these two, it calculates the target's range based on the signal's round-trip time and its velocity based on the frequency shift. It also assesses the signal strength to determine a detection confidence level.
*   Finally, a **Display Unit** receives the calculated information—range, velocity, and signal strength—and presents it to an operator in a readable format.

This entire process happens very rapidly, updating at least 20 times per second, allowing the system to provide continuous, real-time tracking of targets within its operational range of up to 10 kilometers.
