This model describes a formal framework for conducting and documenting analytical studies or investigations. It's not a physical object, but rather a structured, reusable process or methodology for performing an analysis on a specific subject.

The central concept is the "Analysis Case," which represents a single, complete study. Each analysis is focused on a particular "subject"—the system, component, or process being investigated.

An Analysis Case is composed of a series of "Analysis Steps," which are the individual actions taken to carry out the study. Some of these steps may be specific "Analysis Calculations," such as running a simulation, performing a statistical test, or computing a key performance metric.

For more complex investigations, a main Analysis Case can be broken down into several smaller, nested "sub-analyses." This allows a large-scale study, like a full vehicle safety assessment, to be organized into more manageable parts, such as separate analyses for crashworthiness, braking performance, and airbag deployment.

The ultimate goal of every Analysis Case is to produce a formal "result," which is based on an "evaluation" of the findings. This ensures that every study, no matter how simple or complex, concludes with a clear, documented outcome.
