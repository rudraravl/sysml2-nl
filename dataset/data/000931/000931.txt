This model describes a complete, self-contained hydraulic power subsystem, often known as a Hydraulic Power Unit (HPU). Its primary function is to draw hydraulic fluid from a reservoir, pressurize it using a motor-driven pump, and deliver a controlled flow of high-pressure fluid to an outlet for powering external machinery.

The core of the system is a **rotary vane pump** driven by a three-phase **electric motor**. The pump works by using an eccentrically mounted rotor with sliding vanes made of carbon composite. As the rotor spins, these vanes slide in and out, creating expanding chambers that suck in low-pressure fluid from an inlet and contracting chambers that compress the fluid and force it out at high pressure.

The hydraulic fluid is stored in a **reservoir**, which acts as a storage tank. Before entering the pump, the fluid passes through a **check valve** to prevent backflow and an **inlet filter** to remove larger contaminants. After being pressurized by the pump, the fluid is cleaned again by a high-pressure **outlet filter** to ensure its purity.

A critical safety component is the **pressure relief valve**. This spring-loaded valve constantly monitors the system pressure. If the pressure exceeds a safe threshold, the valve opens and diverts excess fluid back to the reservoir, protecting the pump and other components from damage.

The entire subsystem is equipped with a suite of sensors for monitoring and control. **Pressure sensors** are placed at both the pump's inlet and the system's final discharge point. **Temperature sensors** monitor the fluid's operating temperature, and a **flow meter** measures the final output flow rate.

The system is designed to operate through various states, including being idle, priming itself with fluid, running normally, and shutting down. While running, it can actively monitor for adverse conditions such as high pressure, low flow, or overheating. It also has built-in fault detection for critical failures like fluid cavitation (air bubble formation), motor overload, or mechanical damage.
