This model describes a high-precision gravimeter, a scientific instrument designed to measure the local gravitational acceleration of the Earth. Its primary purpose is to continuously detect and record subtle variations in the gravitational field for applications in geophysics, resource exploration, and geodesy.

The core of the instrument is a physical sensing mechanism consisting of a suspended mass, known as a **Proof Mass**, and a sensitive **Spring System**. Changes in the local gravitational pull cause the proof mass to move slightly. This minute displacement is detected by a **Displacement Sensor**, which converts the physical movement into a raw electrical voltage signal.

This raw signal is then processed through a series of electronic components. First, a **Signal Conditioner** amplifies the weak voltage and filters out unwanted noise. Next, an **Analog-to-Digital Converter** transforms the cleaned-up analog signal into a digital format. A **Data Processor** then takes this digital value, applies calibration coefficients, and performs temperature compensation to calculate a highly accurate and precise final gravity value.

The resulting measurement, which includes the gravitational acceleration, a timestamp, and temperature, is formatted into a `GravityReading` and sent to a **Data Logger** for storage or output. The entire system is powered by a dedicated **Power Supply** that provides regulated voltage to all subsystems, while a **Control Unit** manages the overall operation, responding to external commands and reporting the instrument's status.
