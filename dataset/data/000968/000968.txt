This is a modern Airspeed Indicator System for an aircraft, designed to measure and display the aircraft's speed relative to the surrounding air. The system provides pilots with critical flight information and includes integrated safety warnings.

The system's operation begins with two primary air pressure sensors. A **Pitot tube**, which points forward into the airstream, measures the total air pressure created by the aircraft's motion. Simultaneously, **static pressure ports** located on the fuselage measure the ambient, undisturbed air pressure. For safety in icing conditions, the Pitot tube is equipped with a heating element to prevent ice buildup.

These two pressure readings are sent to a **pressure transducer**, which calculates the difference between them. This difference, known as dynamic pressure, is directly proportional to the aircraft's speed. The transducer converts this pressure differential into an electronic signal.

This signal is then fed into an **Air Data Computer**, the "brain" of the system. The computer processes the pressure data to calculate the aircraft's "Indicated Airspeed" (IAS), which is the primary speed reference for the pilot. Using additional environmental data such as air density, temperature, and altitude, the computer can also calculate the "True Airspeed" (TAS), which is the aircraft's actual speed through the air mass.

Finally, the calculated airspeed is sent to a digital **Cockpit Display**. Upon powering on, the display runs a brief self-test before entering its normal operational state, where it continuously shows the Indicated Airspeed to the pilot. The system also actively monitors the flight speed to provide stall warnings if the aircraft is flying too slowly and overspeed warnings if it is flying too fast.
