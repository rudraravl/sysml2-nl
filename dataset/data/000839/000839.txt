This model describes an Acid Egg system, a specialized piece of industrial equipment designed for the safe, batch-transfer of corrosive fluids. It essentially acts as a simple, robust pump with no moving parts in contact with the fluid, making it ideal for handling aggressive chemicals.

The heart of the system is a central pressure vessel, which is designed to hold a specific volume of corrosive liquid. This vessel is connected to four key valves that control the operational cycle: an inlet valve, an outlet valve, a gas valve, and a vent valve.

The system's operation follows a precise, four-stage cycle to move the fluid:
1.  **Filling:** The inlet valve opens, allowing the corrosive fluid from a source to flow into the pressure vessel. A level sensor monitors the volume until the target amount is reached, at which point the inlet valve closes.
2.  **Pressurizing:** The gas valve opens, feeding a pressurized gas (like compressed air or nitrogen) from a supply into the vessel. This gas pushes down on the surface of the liquid. A pressure sensor ensures the vessel reaches the correct operating pressure before the next stage.
3.  **Discharging:** The outlet valve opens, and the built-up gas pressure forces the corrosive fluid out of the vessel and towards its destination.
4.  **Venting:** Once the transfer is complete, the outlet and gas valves close. The vent valve then opens to safely release the remaining gas pressure from the vessel, making it ready for the next cycle.

Safety and precision are critical design considerations. All components that come into contact with the fluid must be made from corrosion-resistant materials. The pressure vessel is built to withstand pressures significantly higher than its normal operating pressure to provide a safety margin. Operationally, the system is required to be highly accurate, transferring a volume of fluid that is within ±2% of the specified target.
