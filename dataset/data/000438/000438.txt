This model describes a laboratory cytocentrifuge system used for preparing biological samples for microscopic analysis. The primary function of this machine is to take a liquid biological sample, which may have a low cell count, and concentrate the cells onto a small, defined area of a microscope slide, forming a thin, uniform layer.

The system is built around a central, high-speed `RotorAssembly` that can hold and process up to twelve samples simultaneously. Each sample is contained within a dedicated `sampleChamber` assembly, which consists of the liquid sample itself, a specialized `FilterCard`, and a standard microscope slide held in a `SlideCarrier`.

The process is driven by a powerful `DriveMotor` that spins the rotor. A central `ControllerUnit` acts as the brain of the system, managing the entire operational cycle. A technician interacts with the machine through a `UserPanel`, where they can select a speed mode (such as low, medium, or high) and start the process.

During operation, the motor accelerates the rotor to a precise, user-defined rotational speed. The centrifugal force pushes the liquid sample through the `FilterCard`. The card's membrane has microscopic pores that allow the fluid to pass through and be absorbed by a pad, but are small enough to trap the cells. These captured cells are gently deposited onto the surface of the adjacent microscope slide.

A critical component is the integrated `SafetySystem`, which prevents operation unless the lid is securely locked and the rotor is properly balanced. It continuously monitors for excessive vibration and includes an emergency stop feature.

The system operates through a clear sequence of states: it starts in an `idle` state, moves to `loading` as samples are inserted, then proceeds through `accelerating`, `spinning` for a set duration, and `decelerating`. Once the rotor has stopped, the cycle is `complete`, and the prepared slides, now containing a high-quality monolayer of cells, can be safely removed for staining and diagnostic analysis.
