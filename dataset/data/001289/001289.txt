This model represents a Time Pressure Gauge, a system designed to monitor and quantify the stress associated with deadlines and workloads, such as in a project management or operational context. Its primary function is to provide a real-time, quantitative measure of the "pressure" a team or individual is under by continuously evaluating the amount of work remaining against the time available to complete it.

The system is composed of several specialized components that work together:

*   A **Workload Monitor** and a **Temporal Monitor** act as the primary sensors. The Workload Monitor tracks key project metrics like the total number of tasks, how many have been completed, and the estimated effort still required. The Temporal Monitor keeps track of time, constantly comparing the current time to the project's final deadline to calculate the time remaining.

*   At the core of the system is a **Pressure Calculator**. This component acts as the system's brain, taking the live data from both the workload and temporal monitors. It synthesizes this information to compute a combined pressure score. This score is then categorized into intuitive levels, such as 'minimal,' 'low,' 'moderate,' 'high,' or 'critical,' making it easy to understand the current situation at a glance.

*   An **Alert Manager** actively watches the calculated pressure level. If the pressure crosses a predefined threshold (for example, reaching a 'high' or 'critical' state), this component automatically generates and issues alerts. These notifications serve as an early warning system, signaling that a project may be at risk of failing to meet its deadline.

*   For user interaction, a **Pressure Display** serves as the dashboard. It visualizes the current pressure score and its category, refreshing at a regular rate to provide a live, continuous view of the operational stress.

*   Finally, a **Data Recorder** logs the system's measurements over time. It periodically saves a snapshot of the pressure level, workload status, and time constraints. This historical data is valuable for later analysis, allowing for performance reviews and the identification of recurring pressure points in a workflow.
