This model describes a comprehensive systems engineering framework for the development of space systems, likely based on European Space Agency (ESA) standards. It is not a model of a specific spacecraft or satellite, but rather a structured vocabulary and methodology for defining, designing, and verifying any complex space mission.

The framework covers the entire lifecycle of a mission, breaking it down into distinct phases, each with an expected duration. It begins by capturing high-level concepts like the overall **Mission Objectives** and the needs of various **Stakeholders** (such as scientists or commercial clients).

The core of the framework is the **System of Interest (SoI)**, which represents the primary system being developed, such as a satellite, rover, or ground station. The design process is broken into two main views:

1.  **The Functional View:** This describes *what the system does*. It defines abstract **Functions** (e.g., "acquire image," "transmit data") and groups them into logical **Functional Chains**. Scenarios are used to describe the dynamic behavior and interactions between these functions to achieve a specific capability.
2.  **The Physical View:** This describes *what the system is made of*. The SoI is composed of physical **Products**, which are the actual hardware and software components. These products are classified by their design heritage—whether they are brand new, modified, or off-the-shelf components, which is critical for risk and cost management. The framework also defines the various types of **Interfaces** between these products, such as electrical, thermal, structural, and data connections.

Operationally, the system is designed to exist in different **Operational Modes** (e.g., safe mode, science mode, data downlink) and transitions between them throughout the mission.

A critical part of this framework is the rigorous process for requirements and verification. Every **Requirement** is formally documented with details like its type (e.g., functional, performance, interface), its justification, and its status. Crucially, each requirement is linked to a **Verification Method** (Analysis, Inspection, Test, or Review of Design). This ensures that every demand placed on the system is formally tested and proven through structured **Verification Scenarios** and **Activities** at different stages of the project, such as on-the-ground qualification and in-orbit commissioning.
