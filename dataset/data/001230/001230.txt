This model describes a SNOTEL (SNOwpack TELemetry) station, an automated environmental monitoring system designed for deployment in remote, mountainous locations. Its primary purpose is to continuously measure snowpack, weather, and soil conditions to provide critical data for water supply forecasting, flood prediction, and climate research.

The station is a self-contained unit built around a suite of specialized sensors. It features a snow pillow, which is a pressure-sensing device that measures the weight of the overlying snow to calculate its water content, and an ultrasonic depth sensor to measure the physical depth of the snowpack. Additional sensors continuously monitor key climate variables, including air temperature, accumulated precipitation (rain and snow), soil temperature, and soil moisture content at a fixed depth below the ground.

All the data from these individual sensors is fed into a central data logger. This unit collects, timestamps, and formats the readings into standardized data packets. On a regular schedule, the data logger sends these packets to a satellite transmitter, which then broadcasts the information to a central data collection facility, often using the GOES satellite system.

To operate autonomously for long periods, the station is powered by a solar panel that charges a rechargeable battery. A dedicated power controller manages this system, distributing electricity to the data logger and transmitter while ensuring the battery remains charged for operation during the night or in poor weather conditions. The entire system operates in a continuous cycle of measuring, aggregating data, and transmitting, with built-in logic to retry transmissions if they fail, ensuring high data reliability from these critical, remote outposts.
