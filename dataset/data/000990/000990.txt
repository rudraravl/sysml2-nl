This model describes an automated Biochip Analysis System, a "lab-on-a-chip" device designed for conducting rapid biochemical assays on biological samples. Its primary function is to automatically analyze a sample, such as blood or saliva, to detect the presence and concentration of a specific target substance (an analyte).

The core of the system is the physical biochip, which integrates several components to manage the analysis process. A fluid pump precisely controls the movement of liquids through a network of tiny microfluidic channels. A biological sample is introduced into the system and guided into a reaction chamber. Here, it is mixed with a specific chemical reagent. A dedicated temperature controller maintains the chamber at a precise, stable temperature to allow the necessary biochemical reaction to occur over a set incubation period.

After incubation, the mixture flows past an optical detector. This sensor uses light at a specific wavelength to measure a resulting signal (e.g., fluorescence or a color change), which is proportional to the amount of the target analyte present. The detector is highly sensitive, designed to identify even very low concentrations.

The system operates through a clear sequence of states. It begins in an `idle` state, moves to `ready` to prepare for a new test, and upon receiving a sample, enters a `processing` state where the assay is performed. After the reaction and detection, it moves to an `analyzing` state to interpret the raw signal, and finally to a `complete` state to output the results. The used fluids are then directed to a waste port, and the system returns to `idle`, ready for the next analysis. The entire process is designed to be fast, completing an analysis in under 15 minutes, allowing for high-throughput screening of many samples. The final output is a structured assay result containing the sample's ID, the measured concentration, the signal strength, and a clear positive or negative determination.
