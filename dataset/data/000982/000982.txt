This model describes an Auxanometer, a precision instrument designed for the automated measurement and recording of plant growth. Its purpose is to continuously monitor the very slow and minute vertical growth of a plant specimen, convert it into measurable data, and store it for scientific or agricultural analysis.

The system functions through a combination of mechanical amplification and electronic sensing. A delicate attachment point rests on the apex of the growing plant. As the plant elongates, this tiny vertical movement is transferred to a lever arm, which magnifies the displacement to a more easily measurable scale. This amplified linear motion is then used to turn a pulley.

The rotation of the pulley is tracked by two methods. For digital recording, a high-resolution rotary encoder measures the precise angle of rotation. This electronic data is sent to a data acquisition unit, which calculates the plant's linear displacement and growth rate at regular intervals. These time-stamped measurements are then passed to a recording device for long-term storage. For direct visual inspection, a physical pointer attached to the mechanism moves along a graduated scale, providing an immediate, at-a-glance reading of the total growth.

The device operates through several states: it can be idle, in a calibration phase to establish a baseline, actively measuring, or paused. An operator can issue commands to start, stop, pause, or resume the measurement process, making it a flexible tool for various experimental setups.
