This is an automated industrial fluid transport system designed to move a liquid from a source to a destination at a precisely controlled rate and pressure. The system is built around a central centrifugal pump and includes all the necessary piping, valves, sensors, and a controller for autonomous operation.

The core of the system is the main pump, which is composed of several key mechanical components. An electric motor provides the power, turning a stainless steel shaft. This shaft is supported by two different types of bearings (one radial, one thrust) to handle the forces of rotation and fluid pressure. The rotating shaft spins a six-bladed impeller, which is the component that actually transfers energy to the fluid. This entire assembly is enclosed within a durable pump casing, which directs the fluid from an inlet to the impeller and then out through a narrower outlet. Two mechanical seals are used to prevent the liquid from leaking out where the shaft enters the casing.

The system operates as a complete circuit. Fluid begins at a source tank and flows through a suction pipe and an isolation valve before entering the pump. The pump then increases the fluid's pressure and pushes it out into a discharge pipe. This discharge line is fitted with another valve for flow regulation, a flow meter, and a pressure sensor before the fluid finally reaches its destination.

A dedicated controller serves as the brain of the system. It continuously monitors the output flow rate and pressure using the installed sensors. Based on a target setpoint, the controller adjusts the speed of the pump's motor to ensure the fluid is delivered at the correct rate.

The system operates through several distinct states. It remains in an `idle` state until it receives a `start` command. It then enters a `starting` phase as the motor spins up. Once at speed, it runs in a normal `operating` state. The system is smart enough to detect a `low-flow` condition and can transition into and out of this state. Upon receiving a `stop` command, it enters a `stopping` sequence. It also includes a `fault` state for safety, which requires a manual reset to clear.
