This SysML model represents a formal, standardized library of physical quantities, units, and definitions for the scientific domain of **thermodynamics**. It is not a model of a physical machine or object, but rather a foundational, machine-readable dictionary based on the international standard **ISO 80000-5**. Its purpose is to provide a precise and unambiguous set of concepts for use in engineering, physics, and system simulation.

The library defines the core concepts needed to describe and analyze systems involving heat and energy. This includes:

*   **Temperature and Expansion:** It defines fundamental temperature scales like Kelvin (thermodynamic temperature) and Celsius, as well as material properties that describe how objects change size with temperature, such as the linear and cubic expansion coefficients.
*   **Heat and Energy Transfer:** It formalizes the concepts of heat, heat flow rate (how fast heat moves), and properties that govern heat transfer, including thermal conductivity (a material's ability to conduct heat), thermal resistance (its ability to insulate), and the coefficient of heat transfer.
*   **Thermodynamic Properties and State Functions:** The model defines critical state functions that describe the energy state of a system, such as internal energy, enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs energy, and Helmholtz energy. It also includes related properties like heat capacity (the energy required to change a substance's temperature) and compressibility.
*   **Humidity and Gas Mixtures:** It provides precise definitions for measuring water content in gases, including relative and absolute humidity, dew-point temperature, and various mass concentrations and ratios related to water vapor in the air.
*   **Efficiency and Processes:** The library includes concepts for analyzing thermodynamic processes, such as the Joule-Thomson coefficient (related to gas cooling upon expansion) and thermal efficiency, which measures how effectively a heat engine converts heat into useful work.

In essence, this is a foundational toolkit that allows engineers and scientists to build models of real-world systems (like engines, power plants, or HVAC systems) with a shared, standard-compliant understanding of all the thermodynamic principles involved.
