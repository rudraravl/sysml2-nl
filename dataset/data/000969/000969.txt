This is a real-time Air-Fuel Ratio (AFR) Meter System, designed to be installed in a vehicle to measure and display the ratio of air to fuel being burned in an internal combustion engine. This type of system is critical for engine tuning, maximizing performance, and ensuring fuel efficiency.

The system is composed of four main components that work together sequentially. First, a **Wideband Oxygen Sensor** is placed directly in the vehicle's exhaust stream. This sensor has an internal heater that brings it up to its required operating temperature. Once active, it continuously measures the oxygen concentration in the hot exhaust gases and generates a corresponding voltage signal.

This raw voltage signal is then sent to a **Signal Conditioner**. This component's job is to clean up the signal by filtering out electrical noise and then amplifying it, ensuring that the signal sent to the main processor is strong and stable.

Next, an **AFR Processor** receives the clean, conditioned voltage signal. This unit acts as the brain of the system, using a pre-programmed conversion table to translate the voltage into a precise air-fuel ratio. It also calculates the lambda value and determines if the current engine mixture is "lean" (too much air) or "rich" (too much fuel).

Finally, the processed data is sent to a **Display Controller**, which drives a screen or gauge for the user. This component formats the AFR data for easy reading and updates the display in real-time. It can also control specific indicators to visually alert the user to lean or rich conditions. The entire system is designed for high performance, with requirements for fast response times (updating within 100 milliseconds) and high accuracy across a wide range of exhaust temperatures.
