This model describes a medical fluid delivery system designed for safely transferring liquids like medication into a patient's IV line without the use of needles. The system's primary goal is to prevent needlestick injuries for healthcare workers and maintain a sterile, closed pathway to protect the patient from infection.

The central component is the **Needleless Connector**, a specialized valve that acts as a secure access port. This connector has two ends: a proximal port that connects to a fluid source (like an IV bag or syringe) and a distal port that connects to the patient's IV line.

Internally, the connector contains several key parts. A **mechanical valve** normally keeps the fluid channel sealed. To transfer fluid, a clinician attaches a **Syringe Adapter** (typically a standard Luer-Lock type) to the connector. This requires a specific amount of force to engage the **activation mechanism**, which in turn opens the valve and allows fluid to flow through the internal channel. The connector's surface is protected by an **antimicrobial barrier** to ensure sterility is maintained each time it is accessed.

The complete system shows how these parts work together: a **Fluid Reservoir** (like an IV bag) is connected to the needleless connector, which is then attached to the patient's **IV line**. The overall process of administering medication involves securely connecting a device, transferring a specific volume of fluid, and then disconnecting. A key feature is that upon disconnection, the connector's internal valve automatically closes and seals itself, preventing any leakage and keeping the fluid path sterile for the next use.
