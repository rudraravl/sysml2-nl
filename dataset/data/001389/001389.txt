This model describes a complete continuum robotic system, designed for complex manipulation, inspection, and navigation tasks. The centerpiece is a flexible, snake-like continuum robot arm, which is approximately 600mm long and composed of three distinct, bendable segments. Unlike a traditional robot with rigid joints, this arm achieves motion by bending and curving its segments.

Movement is precisely controlled by a set of nine pneumatic actuators, with three dedicated to each segment, allowing it to curve and orient itself in any direction within its workspace. To understand its own position and shape, the robot is equipped with three shape sensors, one for each segment.

At the tip of the arm is a two-fingered gripper, or end-effector, designed for grasping and manipulating objects up to 2 kg. This gripper is equipped with its own force sensor to carefully control its grasp and prevent damage to payloads.

The entire system is orchestrated by a dedicated control computer that serves as the brain. This computer continuously runs a control loop, processing feedback from the robot's shape and force sensors, planning the optimal trajectory to a target, and sending commands to the pneumatic actuators to execute the movement. The system is powered by a high-capacity power supply that provides the necessary voltage and current.

Operationally, the system transitions through several states. It begins in an idle mode, moves to a calibration state upon initialization, and then enters a ready state to await tasks. When a task is assigned, it transitions to an executing state, performing its function until it is completed, at which point it returns to ready. A fault state is included to handle any operational errors, requiring a reset to resume functioning. The system is designed to meet specific performance goals, including a positioning accuracy of 2mm, a payload capacity of 2kg, and a high rate of task success and overall reliability.
