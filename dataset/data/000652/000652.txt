This model represents a complete digital radiography system, the kind of modern X-ray machine used in hospitals and clinics for producing diagnostic medical images. Its core function is to generate a controlled beam of X-rays, pass it through a patient's body, capture the resulting image on a digital detector, and process it for a radiologist to review.

The system is comprised of several key physical components working in concert. At the heart of the imaging chain is the **X-Ray Tube**, which produces the radiation, and a powerful **High Voltage Generator** that provides the necessary electrical power. To prevent overheating during use, a dedicated **Cooling System** circulates a coolant like a water-glycol mixture to manage the immense heat generated by the tube.

Before the X-rays reach the patient, they pass through a **Beam Collimator**, an adjustable device with movable blades that shapes the radiation beam to expose only the specific anatomical area of interest, minimizing unnecessary radiation exposure. The patient is positioned for the scan on a motorized **Patient Positioning Table** that can be adjusted in height and position to precisely align the target anatomy with the X-ray beam and detector.

After passing through the patient, the X-ray pattern is captured by a high-efficiency **Digital Detector**, a flat panel that converts the radiation into raw digital data. This raw data is then sent to an **Image Processing Unit**, which applies sophisticated algorithms and calibration data to correct, enhance, and filter the image, turning it into a clear, high-quality diagnostic radiograph.

A critical component is the **Dose Management System**, which calculates the estimated radiation dose before the exposure and monitors the cumulative dose to ensure patient safety and adherence to ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principles. It can generate dose reports for the patient's medical record.

The entire operation is managed by a technician from an **Operator Workstation**. From this console, the operator selects the appropriate imaging protocol, positions the patient using the table controls, initiates the exposure, and then reviews the final processed image on a display. The workstation is also used to archive the image and dose report to a hospital's network.

The system operates through a clear sequence of states: it begins in **standby**, moves to **preparation** as the operator sets up the patient and exposure parameters, then enters a **ready** state. Upon the operator's command, it enters the **exposing** state, followed by **acquiring** and **processing** the image data. Finally, the image is presented for **review**, and if accepted, it is **archived**, after which the system returns to standby. An emergency state is also included to handle events like an accidental overdose.
