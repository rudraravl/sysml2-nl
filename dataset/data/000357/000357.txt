This model represents a comprehensive, standardized library of physical quantities used to describe light and electromagnetic radiation. It is not a physical object itself, but rather a formal digital dictionary based on the international standard ISO 80000-7 ("Light"). The purpose of this library is to provide precise, unambiguous definitions for scientists and engineers to use when modeling or analyzing systems involving light, such as cameras, sensors, lighting systems, and displays.

The library organizes the properties of light into several related categories:

1.  **Radiometry:** This category describes light in terms of its raw physical energy, independent of how it's perceived. It includes fundamental concepts like **radiant energy** (the total energy of the radiation), **radiant flux** (the rate of energy flow, or power, measured in Watts), **irradiance** (the power hitting a surface per unit area), and **radiance** (the power emitted or reflected from a surface in a particular direction).

2.  **Photometry:** This category describes light based on how the average human eye perceives its brightness. It defines quantities that are parallels to the radiometric ones but are weighted by the human eye's sensitivity to different colors. Key concepts include **luminous flux** (the perceived power of light, measured in lumens), **illuminance** (the amount of light illuminating a surface, measured in lux), and **luminance** (the perceived brightness of a surface from a certain angle).

3.  **Photon Quantities:** This provides another way to describe light by counting the number of photons instead of measuring total energy. It defines quantities like **photon flux** (the number of photons flowing per second) and **photon irradiance** (the number of photons hitting a surface per unit area per second).

4.  **Material Interaction:** This section defines how materials and surfaces interact with light. It includes properties like **reflectance** (the fraction of light reflected), **absorptance** (the fraction absorbed), and **transmittance** (the fraction that passes through). It also defines how light is attenuated or absorbed as it travels through a medium.

5.  **Colorimetry:** This specialized area defines quantities for measuring and specifying color as perceived by a human. It includes the standardized **CIE tristimulus values (X,Y,Z)**, which form the basis of modern color science, as well as concepts like **color temperature** to describe the "warmth" or "coolness" of a light source.

For many of these quantities, the library also defines a "spectral" version (e.g., spectral radiance, spectral irradiance), which describes how the quantity is distributed across different wavelengths or colors of light. This allows for a much more detailed and complete analysis of light and color.
