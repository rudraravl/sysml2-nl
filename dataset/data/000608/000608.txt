This describes a flexible fiberscope system designed for remote visual inspection of confined spaces and internal cavities, such as in machinery, engines, or medical procedures, where direct observation is impossible. The system works by channeling light down a flexible tube to illuminate a target area, capturing the reflected image with a lens at the tip, and transmitting this image back through a bundle of optical fibers to an eyepiece for the operator to view.

The system is composed of several key components that work in sequence:

First, a high-intensity **illumination source** generates bright, daylight-like light (150W, 5500K) to ensure the target area is well-lit. This light is directed into the **flexible insertion tube**, a long (1.2 meters) and narrow (5.5 mm) probe designed to be maneuvered through tight and complex pathways. This tube contains a bundle of optical fibers dedicated to carrying the illumination light to the tip.

At the very tip of the insertion tube is an **objective lens assembly**. It takes the light emerging from the tube, projects it onto the target, and then captures the reflected visual image from the inspection area with a wide 75-degree field of view.

To precisely aim this lens, the operator uses an **articulation controller**, which is a four-way lever. This control allows the operator to bend and steer the distal tip of the tube by up to 120 degrees, enabling comprehensive viewing from various angles.

The image captured by the lens is then transmitted back through a separate, coherent bundle of 18,000 optical fibers within the same insertion tube. This bundle relays the image to the **eyepiece viewer**, where the operator can see the final, magnified (12x) view of the inspected area.

The fiberscope operates through a clear sequence of states. It begins in an idle state, then moves to an 'inserting' state as the operator guides the tube to the target depth. It then enters a 'positioning' state where the articulation controls are used to aim the tip. Once the view is correct, it enters an 'imaging' state for detailed inspection. After the task is complete, it is withdrawn and returns to its idle state.
