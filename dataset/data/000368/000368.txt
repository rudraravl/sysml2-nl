This model represents a comprehensive, standardized digital library of physical quantities and units for the fields of physical chemistry and molecular physics. It is not a physical object but rather a formal data framework designed to provide precise, unambiguous, and machine-readable definitions for scientific concepts, based on the international standard ISO 80000-9.

The library defines a wide array of fundamental and derived quantities. This includes core concepts like the 'amount of substance' (moles), 'molar mass' (mass per mole), and 'relative atomic mass'. It details the properties of chemical mixtures, defining various ways to express composition, such as 'mass concentration', 'volume fraction', and 'molality'.

The system also provides a structured vocabulary for thermodynamics, covering properties like 'molar internal energy', 'molar enthalpy', 'molar Gibbs energy', and 'molar heat capacity', which are crucial for describing the energy and stability of chemical systems. It extends to the concepts governing chemical reactions, defining terms like 'stoichiometric number', 'affinity', 'extent of reaction', and various 'equilibrium constants'.

Furthermore, the library describes quantities related to transport phenomena, such as 'diffusion coefficient' and 'mean free path', as well as electrochemical properties like 'ionic strength', 'electrolytic conductivity', and 'molar conductivity'. For each quantity, the system formally specifies its relationship to fundamental base dimensions (like mass, length, time, and amount of substance), ensuring that all quantities and their corresponding units (e.g., grams per mole, joules per mole-kelvin) are consistently and correctly defined.

Ultimately, this system serves as a foundational reference model for scientific and engineering software, enabling accurate simulation, analysis, and data exchange in any application that deals with the properties and behavior of chemical and molecular systems.
